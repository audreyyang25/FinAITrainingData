Topic: unsuitable_variable_annuity_elderly_client
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Adviser Kathryn Sloane recommended that a 79-year-old widow with moderate cognitive decline, a $310,000 IRA, and a twelve-month life expectancy due to terminal illness purchase a B-share variable annuity with a seven-year surrender charge period and high annual mortality and expense fees of 1.95%. Sloane documented the recommendation in a suitability form that listed the client's risk tolerance as 'moderate growth' after conducting a fifteen-minute phone conversation, but did not consult the client's power of attorney or physician despite awareness of the diagnosis. The annuity's deferred tax benefit was redundant given the IRA wrapper, eliminating the primary rationale for the product. Sloane earned a 6.5% commission of $20,150 on the transaction.

QUESTION
--------
Did Sloane's recommendation of the variable annuity violate the Investment Advisers Act's suitability and antifraud provisions?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Investment Advisers Act §206(1) and §206(2) for fraudulent and unsuitable recommendations made without reasonable basis.

ALJs applying §206 have found that recommending a long-surrender-period annuity to a terminally ill elderly client with no realistic prospect of surviving the surrender period demonstrates a complete absence of reasonable basis and evidences scienter sufficient for a §206(1) fraud finding. The tax-deferral redundancy inside an IRA, combined with the 1.95% M&E drag and seven-year illiquidity horizon, rendered the product objectively unsuitable regardless of how the suitability form was completed. The failure to involve the client's POA or assess cognitive capacity when recommending a large, complex, illiquid transaction further supports a finding of willful disregard of the client's interests.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act §206(1), §206(2); FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability, by analogy); SEC Administrative Proceeding File No. 3-16349

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommending an illiquid annuity with a surrender period exceeding a terminal client's life expectancy, inside a tax-deferred account, with no independent capacity assessment, constitutes fraud under §206(1).
