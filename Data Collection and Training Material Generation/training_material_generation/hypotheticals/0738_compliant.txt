Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Robert Weitzel III, a 55-year-old experienced investor and former CFO with a $2.8 million UBS brokerage account, filed a FINRA arbitration claim alleging his UBS financial advisor made unsuitable recommendations by allocating 40% of his portfolio to structured notes and alternative investments. During discovery, UBS produced signed new account documentation showing Weitzel selected 'aggressive growth' as his investment objective and 'high' risk tolerance, and acknowledged in writing that he understood the illiquidity and risks of structured products. UBS also produced email correspondence in which Weitzel actively directed his advisor to increase his structured note allocation, rejected the advisor's suggestion to diversify into investment-grade bonds, and discussed his own independent research on specific structured product issuers. The advisor's notes documented each conversation, and a branch manager conducted and documented a suitability review at account opening and at each subsequent annual review, confirming the allocation was consistent with Weitzel's stated objectives and financial profile, which included $4.5 million in additional liquid assets held elsewhere.

QUESTION
--------
Does the firm have a viable defense against the customer's unsuitability claim given the documented client sophistication and affirmative direction?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Under FINRA Rule 2111, a recommendation must be suitable based on the customer's investment profile, including objectives, risk tolerance, financial situation, and sophistication. Here, the advisor's recommendations were consistent with the client's documented aggressive growth objective and high risk tolerance. The client's sophistication as a former CFO, his affirmative written direction to increase the structured note allocation over his advisor's suggestion to diversify, and his substantial additional liquid assets collectively support the firm's defense. Thorough contemporaneous documentation of supervisory reviews and client communications provides strong evidence that the firm met all three prongs of its suitability obligation. FINRA arbitration panels regularly consider client sophistication and active participation in investment decisions as factors weighing against unsuitability claims.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability) — reasonable-basis, customer-specific, and quantitative suitability obligations

KEY FACTOR
----------
The client's documented sophistication, affirmative written direction overriding the advisor's more conservative suggestions, and comprehensive supervisory review records provide a strong defense to the unsuitability claim.
