Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Bridgewater Advisory Group, a Michigan state-registered investment adviser managing $62 million, was selected for its first-ever coordinated state examination during the February 1 to July 31, 2025 NASAA examination cycle. During the exam, state examiners discovered that the firm's two investment adviser representatives had been recommending a proprietary private placement fund (Bridgewater Income Fund LP) to 31 clients, allocating between 25% and 40% of each client's portfolio to the fund. The fund charged a 2% management fee and 20% performance allocation paid directly to Bridgewater's principals. The firm's Form ADV Part 2A disclosed the existence of the fund but described the conflict of interest in a single sentence buried in Item 10, stating only that 'the firm may recommend affiliated products.' The firm did not disclose the specific fee structure of the private placement, the percentage of client assets allocated, or provide any comparative analysis showing why this fund was preferable to unaffiliated alternatives. Several clients were retirees over age 70 with moderate risk tolerances and income-focused investment objectives.

QUESTION
--------
Did Bridgewater Advisory Group violate state securities laws by inadequately disclosing the material conflict of interest arising from recommending its proprietary private placement fund?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes a violation of the state investment adviser fiduciary duty and disclosure obligations under the Uniform Securities Act and NASAA Model Rules.

State-registered investment advisers owe a fiduciary duty requiring full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts of interest. NASAA Model Rule 102(a)(4)-1 identifies recommending securities in which the adviser has a financial interest without disclosing that interest as a dishonest or unethical business practice. A single vague sentence in Form ADV Part 2A is insufficient to disclose the specific nature and magnitude of the conflict—namely, that the firm's principals receive 2% management fees and 20% performance allocations from the proprietary fund. The concentrated allocations of 25-40% to an illiquid private placement for elderly, income-focused clients further exacerbates the suitability concern and underscores the inadequacy of the disclosure.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Uniform Securities Act Section 502; NASAA Model Rule 102(a)(4)-1 – Unethical Business Practices of Investment Advisers; NASAA Statement of Policy on Dishonest or Unethical Business Practices

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's generic one-sentence disclosure was materially inadequate to inform clients of the specific financial incentive its principals received from the proprietary fund, violating fiduciary disclosure obligations.
