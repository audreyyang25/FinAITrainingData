Source Folder: unknown
Topic: esg_sustainable_investing
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Robert Martinez, age 35, met with his advisor and explicitly stated: 'I don't care about ESG—just give me the best long-term returns. I want maximum growth.' His advisor acknowledged this preference in the client file. However, four months later, without any follow-up conversation, the advisor unilaterally liquidated Robert's $540,000 portfolio (80% S&P 500 Index ETF at 0.03% ER, 20% emerging markets at 0.08% ER) and reinvested 100% in three ESG-focused funds: Parnassus Core Equity (0.76% ER), Parnassus Mid-Cap (0.84% ER), and Calvert Sustainable Equity (0.72% ER). Blended ER increased from 0.04% to 0.77% (73 bps increase annually). The advisor's rationale in the compliance file: 'ESG funds provide better downside protection and align with long-term sustainability trends—good for younger clients.' Robert did not receive advance notice of the change, and only discovered it in his quarterly statement. When confronted, the advisor cited a generic 'ESG is appropriate for all young investors' policy.

QUESTION
--------
Did the advisor violate Reg BI and the duty of loyalty by overriding the client's explicit investment preference and moving to high-cost ESG funds without disclosure or consent?

ANSWER
------
Non-compliant. Clear violation on multiple counts: (1) Reg BI §240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii) requires the advisor to understand the client's investment profile and ensure the recommendation aligns with it. Robert explicitly rejected ESG preference; the advisor ignored this stated priority. (2) Investment Adviser Act §206(1) duty of loyalty: The advisor acted against Robert's best interest by increasing costs by 73 bps annually without client consent, prioritizing the advisor's 'ESG trend' strategy over the client's stated preference for maximum returns. (3) No consent: The advisor made a material portfolio change without advance disclosure or client approval—standard of care violation under Reg BI §240.15l-1(a)(1). (4) The generic 'all young investors' policy contradicts the individualized advice requirement under Reg BI. This is a straightforward case of a fiduciary self-dealing (prioritizing ESG adoption over client preference) and overriding documented client instructions. FINRA enforcement typically results in customer restitution for unauthorized changes and suitability violations.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI §240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii) (understand client profile); Reg BI §240.15l-1(a)(1) (client consent to material changes); Investment Adviser Act §206(1) (duty of loyalty); FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability—recommendation inconsistent with client profile).

KEY FACTOR
----------
Advisor overrode documented client preference for non-ESG investing, made unilateral portfolio change without notice or consent, and increased fees by 73 bps—classic unauthorized/unsuitable change violating both Reg BI and duty of loyalty.
