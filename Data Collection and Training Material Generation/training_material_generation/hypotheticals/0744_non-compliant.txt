Source Folder: 12_FINRA_Arbitration_Awards
Topic: arbitration_liability
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
LPL Financial representative Sandra Voss managed a $540,000 account for 82-year-old retired schoolteacher William Dawson, whose CRS form indicated a conservative risk tolerance and income as a primary investment objective. Between January 2020 and December 2021, Voss executed 94 trades in Dawson's account, generating a turnover ratio of 8.2 and a cost-to-equity ratio of 24.6%. The account held primarily mid-cap equity positions with average holding periods of 17 days. Dawson's account lost $127,000 in net value during this period while generating $58,000 in commissions for Voss. Voss argued in FINRA arbitration that Dawson had verbally authorized each trade via telephone and that his signed new account form listed 'growth' as a secondary objective.

QUESTION
--------
Does the trading pattern in Dawson's account constitute churning and a violation of quantitative suitability under FINRA rules?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant — the trading pattern constitutes churning and a violation of quantitative suitability.

A turnover ratio of 8.2 and a cost-to-equity ratio of 24.6% are far in excess of the thresholds FINRA and courts have consistently used to establish presumptive churning (turnover ratios above 6 and cost-to-equity ratios above 20% are generally considered excessive). The quantitative suitability component of Rule 2111 requires that the series of recommended transactions, taken together, are not excessive in light of the customer's profile. An 82-year-old conservative retiree seeking income should not bear the costs and risks of 94 trades over two years in speculative equities. Verbal authorization of individual trades does not negate churning liability when the representative exercised de facto control over the trading strategy.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) (Quantitative Suitability); SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) (Reg BI Care Obligation); Common Law Breach of Fiduciary Duty

KEY FACTOR
----------
The turnover ratio of 8.2 and cost-to-equity ratio of 24.6% far exceed recognized churning thresholds, and the representative's de facto control over the account of a conservative elderly retiree establishes quantitative unsuitability regardless of verbal trade-by-trade authorization.
