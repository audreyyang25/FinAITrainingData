Source Folder: 05_SEC_Exam_Findings
Topic: wrap_fee_programs
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Brighton Wealth Advisors, an SEC-registered adviser managing $450 million, enrolls 62-year-old client David Park in its wrap fee program charging 1.50% annually on his $620,000 IRA rollover. David's stated investment objective is capital preservation, and his account is invested 80% in a laddered municipal bond portfolio and 20% in a money market fund. Over a 14-month review period, the account executed only three trades—two bond maturities that were reinvested and one rebalancing transaction. The firm conducted no periodic assessment of whether the wrap fee program remained suitable given David's low trading activity, and the firm's compliance manual contains no written policies for evaluating wrap fee appropriateness.

QUESTION
--------
Did Brighton Wealth Advisors comply with its fiduciary obligations and SEC examination standards regarding the suitability of the wrap fee arrangement?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a fiduciary breach involving failure to assess ongoing suitability of a wrap fee program for a low-activity, capital-preservation account.

SEC exam priorities have repeatedly flagged wrap fee programs as an area of focus, specifically examining whether advisers assess whether clients with low trading activity are better served by a transaction-based fee arrangement. A client invested primarily in buy-and-hold municipal bonds and a money market fund with only three trades over 14 months is paying $9,300 annually in wrap fees for minimal advisory and trading services. The absence of any periodic suitability review or written compliance policies for evaluating wrap fee appropriateness demonstrates both a duty-of-care failure and a supervision deficiency.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §§ 206(1) and 206(2); SEC Division of Examinations Wrap Fee Program Risk Alerts and Exam Priorities (FY 2025, FY 2026)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm charged an ongoing 1.50% wrap fee on a low-activity, capital-preservation account without ever assessing whether the wrap structure remained cost-appropriate relative to a transaction-based alternative.
