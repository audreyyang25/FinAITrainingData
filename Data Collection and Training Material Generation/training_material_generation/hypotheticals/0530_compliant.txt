Source Folder: 03_FINRA_AWC
Topic: finra_awc_suitability
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Diana Ruiz, a General Securities Representative at a regional broker-dealer, services a client, Harold Keene, age 74, a retired aerospace engineer with a $1.8 million investment portfolio, $6,200/month in pension and Social Security income that fully covers his living expenses, and a documented aggressive growth investment objective. Keene has 30 years of active investing experience, holds a Series 7 license from a prior career in the industry, and has signed a detailed investment profile update explicitly requesting exposure to emerging market equities and high-yield bonds. Ruiz recommended allocating 35% of Keene's portfolio ($630,000) to a diversified emerging markets equity fund and 15% ($270,000) to a high-yield corporate bond fund. Before executing the recommendation, Ruiz documented a detailed suitability analysis noting Keene's extensive experience, his stable outside income covering all expenses, his long time horizon for legacy planning, and his explicit written request. Ruiz's designated principal reviewed and approved the recommendation, noting the client's sophisticated profile. The firm's Reg BI disclosure documents were provided and acknowledged by Keene, including material facts about the risks and costs of the recommended products.

QUESTION
--------
Did Ruiz violate suitability or Reg BI best interest standards by recommending a significant allocation to emerging market equities and high-yield bonds for a 74-year-old client?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

While advanced age is a significant factor in suitability analysis, it is not dispositive. FINRA Rule 2111's customer-specific obligation and Reg BI's Care Obligation require consideration of the customer's entire investment profile, including financial situation, investment experience, risk tolerance, and stated objectives. Keene has substantial assets, stable income exceeding his needs, decades of sophisticated investing experience, an explicit aggressive growth objective, and a legacy-focused time horizon. The recommendation was proportionate (50% of portfolio), diversified across two asset classes, fully documented, and approved by a principal. This distinguishes the case from the Zachary Taylor AWC pattern, where senior clients had balanced or conservative profiles inconsistent with aggressive recommendations.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; FINRA Rule 2111(a) – Customer-Specific Suitability

KEY FACTOR
----------
The client's documented aggressive investment objective, extensive experience, substantial assets, and stable income independent of the portfolio supported the recommendation despite his age, and the representative thoroughly documented the basis for the recommendation.
