Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Edward Shorten, a former General Securities Representative registered through a FINRA member firm since 1994, recommended that two retired clients—a 72-year-old widow with $180,000 in liquid assets and a 68-year-old retired teacher with $130,000 in savings—each take out home equity lines of credit (HELOCs) totaling $85,000 and $60,000 respectively, and invest those borrowed proceeds into speculative, illiquid private placement offerings sold by Shorten's firm. Neither client had prior experience with alternative investments, both had conservative risk profiles documented in their account records, and both relied on their investment portfolios and Social Security for living expenses. Shorten earned commissions of approximately 7% on each private placement sale. He did not document any reasonable basis for concluding the strategy of investing borrowed funds in illiquid products was in either client's best interest.

QUESTION
--------
Did Shorten satisfy the Reg BI Care Obligation when he recommended that conservative, retired clients borrow against home equity to invest in speculative illiquid private placements?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the Reg BI Care Obligation.

The Care Obligation requires that a broker-dealer exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill to ensure a recommendation is in the retail customer's best interest, considering the customer's investment profile including age, financial situation, risk tolerance, liquidity needs, and investment objectives. As reflected in the Beslagic AWC (No. 2023079509301), recommending that clients access home equity line of credit proceeds for investment purposes, particularly in illiquid or speculative products, raises serious Care Obligation concerns. Here, both clients were elderly, conservative, income-dependent retirees with no experience in alternative investments, and the use of borrowed funds amplified risk to an entirely unsuitable degree. The 7% commission also created a strong financial incentive that Shorten failed to weigh against his clients' interests.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation (reasonable-basis, customer-specific, and quantitative obligations)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommending that conservative, elderly, income-dependent retirees borrow against home equity to invest in speculative illiquid private placements, without any documented reasonable-basis or customer-specific analysis, violates the core of the Care Obligation.
