Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Harron Capital Advisors, a registered investment adviser, is co-managing an international real estate fund alongside its principals' personal investment vehicle. The firm's founder, James Harron, directs the fund to acquire self-storage properties in Latin America at valuations that Harron himself sets without independent appraisal. Harron's personal vehicle co-invests in the same properties but receives a disproportionately favorable allocation of the most profitable assets, while the fund managed on behalf of outside investors receives the lower-performing properties. The fund's PPM discloses that 'affiliates may co-invest alongside the fund' but does not disclose the specific allocation methodology or the conflict inherent in Harron's dual role in setting valuations. Outside investors collectively invested $18 million based on projected returns that assumed pro-rata allocation of deal flow.

QUESTION
--------
Does the investment adviser's failure to disclose the specific conflict of interest in asset allocation between the fund and the principal's personal vehicle violate the Investment Advisers Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the Investment Advisers Act's anti-fraud provisions and fiduciary duty of loyalty.

Under the Investment Advisers Act, an investment adviser owes a fiduciary duty that requires full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts of interest. As illustrated in the Metro Storage v. Harron pattern, where a co-venturer engaged in self-dealing allocation of business opportunities, a generic disclosure that 'affiliates may co-invest' is insufficient when the adviser is actively directing the most favorable assets to its own account. The Robare Group decision further underscores that the SEC requires specificity in conflict disclosures—boilerplate language does not satisfy the adviser's duty to ensure clients can provide informed consent.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §§ 206(1) and 206(2) – Anti-Fraud Provisions; see also Robare Group v. SEC, 922 F.3d 468 (D.C. Cir. 2019); Metro Storage Int'l LLC v. Harron, C.A. No. 2018-0937-JTL (Del. Ch. 2022)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Generic co-investment disclosure is materially inadequate when the adviser is actively exercising discretion to allocate superior assets to its own account at clients' expense.
