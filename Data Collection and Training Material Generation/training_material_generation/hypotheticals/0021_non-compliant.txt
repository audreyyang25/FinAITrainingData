ID: 21
Source Folder: unknown
Topic: reg_bi_conflict
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer's compliance manual explicitly prohibits recommending proprietary mutual funds unless they are the best available option for the customer. A financial advisor recommends a 58-year-old client with $250,000 in a brokerage account and moderate risk tolerance to invest $150,000 in the firm's proprietary large-cap equity fund (expense ratio 0.95%) instead of Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF (expense ratio 0.03%). The advisor documents in the file that the proprietary fund had slightly higher 3-year returns (4.2% vs. 4.1%), but never discloses that the firm receives 12b-1 fees of 0.25% annually from the proprietary fund.

QUESTION
--------
Does the advisor's recommendation comply with Reg BI's conflict of interest obligation?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates Reg BI's requirement to disclose and mitigate conflicts.

The advisor failed to disclose the material 12b-1 conflict (0.25% annual fee differential translating to approximately $375 annually on the recommended position). While the 3-year performance difference (4.2% vs. 4.1%) is marginal and does not justify the conflict, the undisclosed revenue sharing violates the core Reg BI conflict disclosure obligation. Reg BI § 15l-1(b)(2)(i) requires clear disclosure of all material conflicts; the failure to disclose the firm's economic incentive to recommend its own product, even where alternatives exist, is a per se violation. The firm's written policy against recommending proprietary products without demonstrable superiority is not sufficient without disclosure of why the proprietary fund meets that standard given the conflict.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(b)(2)(i) and (ii) — Reg BI requires broker-dealers to disclose all material conflicts of interest and implement policies to mitigate conflicts that put the firm's interests ahead of the customer's.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Undisclosed 12b-1 fees creating material economic incentive for proprietary product recommendation without corresponding disclosure of the conflict to the customer.
