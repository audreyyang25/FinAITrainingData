FACTS
-----
Margaret, age 82, has $850,000 in a diversified portfolio (60% equities, 40% bonds; 0.45% annual fee) with Advisor Chen at a wirehouse. Margaret has stable cognitive function and moderate income needs ($35,000/year). Advisor Chen recommends transferring the entire portfolio into a Fidelity Advisor Series VIP variable annuity with a 7% sales load ($59,500), 1.95% annual mortality & expense (M&E) fee, plus underlying fund fees of 0.65%, totaling 2.60% annually. The annuity includes a guaranteed minimum income rider charging an additional 0.75% per year. Chen documents that Margaret "seeks income" but does not conduct a detailed suitability analysis addressing (1) her age and likely 15–20 year holding period vs. surrender charge period (10 years), (2) comparison of total costs ($22,100 annually vs. $3,825 previously), or (3) tax efficiency (annuity premature payout penalties). Margaret executes the transfer within 2 weeks.

QUESTION
--------
Did Advisor Chen comply with FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability) and FINRA Rule 2330 (Variable Annuities) in recommending this annuity to an 82-year-old client?

ANSWER
------
No. FINRA Rule 2330(d) requires written supervisory procedures for VA sales to seniors (age 65+), including clear justification that the recommendation is suitable considering the client's age, financial situation, and investment objectives. Chen's recommendation fails on multiple grounds: (1) the 7% load plus 2.60% annual costs ($22,100/year) far exceed the prior 0.45% fee structure, creating substantial switching costs absent compelling justification; (2) at age 82, Margaret's likely 15–20 year lifespan (per CDC life tables) conflicts with the 10-year surrender charge; (3) no documented analysis addresses tax consequences or income need alternatives (e.g., bond ladder, dividend stocks); (4) the guaranteed income rider (0.75%) adds redundant cost when Margaret already has Social Security + pension income. FINRA enforcement patterns (e.g., Stifel NA 2017, $1.5M settlement for unsuitable VA sales to seniors) penalize cost-blind recommendations without comparative analysis. The reasonable-basis prong (Rule 2111(a)) is violated because Chen documented no analysis of whether this specific annuity structure met Margaret's needs relative to her existing portfolio.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330(d) (VA suitability to seniors); FINRA Rule 2111(a)–(b) (Suitability: reasonable basis and customer-specific)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommendation of high-cost variable annuity to 82-year-old with established income stream and diversified portfolio, with 7% load and 2.60% annual fees, absent written comparative suitability analysis or justification for switching from 0.45% portfolio.
