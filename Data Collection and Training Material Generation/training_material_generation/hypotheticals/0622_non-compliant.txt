ID: 622
Source Folder: 07_Practitioner_Commentary
Topic: training_supervision
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Meridian Securities, a mid-size broker-dealer with 120 registered representatives, implemented Reg BI compliance procedures on June 25, 2020, just five days before the compliance date. The firm circulated a two-page summary memorandum to its representatives via email but conducted no formal training sessions, recorded no training completions, and did not update its written supervisory procedures to incorporate the best interest standard. During an SEC examination prompted by the 2020 OCIE Examination Priorities focus on Reg BI implementation, examiners discovered that representatives continued using legacy suitability-only checklists when documenting recommendations of complex products such as variable annuities and structured notes to retail customers. One representative, Sarah Melton, recommended a $175,000 variable annuity with a 7-year surrender period and 5.75% surrender charge to a 72-year-old retiree with a moderate risk tolerance and $340,000 in liquid assets, documenting only that the product was 'suitable' without any cost-benefit or reasonably available alternatives analysis.

QUESTION
--------
Did Meridian Securities satisfy the Reg BI Compliance Obligation and Care Obligation with respect to its training program and the recommendation made by Sarah Melton?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the Reg BI Compliance Obligation (SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iv)) and the Care Obligation (SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii)).

The FINRA Reg BI Checklist specifically asks whether firms have 'procedures and training in place to assess recommendations using a best interest standard,' emphasizing that the best interest standard is satisfied only through compliance with all four component obligations: Care, Disclosure, Conflict of Interest, and Compliance. Meridian's last-minute, untested email summary without formal training, completion tracking, or WSP updates fails the Compliance Obligation's requirement for reasonably designed policies and procedures. Furthermore, Sarah Melton's recommendation of a high-cost, long-surrender-period variable annuity to an elderly client without analyzing costs, reasonably available alternatives, or the client's specific needs violates the Care Obligation's requirement to exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iv) – Reg BI Compliance Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; FINRA Reg BI Checklist Item 1

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's failure to implement meaningful Reg BI training, update supervisory procedures, or require representatives to perform best interest analyses—rather than legacy suitability-only reviews—violated both the Compliance and Care Obligations.
