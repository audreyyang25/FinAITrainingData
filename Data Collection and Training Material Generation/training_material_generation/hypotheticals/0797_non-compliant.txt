Source Folder: 15_FINRA_Regulatory_Notices
Topic: senior_investor_protection
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Margaret, an 82-year-old widow with early-stage dementia, holds $320,000 in a brokerage account. Her registered representative, noting declining cognitive ability, recommends she liquidate her portfolio of Treasury bonds and blue-chip dividend stocks and reinvest the proceeds into a portfolio of five alternative mutual funds employing managed futures, long/short credit, and multi-alternative strategies. The representative does not contact Margaret's designated trusted contact person, does not document any assessment of her current cognitive capacity, and does not notify his branch manager of concerns about her mental state. The representative earns $9,600 in commissions on the transactions. Margaret's account loses 18% in the following six months.

QUESTION
--------
Did the registered representative comply with FINRA's senior investor protection rules and suitability obligations in recommending complex alternative mutual funds to this elderly client?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a suitability violation and a failure to follow senior investor protection procedures.

The recommendation of complex Alt Funds to an 82-year-old client with early-stage dementia is unsuitable on its face under FINRA Rule 2111's customer-specific suitability requirement, given her age, cognitive decline, and prior conservative holdings. Additionally, the representative's failure to contact her trusted contact person or escalate concerns about her diminished capacity to a supervisor violates the spirit and procedures established under FINRA Rules 2165 and 4512, which are designed to protect elderly and vulnerable investors. Regulatory Notice 22-11 further underscores heightened obligations when recommending Alt Funds, particularly regarding understanding the client's ability to comprehend product risks.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability); FINRA Rule 2165 (Financial Exploitation of Specified Adults); FINRA Rule 4512 (Trusted Contact Person); FINRA Regulatory Notice 22-11

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative recommended complex alternative mutual funds to a cognitively declining 82-year-old client without any capacity assessment, escalation, or contact with her trusted contact person.
