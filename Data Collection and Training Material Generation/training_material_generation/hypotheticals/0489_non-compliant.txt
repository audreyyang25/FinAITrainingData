Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
David Flores, a registered representative at Summit Financial Group, recommends that Robert and Linda Hayes, a retired couple aged 71 and 68 with $480,000 in combined savings and a conservative income-oriented investment objective, invest $320,000 (approximately 67% of their liquid assets) into a non-traded REIT with a 7-year lock-up period and a 12% upfront commission. David does not discuss the product's illiquidity, the high commission, or lower-cost publicly traded REIT alternatives. Summit's written supervisory procedures require documented suitability analysis for illiquid alternative investments, but David's supervisor approves the transaction based only on a brief verbal conversation.

QUESTION
--------
Did David Flores and Summit Financial Group comply with Reg BI's Care and Disclosure Obligations in recommending the non-traded REIT to the Hayes couple?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates Reg BI's Care Obligation and Disclosure Obligation, as well as the firm's supervisory obligations.

Reg BI requires that a broker-dealer act in the retail customer's best interest by exercising reasonable diligence, care, and skill, including evaluating costs, reasonably available alternatives, and the customer's investment profile. Concentrating 67% of a retired couple's liquid assets in an illiquid, high-commission product with a 7-year lock-up is inconsistent with their conservative, income-oriented objectives. The failure to disclose the illiquidity, high upfront costs, and available alternatives violates the Disclosure Obligation. The supervisor's approval based solely on a verbal conversation, without documented analysis, violates the firm's own written supervisory procedures.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Disclosure Obligation; SEC Release No. 34-86031 (Regulation Best Interest Final Rule)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative concentrated the majority of a retired couple's assets in a high-cost illiquid product without disclosing material risks, costs, or alternatives, and the supervisor failed to conduct the required documented suitability review.
