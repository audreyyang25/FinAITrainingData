ID: 810
Source Folder: 15_FINRA_Regulatory_Notices
Topic: senior_investor_protection
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered representative at a small FINRA member firm services a book of 45 clients, 30 of whom are retirees over age 70. The firm's compliance department has two staff members who also handle operations. Following FINRA's modernization of workplace rules, the firm transitions most client interactions to a remote office model. A 78-year-old widow, Margaret Hollins, with $380,000 in retirement savings and moderate risk tolerance, contacts her representative about generating more income. The representative recommends she concentrate 65% of her portfolio into a single non-traded REIT and a structured note with a 7-year lockup period, communicating the recommendation entirely via phone from his home office. No enhanced supervisory review is conducted for this senior client transaction, and the firm has no written supervisory procedures specifically addressing heightened scrutiny for seniors in a remote supervision environment. The representative's firm does not place a temporary hold or seek additional verification despite the concentration risk and illiquidity concerns for a client of Margaret's age and liquidity needs.

QUESTION
--------
Did the firm satisfy its supervisory obligations under FINRA rules regarding the suitability of complex, illiquid product recommendations to a senior investor in a remote office setting?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision), FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability), and FINRA Rule 2165 (Financial Exploitation of Specified Adults).

AARP's comment letter on FINRA Regulatory Notice 25-07 specifically urged FINRA to maintain strong investor protections for seniors as firms modernize and adopt remote workplace models. FINRA Rule 3110 requires firms to establish and maintain written supervisory procedures reasonably designed to achieve compliance, including when supervision occurs remotely. Concentrating 65% of a 78-year-old retiree's savings in illiquid products with a 7-year lockup fails the customer-specific suitability obligation under FINRA Rule 2111, as it disregards her age, liquidity needs, and time horizon. The firm's failure to implement enhanced procedures for senior transactions — particularly in a remote setting where oversight is inherently more challenging — constitutes a supervisory deficiency.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision); FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability – Quantitative and Customer-Specific); FINRA Rule 2165 (Financial Exploitation of Specified Adults); FINRA Regulatory Notice 25-07 (Modernizing Workplace Rules with Maintained Investor Protections)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm failed to implement heightened supervisory procedures for a senior investor transaction involving concentrated, illiquid complex products, particularly after transitioning to a remote office model where enhanced oversight is critical.
