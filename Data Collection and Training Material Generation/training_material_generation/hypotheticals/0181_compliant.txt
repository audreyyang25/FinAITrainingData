Source Folder: unknown
Topic: concentration
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 50-year-old CFO at a Fortune 500 tech company has $3 million in net worth: $1.2M in company stock (20% of portfolio; highly liquid, 50M daily volume), $400K in RSUs vesting over 3 years, $800K in a 401(k), and $600K in other investments. Her advisor recommends a 5-year diversification plan: (1) annual tax-loss harvesting of $60-80K; (2) RSU sales upon vesting directed to diversified funds; (3) 401(k) reallocation to broad index funds. The advisor documents the plan, discusses SEC Rule 10b5-1 implications for company insiders, and notes that concentration is already below 30% of investable assets and dropping. The client agrees and executes quarterly reviews.

QUESTION
--------
Does this phased diversification approach for an insider with substantial concentrated equity satisfy fiduciary and suitability standards?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this is compliant.

The advisor has satisfied fiduciary duty by: (1) developing a concrete, multi-year diversification strategy rather than ignoring the concentration; (2) respecting the client's insider status (CFO) and compliance constraints (Rule 10b5-1 considerations); (3) using tax-efficient mechanisms (loss harvesting, RSU vesting redirection) to manage the concentrated position; (4) documenting the plan and the business rationale (insider restrictions, tax efficiency); (5) creating measurable progress milestones (RSUs, annual harvesting targets); and (6) conducting quarterly reviews. The concentration is already mitigated (20% of a diversified net worth, with other liquid assets), and the plan will further reduce it. This proactive, documented approach reflects the duty of care and loyalty.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act § 206 (fiduciary duty of care and loyalty); FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability); SEC guidance on insider trading and diversification (Rule 10b5-1 Plans); state law fiduciary duties (e.g., NY Regulation 187 fiduciary standard).

KEY FACTOR
----------
Structured, documented, multi-year plan to reduce concentration using tax-efficient and insider-compliant mechanisms, coupled with quarterly review and clear measurable goals, transforms a potential concentration risk into compliant fiduciary practice.
