Source Folder: 20_SEC_ALJ_OALJ_Decisions
Topic: compliant_supervision_and_trade_review
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Broker-dealer Arcadia Securities implemented a layered supervisory system in which an automated surveillance platform flagged any account with an annualized turnover ratio exceeding 3.0 or a cost-to-equity ratio exceeding 20% for immediate principal review. When a junior representative's client account triggered the alert after generating a turnover ratio of 3.8 over four months, the designated principal conducted a documented review within two business days, interviewed the representative and the client separately, and determined that the trading pace reflected the client's own written instructions requesting active tactical rebalancing consistent with a documented aggressive growth profile. The principal's written review memorandum cited specific account facts, the client's signed trading-frequency acknowledgment, and a benchmark comparison, and was retained in the firm's books and records for seven years.

QUESTION
--------
Did Arcadia Securities fulfill its supervisory obligations under the Exchange Act with respect to the flagged account?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

ALJs evaluating failure-to-supervise claims under Exchange Act §15(b)(4)(E) assess whether a firm established reasonable supervisory procedures and implemented them in good faith upon detection of red flags. Arcadia's automated surveillance triggered a timely principal review that investigated the trading activity through both representative and client interviews, documented its findings with reference to the client's affirmative instructions and risk profile, and retained the record in compliance with Rule 17a-4. The combination of a proactive detection system, prompt responsive review, and thorough documentation satisfies the reasonable supervision standard applied by ALJs even where the underlying trading activity warranted scrutiny.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Exchange Act §15(b)(4)(E); Exchange Act Rule 17a-4 (books and records); FINRA Rule 3110 (supervisory procedures, by analogy to BD obligations before ALJ)

KEY FACTOR
----------
A documented, prompt supervisory response that resolves a red-flag alert through client and representative interviews and record retention demonstrates compliance with the reasonable supervision standard.
