Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Angela Rossi, a financial planner in California, decides to launch her own registered investment advisory firm managing retirement assets for small business owners. Before taking on any clients, she enrolls in the IARD system, funds her daily account, completes Form ADV Parts 1 and 2 (including disclosing her fee schedule, disciplinary history of none, and all material conflicts), and submits the application to the California DFPI. She also registers herself as an investment adviser representative, passes the Series 65 examination, and pays all required filing fees. She does not begin advising clients or collecting fees until she receives confirmation of her registration. Her first-year AUM is $8 million across 22 clients, all of whom receive her Form ADV Part 2A brochure at or before the time of signing their advisory agreements.

QUESTION
--------
Does Angela Rossi's process for establishing and operating her investment advisory firm comply with California state registration requirements?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Rossi followed each of the six required steps outlined by the California DFPI for becoming a registered investment adviser: IARD enrollment, funding the daily account, completing Form ADV, submitting Form ADV, registering as an investment adviser representative (including passing the Series 65), and submitting the complete application. She refrained from conducting advisory business until registration was confirmed and delivered the required brochure to all clients at or before the time of engagement, satisfying both state registration and disclosure requirements.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
California Corporations Code § 25230; DFPI Registration Steps (IARD enrollment, daily account funding, Form ADV completion and submission, IAR registration); NASAA Model Rule on Brochure Delivery

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser completed every required registration step and did not begin advisory activities until state registration was confirmed.
