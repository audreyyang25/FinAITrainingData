FACTS
-----
A private equity fund manager advises three separate co-investment vehicles alongside its flagship $2.8 billion private equity fund. When a potential acquisition target falls through, the manager incurs $4.2 million in broken deal expenses including legal, accounting, and due diligence costs. Mindful of the SEC's 2015 enforcement action against KKR for misallocating over $17 million in broken deal expenses, the manager's CFO applies a detailed written expense allocation policy that was disclosed in each fund's and co-investment vehicle's offering documents. The policy allocates broken deal expenses pro rata based on committed capital across all vehicles that participated in the deal process, rather than shifting expenses disproportionately to the flagship fund. The allocation methodology and amounts are documented in an internal memorandum reviewed by the CCO, and each fund's advisory committee receives a quarterly report detailing the allocation.

QUESTION
--------
Is the private equity manager's allocation of $4.2 million in broken deal expenses across participating fund vehicles consistent with fiduciary obligations under the Investment Advisers Act?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The SEC's action against KKR highlighted the fiduciary breach inherent in misallocating broken deal expenses to flagship funds without proper disclosure or a reasonable allocation methodology. Here, the manager maintained a detailed written expense allocation policy disclosed in offering documents, allocated costs pro rata based on committed capital across all participating vehicles, documented the methodology in an internal memorandum reviewed by the CCO, and reported allocations to advisory committees. This approach directly addresses the regulatory concerns identified in SEC enforcement actions regarding private equity expense allocation and reflects the industry best practices emphasized by the Willkie practitioner commentary.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Sections 206(1) and 206(2) – Fiduciary Duty; SEC Enforcement Guidance on PE Fee and Expense Allocation

KEY FACTOR
----------
The manager's pre-existing, disclosed pro rata allocation policy — applied consistently and documented with CCO oversight and advisory committee reporting — distinguishes this from the misallocation patterns targeted in SEC enforcement actions against private equity firms.
