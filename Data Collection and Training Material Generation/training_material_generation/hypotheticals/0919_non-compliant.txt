ID: 919
Source Folder: 20_SEC_ALJ_OALJ_Decisions
Topic: churning_discretionary_account
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Registered representative Dale Marsh exercised discretionary authority over a retired schoolteacher's $180,000 account and executed 47 trades in a six-month period, generating $22,000 in commissions. The client's account turned over at an annualized rate of 11.2, far exceeding any reasonable investment objective. The client's stated objective was capital preservation with modest income, and she never inquired about or approved the trading frequency. Marsh's firm received no supervisory alerts despite the anomalous turnover ratio.

QUESTION
--------
Does Marsh's trading activity in the client's discretionary account constitute churning in violation of the Exchange Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is churning in violation of Exchange Act §10(b) and Rule 10b-5.

Churning occurs when a broker exercises control over an account and trades with excessive frequency relative to the customer's investment objectives, primarily to generate commissions. An annualized turnover ratio of 11.2 vastly exceeds the threshold of 4.0 that ALJs have historically treated as presumptively excessive, and the conduct was plainly inconsistent with a capital preservation mandate. The scienter element is satisfied by Marsh's intentional use of discretionary authority to maximize commission revenue at the client's expense.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Exchange Act §10(b); Rule 10b-5; SEA Release No. 34-51523 (churning doctrine)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Turnover ratio of 11.2 combined with discretionary control and a conservative client objective establishes churning as a matter of law.
