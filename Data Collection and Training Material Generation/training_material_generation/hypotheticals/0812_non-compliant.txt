Source Folder: 12_FINRA_Arbitration_Awards
Topic: arbitration_liability
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Troy and Elizabeth Magnussen, a married couple in their early 60s nearing retirement in suburban St. Louis, held a joint brokerage account with approximately $620,000 at a regional broker-dealer. Their registered representative, without discussing the risks or obtaining informed consent, concentrated over 72% of the couple's portfolio in speculative, illiquid alternative investments including non-traded REITs and private placements over a 14-month period. The couple's account documents indicated a 'moderate' risk tolerance and an 'income with capital preservation' investment objective. When the Magnussens discovered the concentration after receiving their annual account statement, they filed a FINRA arbitration claim alleging violations of the Illinois Securities Act, breach of fiduciary duty, and unsuitability. The broker-dealer's supervisory system had flagged the account for concentration but the branch manager took no corrective action, merely initialing the exception report without contacting the customers or the representative.

QUESTION
--------
Did the registered representative and broker-dealer violate suitability obligations and supervisory duties by concentrating a moderate-risk-tolerance retirement couple's portfolio in speculative illiquid alternatives without informed consent?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. It constitutes a suitability violation under FINRA Rule 2111, breach of fiduciary duty, and a supervisory failure under FINRA Rule 3110.

Under FINRA Rule 2111, a broker must have a reasonable basis for believing that a recommended investment strategy is suitable for the specific customer based on their investment profile, including risk tolerance, time horizon, and stated objectives. Concentrating 72% of a moderate-risk, income-oriented retirement account in speculative illiquid alternatives directly contradicts the customers' documented objectives. Additionally, FINRA Rule 3110 requires member firms to establish and maintain supervisory systems reasonably designed to achieve compliance — the branch manager's perfunctory initialing of concentration exception reports without follow-up action constitutes a supervisory failure consistent with patterns seen in FINRA arbitration awards such as Case 16-01819 involving customer claims against broker-dealers in St. Louis.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability – Customer-Specific and Quantitative Suitability); FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision); Illinois Securities Act § 12(G)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The pivotal factor is the gross mismatch between the customers' documented moderate risk tolerance and income/preservation objectives and the 72% concentration in speculative illiquid alternatives, compounded by the firm's failure to act on its own supervisory alerts.
