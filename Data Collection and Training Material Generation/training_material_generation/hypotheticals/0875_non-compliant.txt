ID: 875
Source Folder: 18_OCC_Bank_Trust_Guidance
Topic: bank_fiduciary
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Heartland National Bank serves as trustee for a $2.3 million irrevocable family trust established by Margaret Chen, a 78-year-old retiree, with her three adult children as beneficiaries. The trust document specifies conservative income-producing investments. Without obtaining independent review or beneficiary consent, the bank's trust officer invested $900,000 of trust assets in Heartland National Bank's own proprietary money market fund, which pays the bank an annual management fee of 0.45% on top of the standard trustee fee already charged to the trust. The trust officer did not disclose this fee arrangement in the annual account statement sent to the beneficiaries, and no committee review of the self-dealing transaction was documented in the trust file.

QUESTION
--------
Does the bank's investment of trust assets in its own proprietary fund, without disclosure of the dual fee arrangement to beneficiaries, satisfy its fiduciary duty of loyalty under OCC standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a breach of fiduciary duty through undisclosed self-dealing.

The OCC Conflicts of Interest handbook expressly identifies self-dealing between related parties and fiduciary accounts as a primary conflict type requiring disclosure and, in most cases, independent authorization. By investing trust assets in the bank's own proprietary fund without disclosing the additional 0.45% fee to beneficiaries or documenting committee approval, the bank violated its fundamental duty of loyalty to the trust beneficiaries. The failure to disclose the dual compensation arrangement in account statements compounds the violation by preventing beneficiaries from evaluating or objecting to the conflict.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
OCC Comptroller's Handbook – Asset Management (AM), Conflicts of Interest (AM-CI), Version 1.0 (January 2015); 12 CFR 9.12 – Self-Dealing Prohibition

KEY FACTOR
----------
The bank received undisclosed dual compensation by collecting both trustee fees and proprietary fund management fees from the same trust, constituting impermissible self-dealing without required beneficiary disclosure or independent authorization.
