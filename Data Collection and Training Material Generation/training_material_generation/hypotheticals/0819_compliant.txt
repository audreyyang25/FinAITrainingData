Source Folder: 16_Congressional_Materials
Topic: legislative_history
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A dually registered broker-dealer representative recommended that a 45-year-old software engineer with a $1.2 million taxable brokerage account and a 25-year investment horizon purchase $200,000 in a private placement offering in a pre-IPO technology company, paying the representative a 5% commission. The representative's firm maintained the client as a verified accredited investor with $2.8 million in net assets excluding home equity, documented his high risk tolerance and long time horizon, provided a 47-page private placement memorandum, conducted a side-by-side analysis comparing expected returns and risks against publicly traded technology ETFs, disclosed the 5% commission in the written recommendation rationale, and obtained signed acknowledgment of illiquidity risk. The Dodd-Frank §913 study noted that Congress intended the standard of conduct rulemaking to preserve access to a variety of investment products for investors who can bear associated risks, not to categorically prohibit commission-based recommendations in complex securities.

QUESTION
--------
Does the broker-dealer representative's private placement recommendation, supported by comprehensive documentation, comparative analysis, conflict disclosure, and accredited investor verification, satisfy Reg BI's Care and Disclosure Obligations?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Dodd-Frank §913's legislative history explicitly cautioned against interpreting the best-interest standard as a product prohibition, instead requiring that recommendations be grounded in genuine analysis of the specific client's circumstances. Here, the representative documented the client's verified accredited status, high risk tolerance, 25-year horizon, and $2.8M net worth, conducted a comparative analysis against alternatives, and provided full commission disclosure—satisfying each element of the Care and Disclosure Obligations. The commission alone does not render the recommendation non-compliant where the representative has affirmatively placed the client's interest first through documented analysis and conflict mitigation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Disclosure Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; Dodd-Frank Act §913 (preserving product access while mandating best-interest standard).

KEY FACTOR
----------
Comprehensive documentation of the client's financial capacity, comparative product analysis, and explicit conflict disclosure transforms what superficially appears to be a high-commission complex product sale into a fully compliant best-interest recommendation consistent with Dodd-Frank §913's legislative intent.
