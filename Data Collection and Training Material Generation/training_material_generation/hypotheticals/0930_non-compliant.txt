ID: 930
Source Folder: 21_FINRA_OHO_Decisions
Topic: excessive_trading_churning
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Registered representative Priya Nettleton managed a discretionary account for a 58-year-old semi-retired engineer with a moderate risk tolerance and approximately $180,000 in investable assets. Over an 18-month period, Nettleton executed 214 trades in the account, generating an annualized turnover rate of 11.4 and a cost-to-equity ratio of 28%, meaning the account had to earn 28% annually just to break even on commissions and fees. The client received monthly statements but had limited financial sophistication and relied entirely on Nettleton's judgment. Nettleton earned approximately $34,000 in commissions during the period while the account lost 19% of its value.

QUESTION
--------
Does Nettleton's trading pattern in the discretionary account constitute excessive trading in violation of FINRA rules?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is excessive trading (churning) in violation of FINRA Rules 2111 and 2010.

Quantitative suitability under Rule 2111 prohibits a representative with control over an account from engaging in excessive trading inconsistent with the customer's profile; an annualized turnover rate above 6 and a cost-to-equity ratio above 20% are widely recognized as strong indicators of churning. The 11.4 turnover rate and 28% cost-to-equity ratio, combined with the account's net losses and Nettleton's substantial commission income, establish that trading was driven by compensation motives rather than the client's investment interests. This also violates Rule 2010's requirement of just and equitable principles of trade.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability — Quantitative Suitability); FINRA Rule 2010 (Standards of Commercial Honor)

KEY FACTOR
----------
An annualized turnover rate of 11.4 and a 28% cost-to-equity ratio far exceed thresholds indicative of churning, demonstrating trading for commission generation rather than client benefit.
