FACTS
-----
A 71-year-old client with $650,000 in investable assets, a $35,000 annual pension, and $28,000 in Social Security is recommended to liquidate $200,000 of the variable annuity accumulated value (purchased 12 years ago with a now-fully-expired surrender charge schedule) and reinvest the proceeds in a diversified mutual fund portfolio (total annual fees 0.35%). The advisor documents: (1) the client's objective is to reallocate away from the variable annuity's 1.1% M&E fee to reduce ongoing costs; (2) the client is in the RMD (required minimum distribution) phase, and the liquidation allows the client to satisfy this year's $26,000 RMD while redeploying capital to lower-cost vehicles; (3) no surrender charges apply (the 7-year surrender charge schedule expired 5 years ago); (4) the mutual fund portfolio's 0.35% fee versus 1.1% M&E represents $5,200 annual savings; and (5) the advisor makes no new transaction-based commission on the mutual fund purchase.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation to exchange the variable annuity satisfy FINRA Rule 2330(d) and Reg BI?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation is compliant with FINRA Rule 2330(d) and Reg BI.

This exchange recommendation satisfies Rule 2330(d) because: (1) the surrender charge schedule has fully expired (7 years of charges over 12-year ownership), eliminating the primary lock-in cost that originally justified the variable annuity structure; (2) the advisor documented a specific, quantifiable benefit—reducing annual fees from 1.1% M&E to 0.35% mutual fund fees, saving $5,200 annually on the $650,000 portfolio, which compounds to ~$78,000 over 15 years; (3) the recommendation aligns with the client's RMD obligation (age 71, now required to distribute ~$26,000 annually from tax-deferred accounts), and the liquidation facilitates tax-efficient withdrawal planning; (4) the mutual fund alternative is demonstrably lower-cost and serves the same objective (diversified growth/income in a tax-advantaged context for an IRA or non-qualified account), addressing Rule 2330(d)'s requirement to show the exchange benefits the customer; (5) the advisor generates no new transaction commission on the mutual fund redeployment, eliminating compensation conflict; and (6) FINRA guidance recognizes that once surrender charges expire and the original annuity purpose has been served, a transition to lower-cost alternatives is presumptively suitable if properly documented.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330(d) — exchange suitability requires documentation of whether exchange benefits customer; Reg BI Rule 15l-1(b)(2)(i)(C) — recommendation must consider compensation conflicts; Rule 2330(d)(2)(i) — no additional suitability documentation required if surrender charges have expired and original purpose of annuity has been served.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Surrender charges have fully expired (no lock-in cost), the advisor documented quantifiable fee savings ($5,200 annually), the exchange aligns with RMD obligations, and the advisor receives no new transaction commission on the redeployment.
