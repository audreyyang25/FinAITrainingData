ID: 678
Source Folder: 09_Law_Firm_Memos
Topic: compliance_advisory
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer registered representative recommends that Maria Gonzalez, a 72-year-old retired schoolteacher with $340,000 in savings, a moderate risk tolerance, and a need for income stability, invest $280,000 in long-dated municipal revenue bonds rated BBB- with 25-year maturities. The representative earns a 2.8% markup on municipal bond transactions, significantly higher than the firm's average 1.1% markup on comparable fixed-income products. The representative did not evaluate alternative shorter-duration, higher-rated municipal bonds or other income-producing investments, nor did he document any analysis of why these particular bonds were preferable given the client's age, income needs, and risk profile. The firm's supervisory system flagged the markup as elevated but the branch manager approved the trade without additional review.

QUESTION
--------
Did the broker-dealer representative satisfy the Care Obligation under Regulation Best Interest when recommending the long-dated, lower-rated municipal bonds to this retired client?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation.

The Care Obligation under Reg BI requires the representative to exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill in making a recommendation, including understanding the potential risks, rewards, and costs of the recommendation and having a reasonable basis to believe the recommendation is in the customer's best interest. Recommending 25-year maturity BBB- bonds to a 72-year-old retiree with moderate risk tolerance—without evaluating shorter-duration, higher-quality alternatives—fails to demonstrate reasonable diligence. The elevated markup further compounds the failure by imposing costs that were not weighed against the client's best interest. The MSRB's proposed alignment of Rule G-19 with Reg BI, as described in SR-MSRB-2020-02, reinforces that municipal securities recommendations must meet the same best interest standard.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Regulation Best Interest Care Obligation; MSRB Rule G-19 (as aligned with Reg BI per SR-MSRB-2020-02)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative failed to evaluate reasonably available alternatives and did not have a reasonable basis to believe a 25-year, BBB- municipal bond recommendation was in the best interest of a 72-year-old retiree with moderate risk tolerance.
