Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 73-year-old widow with $600,000 in assets, prior experience managing a small business (20 years), and stated interest in 'growth and income' comes to a registered investment adviser for a discretionary account. The adviser conducts a written financial planning analysis documenting the client's goals, time horizon (20+ years, planning to age 95), tax situation, and moderate-to-aggressive risk tolerance based on detailed discussion. The adviser recommends a 65% diversified U.S./international equity allocation and 35% investment-grade bonds with quarterly rebalancing. All holdings are liquid ETFs and mutual funds with expense ratios under 0.20%. The adviser charges 1.0% AUM with a written advisory agreement, delivers a written disclosure document, and meets quarterly to review performance and goals. The client confirms understanding and signs acknowledgments.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation and relationship comply with the Investment Adviser Act fiduciary duty (Section 206) and senior investor standards?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this complies with applicable fiduciary standards.

This relationship satisfies the fiduciary duty of care under Section 206 because the adviser has conducted a thorough documented analysis of the client's financial situation, goals, and risk tolerance before making recommendations. The recommended allocation (65/35 equity/bonds) is reasonable for a 73-year-old with a 20+ year time horizon and stated growth and income objectives, and is supported by the client's prior business management experience (suggesting sophistication and capacity to understand risk). All holdings are low-cost, liquid instruments, addressing SEC concerns about unsuitable complexity or excessive fees. The adviser's practice of quarterly reviews, rebalancing, and written disclosures demonstrates ongoing care and loyalty. The adviser's fee (1.0% AUM) is reasonable and fully disclosed. The client's documented understanding and signed acknowledgments provide evidence of informed consent. Unlike the hypothetical in ID 186, this adviser has performed a genuine suitability analysis appropriate to the client's age, circumstances, and experience level.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act of 1940, Section 206(1) and (2) (fiduciary duty of care and loyalty); SEC Regulation Best Interest Rule 17 CFR § 240.15l-1; SEC senior investor guidance (2013)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The comprehensive written financial planning analysis, documented suitability rationale, low-cost liquid holdings, reasonable disclosed fees, and evidence of informed client understanding and ongoing monitoring.
