Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Steward Capital Advisors (BD and IA registered) onboarded Thomas Wu, age 45, with $600,000 in investable assets and a stated goal of 'growth with annual rebalancing flexibility.' Before account opening, the firm's compliance team generated two Form CRS documents: one for advisory services (0.90% fee-only) and one for brokerage services (commission-based trading). The advisor conducted a detailed discovery meeting, documented Thomas's preference for tax-loss harvesting and quarterly rebalancing (incompatible with commission drag), and recommended the advisory account structure. Thomas signed an engagement letter explicitly stating: 'Your account will be managed on a fee-only basis under Advisory Agreement. Steward acts as your investment adviser and fiduciary, subject to the Investment Adviser Act of 1940.' The firm opened the advisory account and charged 0.90% annually. Eighteen months later, Thomas inquired about 'opportunistic' options trades. The advisor prepared a separate brokerage account application (different entity, distinct Form CRS re-delivered), documented in writing that 'Options trading falls outside advisory scope; we will charge per-trade commissions ($35/trade) in this brokerage account; you remain an advisory client for your core portfolio.' Thomas consented in writing to both account types and fee structures. No material conflict disclosure failures occurred.

QUESTION
--------
Did Steward Capital Advisors comply with Regulation Best Interest and Form CRS requirements in establishing and managing both advisory and brokerage accounts for Thomas Wu?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Steward complied with the regulatory framework. The firm satisfied Reg BI and Form CRS requirements through multiple steps: (1) delivered distinct Form CRS documents before account opening, clearly delineating advisory fiduciary status (0.90% fee) versus brokerage salesperson status (per-trade commissions); (2) documented explicit client consent to each account type via signed engagement letter and separate brokerage application; (3) established separate account structures under different entity registrations, eliminating ambiguity about capacity; (4) disclosed material conflicts (advisory fee vs. commission incentives) in writing; (5) maintained documentation that the brokerage account request originated from the client's stated need (options trading), not firm revenue maximization. The re-delivery of Form CRS upon the brokerage account request satisfied the requirement to refresh disclosures when capacity changes. The advisor's written statement clarifying scope and fee structure aligns with Reg BI's care obligation (17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)) to understand the customer and provide reasoned recommendations.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Regulation Best Interest Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i); Form CRS Rule, 17 CFR §275.204-1; SEC Regulation BI Preamble discussion of dual registrant disclosures; Investment Adviser Act §206(2) duty of care

KEY FACTOR
----------
Compliant disclosure of distinct capacities via separate Form CRS documents, written client consent to each account type, and documented conflict disclosures establish clear fiduciary vs. salesperson roles.
