Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Advisor Garcia recommended Class B shares (1.25% 12b-1 fee, 5-year contingent deferred sales charge declining to 0% in year 6) over Class A shares (4.5% front-end load, 0.60% 12b-1) for a $180,000 equity fund investment by 58-year-old client Margaret Chen with a 12-year investment horizon and stated preference to avoid large upfront reductions. Client held $520,000 in total assets with the firm. Garcia documented in writing that Class B was appropriate because: (1) client's time horizon supported the surrender period; (2) the breakeven analysis showed Class B's 12b-1 fees would be lower than Class A's combined load + 12b-1 at year 6; (3) client's stated preference against front-end loads demonstrated suitability; (4) the 0.60% annual 12b-1 on Class A would exceed Class B's declining charges over 6+ years. Garcia also noted that Class B avoided 'sequence of returns' risk in the surrender period by preserving full capital deployment. The recommendation was documented in the suitability memo.

QUESTION
--------
Did Advisor Garcia comply with FINRA Suitability Rule 2111 and the fiduciary duty of care in recommending Class B shares despite their higher total fees?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Compliant under Rule 2111. The recommendation was suitable because: (1) Garcia conducted a documented breakeven analysis comparing all share classes and demonstrated Class B's lower total cost given the client's 12-year horizon; (2) the client's explicit preference to avoid front-end loads made Class B suitable despite higher 12b-1 fees; (3) Garcia documented reasonable diligence into product features and fee mechanics; (4) Class B's contingent deferred sales charge did not create unsuitable incentives to churn, as the time horizon supported the surrender period. This pattern mirrors FINRA AWC settlements where advisors with documented fee analyses and client preferences were found compliant despite recommending higher-cost share classes. The key is documentation of the comparative analysis and client-driven rationale.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability) and fiduciary duty of care; SEC Reg BI Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Detailed written breakeven analysis comparing share classes + documented client preference to avoid front-end loads = compliant despite higher 12b-1 fees.
