Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Sandra Kim is a financial advisor at a dually-registered firm providing both brokerage and advisory services. She recommends that Thomas Grant, a 55-year-old corporate executive with $1.8 million in investable assets and a high risk tolerance, allocate $350,000 from his brokerage account into an actively managed large-cap equity fund (Class A shares, 3.5% front-end load, 0.72% expense ratio) rather than a comparable index ETF (0.03% expense ratio). Sandra documents in her recommendation file that Thomas specifically expressed a strong preference for active management and stock selection expertise, that Thomas has a long investment horizon of 15+ years, that Thomas was presented with the index ETF alternative and its materially lower costs, and that Thomas declined the passive option after discussion. Sandra's firm's written supervisory procedures require that all higher-cost product recommendations be accompanied by a documented cost-benefit comparison, which Sandra completed and had reviewed by her branch manager.

QUESTION
--------
Does Sandra's recommendation of a higher-cost actively managed fund over a lower-cost index ETF satisfy the Regulation BI Care Obligation, given the significant cost differential?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Regulation BI's Care Obligation requires that costs be considered but does not mandate the lowest-cost option in every case. The SEC has stated that cost is one factor among many, and that an advisor may recommend a higher-cost product where there is a reasonable basis for doing so after considering the client's investment profile and reasonably available alternatives. Sandra documented the cost-benefit analysis, presented the lower-cost ETF alternative to the client, obtained the client's informed preference for active management, and had the recommendation reviewed by a supervisor—all consistent with the SEC's guidance in its 2019 interpretive releases on the standard of conduct.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Regulation Best Interest Care Obligation; SEC Fiduciary Interpretation (2019)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The advisor documented a reasonable basis for the higher-cost recommendation by presenting alternatives, performing a cost-benefit analysis, and obtaining the client's informed preference.
