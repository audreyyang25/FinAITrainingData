ID: 837
Source Folder: 17_SEC_Staff_Bulletins
Topic: conflict_mitigation
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A dually registered broker-dealer and investment adviser representative, working through a firm structured similarly to Cambridge Investment Research, recommends that a 67-year-old retired teacher, Donald Freer, move $430,000 from a fee-based advisory account into a commission-based brokerage account holding variable annuities with surrender charges of up to 8% in years one through seven. The representative earns a 6% upfront commission on the annuity sale totaling approximately $25,800. The firm's written supervisory procedures acknowledge this conflict of interest in its general policies but the representative never specifically disclosed to Mr. Freer that he would earn a materially higher commission from the brokerage account recommendation than from continuing to manage the advisory account, nor did he document any conflict mitigation analysis. The recommendation is made verbally with no Best Interest Analysis form completed.

QUESTION
--------
Did the representative satisfy Reg BI's Conflict of Interest Obligation when recommending the account type change and variable annuity purchase to Mr. Freer?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Reg BI's Conflict of Interest Obligation and the Disclosure Obligation.

Reg BI's Conflict of Interest Obligation requires broker-dealers to establish, maintain, and enforce written policies and procedures reasonably designed to identify and disclose or eliminate material conflicts of interest, including conflicts arising from financial incentives such as differential compensation between account types. General firm-level policy acknowledgment does not satisfy the requirement that specific conflicts be disclosed to the affected retail customer at the point of recommendation. The SEC's staff bulletins and the IAA Conflicts CLE outline emphasize that identifying a conflict in written policies is insufficient without mitigating or disclosing it at the individual transaction level. The failure to complete a Best Interest Analysis and the absence of specific conflict disclosure to Mr. Freer compound the violation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iii) – Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Disclosure Obligation

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative failed to specifically disclose a material financial conflict — a $25,800 differential commission incentive — to the retail customer at the time of the account type and product recommendation.
