Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Atlantic Wealth Partners, a dually registered investment adviser and broker-dealer, recommends that its brokerage clients invest in a proprietary model portfolio consisting of third-party ETFs. Atlantic receives no revenue-sharing payments from the ETF sponsors and charges a flat $75 per-trade commission, which is consistent with or below industry norms for the firm's market segment. One of the ETFs in the model portfolio—a mid-cap value fund with a 0.35% expense ratio—has a close competitor from another sponsor with a 0.30% expense ratio. Atlantic's investment committee selected the 0.35% fund based on a documented analysis showing it had superior risk-adjusted returns over 3-, 5-, and 10-year periods, lower tracking error relative to its benchmark, and greater daily trading liquidity. The firm's Reg BI disclosure (Form CRS and written recommendation documentation) explicitly notes the existence of the lower-cost competitor, explains the rationale for selecting the higher-cost fund, and confirms the firm receives no differential compensation regardless of which ETF is recommended. A 62-year-old pre-retiree client with a moderate risk profile and $420,000 in investable assets receives the model portfolio recommendation.

QUESTION
--------
Does Atlantic Wealth Partners' recommendation of the slightly higher-cost ETF over a lower-cost competitor satisfy Regulation Best Interest's Care Obligation and Conflict of Interest Obligation?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Reg BI's Care Obligation requires that a broker-dealer exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill in making recommendations, considering reasonably available alternatives. It does not mandate recommending the lowest-cost option; rather, it requires a reasonable basis for concluding the recommendation is in the customer's best interest. Atlantic's investment committee conducted and documented a comparative analysis demonstrating the selected ETF's superior risk-adjusted performance, lower tracking error, and greater liquidity—all factors relevant to a pre-retiree with a moderate risk profile. The 5-basis-point expense ratio difference is de minimis and is outweighed by the documented performance and risk characteristics. Furthermore, the absence of any differential compensation eliminates the economic conflict that often triggers heightened scrutiny. The firm's explicit disclosure of the alternative and its rationale satisfies the Disclosure Obligation, and the documented process satisfies the Compliance Obligation under Reg BI.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) and (ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation and Conflict of Interest Obligation; Exchange Act Section 15(l)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's documented, comparative investment analysis providing a reasonable basis for the recommendation—combined with the absence of differential compensation and explicit disclosure of the lower-cost alternative—demonstrates that Reg BI does not require selection of the cheapest available product when a costlier option is demonstrably superior on risk-adjusted metrics.
