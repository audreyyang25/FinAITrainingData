Source Folder: unknown
Topic: ia_duty_of_care
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An adviser receives a written email from a 68-year-old client stating: "I am moving into retirement. I need to shift my portfolio to generate $40,000 per year in income with minimal volatility. I cannot tolerate losses." The client's account holds $750,000. The adviser responds with: "Got it. No changes needed. Your current 80/20 stock/bond allocation has worked great." No formal suitability analysis is conducted. No risk assessment questionnaire is completed. The adviser makes no recommendation, documents nothing, and the portfolio remains at 80/20 for the next two years, generating only $18,000 in income and declining 28% in market downturn.

QUESTION
--------
Does this conduct satisfy the duty of care?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the duty of care.

The adviser received an explicit, written statement of materially changed circumstances (retirement, specific income need of $40,000 annually, minimal volatility preference, loss aversion) and failed to conduct any investigation, analysis, or documentation. Leaving an 80/20 aggressive allocation in place when the client explicitly stated need for income generation and minimal volatility is facially unsuitable and demonstrates failure to conduct reasonable investigation. The adviser provided no documented suitability analysis, no risk assessment, and no rationale for why the allocation meets the client's new objectives. The portfolio's failure to generate required income and 28% decline in value prove the allocation was unsuitable for the client's stated objectives. The adviser's casual email response without formal analysis violates the core requirement of the duty of care: investigation of client circumstances and reasonable basis for recommendations.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206(2); SEC Compliance Rule 206(4)-7; duty of care requires documented investigation of client circumstances, reasonable basis for recommendations, and suitability analysis before providing advice.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Complete absence of formal suitability analysis or documented investigation following an explicit written statement of changed circumstances, retirement, specific income need, and loss aversion.
