Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
In a FINRA arbitration proceeding brought by investor Michael Okafor against his former broker-dealer for unsuitable concentration of retirement assets in speculative oil and gas limited partnerships, Okafor's expert witness presented a damages calculation using the out-of-pocket loss method benchmarked against a suitable alternative portfolio aligned with Okafor's documented conservative risk tolerance and retirement timeline. The expert disclosed all methodological assumptions, including the selection of a blended index benchmark, applied consistent time-period calculations from the date of the unsuitable investment through the date of hearing, and provided sensitivity analyses showing how damages varied under alternative benchmark assumptions. The respondent was given full opportunity to cross-examine the expert and present a competing damages analysis.

QUESTION
--------
Is the investor's damages methodology using a documented benchmark comparison with full disclosure of assumptions and sensitivity analysis compliant with FINRA arbitration standards for presenting damages evidence?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant with FINRA arbitration evidentiary standards, accepted securities damages methodology principles, and the procedural fairness requirements of the FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure.

The out-of-pocket loss method benchmarked against a suitable alternative investment is a recognized and widely accepted methodology in securities arbitration for calculating compensatory damages arising from unsuitable investment recommendations, and courts and arbitrators have consistently upheld its use when applied with methodological transparency. Full disclosure of benchmark selection rationale, consistent time-period application, and sensitivity analyses demonstrating the range of plausible outcomes satisfy the reliability and transparency standards expected of expert damages testimony in FINRA proceedings. Providing the respondent with complete opportunity to cross-examine the expert and present competing analysis ensures the procedural fairness required under the FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure and protects the integrity of the evidentiary record.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure Rules 12604, 12606; Securities Exchange Act §28(a); established securities damages jurisprudence including Affiliated Ute Citizens v. United States

KEY FACTOR
----------
A transparent, methodologically documented damages analysis using an appropriate benchmark and providing the respondent full opportunity for adversarial testing satisfies FINRA arbitration evidentiary standards for expert damages testimony.
