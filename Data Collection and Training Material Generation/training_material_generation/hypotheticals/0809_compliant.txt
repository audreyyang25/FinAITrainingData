Source Folder: 13_FINRA_Rulebook
Topic: suitability_rule
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
David Nowak, a 58-year-old small business owner with a $1.8 million investment portfolio, an annual income of $325,000, and a stated risk tolerance of moderate-to-aggressive, contacts his registered representative at a FINRA member firm and asks to allocate $150,000 (approximately 8% of his portfolio) into a leveraged ETF that tracks the S&P 500 at 2x daily returns. The representative, aware of the product's complexity, conducts and documents reasonable diligence into the leveraged ETF's mechanics — including its daily reset feature, volatility decay risk, and unsuitability for long-term buy-and-hold strategies. The representative explains these risks in detail to David, documents that David has 30 years of active trading experience including prior use of leveraged and inverse products, and confirms David's intent to use the position as a short-term tactical allocation with a 30-day target holding period. The representative documents the entire conversation, David's acknowledgment of the risks, and the rationale for why the allocation is consistent with David's overall portfolio profile, objectives, and experience. The firm's supervisory system flags the trade for heightened review, and the supervising principal reviews and approves the transaction after examining the representative's documentation.

QUESTION
--------
Does the representative's recommendation of a leveraged ETF satisfy the reasonable-basis, customer-specific, and quantitative suitability obligations under FINRA Rule 2111?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Under the reasonable-basis obligation of Rule 2111, the representative performed reasonable diligence to understand the leveraged ETF's nature, risks, and mechanics — including daily reset and volatility decay — satisfying the requirement that there be an investment rationale and that the product not be recommended without understanding. Under the customer-specific obligation, the recommendation was tailored to David's documented profile: substantial net worth, high income, moderate-to-aggressive risk tolerance, decades of experience with complex products, and a short-term tactical intent consistent with how leveraged ETFs are designed to perform. The 8% portfolio allocation is proportionate to the client's overall wealth and risk capacity. The firm's supervisory procedures under Rule 3110 also functioned properly, with the flagged trade receiving documented principal review.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 – Suitability (reasonable-basis obligation under Rule 2111(a) and Supplementary Material .05; customer-specific obligation under Rule 2111.04)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Despite the inherent complexity of the leveraged ETF, the representative's thorough diligence, detailed documentation of the client's extensive experience and short-term tactical intent, and the firm's supervisory review established a reasonable basis for suitability under all three components of Rule 2111.
