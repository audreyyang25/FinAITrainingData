ID: 838
Source Folder: 17_SEC_Staff_Bulletins
Topic: care_obligation_detail
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer representative recommends that 44-year-old software engineer Patricia Sung, who has a moderate risk tolerance, a $95,000 emergency fund already established, and $320,000 in investable assets, liquidate her existing low-cost S&P 500 index fund position (expense ratio 0.03%) and reinvest the proceeds into a proprietary actively managed equity fund with a 5.75% front-end sales load and an annual expense ratio of 1.15%. The representative's written rationale in the Best Interest Analysis form states only that the proprietary fund 'has outperformed its benchmark over the past three years' without analyzing the cost differential, tax consequences of the liquidation (estimated at $18,400 in capital gains), or the statistical likelihood of sustained active management outperformance. The firm had recently introduced a sales incentive program awarding representatives additional paid time off for meeting proprietary fund sales quotas.

QUESTION
--------
Did the representative satisfy Reg BI's Care Obligation in recommending the switch from the low-cost index fund to the proprietary actively managed fund for Ms. Sung?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Reg BI's Care Obligation.

Reg BI's Care Obligation requires a broker-dealer to have a reasonable basis to believe a recommendation is in the retail customer's best interest, which demands understanding the costs, risks, and reasonably available alternatives, not merely citing short-term performance. The SEC's 2022 compliance alert specifically found that firms were failing to adequately document cost comparisons and tax consequences when recommending product switches, particularly where proprietary products with higher fees displace lower-cost alternatives. Three years of benchmark outperformance is insufficient alone to justify a switch that imposes an immediate $18,400 tax liability, a 5.75% sales load, and an annual cost differential of approximately $3,584 on the invested amount. The existence of a non-cash incentive program for proprietary fund sales further undermines the independence of the recommendation and implicates the Conflict of Interest Obligation as well.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Staff Bulletin on Care Obligation (Nov. 2021)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The Best Interest Analysis failed to evaluate the material cost differential, tax consequences, and the availability of lower-cost alternatives, which are core requirements of the Reg BI Care Obligation.
