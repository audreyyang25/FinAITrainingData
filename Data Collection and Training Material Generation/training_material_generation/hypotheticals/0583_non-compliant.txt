ID: 583
Source Folder: 05_SEC_Exam_Findings
Topic: best_execution
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A mid-sized SEC-registered investment adviser managing $450 million across 800 client accounts routes approximately 95% of equity trades to a single broker-dealer, Apex Clearing Services, under a soft-dollar arrangement in which the adviser receives Bloomberg terminal access, Morningstar research subscriptions, and FactSet analytics valued at approximately $185,000 annually. The adviser's Best Execution Committee meets quarterly and reviews execution quality reports, but those reports are generated solely by Apex itself and are not independently verified. Competing broker-dealers have offered the adviser demonstrably tighter spreads—an average of 1.2 basis points narrower per trade—and faster execution speeds, which over the prior year would have resulted in approximately $540,000 in aggregate savings for clients. The adviser's Form ADV Part 2A discloses the existence of soft-dollar arrangements generally but does not quantify the benefits received or explain how the arrangement could create an incentive to route trades to Apex regardless of execution quality. During a 2025 SEC examination, the Division of Examinations requested documentation of the firm's best execution analysis and found no comparative broker-dealer assessments had been conducted in two years.

QUESTION
--------
Did the adviser satisfy its best execution obligations and properly manage its soft-dollar conflict of interest given the concentrated routing, reliance on the executing broker's own quality reports, and absence of comparative analysis?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes a failure to meet best execution obligations and a breach of the adviser's fiduciary duty to manage conflicts of interest arising from soft-dollar arrangements.

SEC EXAMS priorities have consistently emphasized best execution and soft-dollar practices as key examination focus areas. An adviser's best execution obligation requires it to seek the most favorable terms reasonably available under the circumstances, considering factors beyond commission cost, including execution quality, speed, and overall transaction economics. Relying solely on execution reports generated by the counterparty broker-dealer without independent verification, failing to conduct comparative assessments of competing brokers for two years, and routing 95% of volume to one broker while receiving $185,000 in soft-dollar benefits constitutes a failure to adequately evaluate and manage this conflict. The $540,000 in foregone client savings materially demonstrates the harm.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act §§ 206(1) and 206(2); SEC Section 28(e) Safe Harbor for Soft Dollars; SEC EXAMS Risk Alerts on Best Execution; FY 2025/2026 Examination Priorities

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser failed to independently verify execution quality or conduct comparative broker-dealer assessments for two years while routing nearly all trades to a single broker providing $185,000 in soft-dollar benefits, resulting in $540,000 in foregone client savings.
