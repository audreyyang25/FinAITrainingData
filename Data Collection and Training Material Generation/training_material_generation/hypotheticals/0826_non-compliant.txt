Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered broker-dealer, Meridian Capital Advisors, recommends that a 72-year-old retired schoolteacher with $180,000 in savings and a stated conservative risk tolerance roll her entire IRA into a variable annuity with a 7-year surrender period and annual fees of 2.85%. The broker, Marcus Tilden, earns a $9,000 commission on the transaction. When the client asks whether Marcus has any financial interest in the recommendation, he tells her the product is 'no extra cost to her' and never discloses the commission or surrender charges. The Dodd-Frank Act §913 study explicitly flagged broker compensation conflicts as a primary investor harm requiring enhanced disclosure obligations under any uniform fiduciary or best interest standard.

QUESTION
--------
Did Marcus Tilden's failure to disclose his $9,000 commission and the 7-year surrender period to his retired client violate applicable conduct standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a material conflict-of-interest disclosure violation under Reg BI and the Dodd-Frank §913 investor protection framework.

The Dodd-Frank §913 study identified broker compensation conflicts as a central investor harm and directed the SEC to establish rules ensuring brokers act in clients' best interest with full conflict disclosure. Reg BI's Conflict of Interest Obligation requires broker-dealers to establish policies to identify, disclose, and mitigate conflicts including compensation-based incentives. Affirmatively misrepresenting that a product has 'no extra cost' while concealing a $9,000 commission compounds the disclosure violation into potential fraud under Exchange Act antifraud provisions.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Dodd-Frank Act §913; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iii) – Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation; SEC Form CRS Disclosure Requirements

KEY FACTOR
----------
The broker's affirmative misrepresentation that the product had no cost, combined with failure to disclose a $9,000 commission conflict, is a textbook Reg BI conflict-of-interest disclosure failure directly addressed in the Dodd-Frank §913 legislative record.
