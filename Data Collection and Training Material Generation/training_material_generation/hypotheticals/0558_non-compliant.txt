Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Edward Garrison, a general securities representative associated with a mid-sized FINRA member firm, recommended that two retired clients—a 72-year-old widow with $340,000 in liquid assets and a moderate risk tolerance, and an 81-year-old retired teacher with $210,000 in savings and a conservative profile—each take out home equity lines of credit (HELOCs) on their primary residences. Garrison then recommended both clients invest the HELOC proceeds—$85,000 and $60,000, respectively—into speculative, illiquid private placements offered through the firm. Garrison did not document any analysis of why the HELOC-funded investment strategy was in either client's best interest, nor did he consider the clients' existing income needs, the cost of borrowing against home equity, or the illiquidity and high-risk nature of the private placements relative to their conservative profiles. His supervisor did not review or approve the recommendations.

QUESTION
--------
Did Garrison satisfy the Reg BI Care Obligation when recommending that two elderly, conservative clients borrow against home equity to invest in speculative private placements?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation.

The Care Obligation under Reg BI requires that a broker-dealer exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill to understand the potential risks, rewards, and costs of a recommendation and have a reasonable basis to believe the recommendation is in the retail customer's best interest, considering the customer's investment profile. Recommending that elderly, conservative clients with limited liquid assets use borrowed funds—carrying ongoing interest costs and lien risk on their primary residences—to invest in illiquid, speculative private placements plainly fails this standard. This pattern closely parallels the Beslagic AWC (No. 2023079509301), where FINRA found violations for recommending clients access home equity line proceeds to invest, without a reasonable basis that the strategy was appropriate. The absence of any documented analysis and the lack of supervisory review compound the violation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability); FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative recommended elderly, conservative clients borrow against home equity to invest in speculative, illiquid private placements without any documented analysis that the leveraged investment strategy was in their best interest.
