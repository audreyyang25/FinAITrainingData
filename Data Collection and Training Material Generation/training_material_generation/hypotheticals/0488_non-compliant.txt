Source Folder: 01_SEC_Guidance_NoAction_Interpretive
Topic: no_action_reliance
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Beacon Securities, a FINRA-member broker-dealer, relies on a 15-year-old SEC no-action letter that permitted a specific fee-sharing arrangement between affiliated broker-dealers and investment advisers under conditions that included a cap on shared fees at 40% of advisory revenue and quarterly independent audits of shared expenses. Beacon enters into a similar arrangement with its affiliated adviser, Beacon Advisory Group, but sets the fee-sharing percentage at 55% and conducts internal compliance reviews semi-annually instead of independent quarterly audits. Beacon's CCO determines that the original no-action letter provides sufficient legal cover because the 'general principle' of fee-sharing was approved and the differences are 'immaterial.' No updated legal analysis or independent counsel opinion is obtained.

QUESTION
--------
Is Beacon Securities' reliance on the aged no-action letter appropriate given the material deviations from the letter's stated conditions?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is improper reliance on a no-action letter due to material deviation from its conditions.

No-action letters provide relief only to the extent the requesting party's facts and conditions match those described in the letter. Beacon materially deviated from two core conditions — the fee-sharing cap and the independent audit requirement — making reliance on the letter unjustified. The SEC has consistently stated that no-action relief is fact-specific and cannot be extended to materially different arrangements. Beacon's failure to obtain updated legal analysis compounds the deficiency and reflects an inadequate compliance program.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC No-Action Letter reliance principles; Investment Advisers Act of 1940 fiduciary duty; FINRA 2025 Annual Regulatory Oversight Report – Compliance Program observations

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm materially deviated from the specific conditions of the no-action letter it relied upon, exceeding the fee-sharing cap by 15 percentage points and substituting internal reviews for required independent audits.
