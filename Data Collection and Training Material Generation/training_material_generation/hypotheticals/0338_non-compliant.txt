Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Registered Representative David K. (age 48, employed at Summit Advisors, a hybrid broker-dealer/RIA) recommended to Client C (age 62, $520,000 IRA, moderate-to-conservative profile) an exchange from an existing variable annuity (VA-1: American General LifeGuard VA, purchased in 2016 for $345,000, current value $412,000, remaining surrender charge 2%, cost to exit = $8,240). David recommended the client exchange into a new variable annuity (VA-2: Allianz Advantage VA, upfront commission to David = 6% = $24,720, surrender charge schedule 8% declining over 7 years). The existing VA-1 had a 1.15% annual mortality & expense (M&E) fee; VA-2 has 1.38% M&E. David's suitability memo stated: 'New VA offers enhanced income rider with 6% annual step-up (vs. 4% on existing).' The client's expressed goal was 'stable income in retirement; no trading.' David did not document the cost of surrender charges, did not quantify the step-up rider benefit in dollars, and did not explain why exchanging (losing liquidity for 2 years, incurring $8,240 penalty) was superior to retaining VA-1 and purchasing a separate income product. The exchange occurred in November 2023.

QUESTION
--------
Did David K.'s variable annuity exchange recommendation comply with FINRA Rule 2330 and Rule 2111?

ANSWER
------
No. David violated FINRA Rule 2330(d)'s mandatory disclosure obligations and Rule 2111's suitability standard. FINRA Rule 2330(d) requires clear, specific disclosure of: (1) surrender charges, (2) M&E fee differential, (3) material differences in features, and (4) comparison of costs. David failed to quantify the $8,240 surrender charge as a cost or compare total expected lifetime costs between VA-1 and VA-2. The 6% commission incentive created a conflict of interest that was not mitigated by transparent cost analysis. The income rider 'step-up' (6% vs. 4%) lacks quantification—without modeling the income benefit under the client's withdrawal scenario, it is an unsupported assertion. For a 62-year-old seeking stable income with no trading intent, exchanging a 7-year-old, sunk-cost VA (with known features and 2% surrender charge ending in 2 years) into a new VA with an 8% surrender charge schedule is presumptively unsuitable unless the advisor documents why waiting 2 years to exchange (or not exchanging at all) is inappropriate. FINRA enforcement patterns (2019-2023) consistently cite such exchanges as violations when the advisor's compensation is disproportionate to documented client benefit.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330(d) (Variable Product Disclosure), FINRA Rule 2111(a) (Suitability), FINRA Rule 2010 (Fair Dealing)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommending a variable annuity exchange without quantifying surrender charge costs, M&E differentials, or the specific dollar benefit of enhanced riders violates mandatory disclosure duties and creates an unsuitable recommendation driven by disproportionate advisor compensation.
