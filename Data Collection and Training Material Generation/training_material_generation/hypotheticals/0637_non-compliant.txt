Source Folder: 08_State_Fiduciary_Rules
Topic: state_fiduciary_bd
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An investment adviser representative registered in a state that has adopted the NASAA Model Rule 102(a)(4)-1 manages discretionary accounts for several clients. Without obtaining prior written consent or making any disclosure, the representative arranges cross-trades between two client accounts—selling 5,000 shares of a mid-cap equity from Client A's account to Client B's account at the prior day's closing price—to rebalance both portfolios. The representative believes the trade benefits both clients and charges no additional commission, but no written authorization for principal or agency cross-transactions exists in either client's advisory agreement.

QUESTION
--------
Does this cross-trade arrangement comply with the NASAA Model Rule on unethical business practices of investment advisers?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct constitutes an unethical business practice under NASAA Model Rule 102(a)(4)-1.

NASAA Model Rule 102(a)(4)-1 establishes that investment advisers and their representatives are fiduciaries who must act primarily for the benefit of their clients. Effecting cross-trades between discretionary client accounts without prior written disclosure and client consent violates the fiduciary duty of loyalty and the prohibition on conflicts of interest. Even if the adviser subjectively believes the trade benefits both parties, the absence of informed consent and written authorization makes this an unethical business practice under the rule.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
NASAA Model Rule 102(a)(4)-1 – Unethical Business Practices of Investment Advisers, Investment Adviser Representatives, and Federal Covered Advisers

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser executed agency cross-trades between client accounts without obtaining the required prior written disclosure and informed consent from both clients.
