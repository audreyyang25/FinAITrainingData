ID: 756
Source Folder: 13_FINRA_Rulebook
Topic: variable_annuity_rule
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered representative at a mid-sized broker-dealer recommends that Margaret Chen, a 78-year-old retired schoolteacher with $180,000 in liquid savings and a conservative risk tolerance, exchange her existing deferred variable annuity — which had exited its surrender charge period — for a new deferred variable annuity with a 7-year surrender schedule and an annual mortality and expense charge of 1.45%. The new annuity offered a slightly different subaccount lineup but no materially different death benefit or living benefit riders. The representative processed the exchange without obtaining prior registered principal review or approval, noting in his file only that the client 'wanted something new.' The firm's supervisory system flagged the transaction but the branch manager did not review it for 45 days.

QUESTION
--------
Did the representative and the firm comply with FINRA Rule 2330 (formerly Rule 2821) requirements for the deferred variable annuity exchange?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates FINRA Rule 2330.

FINRA Rule 2330(b) requires that, before recommending the purchase or exchange of a deferred variable annuity, the registered representative must have a reasonable basis to believe the transaction is suitable given the customer's age, liquidity needs, surrender period, and existing annuity benefits. Rule 2330(c) mandates that a registered principal review and approve the transaction before it is processed — not 45 days after. Exchanging a surrender-free annuity for one imposing a new 7-year surrender period for a 78-year-old with limited liquid assets and no material product improvement fails both the suitability and principal pre-approval requirements.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330 – Deferred Variable Annuities (Principal Review and Suitability Obligations)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The exchange subjected an elderly, conservative client to a new surrender period with no material benefit, and the firm failed to obtain the required prior registered principal approval.
