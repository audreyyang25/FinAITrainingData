Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An SEC-registered investment adviser managing $85 million in client assets allocates 30% of client portfolios to a private real estate fund managed by the adviser's affiliated entity. The affiliated fund charges a 2% management fee and 20% performance allocation. The adviser's Form ADV Part 2A mentions in general terms that 'the firm or its affiliates may have financial interests in recommended investments,' but does not specifically disclose the affiliated ownership of the real estate fund, the exact fee structure, or the material conflict of interest arising from the dual revenue stream. A retired couple, both age 70, with a $620,000 portfolio and moderate risk tolerance, has $186,000 invested in this illiquid fund with a 5-year lock-up period. The adviser did not discuss the illiquidity risk or the specific conflict with the clients.

QUESTION
--------
Did the investment adviser satisfy its fiduciary duty of loyalty under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 with respect to disclosure of the affiliated fund conflict?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates the investment adviser's fiduciary duty of loyalty under the Advisers Act through inadequate conflict disclosure.

As articulated in SEC Release IA-4889, an investment adviser's fiduciary duty requires full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts of interest sufficient for the client to provide informed consent. Generic, boilerplate language referencing potential affiliate interests is insufficient when the adviser is allocating nearly a third of a client's assets to a specific affiliated fund from which the adviser derives direct economic benefit. The SEC has emphasized that disclosure must be specific enough that a client understands the nature and extent of the conflict. Placing $186,000 of a 70-year-old couple's moderate-risk portfolio in an illiquid, high-fee affiliated vehicle without specific conflict disclosure or discussion of liquidity risk represents a breach of both the duty of loyalty and the duty of care.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §206(1) and §206(2); SEC Release No. IA-4889 (Proposed Interpretation Regarding Standard of Conduct for Investment Advisers)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser used vague, generic conflict disclosure instead of providing specific, full, and fair disclosure about the affiliated fund's ownership, fees, and the material conflict of interest as required by the Advisers Act fiduciary standard.
