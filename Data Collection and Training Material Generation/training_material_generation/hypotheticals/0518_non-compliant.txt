Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Ridgeline Wealth Management, LLC, a registered investment adviser, and its founder and sole owner, Daniel K. Mercer, managed approximately $185 million in discretionary client accounts. Between 2020 and 2023, Mercer directed approximately $28 million of client assets into a series of private placement offerings for real estate development LLCs in which Mercer held undisclosed personal equity stakes ranging from 8% to 15%. The private placements paid Mercer's LLCs management fees and promoted interest, effectively enriching Mercer on both sides of the transaction. Ridgeline's compliance policies and procedures manual, last updated in 2018, contained no provisions addressing personal financial interests in recommended investments and no protocols for identifying or escalating principal or agency cross transactions. When the SEC Division of Examinations conducted a routine examination, Ridgeline's CCO—who was also Mercer himself—was unable to produce any written conflict analyses, client consent records, or evidence that clients were informed of Mercer's personal stakes. Mercer maintained that the investments had performed well and that clients had not complained.

QUESTION
--------
Did Ridgeline Wealth Management and Daniel Mercer violate the Advisers Act by directing client assets into private placements in which Mercer held undisclosed personal equity interests, while also failing to maintain adequate compliance policies?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes violations of Investment Advisers Act §206(1), §206(2), and §206(4) and Rule 206(4)-7 for undisclosed conflicts of interest, self-dealing, and failure to adopt and implement adequate compliance policies and procedures.

Mercer's direction of client capital into private placements in which he held undisclosed personal equity interests constitutes a textbook self-dealing conflict and potential principal transaction that must be fully disclosed and consented to under Sections 206(1) and 206(2). The absence of written conflict analyses or client consent records demonstrates the violation was not merely an oversight but a systemic failure. Additionally, Rule 206(4)-7 requires every registered investment adviser to adopt and implement written compliance policies and procedures reasonably designed to prevent violations of the Advisers Act; Ridgeline's outdated manual with no provisions for personal financial interests in recommended investments represents a material compliance program deficiency. This mirrors the patterns in both the Horter Investment Management (IA-6182) and Tadrus (IA-6933) proceedings, where adviser principals engaged in conflicted transactions without adequate disclosure or compliance infrastructure. Mercer's defense that investments performed well is legally irrelevant—fiduciary disclosure obligations exist independent of investment outcomes.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Sections 206(1), 206(2), 206(4), and Rule 206(4)-7 (Compliance Policies and Procedures); see also SEC IA-6182 (In the Matter of Horter Investment Management, LLC and Drew K. Horter) and SEC IA-6933 (In the Matter of Mina N. Tadrus)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser's principal directed client assets into investments in which he held undisclosed personal financial interests, compounded by a total absence of compliance policies designed to detect or prevent such self-dealing conflicts.
