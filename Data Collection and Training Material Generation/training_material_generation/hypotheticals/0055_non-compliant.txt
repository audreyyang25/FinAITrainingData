Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An adviser recommends that a 68-year-old widow with $600K in retirement savings shift 40% of her portfolio (originally invested in index funds with 0.07% expense ratio) into a separately managed account (SMA) charging 1.2% annual advisory fee (on top of underlying fund fees of 0.35%), claiming superior customization and tax-loss harvesting. The client's stated objective is steady income in retirement. The adviser does not provide written documentation comparing the costs and benefits of the SMA versus the original low-cost index approach, and does not analyze whether the marginal benefit of customization justifies the 1.45% total annual cost increase.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the fiduciary duty of care?

ANSWER
------
No, this recommendation violates the fiduciary duty of care under the Investment Adviser Act.

The adviser fails the duty of care in multiple ways: (1) no written analysis comparing the costs and expected benefits of the recommended strategy versus the incumbent low-cost index approach; (2) no documented investigation of whether the 1.45% increase in annual fees is justified by incremental tax-loss harvesting or customization benefits; (3) no consideration of the client's income objective—a simple income-focused index portfolio may be less complex and cheaper than an SMA. The 1.45% annual cost difference on $240K is $3,480 per year, which could significantly impact an income-focused retiree's cash flow. Under SEC guidance on fee reasonableness (IA Release No. 2781), advisers must base fee recommendations on documented analysis of services, complexity, and alternatives. The lack of any written comparative analysis is a red flag. The duty of care requires the adviser to demonstrate that the recommendation was the product of adequate research and investigation appropriate to the client's profile.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act §206(1) (duty of care requires adviser to exercise reasonable care, skill, prudence, and diligence); SEC Rule 206(4)-8 (adviser must establish written policies and procedures regarding fee reasonableness); SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau, 375 U.S. 180 (1963) (adviser must base recommendations on careful investigation and analysis)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Failure to document comparative analysis of the cost-benefit tradeoff between the recommended 1.45% fee increase and the original low-cost approach breaches the duty of care.
