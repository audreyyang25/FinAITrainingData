FACTS
-----
RIA advisor meets with 42-year-old client with $1,200,000 in liquid assets and 22-year investment horizon. Client explicitly states: 'I want to align my portfolio with my values—I will not invest in fossil fuels, weapons, or tobacco, even if returns are lower.' Advisor documents this preference in writing and client initials it. Advisor recommends: (1) Vanguard ESG U.S. Stock ETF (EUSA, 0.08% ER); (2) iShares MSCI EAFE ESG Select ETF (SXUS, 0.15% ER); (3) Vanguard ESG International Bond ETF (BOND, 0.12% ER). Advisor explicitly discloses in a written memo: 'ESG screening may exclude high-performing sectors (energy, financials) in certain market cycles. Historical data shows ESG funds underperformed broad market benchmarks by 0.8–3.2% annualized over 2010–2020, but have outperformed 2020–2024. You have chosen values-aligned investing over pure performance optimization. We will monitor performance quarterly.' Client acknowledges receipt and consent. Portfolio weighted 50% U.S. equities, 30% international, 20% bonds. No-trade cost: $0.00. Annual advisory fee: 0.65% on assets under management.

QUESTION
--------
Did the advisor comply with Reg BI, the IA Act fiduciary standard, and SEC Marketing Rule guidance when recommending ESG funds matching client's explicit values preference with full disclosure?

ANSWER
------
Yes. The advisor exceeded Reg BI standards: (1) Client preference for ESG integration was documented and initialed, satisfying the care obligation under 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i). (2) Material facts—historical underperformance in certain periods and sector exclusions—were disclosed in writing, satisfying IA Act §206(1) (fiduciary duty of care and loyalty). (3) The product selection (ETFs with low expense ratios: 0.08–0.15%) was cost-appropriate and not used as a commission-generation vehicle. (4) The advisor framed ESG as a 'values-aligned' choice, not a pure performance claim, reducing greenwashing risk under SEC Marketing Rule IA-5653. (5) Ongoing monitoring and documentation created an audit trail. This mirrors compliant practices in FINRA AWC settlements where ESG recommendation was upheld when client preference was documented and conflicts disclosed.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2); IA Act §206(1) fiduciary duty; SEC Marketing Rule IA-5653 (substantiation and avoidance of misleading ESG claims)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Client's express written ESG preference, paired with full disclosure of historical performance trade-offs and low-cost product selection, cures any suitability tension.
