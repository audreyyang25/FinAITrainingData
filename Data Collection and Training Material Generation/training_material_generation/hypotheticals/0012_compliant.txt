FACTS
-----
A 72-year-old retiree with $220,000 in investable assets, low risk tolerance, and a need for current income is recommended a 'bond ladder' strategy consisting of: (1) $120,000 in individual Treasury bonds (ladder from 2-10 years, current yield 3.5-4.1%); (2) $80,000 in a municipal bond fund with 0.25% ER and 3.2% yield; (3) $20,000 in a short-term bond fund (0.15% ER, 4.0% yield) for emergency liquidity. The advisor documents: (1) the strategy's alignment with the client's low risk tolerance and income need (generates ~$7,800 annually from yields); (2) analysis of costs (0.20% weighted average ER plus no sales loads); (3) comparison to alternatives including a variable annuity with income rider (would cost 2.45% but generate contractually guaranteed income), a balanced mutual fund (0.6% ER), and a bond ETF portfolio (0.12% ER but no TreasuryPersonal bonds); (4) tax implications for the retiree's bracket; and (5) specific discussion of interest-rate and credit risks and why they are acceptable for a retiree with 15+ year horizon and $50,000 emergency fund outside this portfolio.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the Reg BI care obligation?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this satisfies the care obligation under Reg BI §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i).

The advisor demonstrated comprehensive care analysis. Costs are transparent and low (0.20% weighted ER, no loads). The alternatives analysis considered both higher-cost options (VA at 2.45% with guaranteed income) and lower-cost options (bond ETF at 0.12%), and the advisor explained why the bond ladder with individual Treasuries + municipal fund was appropriate: Treasuries provide safety and the municipal fund provides tax efficiency for the client's bracket. The strategy explicitly addresses risks (interest-rate and credit) and the advisor documented why they are acceptable (long horizon, emergency fund separate). The income generation ($7,800 annually) is analyzed relative to the client's needs. The recommendation is not the lowest-cost option possible, but the cost differential is justified by the specific benefits (laddering, tax efficiency, safety of Treasuries) relevant to this client's profile.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Regulation Best Interest §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i) — care obligation to consider the costs, risks, rewards, and alternatives in light of the retail customer's profile.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Detailed documentation of alternatives analysis, explicit cost comparison, alignment with client's needs and risk tolerance, and explanation of why a moderate-cost strategy was preferable to both higher-cost (VA) and lower-cost (bond ETF) alternatives.
