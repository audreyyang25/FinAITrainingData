ID: 808
Source Folder: 13_FINRA_Rulebook
Topic: variable_annuity_rule
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered representative at a FINRA member firm recommends that Margaret Chen, a 74-year-old retired schoolteacher with $210,000 in liquid assets and a stated investment objective of capital preservation, exchange her existing deferred variable annuity — which has exited its surrender period — for a new deferred variable annuity with a 7-year surrender schedule and an annual mortality and expense charge 35 basis points higher than her current contract. The representative documents that the new annuity offers a guaranteed minimum income benefit rider, but does not document any analysis of whether Margaret's existing contract's features were inadequate or whether the new surrender period and higher costs are justified given her age, liquidity needs, and conservative objectives. The firm's registered principal approves the exchange the same day it is submitted without requesting the comparative analysis or questioning the suitability of imposing a new surrender period on a 74-year-old client.

QUESTION
--------
Did the registered representative and supervising principal comply with FINRA Rule 2330 (formerly Rule 2821) requirements for the recommended variable annuity exchange?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates FINRA Rule 2330's suitability and principal review requirements for deferred variable annuity exchanges.

FINRA Rule 2330(b) requires that, before recommending a deferred variable annuity exchange, the representative must have a reasonable basis to believe the customer has been informed of the material features of both the existing and proposed annuities — including surrender charges, fees, and benefits — and that the exchange is suitable considering the customer's age, liquidity needs, investment objectives, and the costs of the new surrender period. Rule 2330(c) further requires that a registered principal review and approve the transaction before submission based on documentation that demonstrates the exchange meets these suitability criteria. Here, the representative failed to perform or document a comparative analysis of the two contracts, and the principal rubber-stamped the exchange without the required substantive review, violating both provisions.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330 – Deferred Variable Annuities (specifically Rule 2330(b) suitability considerations and Rule 2330(c) principal review and approval requirements)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative failed to document any comparative analysis justifying why a new 7-year surrender period and higher fees were suitable for a 74-year-old capital-preservation client, and the principal approved the exchange without conducting the substantive review required by Rule 2330(c).
