Source Folder: unknown
Topic: ia_duty_of_care
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 48-year-old client with $600,000 in a joint taxable investment account (held with spouse) requested advice. The adviser met only with the primary account holder without documenting whether the spouse's risk tolerance, objectives, or tax situation differed. The adviser then recommended a concentrated position of 45% in individual technology stocks, 35% in sector-specific ETFs, and 20% in bonds—without documenting any investigation into the household's overall financial situation, whether the spouse had other accounts, or whether this concentration was appropriate for a joint account.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation comply with the duty of care?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the investment adviser's duty of care.

The adviser has breached the duty of care in multiple respects: (1) for a joint account, the adviser has a duty to investigate the risk tolerance and objectives of both account owners, not just one; (2) the adviser failed to investigate the household's overall financial situation, including whether other accounts exist (which could render a 45% technology concentration excessive); (3) the 45% concentrated technology position lacks a documented reasonable basis when the adviser has not investigated whether this concentration is consistent with a joint household's overall financial objectives; and (4) there is no documentation of investment objectives or suitability analysis. For joint accounts, advisers must conduct due diligence that encompasses all beneficial owners and household-level concentration risk.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206; SEC Rule 206(4)-1; SEC guidance on household-level suitability analysis.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Failure to meet with or document risk tolerance/objectives of spouse on a joint account, combined with concentrated 45% technology position without documented investigation of household-level concentration risk, violates reasonable investigation requirement.
