ID: 614
Source Folder: 05_SEC_Exam_Findings
Topic: share_class_selection
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A dually registered investment adviser and broker-dealer recommends Class A shares of a large-cap equity mutual fund to 47 retail retirement accounts over a 14-month period, generating approximately $312,000 in upfront sales charges. A lower-cost institutional share class of the same fund was available on the firm's platform for accounts meeting the $25,000 minimum, and 39 of the 47 accounts exceeded that threshold. The firm's compliance policies reference share class evaluation, but the policies were drafted in 2014 and never updated to reflect the availability of institutional shares added to the platform in 2019. The firm's CRD disclosures note that representatives receive higher compensation for Class A shares but do not specifically disclose the availability of lower-cost share classes. During the SEC Division of Examinations' sweep — consistent with the share class selection initiative announced in OCIE's examination priorities and related risk alerts — examiners found no documentation that any cost comparison was performed for any of the 47 accounts.

QUESTION
--------
Did the firm satisfy its fiduciary and Reg BI care obligations when recommending Class A shares without evaluating reasonably available lower-cost share classes?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the Investment Advisers Act fiduciary duty and SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation regarding share class selection.

The SEC Division of Examinations has repeatedly identified share class selection as a key examination priority area impacting retail investors, particularly those saving for retirement. The Care Obligation under Reg BI requires that a broker-dealer exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill to understand reasonably available alternatives and evaluate costs. Here, the firm failed to compare available share classes, failed to update its compliance policies to reflect platform changes, and generated $312,000 in unnecessary upfront charges for 39 accounts that qualified for lower-cost institutional shares. The absence of any documented cost comparison across 47 accounts demonstrates a systemic failure, not an isolated oversight.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 fiduciary duty; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Share Class Selection Disclosure Initiative (2018)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm systematically recommended higher-cost Class A shares without evaluating or documenting the availability of lower-cost institutional share classes for which the vast majority of client accounts qualified.
