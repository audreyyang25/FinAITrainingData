FACTS
-----
A 58-year-old male client with $850,000 in liquid assets and moderate risk tolerance holds a concentrated position of 42% in XYZ Corp stock (his former employer). His RIA recommends keeping the position after documenting in writing: (1) the client's emotional attachment and desire to retain the holding despite diversification advice; (2) tax-loss harvesting opportunities if the position declines; (3) quarterly reviews to monitor concentration risk; and (4) a specific written instruction from the client declining further diversification recommendations, signed and dated.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation to retain a concentrated position satisfy the fiduciary duty of care and loyalty under the Investment Adviser Act?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant with fiduciary duty standards.

The RIA met the duty of care by: (1) making an informed recommendation contrary to the client's concentrated position, demonstrating independent analysis; (2) documenting the client's informed refusal in writing, creating a clear record that the decision was client-directed, not adviser-directed; (3) establishing a monitoring protocol (quarterly reviews) to reassess suitability going forward; and (4) identifying a potential benefit (tax harvesting) that could justify retention. The written waiver and documented dialogue satisfy the SEC's expectation that advisers document material discussions about risk. This parallels SEC enforcement in which advisers were not sanctioned when they recommended diversification, documented client refusal, and maintained ongoing review—see SEC examination guidance on concentrated positions (IA Exam Priorities 2021–2023).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act § 206 (fiduciary duty); SEC Investment Adviser Examination Priorities (duty of care requires advisers to conduct reasonable diligence and provide advice tailored to client circumstances); common law rule that fiduciaries may honor client-directed decisions when properly informed and documented.

KEY FACTOR
----------
The written documentation of the client's informed refusal, combined with the adviser's initial diversification recommendation and ongoing monitoring, converts what appears to be imprudent concentration into a client-directed exception supported by a clear audit trail.
