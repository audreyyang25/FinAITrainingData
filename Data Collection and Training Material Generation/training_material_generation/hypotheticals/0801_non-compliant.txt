Source Folder: 15_FINRA_Regulatory_Notices
Topic: complex_products
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Greenfield Securities, a mid-size broker-dealer, begins offering alternative mutual funds (liquid alts) including the BlackRock Event Driven Equity Fund and the JPMorgan Hedged Equity Fund to its retail client base. The firm's registered representatives recommend these Alt Funds to approximately 340 customers over a 14-month period, including retirees and moderate-risk investors. The firm has no written supervisory procedures specific to Alt Funds, no product-specific training requirements for representatives, and no heightened suitability review process distinguishing these products from traditional mutual funds. A branch manager signs off on all Alt Fund trades using the same checklist used for conventional equity mutual fund purchases.

QUESTION
--------
Did Greenfield Securities maintain a reasonably designed supervisory system for the recommendation of alternative mutual funds to retail customers?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates FINRA supervisory obligations regarding alternative mutual funds.

Regulatory Notice 22-11 explicitly reminds member firms that they must establish and maintain reasonably designed supervisory systems for recommendations of alternative mutual funds. The notice describes enforcement actions against firms that failed to create product-specific supervisory procedures for Alt Funds, including the absence of tailored training and heightened review processes. Using a generic equity mutual fund review checklist for complex products with distinct risk characteristics—such as the use of derivatives, leverage, and non-traditional strategies—fails to meet FINRA's supervisory standards.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rules 3110 (Supervision) and 2111 (Suitability); FINRA Regulatory Notice 22-11 (Alternative Mutual Funds Sales Practice Obligations)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm applied a generic mutual fund supervisory checklist to complex alternative mutual funds, with no product-specific written supervisory procedures, training, or heightened review—precisely the deficiency cited in FINRA Regulatory Notice 22-11 enforcement actions.
