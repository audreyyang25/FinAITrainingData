Topic: esg_sustainable_investing
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Jennifer Park, age 45, manages a $8.2M defined contribution plan for her employer, a mid-size manufacturing firm. Her advisor, a major wirehouse, recommended adding the firm's proprietary 'Sustainability Leaders Fund' (SLF) as a core holding, allocating $3.1M (37.8% of the plan). The SLF prospectus stated: 'Invests in companies with 'best-in-class' ESG ratings and sustainable practices.' However, a detailed analysis revealed: (1) SLF held 18% in fossil fuel companies with 'improved' ESG scores (refineries with carbon capture programs); (2) the fund's ESG criteria were self-developed and not independently verified by MSCI, Sustainalytics, or other recognized raters; (3) SLF's 5-year return was 7.3% vs. Russell 1000 ESG Index 8.9% and S&P 500 9.4%; (4) SLF's expense ratio was 0.87%, vs. comparable ESG index funds at 0.20–0.25%; (5) the advisor marketed SLF as 'a leader in true ESG screening' in written marketing materials and client presentations, but did not disclose: the self-developed ESG criteria, the fossil fuel holdings, the 1.6% performance drag vs. the S&P 500, or the proprietary fee premium (62–67 bps higher than competitive ESG funds). The advisor earned a 30 bps revenue share from SLF sales.

QUESTION
--------
Did the advisor's recommendation of the proprietary Sustainability Leaders Fund violate Reg BI, the Marketing Rule, and the duty of loyalty by misrepresenting ESG credentials and omitting material conflicts of interest?

ANSWER
------
Non-compliant. Multiple violations: (1) SEC Marketing Rule (IA-5653 §206(4)-1(a)(1)): The claim of 'best-in-class' ESG and being a 'leader in true ESG screening' is misleading because the ESG criteria are proprietary/unverified and the fund holds 18% in fossil fuels, directly contradicting the marketing narrative. No substantiation for 'leader' claim exists. (2) Reg BI §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i): Advisor failed a reasonable investigation—SLF underperformed comparable ESG funds by 1.6% annually and conventional benchmarks by 2.1%, with no documented rationale for the overweight allocation despite material cost and performance disadvantages. (3) Duty of loyalty (Investment Adviser Act §206(1)): The 30 bps revenue share created an undisclosed conflict favoring proprietary product. Advisor recommended SLF to a retirement plan (fiduciary context) without disclosing: the revenue share, the fossil fuel holdings inconsistent with 'true ESG,' and the underperformance. (4) The self-developed ESG criteria without third-party verification, combined with fossil fuel holdings, constitutes greenwashing under SEC 2024 ESG guidance. Similar to SEC enforcement of proprietary fund marketing (2022–2023 sweep pattern).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Marketing Rule IA-5653 §206(4)-1(a)(1) (no misleading ESG claims without substantiation); Reg BI §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i) (reasonable investigation of costs and performance); Investment Adviser Act §206(1) (duty of loyalty—undisclosed conflicts); Investment Advisers Act §206(2) (misleading statements).

KEY FACTOR
----------
Advisor marketed proprietary ESG fund with unsubstantiated 'leader' claims and fossil fuel holdings, failed to investigate underperformance, and omitted disclosure of 30 bps revenue share driving the recommendation.
