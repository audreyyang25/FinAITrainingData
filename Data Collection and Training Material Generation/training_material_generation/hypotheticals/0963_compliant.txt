Topic: damages_methodology_consequential_harm_documentation
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
In a FINRA arbitration involving unsuitable concentration in high-risk speculative energy sector funds, claimant's expert witness Dr. Yuen presented a damages methodology using the out-of-pocket loss measure as the primary calculation while supplementing it with a well-developed rescissory damages analysis under Securities Exchange Act Section 28(a). Dr. Yuen's report explicitly compared the claimant's actual portfolio performance against a properly diversified benchmark portfolio constructed from the claimant's stated investment objectives and risk tolerance as documented in the new account form. The respondent firm was given 30 days to depose Dr. Yuen and submit a rebuttal expert report, which it did, and both methodologies were fully briefed for the arbitration panel before the hearing.

QUESTION
--------
Did the claimant's use of a dual damages methodology with benchmark comparison comply with applicable standards for presenting investor harm in FINRA arbitration?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Section 28(a) of the Exchange Act permits recovery of actual damages, and courts have recognized both out-of-pocket and rescissory damages as valid measures in securities arbitration depending on the nature of the claim. PIABA's advocacy supports use of a benchmark portfolio comparison methodology grounded in the customer's documented investment profile as a rigorous and investor-protective approach to demonstrating consequential harm from unsuitable recommendations. The claimant's methodology was transparent, tied to documented investor objectives, disclosed to opposing counsel with adequate time for rebuttal, and fully subjected to adversarial challenge, satisfying both substantive and procedural fairness standards.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Securities Exchange Act §28(a); FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure Rule 12514; PIABA damages methodology guidelines; Rolf v. Blyth, Eastman Dillon & Co., 570 F.2d 38 (2d Cir. 1978)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Dual damages methodology was grounded in documented investor objectives, supported by expert testimony, and subjected to full adversarial process, satisfying Exchange Act and arbitration procedural standards.
