FACTS
-----
A 42-year-old engineer with $1.2 million in investable assets, an annual income of $185,000, no immediate liquidity needs, and a stated aggressive risk tolerance with a 20+ year time horizon approached her broker seeking recommendations to diversify out of her concentrated employer stock position ($650,000 in a single tech company). The broker recommended allocating $400,000 into a diversified portfolio of low-cost index funds (core), $200,000 into emerging-market equity ETFs, and $150,000 into a concentrated position in three high-growth technology mutual funds (sector play). The broker documented the client's age, income, time horizon, the concentrated position she sought to address, her aggressive risk tolerance, and her stated objective to maintain some sector exposure while reducing single-stock risk. No excessive trading occurred; the recommendation was made once and the account was rebalanced annually based on a written investment policy statement.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy FINRA Suitability Rule 2111?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation complies with FINRA Suitability Rule 2111.

This recommendation satisfies all three prongs of Rule 2111. First, the broker has a reasonable basis: the recommended allocations—core diversified index funds (33%), emerging markets (17%), and high-growth tech (12%)—are well-established strategies suitable for an aggressive investor with a 20+ year horizon and no liquidity constraints. Second, the recommendation is customer-specific: the broker explicitly documented the client's age (42), income ($185,000), time horizon (20+ years), risk tolerance (aggressive), and the specific problem she sought to solve (reducing concentrated position risk). The allocation preserves her sector exposure while diversifying, directly addressing her stated objective. Third, there is no quantitative suitability issue: the recommendation was made once with annual rebalancing only, not frequent trading for commissions. The use of low-cost index funds for the core allocation and a written investment policy statement further demonstrate care and reasonable basis.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a)(1) requires a reasonable basis for believing a recommendation is suitable, considering the customer's investment profile (age, investment experience, financial situation and needs, investment objectives, and other relevant circumstances). Rule 2111(a)(2) requires that the recommendation be suitable to the specific customer. Rule 2111(a)(3) requires that recommendations not result in excessive (churned) trading.

KEY FACTOR
----------
The broker's specific documentation of the customer's investment profile (age, time horizon, risk tolerance, income, and stated objective) combined with a one-time recommendation without excessive trading demonstrates compliance with all three prongs of Rule 2111.
