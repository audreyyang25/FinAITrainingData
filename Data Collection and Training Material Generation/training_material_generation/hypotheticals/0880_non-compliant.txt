ID: 880
Source Folder: 19_CFTC_Guidance
Topic: cta_cpo_suitability
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered CTA, Momentum Capital Advisors LLC, solicits a 68-year-old retired schoolteacher with $320,000 in total investable assets and a fixed monthly pension of $2,100. The CTA recommends allocating $210,000 (approximately 66% of her liquid net worth) to a managed futures trading program focused on short-term contrarian futures strategies in crude oil and natural gas contracts. CFTC research on retail traders in futures markets indicates that individual retail participants typically only appear for a handful of trades (median 4 trades lasting 4 days each) with gains or losses of a few hundred dollars per trading event, and that larger dollar losses on the first trade are significantly associated with permanently leaving the market. The CTA does not document any suitability analysis and the client has no prior derivatives experience.

QUESTION
--------
Does the CTA's recommendation of a 66% allocation to a short-term managed futures program for this retired client satisfy NFA suitability obligations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a suitability violation under NFA Compliance Rule 2-30.

NFA Compliance Rule 2-30 requires CTAs to obtain essential facts about each customer and to have a reasonable basis for believing a recommendation is suitable given the customer's financial situation and investment objectives. Allocating 66% of a retired client's liquid net worth to a high-risk, short-term futures trading program with no documented suitability analysis is facially unsuitable for a fixed-income-dependent retiree. CFTC research confirms the acute loss risk for retail entrants in futures markets, reinforcing the heightened duty of care owed to inexperienced retail clients in this asset class.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
NFA Compliance Rule 2-30 – Know Your Customer and Suitability Obligations for CTAs

KEY FACTOR
----------
The CTA failed to document any suitability analysis before recommending a leveraged futures program consuming the majority of a retiree's liquid savings.
