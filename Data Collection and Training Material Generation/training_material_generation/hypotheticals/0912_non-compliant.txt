Source Folder: 19_CFTC_Guidance
Topic: cta_cpo_suitability
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered CPO, Meridian Futures Management LLC, operates a commodity pool under the CFTC Rule 4.7 exemption, marketing the pool exclusively to purported Qualified Eligible Persons (QEPs). Meridian onboarded a 58-year-old retired schoolteacher, Dorothy Kellerman, with a self-reported investment portfolio of $1,850,000 and no prior derivatives experience. At the time of onboarding in late 2023, Meridian relied on the legacy 4.7 Portfolio Requirement thresholds and failed to reassess QEP eligibility against the CFTC's October 2023 proposed amendments, which would increase minimum financial thresholds to reflect inflation. Meridian provided no disclosure document to Kellerman, citing the 4.7 exemption as a blanket waiver of disclosure obligations, and did not obtain a signed acknowledgment of risk or representation of QEP status supported by documented financial verification. Kellerman invested $500,000 in the 4.7 pool, which subsequently lost 38% of its value trading crude oil futures.

QUESTION
--------
Did Meridian Futures Management LLC's onboarding of Dorothy Kellerman and operation of the 4.7 pool comply with applicable CPO disclosure and QEP eligibility obligations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of CFTC Rule 4.7 QEP eligibility verification requirements and NFA disclosure obligations.

CFTC Rule 4.7 exemptions are conditioned on pool participants being verified QEPs, and the CPO bears the obligation to document and confirm QEP status through financial verification, not mere self-reporting. While the 4.7 exemption reduces certain disclosure burdens, it does not eliminate all disclosure obligations, and NFA guidance and the CFTC's October 2023 proposed rulemaking signal that minimum disclosure requirements apply even to 4.7 funds to ensure participants understand material risks. Meridian's failure to independently verify Kellerman's portfolio composition and its blanket reliance on the 4.7 exemption to withhold all risk disclosure documents constitutes a failure of both eligibility gatekeeping and investor protection obligations. The absence of a documented QEP representation and risk acknowledgment exposes Meridian to regulatory liability under CFTC and NFA rules.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
CFTC Rule 4.7 (17 C.F.R. § 4.7) – Exemptions for Qualified Eligible Persons; NFA Compliance Rule 2-29; CFTC Proposed Rulemaking October 2, 2023 (Minimum Disclosure Proposal and QEP Thresholds Proposal)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Meridian failed to independently verify and document Kellerman's QEP eligibility and improperly treated the Rule 4.7 exemption as a complete waiver of all disclosure obligations, violating both QEP gatekeeping and minimum disclosure requirements.
