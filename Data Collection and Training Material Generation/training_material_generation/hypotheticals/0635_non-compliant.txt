ID: 635
Source Folder: 07_Practitioner_Commentary
Topic: training_supervision
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer firm with 85 registered representatives implements a Reg BI compliance program in June 2020 that includes written policies and a Form CRS delivered at account opening. However, the firm's training program consists solely of a single 45-minute recorded webinar provided to associated persons before the compliance date, with no follow-up, no competency assessment, and no scenario-based training on how to evaluate whether a recommendation satisfies the Care Obligation across reasonably available alternatives. Over the next 18 months, three associated persons consistently recommend a single high-cost proprietary variable annuity product — charging 2.85% in total annual expenses — to virtually all retail customers regardless of age, risk tolerance, time horizon, or financial situation. One representative recommends the product to a 28-year-old customer saving for a home down payment with a two-year time horizon and moderate risk tolerance, investing $75,000. The firm's supervisory system relies on exception reports that flag only transactions exceeding $500,000, missing these recommendations entirely. An internal audit eventually identifies the pattern but the firm takes no corrective action for six months.

QUESTION
--------
Did the firm's compliance program, training, and supervisory systems satisfy the Reg BI Compliance and Care Obligations given the pattern of unsuitable proprietary product recommendations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Reg BI's Care Obligation, Compliance Obligation, and FINRA supervisory requirements.

The FINRA Reg BI Checklist emphasizes that firms must have 'procedures and training in place to assess recommendations using a best interest standard' and that the broker-dealer 'may not place their interests ahead of the retail customer's.' A single recorded webinar with no competency testing or scenario-based training is insufficient to equip associated persons to evaluate reasonably available alternatives — a core element of the Care Obligation. The pattern of recommending a single high-cost proprietary product to all customers regardless of profile evidences a failure of the Care Obligation at the individual recommendation level, while the supervisory system's $500,000 exception threshold was unreasonably designed to catch this conduct. The six-month delay in corrective action after an internal audit finding further demonstrates that the firm's compliance program was not reasonably designed to achieve compliance with Reg BI.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iv) – Reg BI Compliance Obligation; FINRA Rule 3110 – Supervision

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's inadequate one-time training, excessively high supervisory exception thresholds, and failure to act on internally identified compliance deficiencies collectively rendered its Reg BI compliance program not reasonably designed to prevent associated persons from defaulting to a single high-cost proprietary product regardless of customer profile.
