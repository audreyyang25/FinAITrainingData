Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An adviser manages a $3.5M discretionary portfolio for a 55-year-old executive with significant concentrated stock holdings (40% of portfolio in her employer's stock, XYZ Corp, acquired through stock options over 20 years). The adviser recommends a diversification strategy involving a systematic sale and reallocation over 24 months. The adviser is a registered principal at a broker-dealer that receives commissions from securities transactions. The adviser discloses in the advisory agreement that she earns transaction-based compensation from trades executed and recommends that the client use a specific clearing firm that offers electronic trading to minimize commissions. The client consents in writing to the structure.

QUESTION
--------
Does this conduct comply with the fiduciary duty of loyalty?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct satisfies the fiduciary duty of loyalty under the Investment Adviser Act.

The adviser discloses the material conflict of interest (receipt of commissions on trades) in the advisory agreement and obtains written client consent. Additionally, the adviser mitigates the conflict by recommending electronic trading through a low-cost clearing firm to minimize commissions, demonstrating that she is not simply maximizing trading volume for compensation. The recommendation itself (diversification of concentrated single-stock risk) is in the client's best interest—it reduces the executive's exposure to company-specific risk and aligns with sound portfolio management for a 55-year-old nearing retirement. The systematic 24-month approach also demonstrates prudence by avoiding a single large sale that could trigger market impact or accelerate tax consequences. The combination of disclosure, consent, and affirmative steps to minimize the conflict while pursuing an objectively sound strategy satisfies the loyalty obligation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act §206(2); SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau, 375 U.S. 180 (1963) (conflicts permissible if disclosed and client consents); SEC Release IA-1092 (advisers may receive transaction-based compensation if disclosed and reasonable)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Full disclosure of transaction-based compensation combined with client written consent and the adviser's affirmative steps to minimize commissions (electronic clearing) cure the potential conflict of interest.
