Topic: state_vs_federal_standards
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Rachel Torres is a dual-registered representative affiliated with a broker-dealer and an investment advisory firm. She operates in Massachusetts, which applies a fiduciary standard to broker-dealer recommendations under 950 CMR 12.207. A longtime client, Frank Giordano (age 55, moderate risk tolerance, $600,000 portfolio), holds $150,000 in a diversified bond mutual fund. Torres recommends he sell the bond fund and purchase a proprietary structured note issued by her broker-dealer's affiliate, yielding 5.8% with a 7-year maturity and no secondary market. Torres completes a Reg BI disclosure (Form CRS) noting the conflict but does not perform a separate analysis under the Massachusetts fiduciary standard. Her firm's compliance department reviews only for federal Reg BI compliance and determines the transaction passes the care and conflict-of-interest obligations. Torres does not document why the structured note is superior to the existing bond fund holding or other non-proprietary alternatives.

QUESTION
--------
Does Torres's recommendation comply with the Massachusetts fiduciary standard applicable to broker-dealer conduct under 950 CMR 12.207?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates the Massachusetts fiduciary standard under 950 CMR 12.207.

Massachusetts imposes a fiduciary duty on broker-dealers and their agents that goes beyond the federal Reg BI standard. Under 950 CMR 12.207, the recommendation must be in the client's best interest, and the adviser must mitigate—not merely disclose—material conflicts of interest such as proprietary product sales. Torres's reliance solely on Reg BI's disclosure-based conflict approach is insufficient under Massachusetts law. Her failure to document why the illiquid, proprietary structured note is superior to the client's existing bond fund or comparable non-proprietary alternatives demonstrates a lack of the requisite fiduciary analysis that Massachusetts requires.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Massachusetts 950 CMR 12.207 – Fiduciary Duty of Broker-Dealers, Agents, Investment Advisers, and Investment Adviser Representatives

KEY FACTOR
----------
Relying solely on federal Reg BI disclosure of conflicts is insufficient in Massachusetts, where 950 CMR 12.207 imposes a fiduciary duty requiring mitigation of conflicts and documented analysis demonstrating the recommendation's superiority over reasonably available alternatives.
