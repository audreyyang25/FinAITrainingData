Topic: form_crs
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Dual-registered firm Pinnacle Advisory Group operates as both a broker-dealer and a registered investment adviser. The firm files and delivers a two-page Form CRS that describes both its brokerage and advisory relationships in a single document. The Form CRS uses the SEC's prescribed conversation-starter questions, explains the difference between brokerage commission-based and advisory fee-based compensation, and notes that clients can receive both types of services. The document discloses three prior regulatory actions against the firm from 2017 and 2019, directs clients to Investor.gov for a free search tool, and includes a hyperlink to the firm's more detailed Reg BI disclosure and ADV Part 2A brochure. A newly onboarded retail customer, Janet, age 55, with $500,000 in investable assets, receives the Form CRS electronically prior to her first recommendation and confirms receipt via the firm's digital onboarding platform. Janet subsequently opens a fee-based advisory account, and her representative documents why the advisory relationship — rather than a brokerage relationship — is in her best interest given her preference for ongoing portfolio management and financial planning.

QUESTION
--------
Does the firm's combined Form CRS covering both its broker-dealer and investment adviser capacities, delivered electronically with confirmed receipt, satisfy Form CRS requirements?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The SEC permits dual-registrants to file a single Form CRS covering both their broker-dealer and investment adviser capacities, provided it does not exceed two pages and includes all required items for each capacity. The firm's Form CRS includes the required conversation-starter questions, explains the differences in services and compensation, discloses disciplinary history, and provides the required cross-references to Investor.gov and more detailed disclosure documents. Electronic delivery with confirmed receipt satisfies delivery requirements. The additional step of documenting why the advisory account type is in the customer's best interest reflects adherence to the Care Obligation's account-type component. This stands in contrast to firms like Regulus Financial Group, which failed to disclose disciplinary history in their Form CRS.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Form CRS (17 CFR 249.2); SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Disclosure Obligation; SEC Form CRS Instructions

KEY FACTOR
----------
The dual-registrant's combined Form CRS met all content, length, delivery, and disciplinary disclosure requirements, and the firm additionally documented the account-type recommendation rationale.
