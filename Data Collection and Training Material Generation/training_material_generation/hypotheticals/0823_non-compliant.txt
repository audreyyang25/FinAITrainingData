Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A regional broker-dealer's compliance department receives three written customer complaints within six months, all alleging that the same producing branch manager recommended unsuitable high-yield municipal bond funds with front-end loads of 4.75% to low-income investors with less than $50,000 in liquid assets. Rather than conducting a heightened supervision review or escalating under its written supervisory procedures, the firm resolves each complaint through small settlements averaging $3,200 and codes them internally as 'service issues' rather than 'suitability complaints,' thereby avoiding FINRA Rule 4530 reporting obligations. Congressional hearing transcripts from 2024 House Financial Services Subcommittee sessions on investor protection reflect Congressional concern that broker-dealers systematically underreport complaints to obscure patterns of misconduct from FINRA surveillance. The branch manager's production represents 23% of the regional office's annual gross revenue, creating an undisclosed structural conflict that the firm's supervision protocols do not address.

QUESTION
--------
Does the broker-dealer's practice of settling pattern complaints as service issues, failing to report under FINRA Rule 4530, and maintaining a structurally conflicted supervision arrangement for a high-producing manager comply with applicable supervisory and reporting obligations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of FINRA Rules 3110, 4530, and Reg BI's Conflict of Interest Obligation.

FINRA Rule 3110 requires firms to establish and maintain supervisory systems reasonably designed to achieve compliance, and a producing branch manager supervising their own recommendations with no independent oversight layer fails this standard categorically. Miscoding suitability complaints as service issues to avoid FINRA Rule 4530 reporting constitutes an independent violation by depriving FINRA of information necessary to identify patterns of misconduct, a concern directly reflected in congressional oversight hearings. Reg BI's Conflict of Interest Obligation requires firms to identify and mitigate conflicts, including revenue-concentration conflicts that create incentives to protect high producers from scrutiny. The systematic nature of the three complaints constitutes a red flag that triggered mandatory escalation obligations the firm deliberately circumvented.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision); FINRA Rule 4530 (Reporting Requirements); SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iii) – Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation; Dodd-Frank Act §913 (Congressional mandate to address broker-dealer conflicts)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's deliberate miscoding of pattern suitability complaints combined with structurally conflicted supervision of a revenue-critical manager constitutes compounded supervisory and reporting failures.
