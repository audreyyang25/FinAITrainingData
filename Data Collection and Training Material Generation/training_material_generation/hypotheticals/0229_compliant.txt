Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
BD advisor recommends $180,000 interval fund (minimum investment $50K, 2.8% expense ratio, 7% redemption fee, semi-annual liquidity windows) to 62-year-old semi-sophisticated client with $890,000 portfolio (40% stocks, 25% bonds, 20% cash, 15% alternatives), stable $150K annual income, and stated 10+ year horizon for legacy planning. Client holds CPA license, owns investment property, has reviewed fund's 33-page prospectus. Advisor documents: (1) client's illiquidity tolerance confirmed via signed supplemental questionnaire; (2) interval fund's higher yield (4.2% vs. 2.1% for comparable bond fund) addresses client's goal of maximizing income within current allocation; (3) rationale that 7-year holding experience + $890K portfolio supports concentration risk; (4) no competing recommendations evaluated; (5) advisor receives 0.75% trailer on interval fund vs. 0% on alternative bond fund. Client executes separate acknowledgment that interval funds entail liquidity restrictions and above-average fees.

QUESTION
--------
Does the advisor's recommendation of an interval fund comply with Reg BI's care obligation despite higher costs and illiquidity, given the client's stated goals and profile?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Reg BI's care obligation does not require advisors to recommend the lowest-cost product categorically; it requires reasonable diligence into client characteristics and a reasonable basis that the recommendation serves the client's best interest. Here, the advisor documented: (1) client's affirmative illiquidity tolerance and sophisticated profile (CPA, property investor, prospectus review); (2) a legitimate, documented benefit—4.2% yield vs. 2.1%—tied to the client's income maximization goal; (3) confirmation of adequate liquid reserves ($180K cash); (4) appropriate concentration limits (20% of $890K portfolio). The advisor's compensation conflict (0.75% trailer) is disclosed and documented. Although the advisor did not compare to alternative bond funds, Reg BI does not mandate exhaustive comparison documentation if the recommendation is reasonable for the client's stated objectives. The signed acknowledgment of illiquidity and fees further supports that the client made an informed decision. This is distinguishable from steering to expensive products for unsuitable clients; here, product features align with documented client needs.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation – Reasonable Inquiry & Basis, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)(A)–(C)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented alignment between client's stated illiquidity tolerance, income goals, portfolio sophistication, and interval fund's yield premium, combined with client acknowledgment, supports reasonable basis for recommendation despite higher costs.
