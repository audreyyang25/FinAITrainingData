FACTS
-----
A 45-year-old corporate executive with $800,000 in investable assets, $250,000 annual salary, and stated aggressive risk tolerance requested exposure to international emerging markets. The broker recommended a 20% allocation to a low-cost emerging markets index ETF (0.08% ER) within a diversified portfolio. The recommendation was based on documented analysis showing the client's 20-year time horizon, high risk tolerance, strong income, and minimal near-term withdrawal needs. The broker discussed currency risk and emerging market volatility in writing.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy FINRA Rule 2111 suitability?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this is compliant.

The broker satisfied all three prongs of the suitability rule. The reasonable-basis prong is met: the broker documented that emerging markets are a standard diversification tool appropriate for aggressive long-term investors, and the 0.08% ER is competitively priced. The customer-specific prong is satisfied: the 45-year-old client has an aggressive risk tolerance, a 20-year time horizon, strong income, and no stated near-term liquidity needs, all supporting international equity exposure. The 20% allocation is quantitatively reasonable—not excessive for an aggressive portfolio. The broker's written discussion of currency and volatility risks demonstrates customer-specific suitability analysis. This recommendation would survive FINRA review under Rule 2111.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) requires a reasonable basis to believe the recommendation is suitable given the customer's investment profile (age, risk tolerance, time horizon, financial situation, and needs).

KEY FACTOR
----------
A 20-year time horizon, aggressive risk tolerance, high current income, and substantial assets make emerging market exposure suitable, and documentation of the analysis demonstrates reasonable basis.
