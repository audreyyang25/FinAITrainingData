Source Folder: 16_Congressional_Materials
Topic: gao_findings
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A dually registered investment adviser and broker-dealer employs an advisor who manages a $950,000 brokerage account for a 58-year-old software engineer nearing retirement. Over 18 months, the advisor executes 47 transactions generating $28,500 in commissions, with an annualized cost-to-equity ratio of 31%, while the account earns a net return of negative 4%. GAO reports on Reg BI implementation had flagged that dually registered firms often fail to clearly disclose to clients when advisors are acting in a broker capacity versus an advisory capacity. The advisor never provided the client a written explanation distinguishing the two roles and never disclosed that the commission-based compensation structure created an incentive to trade actively even when holding would have been more advantageous.

QUESTION
--------
Does the advisor's pattern of trading and failure to disclose his dual-registration status and compensation conflicts satisfy Reg BI's Disclosure and Care Obligations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Reg BI's Disclosure Obligation and Care Obligation, and constitutes excessive trading.

GAO findings on Reg BI implementation highlighted persistent gaps in how dually registered firms communicate role distinctions and conflicts of interest to retail clients. A 31% annualized cost-to-equity ratio is a recognized benchmark suggesting excessive trading under FINRA standards, and a net negative return over 18 months further evidences that the trading served the advisor's compensation interests rather than the client's best interest. The failure to disclose the commission-based conflict in writing at account inception violates Reg BI's Disclosure Obligation, which requires that material conflicts be disclosed in writing prior to or at the time of the recommendation. The SEC's mandate under the Exchange Act and Dodd-Frank to protect investors and maintain fair markets is directly undermined by this undisclosed conflict.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Disclosure Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability); Dodd-Frank Act §913

KEY FACTOR
----------
The advisor's failure to disclose his commission-based conflict of interest in a dually registered context, combined with a 31% cost-to-equity ratio indicating churning, violates both the Disclosure and Care Obligations under Reg BI.
