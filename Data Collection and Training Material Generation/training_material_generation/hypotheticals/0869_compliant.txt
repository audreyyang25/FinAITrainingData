Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Midwest Consolidated National Bank operates a national fiduciary program and has been appointed as sole corporate trustee of a $9.1 million irrevocable dynasty trust sited in South Dakota for the Kowalczyk family, administered from the bank's main trust office in Illinois, a state where the bank has full-service branches. The trust document expressly authorizes the trustee to delegate investment management to qualified third-party sub-advisors. The bank's trust department selects and hires an SEC-registered investment advisor, Lakeshore Capital Management LLC, to manage the equity sleeve ($5.5 million) of the trust portfolio, executing a formal sub-advisory agreement that specifies Lakeshore's fiduciary obligations, fee structure (0.45% annually), reporting requirements, and the bank's retained oversight responsibilities. The bank's trust officer conducts quarterly performance and compliance reviews of Lakeshore's management, documents findings in trust committee reports, and retains ultimate authority to terminate the delegation. The annual trust account statement provided to beneficiaries discloses the delegation arrangement, Lakeshore's identity, and the associated fees separately from the bank's trustee fee.

QUESTION
--------
Does the bank's delegation of investment management for a dynasty trust equity sleeve to a third-party SEC-registered sub-advisor, under a documented agreement with retained oversight and full beneficiary disclosure, satisfy OCC fiduciary standards for investment delegation?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

OCC asset management guidance and the Uniform Prudent Investor Act both permit a corporate trustee to delegate investment functions to qualified agents where the delegation is prudent, memorialized in a written agreement that establishes the agent's scope and duties, and subject to ongoing oversight by the trustee. The bank satisfied each element: it assessed Lakeshore's qualifications, executed a comprehensive sub-advisory agreement defining fiduciary obligations and termination rights, maintained quarterly oversight documented in trust committee records, and retained ultimate investment authority. Full beneficiary disclosure of the delegation arrangement and its fee structure further reflects the transparency required under OCC fiduciary standards, distinguishing permissible delegation from an impermissible abdication of trustee responsibility.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
OCC Comptroller's Handbook – Asset Management, Version 1.0 (June 2023); OCC Asset Management Operations and Controls (AM); 12 CFR 9.18; Uniform Prudent Investor Act – Delegation Provisions; ERISA §403(c)(3) (by analogy for best practices)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The bank's delegation was rendered compliant by the combination of a prudent selection process, a written sub-advisory agreement establishing the agent's fiduciary obligations, documented ongoing supervisory oversight, and transparent beneficiary disclosure—satisfying OCC and prudent investor standards for permissible investment delegation.
