Topic: reg_bi_effectiveness

Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A dually registered broker-dealer and investment adviser representative recommends that a 62-year-old Gen X client approaching retirement roll over her $410,000 employer 401(k) into a proprietary variable annuity product with a 7-year surrender period and 1.85% total annual expense ratio. The representative's firm receives 12b-1 fees and a 4.5% upfront commission on the annuity sale. The client expressed concern about retirement confidence—consistent with industry data showing only 53% of Gen X workers feel confident about retiring on their own terms—and asked for conservative, low-cost options. The representative's documentation states the recommendation was 'suitable' but does not memorialize any consideration of lower-cost alternatives such as a direct IRA rollover into index funds, nor does it document a comparison of fees between the existing 401(k) plan and the recommended annuity. The firm's Reg BI policies require a documented cost-benefit analysis for rollover recommendations but the representative did not complete one.

QUESTION
--------
Did the representative satisfy the Reg BI Care Obligation when recommending the proprietary variable annuity rollover to the pre-retiree client?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates the Reg BI Care Obligation and the SEC's specific guidance on rollover recommendations.

The Care Obligation under Reg BI requires that a broker-dealer exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill when making a recommendation, including understanding and considering reasonably available alternatives. The SEC has specifically highlighted rollover recommendations as requiring heightened analysis of costs, services, and investment options in the existing plan versus the recommended account. The representative failed to document any comparison of the 401(k) plan's costs against the annuity's 1.85% expense ratio and 4.5% commission, failed to consider lower-cost alternatives the client explicitly requested, and did not complete the firm's own required cost-benefit analysis. This is a textbook Care Obligation failure compounded by ignoring the client's stated preference for conservative, low-cost options.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Staff Bulletin on Account Type Recommendations (Rollovers)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative failed to perform and document the required cost-benefit comparison for a rollover recommendation, ignoring both reasonably available lower-cost alternatives and the client's expressed preference.
