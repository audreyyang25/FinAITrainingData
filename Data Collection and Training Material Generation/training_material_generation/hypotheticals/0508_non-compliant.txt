Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Horter & Bellweather Investment Management, LLC, a registered investment adviser, allocated approximately $310 million of client assets across 47 different sub-advisory strategies managed by third-party sub-advisers. Drew Kellman, the firm's CEO and managing member, negotiated revenue-sharing arrangements with 12 of these sub-advisers, under which the sub-advisers paid the firm between 25 and 50 basis points annually on client assets directed to their strategies. These payments totaled approximately $1.1 million annually. The firm's Form ADV Part 2A disclosed that it 'may receive compensation from third-party managers,' but did not identify which sub-advisers paid revenue-sharing fees, the basis-point ranges, or how these payments created incentives to favor certain sub-advisers over others. When allocating client assets, Kellman disproportionately directed assets to the 12 revenue-sharing sub-advisers even when comparable or better-performing non-paying sub-advisers were available on the firm's platform. The firm charged clients a 1.25% wrap fee in addition to the underlying sub-advisory fees, and clients were not informed that a portion of their sub-advisory fees was being rebated to the adviser.

QUESTION
--------
Did the firm and its principal violate their fiduciary duties under the Investment Advisers Act by failing to adequately disclose revenue-sharing arrangements with sub-advisers and by allocating client assets in a manner driven by the adviser's own financial incentive rather than clients' best interests?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes violations of Investment Advisers Act §206(1) and §206(2) for breaches of fiduciary duty through inadequate conflict disclosure and conflicted allocation practices.

Under §206(1) and §206(2), investment advisers owe an affirmative duty of utmost good faith and full disclosure of all material conflicts of interest. Revenue-sharing arrangements that incentivize the adviser to favor certain sub-advisers constitute material conflicts that must be disclosed with sufficient specificity for clients to evaluate the conflict's impact on the adviser's recommendations. The Horter Investment Management enforcement action established that vague disclosures stating the adviser 'may receive compensation' from third parties are insufficient when specific, quantifiable revenue-sharing arrangements exist and demonstrably influence allocation decisions. The combination of inadequate disclosure and self-interested allocation creates both a fraud and a breach of the adviser's duty of loyalty.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §206(1) and §206(2); SEC enforcement precedent in In re Horter Investment Management, LLC and Drew K. Horter (IA Release No. 6182, 2022)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser's allocation decisions were systematically driven by undisclosed revenue-sharing payments from sub-advisers rather than by a good-faith assessment of which strategies best served clients' investment objectives.
