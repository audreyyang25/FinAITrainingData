ID: 656
Source Folder: 06_DOL_Fiduciary
Topic: dol_rollover
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A financial advisor at a broker-dealer recommends that Maria Gonzalez, a 58-year-old school administrator, roll over her $340,000 403(b) plan into an IRA upon changing school districts. The advisor recommends a variable annuity with a 1.45% annual mortality and expense charge plus a 6-year surrender period, generating a $12,000 upfront commission for the advisor. The advisor does not document any comparison of the fees, investment options, or services available in Maria's existing 403(b) plan versus the recommended IRA, nor does the advisor consider that Maria's current plan offers low-cost index funds with an average expense ratio of 0.08%. The advisor's firm has a written policy requiring rollover documentation under PTE 2020-02 but the advisor did not follow it, and no supervisory review of the rollover recommendation was conducted before the transaction was executed.

QUESTION
--------
Did the advisor comply with PTE 2020-02's conditions when recommending the 403(b)-to-IRA rollover into a variable annuity?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates PTE 2020-02's Impartial Conduct Standards and documentation requirements for rollover recommendations.

PTE 2020-02 requires that investment advice fiduciaries provide advice in the retirement investor's best interest, which includes evaluating the alternatives reasonably available, such as remaining in the existing plan. The exemption specifically requires that when recommending a rollover, the fiduciary must document the reasons why the rollover is in the investor's best interest, including a comparison of fees, level of services, and available investment options between the existing plan and the IRA. The advisor's failure to compare the 403(b)'s low-cost index funds (0.08% expense ratio) against the variable annuity's 1.45% M&E charge, failure to document the rollover analysis, and the firm's failure to conduct supervisory review all represent violations of PTE 2020-02's conditions, meaning the exemption is unavailable and the transaction constitutes a prohibited transaction under ERISA §406 and IRC §4975.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
DOL PTE 2020-02 – Impartial Conduct Standards (Best Interest); Section II(a) Care Obligation and Section II(d) Documentation Requirement for Rollover Recommendations

KEY FACTOR
----------
The advisor failed to document any comparative analysis of the existing 403(b) plan versus the recommended IRA rollover, as specifically required by PTE 2020-02 for rollover recommendations.
