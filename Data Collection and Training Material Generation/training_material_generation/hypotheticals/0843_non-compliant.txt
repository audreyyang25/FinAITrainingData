ID: 843
Source Folder: 17_SEC_Staff_Bulletins
Topic: conflict_mitigation
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A large broker-dealer firm maintains a 'preferred product shelf' through which it directs registered representatives toward proprietary mutual funds that generate 40–60 basis points more in revenue to the firm than comparable third-party funds with similar risk-return profiles. The firm discloses in its Form CRS that 'we may have financial incentives to recommend certain products,' but provides no further specificity regarding which products, the magnitude of the revenue differential, or how the firm mitigates this conflict. The compliance department has implemented a 'principles-based' conflict management program consisting solely of annual training reminders and a general policy statement, without any supervisory overlay, product-level conflict scoring, or structured exception review process. In 2022, 78% of the firm's retail mutual fund recommendations were from the preferred shelf.

QUESTION
--------
Does the firm's conflict management framework satisfy Reg BI's Conflict of Interest Obligation with respect to its preferred product shelf incentive structure?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Reg BI's Conflict of Interest Obligation.

Reg BI's Conflict of Interest Obligation requires firms to establish, maintain, and enforce written policies and procedures reasonably designed to identify and at a minimum disclose, and in some cases mitigate or eliminate, material conflicts of interest. The SEC's 2022 Conflicts Staff Bulletin explicitly states that vague, high-level disclosures in Form CRS do not satisfy the disclosure requirement when the conflict is specific and quantifiable. A 40–60 basis point revenue differential favoring proprietary funds is a material, quantifiable conflict requiring specific disclosure and active mitigation measures beyond annual training. The 78% concentration on the preferred shelf, absent a structured supervisory review, indicates the conflict is systematically influencing recommendations without adequate mitigation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iii) – Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation; SEC Staff Bulletin: Standards of Conduct for Broker-Dealers and Investment Advisers – Conflicts of Interest (August 2022)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's generic Form CRS conflict language and principles-based training program are insufficient to satisfy Reg BI's requirement to mitigate specific, quantifiable financial incentives embedded in its preferred product shelf structure, as clarified in the SEC's 2022 Conflicts Staff Bulletin.
