Source Folder: 18_OCC_Bank_Trust_Guidance
Topic: bank_fiduciary
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
First Heartland National Bank serves as trustee for the Delacroix Family Irrevocable Trust, a $2.4 million estate planning trust with two adult beneficiaries. The bank's trust department recently established a Collective Investment Fund (CIF) investing primarily in Guaranteed Investment Contracts (GICs) issued by affiliated insurance subsidiaries. Without disclosing the affiliation or seeking court or beneficiary approval, the bank's trust officer transferred 60% of the Delacroix trust assets—approximately $1.44 million—into the CIF, generating management fee income for the bank on both the trust account and the CIF itself. The trust officer noted internally that GIC yields were competitive with market alternatives but did not document any independent comparative analysis or conflict review.

QUESTION
--------
Did the bank trust department's transfer of Delacroix Family Trust assets into its own affiliated CIF without disclosure or conflict review violate its fiduciary duty of loyalty?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a breach of the fiduciary duty of loyalty through undisclosed self-dealing.

OCC Interpretive Letter #734 expressly requires that national bank fiduciaries act solely in the best interest of those relying on them and must not become involved in conflicts of interest that might affect recommendations made for clients' benefit, avoiding even the appearance of impropriety. By investing trust assets in a CIF linked to affiliated insurance entities and generating layered fee income without independent analysis or beneficiary disclosure, the bank violated its duty of loyalty. The failure to document a comparative market analysis or obtain informed consent from beneficiaries compounded the conflict, as 12 C.F.R. Part 9.18 governs CIF administration and requires that participation serve the interest of the accounts, not the bank's revenue objectives.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
OCC Interpretive Letter #734, Part 2 – Duty of Loyalty; 12 C.F.R. Part 9; Scott on Trusts § 170 (Duty of Loyalty), § 16004 Cal. Prob. Code (Self-Dealing)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The bank generated undisclosed layered fees by investing trust assets in its own affiliated CIF without documenting independent analysis or obtaining beneficiary consent, constituting prohibited self-dealing under OCC fiduciary standards.
