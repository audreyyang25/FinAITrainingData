FACTS
-----
Ridgeline Wealth Advisors, a dually registered investment adviser and broker-dealer, recommends that James Chen, a 55-year-old corporate executive with $2.1 million in investable assets and a growth-oriented risk profile, roll over $850,000 from his employer's 401(k) plan into a fee-based advisory IRA account. Before making the recommendation, the adviser conducts a documented comparative analysis of the 401(k) plan's institutional share-class funds (averaging 0.04% expense ratios) against the proposed advisory IRA portfolio (0.85% total annual cost including the advisory fee). The analysis identifies that the IRA provides access to alternative investments, direct indexing for tax-loss harvesting projected to add 0.50-0.80% in annual after-tax alpha given Chen's high tax bracket, holistic financial planning integration, and beneficiary planning flexibility not available in the 401(k). The adviser delivers a written rollover recommendation summary to Chen and maintains the analysis in the client file. The firm's compliance department reviews and approves the recommendation prior to execution.

QUESTION
--------
Does the adviser's recommendation to roll over the 401(k) to a higher-cost advisory IRA comply with the SEC's investment adviser duty of care and Reg BI standards?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The SEC's 2019 Fiduciary Interpretation articulates that the duty of care requires an adviser to provide advice in the best interest of the client, including a reasonable investigation into the costs and benefits of a recommendation such as a rollover. Here, the adviser conducted and documented a thorough comparative analysis weighing the higher cost against specific, quantifiable benefits (tax-loss harvesting alpha, alternative asset access, holistic planning) tailored to Chen's individual circumstances. The written rollover recommendation summary and compliance pre-approval demonstrate the kind of reasonable inquiry and documentation the SEC expects. Even if the SEC Staff Bulletins on standards of conduct are withdrawn as contemplated in reform proposals, the underlying duty of care under Section 206 of the Advisers Act remains operative.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Fiduciary Interpretation (IA Release No. 5248) – Investment Adviser Duty of Care; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Staff Bulletin on Account Recommendations (rescission pending per Atkins agenda)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser documented a comprehensive comparative cost-benefit analysis specific to the client's circumstances before recommending the higher-cost rollover, satisfying the duty of care's requirement for reasonable investigation.
