FACTS
-----
An RIA recommends a Class A mutual fund (5.75% load, 0.92% ER) to a 62-year-old widow with $650,000 in assets and stated goal to preserve capital with 6% annual income target. The RIA also offers a lower-load Class R-4 (1% load, 0.55% ER) and a low-cost index ETF alternative (0.09% ER, no load). The advisor's written recommendation memo states: 'Class A recommended despite higher front-end cost because: (1) client seeks professional active management with proven 12-year track record of outperforming benchmark by 1.2% annually; (2) fund manager has 22-year tenure; (3) fund's income yield of 5.8% directly supports 6% income goal; (4) client will hold >20 years (age to 82+); (5) advisor analyzed total cost of ownership: Class A cumulative cost 15.9%, Class R-4 cost 12.7%, index ETF cost 1.35% over 20 years, but active management's 1.2% annual alpha offsets differential.' Client initials recommendation and acknowledges trade-off.

QUESTION
--------
Did the RIA comply with Reg BI's care obligation when recommending Class A despite lower-cost alternatives?

ANSWER
------
Compliant. The RIA documented a specific, fact-based rationale connecting product features to client circumstances: income objective (5.8% yield matches 6% goal), long holding horizon (20+ years justifying amortization of load), and manager track record showing persistent alpha (1.2% annual outperformance) sufficient to offset cost differential. The total-cost-of-ownership analysis explicitly compared alternatives and quantified the alpha thesis. The recommendation is not motivated by advisor compensation (RIA fee-only structure). While the client could have chosen the low-cost ETF, Reg BI permits recommendations of higher-cost products when the advisor has a reasonable basis grounded in client-specific facts and performance data. Documentation clearly shows care-obligation analysis. This contrasts sharply with generic or compensation-driven Class A recommendations.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)(A); reasonable basis standard permits higher-cost products if tied to specific client profile and documented analysis

KEY FACTOR
----------
RIA's detailed memo documenting specific fund features, client goals, performance alpha, and total-cost-of-ownership comparison across alternatives satisfies care obligation despite higher costs.
