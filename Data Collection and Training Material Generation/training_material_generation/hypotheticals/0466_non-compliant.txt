Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Advisor Stevens recommended a variable annuity (VA) with a 2.15% annual mortality and expense (M&E) risk charge, 1.35% annual subaccount expense ratio, 0.75% annual insurance charge, and 7% contingent deferred sales charge (CDSC) declining to 0% in year 8, for 72-year-old widow Dorothy Hanson's $425,000 inheritance from her late husband's 401(k) rollover. Hanson had $180,000 in other liquid assets, lived on $52,000/year Social Security + $24,000/year pension, and stated a conservative 'do not lose money' objective with 15+ year time horizon. Stevens documented: (1) the VA's guaranteed lifetime income rider (valued at 0.65% annually) as 'essential for her peace of mind'; (2) the total cost of 4.25% annually; (3) a brief suitability statement that 'VA provides safety and income security for retirees.' However, Stevens did not document: (a) comparison to non-annuity alternatives (low-cost index funds, immediate annuity, bond ladder); (b) analysis of the CDSC surrender charge impact if Hanson needed liquidity before age 80; (c) the suitability of the growth-oriented subaccounts (60% equities) for a stated 'do not lose money' client.

QUESTION
--------
Did Advisor Stevens comply with FINRA Rule 2330 (variable annuity suitability) and Reg BI's care obligation in recommending this VA for a 72-year-old widow with conservative objectives?

ANSWER
------
No. Non-compliant. Rule 2330(a) requires specific written analysis of: (1) the client's financial situation, investment experience, and goals; (2) the appropriateness of the VA relative to 'all significant characteristics' of the client; (3) comparison to non-annuity alternatives. Stevens failed on multiple counts: (1) the 4.25% annual cost is excessive for a conservative client with only $605K total assets and limited liquidity needs; (2) the recommended 60% equity subaccount allocation conflicts with the stated 'do not lose money' objective; (3) no documented analysis of surrender charge risk—at age 72, Hanson might need emergency liquidity, and the 7% CDSC would impose a material penalty; (4) no analysis of the guaranteed income rider's true value (0.65% cost for promised monthly payments) vs. a cheaper immediate annuity or bond ladder. The CDSC structure also creates incentive for Stevens to avoid contact, contrary to the client's best interest. Similar to FINRA AWC 2021-11 pattern, failure to document comparative analysis and misalignment between product fees and client needs is non-compliant.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330 (VA Suitability); Reg BI Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i); Rule 2111 best execution

KEY FACTOR
----------
No documented comparison to non-VA alternatives + product cost (4.25%) excessive for client's assets + surrender charge risk undisclosed + subaccount allocation misaligned with stated objectives = non-compliant.
