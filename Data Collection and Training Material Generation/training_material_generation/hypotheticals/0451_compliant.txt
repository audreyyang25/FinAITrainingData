Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
RIA advised 58-year-old client with $2.3M portfolio to maintain 65% equities despite client's stated risk tolerance of "moderate." Client had disclosed $4.2M contingent liability from personal guarantee on commercial real estate loan maturing in 7 years. Adviser documented in writing that conservative equity allocation would be inadequate to fund retirement + liability payoff; higher equity exposure necessary. Adviser conducted annual reviews showing liability tracking and rebalancing logic. Client signed acknowledgment form. After 3 years, liability was forgiven; client requested shift to 40% equities. Adviser complied immediately.

QUESTION
--------
Did the adviser's maintenance of a higher-equity portfolio than the client's stated risk tolerance suggest violated Reg BI care obligation?

ANSWER
------
No. Reg BI care obligation (17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)) requires reasonable basis for recommendation considering client's financial situation, not merely stated preference. Adviser documented material off-balance-sheet liability, reasoned quantitatively why conservative allocation was insufficient, and obtained written acknowledgment. Annual review and prompt adjustment post-liability discharge demonstrate ongoing compliance. This mirrors SEC guidance that adviser may recommend above client's comfort if supported by documented analysis of actual needs.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i); Reasonable Basis Standard

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented analysis of material liability outside stated risk tolerance, with written client acknowledgment, satisfies care obligation despite apparent mismatch to stated preference.
