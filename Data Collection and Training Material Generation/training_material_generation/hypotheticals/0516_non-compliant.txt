Source Folder: 02_SEC_Enforcement_Actions
Topic: sec_enforcement_ia
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Greenfield Capital Advisors, LLC, a registered investment adviser managing approximately $1.2 billion in assets through several private equity funds, routinely directed fund portfolio companies to engage a consulting firm co-owned by Greenfield's managing partner. Over a three-year period, the consulting firm collected $4.8 million in fees from portfolio companies held within the funds. Greenfield's Form ADV Part 2A and fund offering documents made no mention of the managing partner's ownership interest in the consulting firm, nor did they disclose the conflict of interest arising from steering fund-related business to an entity that financially benefited the adviser's principal. Investors in the funds, including a state pension system and several university endowments, were never informed of these arrangements.

QUESTION
--------
Did Greenfield Capital Advisors violate the Investment Advisers Act by failing to disclose the managing partner's financial interest in the consulting firm that received fees from fund portfolio companies?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes a violation of Investment Advisers Act §206(2) for breach of fiduciary duty through failure to disclose material conflicts of interest.

Under Sections 206(1) and 206(2) of the Advisers Act, an investment adviser owes a fiduciary duty to its clients, including fund investors, and must provide full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts of interest. The managing partner's ownership interest in a consulting firm receiving millions in fees from fund portfolio companies is a material financial conflict that directly implicates the adviser's loyalty to its clients. This fact pattern closely mirrors the SEC's action against First Reserve Management (IA-4529), where the Commission found that a private equity adviser's failure to adequately disclose financial conflicts of interest related to affiliated entities violated the antifraud provisions. The absence of any disclosure in Form ADV or offering documents renders the omission a clear breach.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Sections 206(1) and 206(2) – Antifraud provisions prohibiting fraud and deceit, including undisclosed conflicts of interest by investment advisers

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser's complete failure to disclose a material financial conflict—the managing partner's ownership in a consulting firm receiving fees from fund portfolio companies—constitutes a straightforward breach of fiduciary duty under §206.
