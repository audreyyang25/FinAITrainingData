Source Folder: 16_Congressional_Materials
Topic: congressional_oversight
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A large broker-dealer's home office compliance team, responding to Congressional oversight pressure following published GAO reports critical of Reg BI supervision gaps, implements a new automated surveillance system that flags recommendations of high-cost proprietary mutual funds to clients with conservative risk profiles. The system flags 340 such recommendations in Q1 2024, but the branch manager systematically overrides the flags for 290 of them without written documentation of the basis for the override, telling registered representatives via group text message that 'the system is overly sensitive and you know your clients best.' The proprietary funds carry expense ratios averaging 1.85% versus 0.45% for comparable index funds, and the firm earns 12(b)-1 fees of 0.75% annually on the proprietary products. None of the affected clients received written disclosure of the fee differential or the 12(b)-1 revenue arrangement.

QUESTION
--------
Does the branch manager's systematic undocumented override of compliance flags for high-cost proprietary fund recommendations constitute a supervisory violation under applicable broker-dealer standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of FINRA Rule 3110 supervisory obligations and Reg BI's Conflict of Interest and Disclosure Obligations.

Congressional oversight of Reg BI implementation, reflected in GAO reports, specifically identified inadequate supervisory systems and undisclosed conflicts arising from proprietary product distribution as key implementation failures at broker-dealers. FINRA Rule 3110 requires that supervisory overrides be documented with a written rationale, and a branch manager's informal group-text instruction to disregard 290 compliance flags provides no audit trail and constitutes a systemic supervisory failure. The 1.40% expense ratio differential between proprietary and comparable index funds, combined with 0.75% 12(b)-1 fees flowing to the firm, constitutes a material conflict of interest that must be disclosed in writing under Reg BI's Conflict of Interest and Disclosure Obligations. The pattern of non-disclosure directly contradicts the investor-protection goals articulated in the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and reinforced through Dodd-Frank.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision); SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iii) – Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Disclosure Obligation; Dodd-Frank Act §913

KEY FACTOR
----------
The branch manager's undocumented override of 290 compliance flags for high-cost proprietary fund recommendations, with no written disclosure of the fee differential or 12(b)-1 conflict, constitutes simultaneous supervisory, conflict-of-interest, and disclosure violations under Reg BI and FINRA Rule 3110.
