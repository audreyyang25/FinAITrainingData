ID: 619
Source Folder: 06_DOL_Fiduciary
Topic: ira_advice
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
David Carpenter, a registered representative at a large independent broker-dealer, provides a one-time recommendation to Patricia Owens, age 71, that she transfer $275,000 from her existing IRA at Vanguard — invested in a diversified portfolio of index funds with a 0.07% weighted average expense ratio — into a variable annuity with a guaranteed minimum income benefit rider. The variable annuity has total annual fees of 3.15% (including mortality and expense charges, sub-account fees, and the rider fee) and a 7-year declining surrender charge schedule starting at 8%. David's firm acknowledges he is a fiduciary under the DOL's expanded definition because the recommendation is made in a professional capacity, for a fee, and Patricia relies on it as a basis for her investment decision. David provides Patricia a written disclosure of his compensation and conflicts, and his firm has current written policies and has conducted its most recent retrospective review. However, David's documentation states only that Patricia 'expressed interest in guaranteed income,' without any analysis of whether the annuity's cost is justified relative to lower-cost alternatives such as a systematic withdrawal strategy from her existing index fund portfolio, or whether the surrender charges are appropriate given Patricia's age and potential need for liquidity.

QUESTION
--------
Does the advisor's rollover recommendation from a low-cost IRA to a high-cost variable annuity satisfy the Impartial Conduct Standards under PTE 2020-02, even though the firm has otherwise satisfied the exemption's procedural conditions?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates PTE 2020-02's Impartial Conduct Standards, specifically the best interest obligation requiring consideration of reasonably available alternatives and cost.

While David's firm has satisfied the procedural conditions of PTE 2020-02 (written policies, retrospective review, disclosure), the exemption also requires that each individual recommendation satisfy the Impartial Conduct Standards. The best interest standard demands that the fiduciary exercise the care, skill, and prudence of a knowledgeable professional and consider the costs and reasonably available alternatives. Documenting only that the client 'expressed interest in guaranteed income' without analyzing whether the 3.15% all-in annuity cost is justified relative to lower-cost income strategies, or whether the 8% surrender charge is appropriate for a 71-year-old with potential liquidity needs, fails this standard. Satisfying the procedural conditions of PTE 2020-02 does not excuse a substantively deficient recommendation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
DOL PTE 2020-02 §II(a)(1)-(3) – Impartial Conduct Standards (Best Interest, Reasonable Compensation, No Misleading Statements); ERISA §404(a)(1)(B)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Despite satisfying PTE 2020-02's procedural requirements, the advisor failed the substantive best interest standard by not analyzing whether the dramatically higher-cost annuity was justified relative to readily available lower-cost alternatives for a 71-year-old client.
