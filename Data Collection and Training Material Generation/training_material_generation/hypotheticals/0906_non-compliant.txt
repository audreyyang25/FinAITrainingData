Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Nicholas Farrow, a registered representative at a mid-size broker-dealer, was convicted in federal district court of wire fraud in connection with diverting $340,000 from three elderly retail clients by forging wire transfer instructions over a 22-month period. The broker-dealer received notice of the federal criminal conviction but did not file a Form U5 disclosing the conviction until 11 months after sentencing, and Farrow continued to service client accounts in an advisory capacity during that interval. The firm argued that because the conviction was under appeal, no disclosure obligation had yet been triggered. The firm had no written policy addressing the timing of Form U5 amendments following criminal convictions under appeal.

QUESTION
--------
Did the broker-dealer's 11-month delay in filing a Form U5 amendment following Farrow's federal wire fraud conviction, justified by the pendency of his appeal, comply with applicable disclosure and supervisory standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Exchange Act Section 15(b) disclosure obligations and FINRA Rule 2010 for failing to timely amend Form U5 following a statutory disqualifying criminal conviction.

As demonstrated in SEC Initial Decision Release No. 729 (Skaltsounis), a federal criminal conviction for securities-related fraud triggers immediate statutory disqualification and mandatory disclosure obligations under Exchange Act §15(b), regardless of whether an appeal is pending. The pendency of an appeal does not toll the firm's obligation to amend Form U5 within 30 days of a disqualifying event. Allowing Farrow to continue servicing client accounts during this period exposed clients to ongoing harm from a statutorily disqualified person. The absence of a written supervisory procedure addressing post-conviction disclosure timing is itself an independent supervisory violation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Exchange Act §15(b); FINRA Rule 2010; SEC Initial Decision Release No. 729 (Nicholas D. Skaltsounis) – associational bar following criminal conviction

KEY FACTOR
----------
A pending appeal of a federal criminal conviction does not suspend the broker-dealer's obligation to timely amend Form U5 or to restrict the convicted representative's client-facing activities.
