Source Folder: unknown
Topic: reg_bi_care_expanded
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 52-year-old retail investor with $185,000 in a taxable brokerage account and moderate risk tolerance met with Advisor M at a wirehouse. The investor's stated goal was long-term growth over 13 years before retirement. Advisor M recommended a Class A share mutual fund (Fidelity Growth Fund) with a 5.75% front-end load, 78 basis points annual expense ratio (ER), and 0.82 turnover ratio. The same fund was available in Admiral Shares (minimum $10,000) with 0% load and 0.45% ER. Advisor M's compensation was $3,688 on the Class A sale (vs. $540 on Admiral Shares). The advisor's file contained a brief note: "Client prefers professional management; Class A appropriate for long-term investor." The advisor did not document any analysis of front-load impact on long-term returns, available share classes, or breakpoint discounts at $100,000+.

QUESTION
--------
Did Advisor M comply with the Reg BI care obligation under 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)–(iii) (reasonable basis, disclosure, and suitability)?

ANSWER
------
No. While Class A shares may be suitable for long-term investors, Advisor M failed the care obligation's core requirement: reasonable investigation of the recommendation and the material facts. The advisor documented no comparative analysis of share classes, cost differences ($5,310 front load vs. $0), or break-even analysis (typically 5–7 years for Admiral Shares to overcome load). The compensation conflict ($3,148 incentive spread) was undisclosed. Under SEC Exam Priorities and FINRA AWC 2020-007 (RBC Capital Markets), recommending expensive share classes without documented cost-benefit analysis violates the care obligation even if the product passes suitability. The note is conclusory, not reasoned.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)–(iii); SEC Exam Priorities 2020, Share Class Analysis

KEY FACTOR
----------
Failure to document comparative analysis of materially cheaper share classes available under the same fund family despite 13-year holding period.
