Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Paradigm Ridge Advisory, LLC, a registered investment adviser with approximately $340 million in AUM serving primarily retail clients, maintained an undisclosed arrangement with two mutual fund families under which it received revenue-sharing payments of 12 to 18 basis points on client assets invested in those fund families' products. Paradigm Ridge's investment advisory representatives consistently recommended these two fund families over comparable lower-cost alternatives, including index funds with expense ratios 40-65 basis points lower. The firm's Form ADV Part 2A disclosed that it 'may receive compensation from third parties' but did not identify the specific revenue-sharing arrangements, the fund families involved, or the magnitude of the payments, which totaled approximately $475,000 annually. The firm had no policies or procedures to evaluate whether the recommended funds were in clients' best interests given the conflict.

QUESTION
--------
Did Paradigm Ridge Advisory's undisclosed revenue-sharing arrangements and failure to adopt conflict-mitigation policies violate its fiduciary obligations under the Advisers Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes violations of Sections 206(2) and 206(4) of the Investment Advisers Act and Rule 206(4)-7 for inadequate disclosure of conflicts and failure to adopt and implement compliance policies reasonably designed to prevent violations.

The SEC's proceeding against Paradigm Wealth Advisory, LLC (IA Release No. 5852) established that maintaining undisclosed investment conflicts constitutes a violation of the adviser's fiduciary duty under Section 206. Here, Paradigm Ridge's boilerplate Form ADV language about potentially receiving third-party compensation was materially inadequate because it failed to identify the specific revenue-sharing arrangements, the conflicted fund families, or the approximate dollar amounts involved. Additionally, the firm's complete absence of policies to evaluate whether conflicted recommendations served clients' best interests violates Rule 206(4)-7's requirement that advisers adopt and implement written compliance policies reasonably designed to prevent Advisers Act violations.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Sections 206(2), 206(4), and Rule 206(4)-7 – Compliance Programs; fiduciary duty of full and fair disclosure

KEY FACTOR
----------
Generic, boilerplate disclosure of potential third-party compensation is materially inadequate when the adviser has specific, quantifiable revenue-sharing arrangements that incentivize recommendations of higher-cost products over suitable lower-cost alternatives.
