Source Folder: unknown
Topic: concentration
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer advisor at a regional firm recommends that a 45-year-old client with $850,000 in investable assets, moderate risk tolerance, and a 20-year horizon allocate $255,000 (30%) to a non-traded REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust) sold by the firm's affiliate, with a 7% sales load, 2% annual management fee, and a 10-year liquidity lockup period. The client has 60% of liquid net worth in the REIT allocation and requested 'some real estate exposure.' The firm received 1.2% trailing revenue from the REIT sponsor. The suitability statement says 'client likes real estate' but does not address: (1) the concentration of 30% in a single illiquid vehicle; (2) the liquidity mismatch given the client's moderate risk tolerance; (3) alternative real estate products (REITs, mutual funds, 60/40 allocations); or (4) the firm's compensation conflict.

QUESTION
--------
Does this non-traded REIT recommendation satisfy FINRA Suitability Rule 2111 and Regulation Best Interest?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates both standards.

This recommendation fails on multiple grounds under both FINRA 2111 and Reg BI. First, FINRA Rule 2111(a)(1)(B) explicitly requires suitability analysis to include the customer's need for liquidity; here, a moderate-risk-tolerance investor is subjected to a 10-year liquidity lockup for 30% of liquid net worth—a severe liquidity mismatch that is never documented or addressed. Second, under Reg BI's obligation to recommend a strategy in the customer's best interest, the advisor failed to consider or present reasonable alternatives (liquid REITs, real estate mutual funds, or a smaller allocation to non-traded REITs). Third, the firm's 1.2% trailing revenue from the REIT sponsor creates a material conflict of interest that must be disclosed and addressed under Reg BI; the suitability statement fails to mention this conflict or explain why the non-traded vehicle (with higher fees and locks) is better than alternatives. Fourth, the concentration of 30% in a single illiquid security without documented analysis of concentration risk violates FINRA Rule 2111(a). This pattern mirrors real FINRA disciplinary actions (e.g., firms sanctioned for recommending non-traded REITs without adequate liquidity analysis and without disclosure of conflicts). The fact that the client said 'likes real estate' is not sufficient suitability documentation—FINRA requires affirmative, documented analysis.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability rule), particularly 2111(a)(1)(B) (suitability analysis must include customer's need for liquidity); SEC Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI), 17 CFR 240.15l-1(a)(1) (recommendation must be in customer's best interest), with particular application to conflicts of interest and consideration of reasonable alternatives; FINRA Rule 2330 (non-traded REIT communications and suitability requirements).

KEY FACTOR
----------
The 10-year liquidity lockup on 30% of assets combined with undisclosed conflicts of interest (1.2% trailing revenue), failure to present alternative real estate products, and inadequate suitability documentation create clear violations of both FINRA 2111 (liquidity requirement) and Reg BI (best-interest standard and conflict disclosure).
