FACTS
-----
A 63-year-old participant with $780,000 in a 401(k) plan that offers only proprietary mutual funds (1.25% average ER) and limited investment options requests a rollover to an IRA. The advisor, acting as a fiduciary under PTE 2020-02, documents a written analysis showing that the proposed IRA offers (a) a broader range of low-cost index funds (0.03–0.08% ER), (b) access to institutional-share classes unavailable in the 401(k), and (c) reduced restrictions on rebalancing. The advisor executes a PTE 2020-02 Best Interest Contract Exemption that discloses all material conflicts of interest, identifies the rollover as in the participant's best interest, charges a flat $1,500 advisory fee (not contingent on the rollover), and provides a 30-day rescission right. The participant retains stable employment but is five years from retirement.

QUESTION
--------
Does this rollover recommendation comply with DOL ERISA fiduciary duty and PTE 2020-02 requirements?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation satisfies DOL ERISA fiduciary duty and fully complies with PTE 2020-02 Best Interest Contract Exemption standards.

The advisor satisfied ERISA § 404(a)(1)(A) by conducting a documented, objective analysis comparing the 401(k) plan to the proposed IRA. The material facts support a rollover: the 401(k) offers only proprietary funds at 1.25% ER with limited options, while the IRA provides diversified, lower-cost alternatives (0.03–0.08% ER) and greater operational flexibility. The cost savings alone—approximately 1.17% annually on $780,000—represent material economic value. Critically, the advisor complied with PTE 2020-02 by executing a written Best Interest Contract Exemption with full disclosure of conflicts, a flat (non-rollover-contingent) fee, and a 30-day rescission right. The 5-year horizon to retirement does not undermine the recommendation because the advisor's documented analysis establishes that the IRA's superior investment menu and lower costs benefit the participant's long-term retirement security. The advisor's conduct reflects the prudence standard: a fiduciary must consider the participant's circumstances and the relative merits of available options.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
ERISA § 404(a)(1)(A) (fiduciary prudence and loyalty); 29 CFR § 2550.404a-3(b)(1); PTE 2020-02 § I (rollover recommendations must be in participant's best interest, documented, and comply with BICE disclosure and fee standards).

KEY FACTOR
----------
Comprehensive written analysis documenting the rollover as in the participant's best interest, coupled with full PTE 2020-02 BICE compliance (disclosure, flat fee, and rescission rights) and absence of self-dealing incentives.
