Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Greenfield Capital Partners, LP, a registered investment adviser managing three private equity funds with combined AUM of $2.1 billion, routinely directed its fund portfolio companies to engage an affiliated consulting firm for operational improvement services. The consulting firm, wholly owned by Greenfield's managing partners, collected approximately $14.7 million in consulting fees over four years from the portfolio companies. Greenfield's Form ADV Part 2A disclosed that the adviser 'may have conflicts of interest related to affiliated entities' but did not specifically identify the consulting arrangement, the affiliated firm, or the magnitude of fees flowing to related persons. Fund limited partnership agreements were similarly silent on the arrangement. Greenfield never sought or obtained informed consent from the fund advisory committees or limited partners regarding these related-party transactions.

QUESTION
--------
Did Greenfield Capital Partners violate its fiduciary obligations under the Investment Advisers Act by failing to adequately disclose the affiliated consulting arrangement and associated fee income to its fund clients and investors?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes a violation of Investment Advisers Act §206(2) for inadequate disclosure of material conflicts of interest.

Section 206(2) of the Advisers Act prohibits investment advisers from engaging in transactions, practices, or courses of business that operate as a fraud or deceit upon clients. An investment adviser owes a fiduciary duty to its fund clients to provide full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts of interest, including affiliated-party transactions that generate fees for related persons. As demonstrated in the First Reserve enforcement action, vague or generic conflict-of-interest disclosures are insufficient when specific arrangements involving affiliated entities and quantifiable fee streams exist. Greenfield's failure to identify the specific consulting arrangement, the affiliated entity, and the scope of fee payments deprived investors of the information needed to evaluate and consent to the conflict.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §206(2) – Prohibition on fraud and deceit; SEC enforcement precedent in In re First Reserve Management, LP (IA Release No. 4529, 2016)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Generic boilerplate conflict disclosures failed to identify the specific affiliated consulting arrangement and the $14.7 million in fees flowing to the adviser's related persons, rendering the disclosures materially inadequate.
