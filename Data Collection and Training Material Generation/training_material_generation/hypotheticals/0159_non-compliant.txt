Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 55-year-old client, an engineer with $620,000 in a retirement account, consults a broker-advisor about preserving capital approaching retirement. The advisor documents the client's stated objective as 'moderate income with low volatility.' The broker executes a series of 34 trades over 14 months in covered call strategies and short-term option positions, generating $11,200 in commissions. The annualized turnover ratio is 1.8x. The client reports that the advisor did not explain the option strategies in detail before implementation and verbally stated 'I'll make money for you by trading actively.' The account suffers a 12% loss while the S&P 500 gained 8% over the same period.

QUESTION
--------
Does this conduct violate suitability and anti-churning rules?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates FINRA Rule 2111(a) (suitability) and Rule 2010 (prohibition on excessive trading/churning).

Multiple violations appear here. First, the 1.8x annualized turnover ratio combined with $11,200 in commissions on a $620,000 account (1.8% annually) is excessive for a client with a stated 'moderate income, low volatility' objective—short-term option strategies are inherently high-volatility and unsuitable for this profile. Second, the advisor's oral statement ('I'll make money for you by trading actively') demonstrates scienter (knowledge/intent) that commissions—not client benefit—motivate the trading, which is the hallmark of churning under Tambone. Third, the client did not receive detailed disclosure or approval documentation for option strategies, violating Rule 4512 and Rule 2111(a) suitability analysis. Fourth, the cost-equity ratio is negative: the account lost 12% while incurring $11,200 in commissions, compared to an S&P 500 gain of 8%, suggesting the trading harmed rather than benefited the client. Finally, the 34 trades in 14 months (average 11-day holding period) in options positions exhibits classic in-and-out trading pattern inconsistent with 'income and low volatility.' This matches FINRA enforcement pattern: excessive commissions + unsuitable strategies + documented advisor scienter + negative performance = churning violation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) (suitability requirement); FINRA Rule 2010 (general conduct standard); FINRA Rule 4512 (options approval requirement); SEC v. Tambone, 550 F.3d 106 (1st Cir. 2008) (churning defined by excessive trading, control, and scienter of advisor generating commissions); FINRA guidance on cost-equity ratio and in-and-out trading.

KEY FACTOR
----------
The combination of (1) 1.8x turnover ratio and $11,200 in commissions on a low-volatility-seeking client, (2) advisor's oral statement revealing commission motive, (3) unsuitable short-term option strategies, and (4) negative cost-equity ratio (account lost 12% vs. S&P +8%) establishes churning under Rule 2010 and Rule 2111(a).
