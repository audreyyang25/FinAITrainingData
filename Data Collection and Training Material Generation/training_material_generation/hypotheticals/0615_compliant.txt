ID: 615
Source Folder: 05_SEC_Exam_Findings
Topic: wrap_fee_programs
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An SEC-registered investment adviser manages a wrap fee program charging 1.50% annually on assets under management, which covers advisory fees, trading costs, and custodial services. A 58-year-old client with a $1.2 million moderate-growth portfolio executes an average of 62 equity trades per year as part of a tactical allocation strategy. During the Division of Examinations' review — reflecting 2022 and 2025 exam priorities targeting wrap fee program suitability — examiners note that the client's trading frequency could suggest the wrap fee is cost-effective compared to a per-transaction commission model. The adviser maintains a quarterly suitability review process that documents the client's trading activity, compares annualized wrap costs ($18,000) against estimated per-trade commission costs (estimated at $24.80 per trade × 62 trades = $1,537.60 in commissions alone, plus a standalone advisory fee of approximately 1.00%, totaling roughly $13,538), showing the wrap program costs approximately $4,462 more per year. However, the adviser's documentation explains that the wrap fee also bundles research access, unlimited financial planning consultations, and tax-loss harvesting services the client actively uses, and the client signed an updated disclosure acknowledging the cost differential and affirming the value of bundled services after an annual best-interest review meeting.

QUESTION
--------
Is the adviser's continued placement of this client in a wrap fee program compliant despite the wrap fee exceeding estimated à la carte costs?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

SEC exam priorities have consistently flagged wrap fee programs where clients trade infrequently and would pay less under a transaction-based model, making the wrap fee effectively a reverse churning concern. Here, however, the adviser conducted and documented a thorough cost-benefit analysis, disclosed the cost differential transparently to the client, and demonstrated that bundled services — including tax-loss harvesting, financial planning, and research access actively utilized by the client — provide value beyond mere trade execution. The client's informed, documented consent after a clear comparison satisfies the adviser's fiduciary obligation to act in the client's best interest and to make full and fair disclosure of material conflicts.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 fiduciary duty; SEC Division of Examinations wrap fee program risk alerts and examination priorities (2016–2025)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser's detailed documentation of cost comparisons, disclosure of the wrap fee cost differential, and evidence that the client actively uses bundled services beyond trade execution demonstrate that the wrap fee recommendation was made in the client's best interest despite higher headline costs.
