Source Folder: 02_SEC_Enforcement_Actions
Topic: sec_enforcement_ia
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Crestview Partners Advisory, LLC, a registered investment adviser managing two private equity funds with combined AUM of $600 million, engages a portfolio company consulting firm in which one of Crestview's senior partners holds a 10% passive ownership interest. Before engaging the firm, Crestview's CCO conducted a thorough review comparing the consulting firm's qualifications and fees against three independent competing firms, documenting that the affiliated firm's rates were at or below market and its industry expertise was superior for the specific engagement. Crestview's Form ADV Part 2A contains a detailed disclosure identifying the senior partner's ownership interest in the consulting firm, the nature and scope of services the firm provides to portfolio companies, and the approximate range of annual fees. The fund's Limited Partnership Agreement explicitly authorizes the adviser to engage affiliated service providers provided the conflicts are disclosed and fees are at arm's-length terms. Crestview's advisory committee, composed of independent limited partners, reviewed and approved the engagement.

QUESTION
--------
Is Crestview Partners Advisory's use of an affiliated consulting firm for portfolio company services compliant with its fiduciary obligations under the Advisers Act, given the senior partner's ownership interest?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

While the senior partner's ownership interest in the consulting firm creates a conflict of interest similar to the facts in First Reserve Management (IA Release No. 4529), Crestview has taken the steps the SEC expects to satisfy its fiduciary obligations. The conflict is specifically and prominently disclosed in the Form ADV, the LPA authorizes affiliated transactions with disclosure and arm's-length terms, independent price benchmarking was conducted and documented, and the independent advisory committee provided informed consent. The SEC has consistently indicated that conflicts of interest do not per se violate the Advisers Act—rather, it is the failure to disclose and manage them that constitutes a breach. Here, the adviser's comprehensive disclosure and procedural safeguards satisfy the duty of loyalty and care.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Sections 206(1) and 206(2) – fiduciary duty of disclosure and duty of care; SEC guidance on conflicts of interest in private fund management

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser transformed a potentially violative affiliated-party arrangement into a compliant one through specific Form ADV disclosure, documented arm's-length fee benchmarking, LPA authorization, and independent advisory committee approval.
