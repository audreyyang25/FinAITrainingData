Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Registered representative Marcus T. recommended to 58-year-old client Patricia M. (age 58, $180,000 portfolio, moderate risk tolerance) a switch from Vanguard Wellington A-share mutual fund (held 18 months, 5.75% front-end load already paid) to American Funds Growth Fund of America A-share (new 5.75% front-end load, $10,350 immediate charge). Client received no written switching analysis. Wellington had returned 7.2% annualized; Growth Fund offered similar objectives and expense ratios (0.58% vs. 0.60%). The switch was executed within 30 days. No documentation showed suitability analysis or discussion of cumulative loads or holding period adequacy.

QUESTION
--------
Did the representative comply with FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability) and Rule 2010 (fair dealing) in recommending the A-to-A fund switch?

ANSWER
------
No. The switch violated both rules. Rule 2111 requires representatives to have reasonable basis to believe recommendations are suitable, considering client's age, financial situation, needs, and investment objectives. Here, the new fund had identical objectives and nearly identical expense ratios; switching incurred a second 5.75% load ($10,350) within 18 months, materially disadvantaging the client. Rule 2010 prohibits high-pressure tactics and charging unreasonable commissions. The absence of written switching analysis documenting comparative costs and benefits is classic FINRA enforcement pattern (similar to multiple 2019-2022 AWCs). The representative earned $516.75 commission on the switch alone, creating incentive misalignment. This pattern has resulted in $100K–$500K fines plus restitution in comparable cases.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) (suitability); FINRA Rule 2010(a)–(b) (fair dealing, commission reasonableness)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommending A-share fund switch incurring duplicate front-end load without documented suitability analysis or switching justification, violating suitability and fair-dealing obligations.
