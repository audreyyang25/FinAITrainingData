ID: 845
Source Folder: 17_SEC_Staff_Bulletins
Topic: conflict_mitigation
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A dual-registrant firm operates both a commission-based broker-dealer and an SEC-registered investment adviser subsidiary. A representative recommends that a 58-year-old married couple with $675,000 to invest open a fee-based advisory account at 1.0% annually rather than a commission-based brokerage account, noting that the couple plans to make approximately three to four trades per year. Form CRS is delivered at account opening, disclosing that the advisory account generates ongoing fees to the firm regardless of trading activity and that this creates an incentive to recommend the advisory account over a brokerage account. The representative's written account-type analysis documents that, at the anticipated trading frequency, the commission-based alternative would generate approximately $1,800 annually versus $6,750 under the advisory structure, and concludes that the advisory account's comprehensive financial planning services, tax-loss harvesting, and automatic rebalancing justify the higher cost for this client's retirement accumulation goals. The analysis is reviewed and approved by the branch compliance officer.

QUESTION
--------
Does the representative's recommendation of the higher-cost advisory account over a less expensive brokerage account satisfy Reg BI's Care and Conflict of Interest Obligations despite the firm's financial incentive to recommend advisory accounts?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Reg BI expressly applies to recommendations of account types, requiring that the recommendation be in the retail customer's best interest after considering the customer's investment profile and the costs, risks, and benefits of each account type. The SEC's guidance acknowledges that a higher-cost account type may be in the client's best interest when qualitative benefits such as planning services and rebalancing provide genuine value relative to the client's specific goals. The firm satisfies the Conflict of Interest Obligation by specifically disclosing the financial incentive in Form CRS and by requiring documented cost-benefit analysis reviewed by a compliance officer, constituting a structural mitigation measure rather than mere acknowledgment of the conflict. The written analysis quantifying the $4,950 annual cost differential and articulating service-based justifications provides a defensible, client-specific basis for the recommendation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) and (iii) – Reg BI Care and Conflict of Interest Obligations; SEC Staff Bulletin on Care Obligation Regarding Account Type Recommendations (2020)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's combination of specific conflict disclosure in Form CRS, a documented quantitative cost-benefit analysis, and compliance officer review constitutes adequate conflict mitigation and a reasonable basis for the higher-cost advisory account recommendation under Reg BI.
