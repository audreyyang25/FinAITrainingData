ID: 599
Source Folder: 06_DOL_Fiduciary
Topic: ira_advice
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Lisa Romero, age 58, is leaving her employer and has $275,000 in a 401(k) plan that offers a limited menu of 12 target-date funds and 4 index funds, with no access to individual bonds or alternative investments. Lisa has expressed a strong preference for generating current income in retirement, needs to consolidate three existing IRAs totaling $185,000, and wants personalized financial planning. Her advisor, James Park at Meridian Financial, recommends rolling the 401(k) into an IRA at Meridian where she would be invested in a diversified portfolio of individual investment-grade bonds, dividend-paying ETFs (average expense ratio 0.12%), and a small allocation to a low-cost balanced fund. James's total advisory fee is 0.85% annually. James prepares a detailed written rollover analysis comparing the 401(k)'s limited fund menu and lack of planning services against the IRA's broader investment options, lower blended costs, consolidation benefits, and access to ongoing comprehensive financial planning. The analysis specifically documents that while the 401(k) plan's index fund expense ratios are lower (0.03%-0.06%), the overall value proposition of the IRA—including personalized income planning, account consolidation, and broader investment flexibility—justifies the advisory fee. James discloses his compensation, the firm's conflicts, and provides this analysis to Lisa before she authorizes the rollover.

QUESTION
--------
Is this rollover recommendation compliant with PTE 2020-02 even though the IRA's total cost exceeds the 401(k) plan's investment expenses?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

PTE 2020-02 does not mandate that the lowest-cost option must always be recommended; rather, it requires that the advice be in the retirement investor's best interest considering the totality of circumstances. The DOL has acknowledged that factors beyond fees—including the breadth of investment options, access to personalized financial planning, account consolidation, and the investor's specific income needs—are legitimate considerations in a rollover analysis. James prepared a thorough, individualized written analysis that explicitly addressed the fee differential and documented the specific additional services and benefits justifying the higher cost. He also made full disclosure of his compensation and conflicts prior to the transaction, satisfying PTE 2020-02's disclosure requirements.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
DOL PTE 2020-02, Section II(a) – Care Obligation and Best Interest Standard; Retirement Security Rule (April 23, 2024)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The advisor prepared a detailed, individualized written rollover analysis that documented legitimate non-cost benefits justifying the higher IRA expenses and made full pre-transaction disclosure of compensation and conflicts.
