Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An 82-year-old client with moderate cognitive decline (documented in notes as 'sometimes forgetful, repeats questions') and $250,000 in savings meets with a new advisor. The advisor, without consulting a family member or healthcare proxy, sells the client four different variable annuities with surrender charges of 7 years, total annual costs of 3.2%, and complex rider options the client does not understand. The client signs documents but later does not remember the conversation. No capacity assessment was conducted.

QUESTION
--------
Does this conduct comply with FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability), FINRA Rule 2330 (Variable Annuity sales), and standards for clients with diminished capacity?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates multiple applicable standards.

This conduct violates suitability standards because (1) variable annuities with 7-year surrender charges are unsuitable for an 82-year-old with modest assets and documented cognitive decline, as they restrict access to funds and create incentive misalignment; (2) the total annual costs of 3.2% are excessive for an elderly client with limited life expectancy; (3) no documented analysis of the client's goals, risk tolerance, or suitability for variable annuities was performed; and (4) the advisor failed to assess the client's capacity to understand the complex rider options, violating FINRA's heightened duty under Notice 13-25 to conduct special due diligence with vulnerable seniors. The fact that the client later did not remember the transaction is strong evidence that she lacked capacity to consent. Under Regulation Best Interest, the advisor had an obligation to act in the customer's best interest, which selling high-cost annuities with long surrender periods to a cognitively declining senior clearly violates. The sale of four annuities (rather than one consolidated holding) suggests churning or overconcentration.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability Rule); FINRA Rule 2330 (variable annuity sales); FINRA Rule 4512 (suitability for senior customers with documented cognitive vulnerability); SEC Regulation Best Interest Rule 17 CFR § 240.15l-1 (duty to act in customer's best interest); FINRA Notice 13-25 (senior investor protections)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The combination of documented cognitive decline, absence of capacity assessment, sale of illiquid variable annuities with high surrender charges and excessive fees to an 82-year-old with modest assets, without consulting family or documenting suitability.
