Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Holloway Investment Group is a dual-registrant firm whose advisers receive higher compensation for recommending proprietary mutual funds compared to equivalent third-party products. In January 2023, adviser Patricia Ngozi recommends Holloway Growth Fund (a proprietary product with a 0.95% expense ratio) to client James Whitfield, 45, a technology manager with $320,000 in investable assets and a growth-oriented risk profile. Patricia's client file includes a written conflicts disclosure presented to Whitfield specifically identifying the differential compensation structure as a material conflict, a documented comparison of three non-proprietary growth funds with expense ratios between 0.88% and 1.10%, and a narrative explanation concluding that the Holloway Growth Fund's superior 5-year risk-adjusted return (Sharpe ratio 0.87 vs. peer average 0.71) and Whitfield's existing holdings in the Holloway platform justified the recommendation. Whitfield signed an acknowledgment of the conflict disclosure prior to the transaction.

QUESTION
--------
Does Patricia Ngozi's recommendation of the proprietary fund satisfy Reg BI's Conflict of Interest Obligation and Care Obligation given the firm's differential compensation structure?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Under the Conflict of Interest Obligation, Reg BI does not prohibit recommending proprietary products or receiving differential compensation, but requires firms to establish policies to identify and, where possible, mitigate such conflicts so they do not compromise the representative's judgment. The IAA Conflicts CLE materials confirm that full, specific disclosure of a conflict combined with a substantive best-interest analysis can satisfy the duty of loyalty where the recommended product genuinely serves the client's interests. Here, Patricia satisfied both prongs: the conflict was disclosed with specificity (not buried generically), and the Best Interest Analysis documented a quantitative comparison of alternatives with a reasoned basis for concluding the proprietary fund was superior on a risk-adjusted basis for this particular client. The client's written acknowledgment further demonstrates informed consent.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) and (iii) – Reg BI Care Obligation and Conflict of Interest Obligation; SEC Advisers Act § 206 Fiduciary Duty of Loyalty

KEY FACTOR
----------
Specific written disclosure of the differential compensation conflict, combined with a documented comparative analysis showing the proprietary fund's risk-adjusted superiority, demonstrates that the conflict was identified, disclosed, and did not compromise the recommendation's basis in the client's best interest.
