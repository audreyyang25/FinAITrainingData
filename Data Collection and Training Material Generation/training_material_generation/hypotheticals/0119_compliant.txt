FACTS
-----
A 68-year-old widow with $250,000 in a rollover IRA from her late spouse's 401(k) seeks income and capital preservation. A broker recommends a deferred variable annuity with a 5% surrender charge, 1.0% M&E fee, 0.45% subaccounts, and a guaranteed income rider (GLWB) promising 5% annual income floors regardless of market performance. The client has no liquidity needs, is risk-averse, and wants longevity protection. The broker documents the guaranteed income feature, the client's low risk tolerance, and the 5-year timeline before the GLWB becomes most valuable (age 73).

QUESTION
--------
Does this variable annuity recommendation with a guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit (GLWB) rider satisfy FINRA Rule 2330?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation is compliant.

The recommendation satisfies Rule 2330 because: (1) the client is a retiree age 68 with a primary goal of income and capital preservation (the GLWB rider directly addresses this); (2) the client is risk-averse and the guaranteed income floor (5% annually) mitigates sequence-of-returns risk; (3) the client has no stated liquidity needs, reducing surrender charge burden; (4) the fees (~1.45% total) are reasonable for a variable annuity with an income guarantee rider; (5) the rollover IRA context is appropriate for annuitization (funds are already locked in the retirement account); and (6) the advisor documented the suitability reasoning, specifically identifying how the guarantee feature aligns with the client's profile. FINRA's 2013 guidance acknowledges that guaranteed income riders serve a legitimate hedging function for risk-averse retirees, distinguishing this from unsuitable annuitization of liquid savings.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330(a)(1) (reasonable basis for suitability) and FINRA Regulatory Notice 13-47 (variable annuity with guaranteed riders can be suitable for retirees seeking longevity protection and income guarantees).

KEY FACTOR
----------
GLWB rider directly addresses the client's primary stated need (guaranteed income in retirement); documented suitability analysis ties product features to client objectives.
