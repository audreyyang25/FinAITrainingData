Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
RIA Advisor Chen recommended American Funds Growth Fund of America (AGTHX) R6 class (0.58% ER, institutional minimum $1M) for a 45-year-old client with $1.8M investable assets, 30-year horizon, aggressive risk tolerance, and documented preference for actively managed equity exposure. The advisor documented that: (1) the client met the minimum; (2) historical analysis showed AGTHX beat its benchmark (S&P 500) by 1.2% annualized over 15 years after fees; (3) alternative index fund (SPY at 0.03% ER) was considered but rejected because client explicitly stated preference for active management; (4) the higher cost was disclosed with quantified 30-year impact analysis ($284,000 vs. index alternative). The client signed off on the recommendation in writing.

QUESTION
--------
Did Advisor Chen comply with Reg BI's care obligation in recommending the higher-cost actively managed fund with documented active management preference and cost disclosure?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Reg BI's care obligation (§240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)) requires the advisor to "obtain client investment profile information" and "recommend" the product that "best serves the customer's best interest, considering the customer's investment profile and all the relevant facts and circumstances." Here, the advisor documented: (1) client's explicit active management preference; (2) historical performance data showing outperformance; (3) quantified cost comparison; (4) client's eligible account size; and (5) written acknowledgment. The higher cost is justified by superior historical performance and documented client preference. This recommendation reasonably prioritizes the customer's stated investment goals over pure cost minimization, which Reg BI permits where documented and conflicts are disclosed.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation (17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i))

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented client preference for active management, verified outperformance, and quantified cost disclosure support compliance despite higher expense ratio.
