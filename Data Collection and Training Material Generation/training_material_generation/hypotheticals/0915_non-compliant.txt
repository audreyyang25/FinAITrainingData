ID: 915
Source Folder: 20_SEC_ALJ_OALJ_Decisions
Topic: undisclosed_principal_transactions
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Apex Advisory Partners, a dually registered broker-dealer and investment adviser, maintained a proprietary inventory of municipal bonds. Portfolio manager Sandra Osei routinely sold bonds from Apex's inventory into client advisory accounts at marked-up prices without disclosing to clients that Apex was acting as principal in those transactions. Osei obtained client consent to the advisory agreement at account opening but provided no transaction-specific disclosure or consent before executing the principal trades. Apex's compliance manual acknowledged the principal transaction disclosure requirement but contained no enforcement mechanism.

QUESTION
--------
Did Apex's practice of executing undisclosed principal transactions in client advisory accounts violate applicable securities law?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Investment Advisers Act §206(3) requiring prior written disclosure and client consent for each principal transaction.

Section 206(3) specifically prohibits an investment adviser from acting as principal for its own account in transactions with advisory clients without disclosing the capacity and obtaining client consent before completion of each transaction. A blanket consent at account opening does not satisfy the transaction-specific requirement of §206(3). The ALJ would further find a failure-to-supervise violation arising from the compliance manual's acknowledgment of the rule without any operative enforcement mechanism.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §206(3); IA Release No. 1732 (1998)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Transaction-specific prior disclosure and consent is required for each principal trade; a general account-opening consent is legally insufficient under §206(3).
