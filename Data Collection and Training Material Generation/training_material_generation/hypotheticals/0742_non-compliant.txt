ID: 742
Source Folder: 12_FINRA_Arbitration_Awards
Topic: arbitration_damages
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Hisako Ogawa, a 68-year-old retired widow with $320,000 in savings and a conservative risk profile, opened a brokerage account with registered representative Craig Millburn at Scottrade, Inc. Millburn placed Ogawa into a concentrated portfolio of leveraged ETFs and small-cap technology stocks, generating over $47,000 in commissions over 18 months while Ogawa's account value declined by $112,000. Ogawa, who had limited English proficiency, testified that Millburn told her the investments were 'safe income products.' When Ogawa filed a FINRA arbitration claim pro se alleging unsuitability, common law fraud, and breach of fiduciary duty, she initially appeared but failed to appear at the final hearing date. The arbitration panel proceeded to evaluate the evidence on the record.

QUESTION
--------
Is the registered representative liable for unsuitable recommendations and fraud based on the evidence presented?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant — the representative is liable for unsuitable recommendations and common law fraud.

Under FINRA Rule 2111(a), a representative must have a reasonable basis to believe a recommended transaction is suitable for the specific customer based on their investment profile, including age, risk tolerance, and financial situation. Placing a 68-year-old conservative retiree into leveraged ETFs and concentrated speculative equities violates the customer-specific suitability obligation. The misrepresentation that these were 'safe income products' constitutes common law fraud through material misstatement of fact upon which the client relied to her detriment. The claimant's failure to appear at the final hearing, as seen in Patten v. TD Ameritrade, does not preclude the panel from evaluating evidence already in the record.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability — Customer-Specific Obligation); Common Law Fraud (material misrepresentation of investment characteristics)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative materially misrepresented speculative, high-risk leveraged products as 'safe income products' to a conservative elderly retiree with limited English proficiency, constituting both a suitability violation and common law fraud.
