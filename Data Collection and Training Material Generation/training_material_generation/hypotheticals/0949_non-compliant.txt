Topic: expungement_standard_factually_impossible
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered representative, James Weller, settled a customer complaint for $45,000 after the customer alleged unsuitable recommendations in a variable annuity. Weller subsequently filed an expungement claim in FINRA arbitration. The sole arbitrator granted expungement after finding only that the settlement amount was modest and that Weller had a long career without prior complaints, without making any finding that the claim was factually impossible, clearly erroneous, or that Weller was not involved.

QUESTION
--------
Is the arbitrator's expungement award compliant with FINRA's expungement standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of FINRA Rule 2080 and the required expungement threshold standards.

FINRA Rule 2080 requires that before expungement is granted, the arbitrator must affirmatively find that the claim, allegation, or information is factually impossible or clearly erroneous, that the registered person was not involved in the alleged investment-related sales practice violation, or that the claim was false. A finding based solely on settlement amount and career length does not satisfy any of the three required threshold findings under Rule 2080(b)(1). PIABA has consistently argued that arbitrators granting expungement without rigorous application of these standards undermine the CRD system's investor protection function.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2080; FINRA Regulatory Notice 17-42; FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure Rule 12805

KEY FACTOR
----------
The arbitrator failed to make any of the three mandatory threshold findings required by FINRA Rule 2080(b)(1) before recommending expungement.
