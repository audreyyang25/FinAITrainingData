Source Folder: unknown
Topic: ia_duty_of_care
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An adviser manages $2.3 million for a 55-year-old client. The client completed a risk tolerance questionnaire five years ago indicating "moderate risk tolerance" and a 15-year horizon to retirement. The client's circumstances have substantially changed: he inherited $1.5 million in additional assets, was promoted to a high-income position, and explicitly stated he could now accept "moderately aggressive risk." The adviser has not updated the client risk profile or suitability documentation in five years. No conversation occurred about adjusting the portfolio to match the client's newly stated, materially different risk profile and time horizon.

QUESTION
--------
Does the adviser's conduct satisfy the duty of care?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the duty of care.

The adviser failed to conduct timely review and update of client circumstances after material changes in the client's financial situation, income, risk capacity, and explicitly stated risk tolerance. SEC Rule 206(4)-7(d) requires periodic review of recommendations and adjustment when circumstances warrant. The client's inheritance, income increase, and explicit statement that he can now accept moderately aggressive risk are material changes that require reassessment. The adviser's failure to document or discuss these changes, or to adjust the portfolio allocation accordingly, means the adviser lacks a current reasonable basis for the existing recommendations. The outdated five-year-old risk profile no longer reflects the client's actual circumstances, and continued reliance on it violates the duty of care's requirement to maintain current client information and periodically reassess suitability.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206(2); SEC Compliance Rule 206(4)-7(d) requires advisers to periodically review recommendations and adjust when client circumstances materially change; lack of documentation of changed circumstances violates reasonable basis requirement.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Failure to update client risk profile and suitability documentation for five years despite material changes in the client's financial circumstances, income, and explicitly stated risk tolerance.
