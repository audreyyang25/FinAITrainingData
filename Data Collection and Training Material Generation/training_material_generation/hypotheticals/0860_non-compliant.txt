ID: 860
Source Folder: 18_OCC_Bank_Trust_Guidance
Topic: bank_fiduciary
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
First National Trust Bank serves as trustee for a $1.2 million irrevocable family trust established under a 2018 trust agreement that explicitly requires the trustee to invest only in investment-grade fixed income securities and U.S. government bonds. The trust officer, seeking to improve the trust's yield in a low-interest-rate environment, reallocates 35% of the portfolio into high-yield corporate bonds rated BB and below without consulting the trust document or seeking court modification. The trust's 72-year-old income beneficiary relies on monthly distributions of approximately $3,800 to supplement Social Security income. The bank never sought beneficiary consent or probate court approval for the deviation from investment directives.

QUESTION
--------
Does the trust officer's reallocation into high-yield bonds comply with the bank's fiduciary duties under OCC standards and the governing trust instrument?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a breach of the duty of loyalty and the duty to follow the terms of the governing trust instrument.

OTS Trust Handbook Section 110 expressly requires the fiduciary to adhere to 'the provisions of the will, pension plan or trust document' as the first and most fundamental governing source. By investing in below-investment-grade securities contrary to explicit trust instrument restrictions, the trust officer violated the duty of loyalty and the duty to administer the trust in accordance with its terms. No probate court order or beneficiary consent was obtained to authorize deviation from the governing investment mandate, as would be required under applicable state trust law and OCC fiduciary standards.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
OCC Comptroller's Handbook – Asset Management (June 2023); OTS Trust Handbook Section 110 – Fiduciary Duties; 12 CFR 9.11 (Investment of Fiduciary Funds)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The trust officer disregarded explicit investment restrictions in the governing trust document without obtaining court modification or beneficiary consent, constituting a breach of the fundamental fiduciary duty to follow the trust instrument.
