Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Meridian Capital Advisors, LLC is a state-registered investment adviser with $87 million in assets under management, currently registered with the California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation ('DFPI') and examined by state regulators as part of the 2025 NASAA Coordinated Investment Adviser Examination program conducted between February 1 and July 31, 2025. During the examination—the first such examination Meridian had undergone as a state-registered firm—examiners noted that two of Meridian's investment adviser representatives ('IARs') had recently passed the Series 65 examination and were provisionally listed on Form ADV Part 1 before their individual IAR applications were fully processed by the IARD system, a processing delay of 11 business days caused by a documented IARD system backlog. Meridian's compliance officer had submitted the IAR applications through the IARD system contemporaneously with the representatives commencing advisory activities, funded the daily account, and maintained written evidence of the submission timestamps. The two IARs did not solicit new clients during the 11-day processing window and only serviced existing accounts under direct supervision of Meridian's principal.

QUESTION
--------
Does Meridian Capital Advisors' temporary gap between IAR application submission and IARD system processing confirmation, during which the IARs serviced only existing accounts under principal supervision, constitute a state registration violation actionable by the California DFPI?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

California and NASAA model rules require that IAR applications be filed through the IARD system prior to or contemporaneously with the commencement of advisory activities, but enforcement practice and DFPI guidance distinguish between a failure to file and a ministerial processing delay outside the firm's control. Meridian's compliance officer submitted applications and funded the IARD daily account contemporaneously with the IARs beginning work, satisfying the firm's affirmative regulatory obligation to initiate registration. Because the IARs did not solicit new clients and operated exclusively under the supervision of a fully registered principal during the processing window, no investor was exposed to the risks that registration requirements are designed to prevent. State examination findings from coordinated IA exams consistently treat documented, good-faith submission delays attributable to IARD system backlogs as non-actionable where supervisory controls are in place.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
California Corporations Code § 25230; California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation IAR Registration Requirements (IARD-based registration); NASAA Model Rule on Investment Adviser Representative Registration

KEY FACTOR
----------
Contemporaneous, documented IARD filing by the firm, combined with restrictions on new client solicitation and direct principal supervision during the processing delay, demonstrates good-faith registration compliance and negates the elements of an actionable state registration violation.
