Source Folder: 06_DOL_Fiduciary
Topic: pte_2020_02
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
James Liu, age 64 and recently retired, has $520,000 in a traditional IRA. His advisor, Stephanie Wells, who operates under a dual-registered broker-dealer and RIA, recommends James invest $310,000 in a non-traded REIT with a 7-year lock-up period and 12% upfront commission, and $150,000 in a fixed indexed annuity paying her a 7% commission. Stephanie provides James a written disclosure acknowledging her firm's material conflicts of interest and the compensation she will receive. However, her firm's retrospective compliance review, required under PTE 2020-02, has not been conducted for the past two years, and the firm's written compliance policies regarding IRA investment advice were last updated in 2019 — before PTE 2020-02 became effective. Stephanie's individual recommendation documentation states the products are 'suitable for a retiree seeking income' but contains no analysis of lower-cost alternatives or liquidity needs.

QUESTION
--------
Does the advisor's conduct satisfy the conditions of PTE 2020-02 for relying on the exemption from prohibited transaction rules under ERISA and IRC §4975?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates PTE 2020-02's conditions requiring retrospective compliance reviews, updated written policies, and a best interest recommendation standard that goes beyond mere suitability.

PTE 2020-02 conditions relief from prohibited transaction provisions on several cumulative requirements: the advice must satisfy Impartial Conduct Standards (best interest, reasonable compensation, no materially misleading statements), the financial institution must maintain written policies and procedures that are current, and the institution must conduct a retrospective compliance review at least annually. Stephanie's firm has failed to conduct the required retrospective review for two years and has not updated its written policies since before PTE 2020-02 took effect. Additionally, stating the products are merely 'suitable' without analyzing lower-cost alternatives or James's liquidity needs fails the best interest standard, which requires more than traditional suitability analysis.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
DOL PTE 2020-02 §II(a)-(d) – Conditions for Relief including Impartial Conduct Standards, Policies and Procedures, and Retrospective Review

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's failure to conduct the required annual retrospective compliance review and to maintain current written policies means the firm cannot rely on PTE 2020-02's prohibited transaction exemption, regardless of the individual disclosure provided.
