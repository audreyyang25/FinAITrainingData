FACTS
-----
Advisor Reeves at Sterling Capital advised client Thompson, age 62, to exchange his existing variable annuity (Guardian LifeGuard VA, issued 2010, surrender charge 7% in year 1 declining to 0% in year 8, cost basis $300,000, current value $420,000, $35,000 deferred gain, contract in year 14 with no surrender charge). Reeves recommended a new Equitable eq. Elite Pinnacle VA with 0.65% M&E fee + 0.45% subaccount fees = 1.10% total, versus Guardian's 1.25% M&E + subaccount mix = 1.55%. Reeves performed a written analysis showing: (1) no surrender charge applies (contract in free-look period end); (2) new VA's fee savings of 0.45% annually on $420,000 = $1,890/year; (3) 7-year breakeven after accounting for $5,250 exchange/underwriting costs; (4) Thompson's investment objective (age-appropriate income + growth) supported the exchange; (5) tax treatment: no 1035 exchange tax consequence. Reeves documented a suitability memorandum, received written consent from Thompson, and disclosed the exchange costs. Thompson was risk-tolerant, in good health, and had no early liquidity needs.

QUESTION
--------
Did Advisor Reeves comply with FINRA Rule 2330 (Variable Annuity Suitability) and Rule 2111 (Suitability) in recommending the VA exchange?

ANSWER
------
Yes. FINRA Rule 2330 requires that prior to recommending a variable annuity exchange, the member must perform a thorough analysis comparing the existing contract and the proposed contract, including age, health, liquidity needs, surrender charges, and cost differential. Reeves satisfied each prong: (1) conducted written exchange analysis; (2) identified no surrender charge liability (key risk eliminated); (3) calculated fee savings ($1,890/year on $420K over 7 years demonstrating objective economic benefit); (4) confirmed suitability to client's stated investment objective (income + growth appropriate for 62-year-old); (5) obtained written consent; (6) disclosed exchange costs ($5,250) transparently. The 0.45% annual savings, combined with Thompson's long life expectancy (no early-surrender risk) and risk tolerance, support the exchange recommendation under Rule 2330(d). This scenario mirrors compliant FINRA enforcement precedent where advisors document cost-benefit analysis, verify surrender-charge status, and obtain affirmative client consent.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330(d) (Variable Annuity Exchange—Suitability Analysis) and FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability—Reasonable Basis and Customer-Specific Factors)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented written exchange analysis, no surrender charge, demonstrated fee savings, and verified suitability to client's investment objective with disclosed costs satisfy Rule 2330 and Rule 2111 requirements.
