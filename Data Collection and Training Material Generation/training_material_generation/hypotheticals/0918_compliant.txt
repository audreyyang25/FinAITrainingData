ID: 918
Source Folder: 20_SEC_ALJ_OALJ_Decisions
Topic: compliant_supervisory_system_broker_dealer
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Pinnacle Brokerage Services implemented a layered supervisory system in which each registered representative's daily trade blotter was reviewed by a branch supervisor within 24 hours, with automated surveillance flags for concentration exceeding 20% in a single security, annualized turnover ratios above 3.0, and commissions exceeding 2% of account value in any 90-day period. When the system flagged representative Daniel Crane for two annuity replacement recommendations involving clients over age 70, the branch supervisor conducted a documented review, interviewed Crane, obtained written client attestations confirming understanding of surrender charges, and escalated the matter to the compliance department, which found the replacements consistent with the clients' updated financial profiles. All review steps were memorialized in a contemporaneous written supervisory procedure log.

QUESTION
--------
Did Pinnacle's supervisory response to the flagged annuity replacements satisfy its obligations under the Exchange Act?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Exchange Act §15(b)(4)(E) and FINRA Rule 3110 require broker-dealers to establish and enforce a supervisory system reasonably designed to achieve compliance with applicable securities laws. Pinnacle's automated surveillance, tiered escalation, documented interviewing, and client attestation procedures collectively constitute a system that goes beyond perfunctory review and demonstrates active enforcement. An ALJ applying the 'reasonably designed' standard would find that Pinnacle's response to the flag was proportionate, documented, and resolved through substantive analysis rather than rubber-stamp approval.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Securities Exchange Act of 1934 §15(b)(4)(E); Exchange Act Rule 17a-4 (recordkeeping); FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision)

KEY FACTOR
----------
A supervisory system is compliant when it includes automated detection, documented human review, client confirmation, and contemporaneous records demonstrating substantive rather than perfunctory oversight.
