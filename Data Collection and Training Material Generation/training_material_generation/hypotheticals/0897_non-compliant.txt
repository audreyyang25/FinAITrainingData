ID: 897
Source Folder: 19_CFTC_Guidance
Topic: cta_cpo_suitability
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A CPO operates a 4.7 Fund exclusively for QEP participants and, in anticipation of the CFTC's October 2023 proposed rulemaking, reviews whether its existing participants would satisfy the proposed increased Portfolio Requirement financial thresholds reflecting inflation adjustments. One participant—a 45-year-old technology professional—qualified as a QEP under the existing thresholds with $2.1 million in securities and futures positions, but would fall short of the proposed elevated thresholds. Rather than waiting for final rules, the CPO proactively sends existing participants a one-page internal memo characterizing the proposed rulemaking as 'a minor administrative update with no impact on current fund operations or participant eligibility,' without disclosing that the participant could lose QEP status upon rule finalization and that the CPO has not yet determined how it will treat non-qualifying participants under revised standards.

QUESTION
--------
Did the CPO's communication to fund participants about the proposed CFTC Rule 4.7 amendments satisfy its disclosure obligations regarding material regulatory developments affecting participant eligibility?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a material omission and disclosure violation under CFTC Regulation 4.7 and NFA disclosure standards applicable to 4.7 Fund operators.

Even under the reduced disclosure regime of Rule 4.7, CPOs retain affirmative obligations to communicate material information to fund participants, and a proposed regulatory change that could strip existing participants of their QEP eligibility status is unambiguously material. The CPO's characterization of the proposed rulemaking as having 'no impact on current fund operations or participant eligibility' is affirmatively misleading given that at least one participant falls below the proposed elevated Portfolio Requirement thresholds. The omission of the CPO's contingency planning—or lack thereof—for non-qualifying participants further denies investors information necessary to evaluate their continued participation in the fund.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
CFTC Regulation 4.7(b) – Disclosure Obligations for 4.7 Funds; NFA Compliance Rule 2-29 – Communications with Participants

KEY FACTOR
----------
Affirmatively mischaracterizing a proposed regulatory change as immaterial when it could eliminate a participant's QEP eligibility constitutes a disclosure violation regardless of a 4.7 Fund's reduced disclosure requirements.
