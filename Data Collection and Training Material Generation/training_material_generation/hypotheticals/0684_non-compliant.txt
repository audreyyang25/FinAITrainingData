Topic: investor_harm_empirical
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer registered representative recommends that a 28-year-old millennial earning $52,000 annually with $15,000 in student loan debt invest her $8,000 emergency savings into a non-traded REIT with a minimum 5-year holding period, 7% upfront commission, and no secondary market liquidity. The representative's firm offers a higher payout ratio on non-traded REITs (90% of commission) compared to mutual funds (40% of commission). The client's new account form indicates a 'conservative' risk tolerance and 'short-term' investment time horizon. No alternative liquid investments were discussed or documented.

QUESTION
--------
Was the broker-dealer representative's recommendation of the non-traded REIT to this client consistent with Reg BI's Care Obligation?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates the Reg BI Care Obligation due to a clear mismatch between the product characteristics and the client's profile.

The Care Obligation requires that a recommendation be in the retail customer's best interest and not place the financial professional's interest ahead of the customer's. Recommending a 5-year illiquid, high-commission non-traded REIT to a client with conservative risk tolerance, a short-term time horizon, student loan debt, and whose only savings constitute an emergency fund is patently inconsistent with the client's investment profile. The significantly higher payout ratio on non-traded REITs further suggests the representative's financial interest drove the recommendation. FINRA has specifically highlighted suitability concerns with non-traded REITs for clients who need liquidity.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; FINRA Regulatory Notice 15-02 (Non-Traded REITs)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommending an illiquid, high-commission non-traded REIT as the sole investment for a conservative, short-term investor's emergency savings demonstrates a clear failure to prioritize the client's best interest.
