ID: 660
Source Folder: 08_State_Fiduciary_Rules
Topic: state_fiduciary_annuity
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A New York-based insurance producer recommends that a 72-year-old retired widow replace her existing fixed annuity (earning 3.2% with no surrender charges remaining) with a new indexed annuity carrying a 10-year surrender period and a 7% surrender charge in year one. The client has $185,000 in liquid savings, a modest Social Security income of $1,850/month, and has expressed a primary goal of preserving principal for healthcare expenses. The producer earns an 8% first-year commission on the new indexed annuity versus 0% trailing commission on the existing product. The producer documents the client's risk tolerance as 'moderate growth' but does not document any analysis of why the replacement is in the client's best interest compared to retaining the existing annuity, nor does he disclose the material conflict arising from the significantly higher commission on the replacement product.

QUESTION
--------
Under NY Insurance Regulation 187, did the producer satisfy the best interest standard when recommending the annuity replacement?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates NY Insurance Regulation 187's best interest and disclosure requirements for annuity transactions.

Reg 187 requires that any recommendation to purchase or replace an annuity contract issued or delivered in New York must be in the consumer's best interest at the time of the transaction. The regulation mandates that the producer must document the basis for the recommendation, including why a replacement is superior to the existing product. Additionally, Reg 187 requires disclosure of material conflicts of interest, including compensation differentials. Here, the producer failed to document a best-interest analysis comparing the existing annuity to the replacement, failed to disclose the significant commission differential constituting a material conflict, and recommended a product with a 10-year surrender period and high surrender charges to a 72-year-old client who needs liquidity for healthcare—factors that are facially inconsistent with the client's stated objectives.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
NY Insurance Regulation 187 – Suitability & Best Interests in Life Insurance & Annuity Transactions (11 NYCRR 224)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The producer failed to document any best-interest analysis for the replacement and failed to disclose the material compensation conflict, both of which are core requirements under NY Reg 187 for annuity transactions.
