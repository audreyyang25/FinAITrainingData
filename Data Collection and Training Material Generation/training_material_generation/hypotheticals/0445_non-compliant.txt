Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Robert, age 78, is a widower with $2.2M in assets managed by Advisor Davis at a regional broker-dealer. Robert's son, Michael (age 48), obtains a power of attorney (POA) over Robert's financial accounts citing Robert's increasing forgetfulness and difficulty managing bills. Over 18 months, Michael directs Davis to execute 47 trades in Robert's account, concentrating the portfolio into micro-cap biotech stocks (now 78% of portfolio value, up from 5%). Michael also requests $180,000 in distributions every 6 months to pay for his own business venture, framed to Davis as "Robert's wish to support family." Robert's investment profile documents (signed 2 years prior) stated: "Conservative, age-appropriate portfolio; income focus; avoid concentrated positions." When Davis contacts Robert directly (without Michael present), Robert expresses confusion: "I don't remember approving all these biotech stocks. I just want my dividends." Davis continues executing Michael's trading and distribution instructions without independent verification of Robert's current cognitive capacity or whether Robert actually authorized the concentration strategy.

QUESTION
--------
Did Advisor Davis comply with fiduciary duties and FINRA Rule 2165 (account holds) when managing Robert's account under a POA while receiving instructions that conflict with Robert's documented objectives and Robert's direct statement?

ANSWER
------
No. Davis violated multiple standards: (1) FINRA Rule 2165 requires a temporary hold on disbursements when a firm has a reasonable basis to believe financial exploitation has occurred. The facts—Michael's frequent large distributions, concentration of portfolio contrary to Robert's documented conservative objectives, Robert's confusion when contacted directly—constitute red flags for exploitation. Davis had an affirmative duty under Rule 2165 to place a hold and investigate, not merely execute instructions. (2) Under Reg BI Care Obligation (17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)), Davis must act in the client's best interest, not mechanically follow a POA holder's directives that contradict the client's expressed wishes and documented profile. (3) The widening gap between Robert's stated preferences (conservative, income-focused) and the executed strategy (78% micro-cap biotech, heavy trading, distributions for Michael's business) should have triggered heightened scrutiny. (4) SEC/FINRA advisory notes (2018 Senior Investor Report) require firms to verify that POA holders' instructions align with the principal's interests, particularly when the principal expresses contradictory preferences. Davis's failure to challenge Michael's instructions or pause for investigation—despite direct evidence of Robert's confusion—constitutes neglect of fiduciary duty and violation of Rule 2165's mandate.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2165 (Temporary Hold on Disbursements for Suspected Financial Exploitation); Reg BI Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i); SEC 2018 Senior Investor Report (Financial Exploitation and Undue Influence)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Advisor continued executing POA holder's high-risk instructions without pause despite client's direct expression of confusion, portfolio concentration opposite to client's documented conservative profile, and red flags for exploitation under Rule 2165 (large distributions, uncharacteristic trading).
