ID: 890
Source Folder: 19_CFTC_Guidance
Topic: cta_cpo_suitability
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered CTA solicits a 68-year-old retired schoolteacher with a fixed income of $42,000 per year and a total liquid net worth of $85,000 to participate in a managed futures trading program focused on crude oil and agricultural commodity contracts. The CTA's disclosure document describes the program as suitable for 'sophisticated investors who can sustain a total loss of invested capital.' The client invests $40,000, representing 47% of her liquid net worth. Within 60 days, consistent with CFTC research showing retail traders typically experience gains or losses of a few hundred dollars per trading event concentrated in a handful of trades, the account loses $31,500. The CTA never conducted a formal suitability inquiry and has no documented basis for recommending the program to this client.

QUESTION
--------
Did the CTA violate its suitability obligations by recommending a managed futures program to this client without conducting a documented suitability inquiry?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a suitability violation under NFA Compliance Rule 2-30.

NFA Compliance Rule 2-30 requires CTAs to obtain essential facts about each customer, including financial situation, investment experience, and risk tolerance, before recommending any trading program. The CTA here made no documented suitability determination despite the client's limited liquid net worth, fixed retirement income, and lack of derivatives experience. Placing 47% of a retiree's liquid assets into a program explicitly described as suitable only for those who can sustain total loss, without any suitability analysis, is a textbook violation. The subsequent rapid, substantial loss underscores the material unsuitability of the recommendation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
NFA Compliance Rule 2-30 – Know Your Customer and Suitability Obligations for CTAs

KEY FACTOR
----------
The CTA failed to conduct any documented suitability inquiry before recommending a high-risk managed futures program to an elderly, income-limited retiree with concentrated exposure.
