ID: 522
Source Folder: 02_SEC_Enforcement_Actions
Topic: sec_enforcement_ia
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Paradigm Horizon Advisory, LLC, a registered investment adviser with $340 million in AUM serving approximately 680 retail clients, maintained an undisclosed arrangement with three mutual fund families. Under the arrangement, Paradigm Horizon received revenue-sharing payments of 12 to 25 basis points on client assets invested in certain share classes of these fund families. Paradigm Horizon's investment adviser representatives consistently recommended the revenue-sharing share classes over lower-cost institutional or clean share classes that were available and for which the clients qualified. Paradigm Horizon's Form ADV Part 2A disclosed that it 'may receive compensation from third parties' but did not specifically identify the revenue-sharing arrangements, the fund families involved, or the magnitude of the payments. Over three years, the firm received approximately $1.1 million in undisclosed revenue-sharing payments while clients collectively paid an estimated $870,000 more in fund expenses than they would have paid in the lower-cost share classes.

QUESTION
--------
Did Paradigm Horizon Advisory's receipt of undisclosed revenue-sharing payments while recommending higher-cost share classes violate its fiduciary duty under the Advisers Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes violations of Sections 206(2) and 206(4) of the Investment Advisers Act for breach of fiduciary duty through inadequate conflict disclosure and for placing the adviser's financial interest ahead of clients' interests.

An investment adviser's fiduciary duty requires full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts of interest, particularly financial incentives that could influence investment recommendations. Generic boilerplate language stating the adviser 'may receive compensation from third parties' is insufficient to satisfy the disclosure obligation when the adviser is actively receiving specific, quantifiable revenue-sharing payments that directly incentivize recommendations of higher-cost products. This fact pattern closely parallels the SEC's action in In the Matter of Paradigm Wealth Advisory, LLC (IA-5852), where the Commission found that undisclosed investment conflicts violated the Advisers Act. The duty to disclose requires specificity—clients must understand the nature, scope, and financial magnitude of the conflict to provide informed consent.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Sections 206(2) and 206(4); SEC Rule 206(4)-7 (Compliance Policies and Procedures)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The use of vague, generic disclosure language about potential third-party compensation was materially inadequate to inform clients of the specific, ongoing revenue-sharing arrangement that incentivized the adviser to recommend higher-cost share classes.
