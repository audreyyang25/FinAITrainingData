ID: 817
Source Folder: 16_Congressional_Materials
Topic: gao_findings
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A dually registered broker-dealer and investment adviser affiliated with a large wirehouse recommended that a 58-year-old engineer with $620,000 in savings roll his 401(k) into a variable annuity subaccount invested primarily in proprietary mutual funds managed by the firm's affiliate, generating both a 6% surrender-charge annuity commission and ongoing 1.25% annual advisory fees once the annuity was placed in the advisory account. GAO reports examining Reg BI implementation found that firms frequently failed to document why a rollover from a low-cost 401(k) into a higher-cost proprietary product was in the retail customer's best interest, particularly when the firm received enhanced compensation. The representative's written rationale stated only 'client desires guaranteed income features' without comparative cost analysis or evaluation of the client's existing 401(k) investment options.

QUESTION
--------
Does the representative's rollover recommendation, supported only by a generic rationale and without documented comparative cost analysis, satisfy Reg BI's Care and Conflict of Interest obligations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Reg BI's Care Obligation and Conflict of Interest Obligation due to inadequate best-interest analysis and undisclosed layered compensation conflicts.

GAO findings on Reg BI implementation highlighted that documentation of rollover recommendations must include a genuine comparative analysis of costs, benefits, and alternatives—not merely a restatement of the client's stated preference. The layered compensation structure (upfront annuity commission plus ongoing advisory fees from proprietary funds) creates a material conflict requiring specific mitigation under the Conflict of Interest Obligation. A generic rationale cannot substitute for the quantitative cost comparison the Care Obligation demands when recommending a move from a low-cost employer plan.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iii) – Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation; GAO-22-104234 (examining Reg BI implementation deficiencies in rollover recommendations).

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative's failure to document a comparative cost-benefit analysis between the existing 401(k) and the proprietary variable annuity, while receiving layered compensation, is precisely the implementation gap GAO identified as a systemic Reg BI compliance failure.
