Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An SEC-registered investment adviser managing three private equity funds charges each fund a flat $250,000 annual compliance and regulatory expense allocation. The adviser does not disclose to limited partners that $95,000 of this allocation covers the salary of an in-house compliance analyst who spends approximately 60% of her time on the adviser's registered investment company business, not the private funds. The fund's PPM and LPA state that only 'expenses directly attributable to the fund' may be charged. The adviser has not conducted an expense allocation review in over two years.

QUESTION
--------
Did the registered investment adviser violate its fiduciary duty of care and the private fund rules by allocating non-fund-related compliance expenses to the private funds?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the investment adviser's fiduciary duty of care under Section 206 of the Advisers Act and the fund's governing documents.

The SEC's Fiduciary Interpretation clarifies that the duty of care requires an investment adviser to have a reasonable belief that the advice it provides — including expense allocation decisions — is in the best interest of the client. Charging private fund investors for compliance staff time predominantly spent on other business lines contradicts the fund documents' requirement that only directly attributable expenses be charged and constitutes a breach of fiduciary duty. The absence of a periodic expense allocation review further evidences inadequate compliance procedures.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206 (Anti-Fraud); SEC Fiduciary Interpretation (IA-5248) – Duty of Care (Expense Allocation); SEC Private Fund Adviser Rules

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser misallocated compliance expenses that were predominantly attributable to a separate business line, breaching both the fund's governing documents and the adviser's fiduciary duty of care.
