FACTS
-----
A 45-year-old executive with $2.1 million in investable assets, high risk tolerance, and a 20-year time horizon holds a non-qualified deferred compensation plan balance of $650,000 that will be distributed as a lump sum in 18 months per contract terms. The advisor recommends a 10-year B-share variable annuity (8.25% front-end load, declining to 0% after 10 years; 1.85% annual M&E; 3.5% annual subaccount fees; surrender charge declining from 7% to 0% over 10 years) as a temporary repository for the lump sum, with explicit plan to exchange into an equal-value no-load, low-fee annuity (0.50% annual cost) upon receipt of funds in 18 months. The advisor documents in writing: (1) the temporary nature of the holding; (2) that the B-share structure aligns with the 18-month liquidity horizon (no surrender charge in years 0–1.5); (3) that total cost over 18 months is $31,590, compared to holding in a money market fund at $1,620; (4) the client's acknowledgment that he understands the cost premium and the redemption strategy; and (5) a suitability letter explaining that the client's substantial wealth and aggressive profile support the ability to absorb costs in exchange for principal protection and guaranteed income optionality during the transition.

QUESTION
--------
Does this variable annuity recommendation comply with FINRA Rule 2330 and Reg BI?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation is compliant with FINRA Rule 2330 and Reg BI.

This recommendation is compliant because the advisor has satisfied all elements of Rule 2330(b)(1): (1) Reasonable Basis: The B-share structure with no surrender charge during the 18-month holding period is rationally aligned with the client's known liquidity date and time horizon—this is not a typical long-term VA play but a documented tactical use. (2) Customer-Specific Suitability: The client is 45, wealthy ($2.1M+), aggressive risk-tolerant, and can afford the cost premium; the annuity purchase does not impair his other financial objectives. (3) Disclosure: The advisor explicitly quantified the cost differential ($31,590 vs. $1,620) and obtained written acknowledgment, satisfying Reg BI's transparency requirement under Rule 15l-1(a)(2). (4) Best Interest: While the absolute fee is high, the advisor's documented rationale—principal protection and income optionality during a transition period for a client who can afford it—is not per se unreasonable; the plan to exchange into a lower-cost vehicle after 18 months shows prudent construction. The explicit documentation and client sophistication and acknowledgment align this with best practice in FINRA case law.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330(b) requires suitability analysis considering customer age, other investments, financial situation, and needs; FINRA Notice 13-45 requires that recommendations be based on reasonable basis investigation into the merits of the security relative to alternatives. Reg BI Rule 15l-1(b) requires recommendations in the customer's best interest after considering conflicts and providing clear disclosure. Key case: In re Merrill Lynch Pierce Fenner & Smith Inc., Exchange Act Rel. No. 84790 (Oct. 16, 2018), the SEC affirmed that suitability for VAs can exist where documented analysis shows alignment with customer profile and explicit acknowledgment of costs.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Detailed written documentation of the specific, time-limited purpose of the VA purchase, explicit quantification of costs relative to alternatives, and client acknowledgment that he understands and accepts the cost-benefit tradeoff demonstrates compliance with Rule 2330(b)(1)(C) and Reg BI Rule 15l-1(b).
