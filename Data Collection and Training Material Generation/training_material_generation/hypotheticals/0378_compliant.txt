FACTS
-----
Patricia, age 64, has $420,000 in a 403(b) plan at her university employer offering limited investment options: three high-cost mutual funds (average 1.34% ER) and a stable value option (4.1% return, 0.18% ER). Patricia is four years from retirement, moderately conservative, and plans to take distributions. Fee-only RIA David recommends rolling to an IRA and investing in a diversified portfolio: bond funds and equity ETFs with blended ER of 0.22%, charging a flat annual fee of $3,600 (0.86% of assets, declining annually). David prepares a written ten-page analysis documenting: (1) comparison of 403(b) mutual funds (1.34% ER) versus recommended IRA portfolio (0.22% ER) plus advisory fee showing net $600 annual savings after ten years; (2) 403(b) distribution restrictions versus IRA flexibility for Patricia's pre-retirement distribution strategy; (3) acknowledgment that stable value was not recommended because it offered insufficient purchasing power growth for her 20+ year post-retirement period; (4) analysis of whether remaining in plan was viable; (5) client acknowledgment of rollover recommendation dated and signed.

QUESTION
--------
Does David's 403(b) rollover recommendation comply with PTE 2020-02 best interest requirements?

ANSWER
------
Yes. David's recommendation satisfies all Five-Part Test requirements under PTE 2020-02. Compliance is demonstrated by: (1) documented best interest analysis directly comparing 403(b) fund costs and distribution features to recommended IRA structure, with quantified cost savings of $600 annually; (2) flat-fee compensation structure (not AUM) that does not incentivize rollover; (3) explicit written analysis of the stable value option and why it was rejected based on inadequate growth for Patricia's 20+ year retirement horizon, not cost avoidance; (4) acknowledgment of IRA distribution flexibility as genuine added value matching Patricia's stated distribution needs; (5) comprehensive written policies addressing rollover analysis and documentation provided; (6) client-signed acknowledgment of recommendation. The rollover directly serves the client's best interest (lower ongoing costs + distribution flexibility aligned with her retirement timeline), and the adviser's compensation ($3,600) is reasonable under ERISA §408(b)(14). This follows compliant DOL exemplar patterns.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
DOL PTE 2020-02 Best Interest Standard; Five-Part Test (all five parts); ERISA §408(b)(14)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented best interest analysis comparing 403(b) fund costs, distribution features, and alternatives, with quantified cost savings and client-signed acknowledgment, satisfying all five PTE 2020-02 requirements.
