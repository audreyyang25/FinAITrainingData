Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker recommends a concentrated position of 15,000 shares of a technology company stock (current value $450,000, representing 90% of client's $500,000 investable assets) to a 45-year-old executive officer at that same technology company. The executive receives an annual salary of $200,000, annual bonus of $100,000, and stock option grants. The client states that he 'understands the company well, has high conviction in its long-term prospects,' and explicitly requests concentration as a wealth-building strategy. The broker documents the client's income stability, professional expertise in the sector, and written acknowledgment of concentration risk. The recommendation includes a diversification plan contingent upon the client's vesting schedule.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy FINRA Suitability Rule 2111's customer-specific prong, despite apparent concentration risk?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation satisfies FINRA Suitability Rule 2111.

Although the position is concentrated, Rule 2111(a)(2) requires suitability to be assessed relative to the customer's specific financial profile, investment objectives, and risk tolerance—not in a vacuum. Here, several factors support suitability: (1) The client has direct professional expertise and insider knowledge of the company's operations, competitive position, and prospects—not merely retail speculation. (2) The client has substantial diversified income ($300,000 annually in salary + bonus) independent of the stock position, mitigating total portfolio risk. (3) The client has explicitly requested and consented to concentration as a deliberate wealth-building strategy; Rule 2111(a)(2) permits recommendations aligned with customer objectives if reasonable. (4) The broker has documented the client's understanding of concentration risk and income stability, creating an evidentiary record of customer-specific analysis. (5) The broker has included a diversification plan tied to vesting, showing ongoing suitability management. While concentration is ordinarily concerning, FINRA and SEC enforcement recognize that highly knowledgeable clients with stable non-investment income and explicit concentration preference may suitability hold concentrated positions. The broker's documentation and risk acknowledgment support compliance.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a)(2) (customer-specific suitability), considering client's unique circumstances, expertise, and explicit direction; SEC guidance on concentrated positions (In re Merrill Lynch, SEC Admin. Proc., 2015)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Client's professional expertise, stable diversified income ($300K/year salary + bonus), explicit concentration preference, and broker's documented risk acknowledgment + diversification plan overcome the apparent concentration concern.
