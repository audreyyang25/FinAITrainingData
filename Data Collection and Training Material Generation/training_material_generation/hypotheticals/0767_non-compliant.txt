Source Folder: 14_NASAA_State_Enforcement
Topic: state_fraud
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Kuber Wealth Partners, LLC, a state-registered investment adviser in Georgia with CRD No. 310882, managed approximately $28 million in assets for 74 clients. The firm's principal, Raj Anand, maintained custody of six clients' funds by having them sign limited powers of attorney granting the firm authority to withdraw advisory fees directly from their brokerage accounts. However, Anand never notified the state administrator of the firm's custody status on Form ADV, did not ensure the clients' funds were held by a qualified custodian in properly segregated accounts, and failed to arrange for an annual surprise examination. One client, a 72-year-old retiree, discovered $14,500 in unauthorized fee withdrawals over eight months.

QUESTION
--------
Did Kuber Wealth Partners violate NASAA Model Custody Rule 102(e)(1)-1 by failing to meet custody safekeeping requirements?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes multiple violations of the NASAA Model Custody Rule, including failure to notify the administrator, failure to use a qualified custodian with proper account segregation, and failure to arrange a surprise examination.

Under NASAA Model Rule 102(e)(1)-1(a), it is deemed a fraudulent, deceptive, or manipulative act for a registered investment adviser to have custody of client funds unless the adviser promptly notifies the administrator in writing via Form ADV, maintains funds with a qualified custodian in separate accounts under each client's name or in properly designated omnibus accounts, and complies with the surprise examination requirement. Kuber Wealth Partners failed on all three prongs. The unauthorized fee withdrawals from the retiree's account further evidence the risks the custody rule is designed to prevent.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
NASAA Model Rule 102(e)(1)-1 – Custody of Client Funds or Securities by Investment Advisers (Sections (a)(1), (a)(2), and surprise examination requirements)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm held custody of client funds without notifying the state administrator, without using a qualified custodian with proper account segregation, and without undergoing an annual surprise examination.
