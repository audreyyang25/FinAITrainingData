Source Folder: 18_OCC_Bank_Trust_Guidance
Topic: bank_fiduciary
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Great Lakes National Bank operates a national fiduciary program, consistent with OCC Interpretive Letter #995, marketing trust services across states where it maintains only trust representative offices rather than full-service branches. The bank accepts appointment as successor trustee for a $9.1 million special needs trust (SNT) established under state law for a 34-year-old beneficiary with severe developmental disabilities who relies on Medicaid and SSI benefits. Over 18 months, the bank's trust officer makes 23 discretionary distributions totaling $318,000 for the beneficiary's 'general support,' including rent, groceries, and utility payments — expenditures that are countable resources under Medicaid rules — without consulting a benefits planning specialist or reviewing the SNT's governing document, which expressly prohibits distributions for expenses covered by public benefits. The trust officer also fails to file required annual accountings with the probate court having jurisdiction over the SNT, despite two written requests from the court.

QUESTION
--------
Does the bank trustee's pattern of non-conforming distributions from the special needs trust and failure to file court-required accountings constitute a breach of fiduciary duty?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a breach of fiduciary duty through violation of the trust's governing document, failure to administer the trust in accordance with applicable state law and court orders, and jeopardizing the beneficiary's public benefits eligibility.

OTS Trust Handbook Section 110 identifies compliance with the provisions of the governing trust document as among the foremost fiduciary obligations, and the bank's 23 distributions for Medicaid-countable expenses directly contravened the SNT's express prohibition — exposing the beneficiary to potential loss of Medicaid and SSI eligibility and constituting a breach of the duty to administer the trust according to its terms. The OCC Asset Management Handbook requires trustees to act in accordance with state and federal statutory law, and failure to file court-ordered accountings violates the court's jurisdiction over the SNT administration as contemplated by OTS Section 110. The bank's failure to consult a benefits planning specialist before making distributions from a specialized public-benefits-preservation trust reflects a failure of the prudent administration standard applicable to corporate trustees held to a higher standard than lay trustees. Taken together, these acts and omissions constitute a systemic breach of the duty of care and the duty to follow the trust's governing instrument.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
OTS Trust Handbook Section 110 – Fiduciary Duties (duty to administer per governing instrument, applicable state law, and court orders); OCC Comptroller's Handbook – Asset Management (2023), Fiduciary Services; 12 USC 92a; Common Law Duty of Loyalty and Duty to Follow Trust Terms

KEY FACTOR
----------
The bank trustee made 23 distributions expressly prohibited by the SNT governing document and ignored court accounting requirements, directly threatening the disabled beneficiary's Medicaid eligibility and breaching core OCC fiduciary duties.
