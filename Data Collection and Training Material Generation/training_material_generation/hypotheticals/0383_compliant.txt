FACTS
-----
Jennifer, age 51, is employed at TechCorp and holds a 401(k) with $625,000 in a mix of mutual funds. The plan offers 18 investment options with average expense ratio of 0.62%. She is considering a rollover to an IRA managed by her adviser, Rachel, at 0.85% AUM. Rachel prepares a detailed written analysis showing: (1) Fee comparison: 0.62% plan average vs. 0.85% IRA fee (net 0.23% higher, approximately $1,438 annually); (2) Identified plan advantages: access to institutional share classes, employer match eligibility (Jennifer still employed), creditor protection under ERISA; (3) IRA advantages: broader investment universe (2,500+ funds vs. 18), lower ongoing taxes if Roth conversions planned, death beneficiary naming flexibility; (4) Conclusion: 'Recommendation is NOT to rollover at this time. Client is better served leaving assets in plan due to ongoing employer match eligibility ($12,500 annually) and creditor protection. If circumstances change (separation of service), review recommendation.' Rachel documents that Jennifer confirmed understanding and elected to not rollover. Compensation is transparent (0.85% disclosed in advisory contract).

QUESTION
--------
Did Rachel's analysis and recommendation fully comply with PTE 2020-02 requirements?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Rachel satisfied all five PTE 2020-02 requirements: (1) Provided comprehensive best-interest analysis comparing fees, plan features, and tax implications; (2) Identified material plan advantages (employer match, creditor protection) that favored non-rollover; (3) Documented analysis in writing with specific dollar amounts; (4) Disclosed her compensation (0.85% fee) and identified the 0.23% net cost difference; (5) Reached conclusion against rollover despite personal financial incentive to recommend IRA management. Rachel's recommendation prioritized Jennifer's interest over generating advisory fee revenue. Her analysis demonstrates the required fiduciary care, includes quantified fee comparison, addresses relevant non-fee factors (match, protection), and is supported by contemporaneous documentation. This exemplifies compliant PTE 2020-02 practice.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
PTE 2020-02 Five-Part Test; DOL ERISA §408(b)(14); Best-Interest Exemption

KEY FACTOR
----------
Comprehensive documented analysis identifying plan advantages, transparent fee disclosure, and conclusion against rollover despite adviser's financial incentive demonstrates best-interest compliance.
