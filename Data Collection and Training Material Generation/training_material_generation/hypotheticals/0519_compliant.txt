ID: 519
Source Folder: 02_SEC_Enforcement_Actions
Topic: sec_enforcement_ia
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Crestview Partners Advisory, LLC, a registered investment adviser managing $620 million through three private equity funds, negotiated arrangements under which certain fund portfolio companies engaged a technology services firm in which Crestview's CEO held a 12% equity interest. Before implementing these arrangements, Crestview's CCO conducted a detailed conflict-of-interest review and prepared a written analysis documenting the CEO's interest, the arms-length nature of the pricing (benchmarked against three independent proposals), and the rationale for selecting the firm. Crestview updated its Form ADV Part 2A to specifically describe the CEO's equity interest in the technology services firm and the potential for fund portfolio companies to engage the firm. Additionally, Crestview's fund limited partnership agreements contained provisions explicitly authorizing the adviser to cause portfolio companies to engage affiliated service providers, subject to pricing being at or below market rates, and the fund advisory committee—composed of independent limited partners—was briefed on each engagement and provided written consent. Over two years, the technology services firm received $1.9 million in fees, all at rates confirmed to be at or below competitive market pricing.

QUESTION
--------
Did Crestview Partners Advisory comply with the Advisers Act despite its CEO holding a personal equity interest in a service provider engaged by fund portfolio companies?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

While the CEO's equity interest in the technology services firm creates a material conflict of interest, Crestview took comprehensive steps to satisfy its fiduciary obligations. The firm conducted a documented conflict analysis, updated its Form ADV with specific disclosure of the interest, ensured fund governing documents explicitly authorized such arrangements subject to market-rate pricing, and obtained informed consent from an independent advisory committee of limited partners. The pricing was benchmarked against independent proposals and confirmed to be at or below market rates. This stands in direct contrast to cases like First Reserve (IA-4529), where the failure lay not in the existence of the conflict itself but in the inadequacy of disclosure. Here, disclosure was specific, timely, and accompanied by meaningful informed consent mechanisms.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Sections 206(1) and 206(2) – Fiduciary duty of loyalty and full and fair disclosure of material conflicts of interest

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser's specific, documented disclosure of the conflict in Form ADV and fund documents, combined with arms-length pricing verification and independent advisory committee consent, satisfied the fiduciary duty of loyalty despite the existence of a material personal interest.
