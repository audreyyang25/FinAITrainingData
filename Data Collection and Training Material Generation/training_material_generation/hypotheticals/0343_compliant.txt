FACTS
-----
Registered representative Edward S. recommended to 45-year-old corporate executive Thomas B. (age 45, $520,000 investable assets, stated objective 'long-term growth with 10+ year horizon,' moderate-to-aggressive risk tolerance) a mutual fund switch from Fidelity Small Cap Growth A-share (held 5 years, 5.75% load already sunk, 0.71% expense ratio, YTD return –8.3%) to Vanguard Small Cap Index Fund (no load, 0.08% expense ratio, YTD return –7.9%). Edward prepared a written switching analysis showing: (1) cost differential: $29,900 load avoided over 10-year period assuming 7% annual return; (2) expense ratio savings: 0.63% annually ($3,276 first year on $520K); (3) comparable or superior performance metrics. Edward obtained signed acknowledgment from Thomas that the prior load was sunk and not recoverable, and documented that Thomas agreed the index approach aligned with his long-term buy-and-hold strategy. No additional transactions were recommended for 18 months.

QUESTION
--------
Did Edward S. comply with FINRA Rule 2111 and best-execution principles in recommending the fund switch?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Edward complied with Rule 2111 suitability standards. The recommendation was suitable because: (1) the new fund aligned with Thomas's stated long-term (10+ year) objectives and risk tolerance; (2) the switch avoided a substantial load ($29,900 over 10 years) and reduced annual costs by 63 basis points, materially benefiting the client; (3) Edward documented the analysis in writing and obtained informed consent, showing Thomas understood the prior load was unrecoverable; and (4) the subsequent inactive period (18 months without follow-up recommendations) demonstrated absence of churning motive. The prior fund's poor performance (–8.3% YTD) in a growth allocation further supported the switch. This pattern—load-free index alternatives, written analysis, documented consent, and client benefit—reflects compliant switching under FINRA precedent. The lack of commission incentive and transparent cost disclosure strengthened compliance.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a)–(b) (reasonable basis and customer-specific suitability)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommendation was supported by written switching analysis demonstrating substantial client benefit (load and expense ratio reduction), documented consent, and absence of compensation incentive, satisfying suitability and fair-dealing obligations.
