Topic: reg_bi_care
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 58-year-old retail customer with $250,000 in liquid savings and a moderate risk tolerance (stated preference for 60/40 stocks/bonds) was recommended by his broker-dealer to invest $180,000 in a structured note with 6% upfront sales credit (paid to the firm), 2.5% annual mortality and expense charges, and principal protection only if held to maturity in 7 years. The broker did not discuss or present a comparable low-cost index fund portfolio (0.10% expense ratio, no surrender period) that would have provided similar diversification with identical bond exposure and lower equity volatility.

QUESTION
--------
Does the recommendation to purchase the structured note satisfy the Reg BI care obligation?

ANSWER
------
No, this recommendation violates the Reg BI care obligation. The broker failed to consider and disclose lower-cost alternatives that would better serve the customer's stated moderate risk tolerance and retirement timeline.

While the structured note may have been 'suitable' under traditional suitability standards, Reg BI's heightened care obligation requires the broker to prioritize the customer's best interest by identifying and discussing lower-cost alternatives before recommending a product with significant embedded conflicts (6% upfront commission and 2.5% annual fees). The customer's moderate risk tolerance and 7-year timeline would be equally well-served by a diversified 60/40 index portfolio with 0.10% expenses, no surrender restrictions, and daily liquidity. The broker's failure to conduct a comparative analysis of costs and alternatives, and to explain why the structured note was preferable despite its higher fees, violates the affirmative care obligation under § 240.15l-1(a)(2)(i). The 6% commission created a substantial conflict of interest that was not mitigated by transparent disclosure of alternatives.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Regulation Best Interest § 240.15l-1(a)(2)(i) (broker-dealer must act in retail customer's best interest when making a recommendation, considering compensation, alternatives, and costs); also FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability) and FINRA AWC In the Matter of Morgan Stanley & Co. (2020) (enforcement for recommending complex, high-fee products to unsuitable customers without presenting lower-cost alternatives).

KEY FACTOR
----------
The broker recommended a significantly higher-cost product without analyzing or disclosing a lower-cost alternative that would satisfy the customer's stated risk profile and retirement objectives, creating an undisclosed conflict of interest that violated the Reg BI care obligation to prioritize the customer's best interest.
