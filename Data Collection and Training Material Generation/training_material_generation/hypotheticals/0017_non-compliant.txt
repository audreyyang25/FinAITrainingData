Source Folder: unknown
Topic: reg_bi_care
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 55-year-old customer nearing retirement with $600,000 in investable assets and conservative risk tolerance meets with a broker. The customer states that capital preservation and stable income are primary objectives. The broker recommends a portfolio consisting of $400,000 (67%) in actively managed bond mutual funds with 1.25% expense ratios and 2% front-end loads, and $200,000 (33%) in fixed annuities with 3.5% surrender charges and implicit annual costs of 1.10%. The broker documents that the customer's risk tolerance is 'conservative' and objectives are 'income and preservation.' However, the broker does not compare costs to alternative bond investments (bond index funds with 0.10-0.15% expense ratios, Treasury bonds, bond ladder strategies), does not justify why active management is necessary for bond exposure, and does not document the rationale for the 1.25% + 2% load cost structure versus alternatives. The broker's compensation is higher on the annuity sale (5% commission) than on mutual fund Class A shares (approximately 4%).

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the Reg BI care obligation to consider costs and alternatives?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the Reg BI care obligation.

The recommendation fails the Reg BI care obligation because the advisor selected high-cost bond products without documented analysis of materially lower-cost alternatives. For a 55-year-old conservative investor seeking stable income, bond index funds (0.10-0.15% expense ratio) or individual Treasury bonds would deliver equivalent interest income with costs 1.10-1.40% lower than the recommended actively managed bond funds and annuities. The advisor's compensation structure (5% on annuities vs. 4% on mutual funds) creates potential conflict, yet the advisor did not document any analysis of whether active management or annuitization added sufficient value to justify these cost differences. The absence of documented comparison between recommended products and index-based or direct bond alternatives (which would be especially suitable for a customer focused on income preservation rather than growth) demonstrates failure to consider costs and alternatives as required by Reg BI. A conservative investor's income needs are typically well-served by low-cost, transparent bond alternatives; the advisor's failure to analyze or discuss these represents a material breach.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Regulation Best Interest, Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i): broker-dealer must act in the retail customer's best interest, including consideration of costs, risks, rewards, and alternative investments available to the customer.

KEY FACTOR
----------
No documented analysis or discussion of materially lower-cost bond alternatives (index funds at 0.10-0.15%, Treasury bonds) for a conservative customer seeking income preservation, despite cost differential exceeding 1% annually and compensation incentive favoring higher-cost products.
