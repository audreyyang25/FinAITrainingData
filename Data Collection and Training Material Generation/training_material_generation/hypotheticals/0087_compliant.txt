FACTS
-----
A FINRA broker recommends a 45-year-old software engineer with $400,000 in liquid assets, 25-year time horizon, stated aggressive risk tolerance, and no need for current income, to allocate 60% to a diversified portfolio of large-cap growth ETFs (0.08% ER) and 40% to international developed-market index funds (0.12% ER). The broker documents the customer's profile, discusses volatility risk, confirms understanding of downside scenarios, and obtains written acknowledgment. The customer has stable $180,000 annual income and minimal debt.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy FINRA Rule 2111 suitability?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation is compliant with FINRA Rule 2111 suitability requirements.

The broker satisfies all three prongs of Rule 2111 suitability. (1) Reasonable basis: the broker recommends low-cost, broadly diversified equity index funds appropriate for long-term growth, a recommendation supported by standard portfolio theory. (2) Customer-specific: the recommendation is tailored to a 45-year-old with 25-year horizon, stated aggressive tolerance, stable high income, and no near-term income needs—all factors supporting growth-oriented allocation. (3) Quantitative suitability: the broker documents no pattern of excessive trading or churning, and the recommendation is buy-and-hold oriented with low turnover. The documented customer profile and written acknowledgment constitute evidence of customer-specific analysis required under Rule 2111(b).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a): broker must have reasonable basis to believe recommendation is suitable based on customer's investment profile (age, risk tolerance, investment objectives, financial situation, needs).

KEY FACTOR
----------
All three Rule 2111 suitability prongs satisfied: reasonable basis for growth recommendation, customer-specific profile (age, horizon, income, risk tolerance), and no quantitative trading violations.
