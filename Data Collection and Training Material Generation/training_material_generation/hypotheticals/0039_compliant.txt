Topic: reg_bi_disclosure

Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer firm sends each new client a Form CRS within three business days of account opening that clearly identifies the firm as a broker-dealer (not an investment adviser), states that brokerage accounts operate on a transaction-by-transaction basis with commissions on trades, discloses the typical range of commissions (0.5% to 2.0% depending on asset type), and explains that the firm earns additional revenue when it sells proprietary mutual funds. The Form CRS is written in plain English at a 7th-grade reading level and includes a specific example showing how a $10,000 stock trade would generate a $50–$200 commission.

QUESTION
--------
Does this Form CRS satisfy Reg BI's initial disclosure requirements?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this is compliant.

The Form CRS clearly and concisely satisfies Reg BI's disclosure obligations by (1) identifying the firm as a broker-dealer upfront, (2) explaining the transactional fee model, (3) providing a specific, realistic fee range, (4) disclosing the firm's revenue from proprietary products, and (5) using plain language with a concrete example. The 7th-grade reading level and dollar example allow the client to immediately understand the economic relationship. The timing of delivery (within three business days) and the proactive nature of the disclosure demonstrate good-faith compliance. This approach aligns with the SEC's intent for Reg BI to enable retail investors to understand the material facts about their broker's relationship and compensation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Regulation Best Interest (17 CFR 240.15l-1), Form CRS, Item 1 (description of services and relationships) and Item 2 (fees and compensation), and SEC guidance on plain-language disclosure.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Early, clear, plain-language written disclosure with a specific example showing the actual fee impact on a typical transaction.
