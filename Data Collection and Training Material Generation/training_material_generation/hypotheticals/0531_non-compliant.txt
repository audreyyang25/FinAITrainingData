Source Folder: 03_FINRA_AWC
Topic: finra_awc_churning
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Marcus Webb, a General Securities Representative registered with a mid-size broker-dealer, managed accounts for three senior customers aged 72, 76, and 81, each with balanced allocation investment objectives and moderate risk tolerances. Between January 2021 and March 2023, Webb executed over 340 trades across these three accounts, generating approximately $187,000 in commissions. The annualized cost-to-equity ratios for the accounts ranged from 14% to 22%, and the turnover rates ranged from 6.1 to 9.4. The customers, all retirees living on fixed incomes with account values between $180,000 and $350,000, did not request the frequency of trading and relied on Webb's recommendations. Webb's firm terminated him after an internal review and filed a Form U5 stating he was 'unable to provide sufficient documentary evidence to support his contention that he had authority for all trades in a client's account.'

QUESTION
--------
Did Webb's pattern of frequent trading in senior customers' balanced-allocation accounts comply with FINRA suitability and best interest standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is excessive trading (churning) in violation of FINRA Rules 2111 and 2010, and Regulation Best Interest's Care Obligation.

FINRA Rule 2111's quantitative suitability component prohibits excessive trading in a customer's account. Cost-to-equity ratios above 20% and turnover rates above 6 are widely recognized as presumptive indicators of churning. These elderly customers with balanced investment objectives and moderate risk tolerances had no need for the level of active trading Webb conducted, and the trading primarily served to generate commissions for Webb rather than to benefit the customers. Post-June 2020, Reg BI's Care Obligation additionally requires that the representative not place his financial interest ahead of the retail customer's interest.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability) – Quantitative Suitability; FINRA Rule 2010 (Standards of Commercial Honor); SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation

KEY FACTOR
----------
Annualized cost-to-equity ratios of 14%-22% and turnover rates of 6.1-9.4 in balanced-allocation senior accounts are strong quantitative indicators of excessive trading (churning).
