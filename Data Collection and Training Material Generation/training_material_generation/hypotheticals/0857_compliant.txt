Source Folder: 17_SEC_Staff_Bulletins
Topic: care_obligation_detail
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Patricia Hsu, a registered representative at Meridian Wealth Partners, recommends that her 45-year-old retail client, Dr. James Okafor, purchase $150,000 of a structured note tied to the S&P 500 with a 15% downside buffer and a 3-year term, even though a comparable low-cost S&P 500 index fund is available at significantly lower cost. Hsu documents in the client file that Dr. Okafor has expressed explicit concern about sequence-of-returns risk due to an anticipated partial retirement in three years, that he has a $900,000 overall investment portfolio with substantial liquid reserves, and that the downside buffer addresses his specific risk tolerance more precisely than the index fund alternative would. Hsu's written recommendation rationale notes she reviewed at least three alternative products, compared total costs including the 1.8% embedded fee on the structured note against the client's stated need for capital protection, and concludes the structured note's defined outcome profile is in Dr. Okafor's best interest given his specific three-year liquidity horizon and documented risk aversion. The firm's supervisory review confirms this documentation was completed prior to the recommendation being made.

QUESTION
--------
Does Hsu's recommendation of a higher-cost structured note over a lower-cost index fund satisfy Reg BI's Care Obligation when the client has a specific, documented investment need that the structured product addresses?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Reg BI's Care Obligation requires that a recommendation be in the retail customer's best interest based on that customer's investment profile, and does not categorically prohibit recommending higher-cost products where a documented, specific investment need justifies the additional cost. Hsu performed a reasonable basis inquiry by evaluating multiple alternatives, compared costs explicitly, and matched the structured note's defined-outcome and downside-buffer features to Dr. Okafor's documented sequence-of-returns concern and three-year liquidity horizon. The SEC has clarified that cost is an important but not dispositive factor, and that a more expensive product may be in the customer's best interest if it provides features specifically tailored to the customer's investment profile that lower-cost alternatives cannot replicate. The pre-recommendation documentation and supervisory review further demonstrate the firm's procedural compliance with the Care Obligation's requirements.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Staff Bulletin on Care Obligation (April 2023)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Thorough pre-recommendation documentation tying the structured note's specific downside-buffer feature to the client's explicitly stated and documented investment objective legitimately justifies the higher cost, satisfying the Care Obligation's requirement to act in the customer's best interest based on their individualized investment profile.
