Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Greenfield Financial Group, a registered broker-dealer, had a registered representative, Thomas Alcott, who failed to timely file two required disclosure amendments to his Form U4 following a 2019 tax lien of $48,000 and a 2020 civil judgment of $31,500, both of which FINRA Rule 1122 required to be reported within 30 days. Upon discovering the omissions during a routine annual compliance review, the firm immediately self-reported to FINRA, filed amended Form U4s within 48 hours, placed Alcott on heightened supervision for six months, required him to complete an additional 12 hours of compliance training, and retained an outside compliance consultant to audit the firm's disclosure reporting procedures. Greenfield's CCO documented all remedial steps and provided a detailed written narrative to FINRA's examination staff.

QUESTION
--------
Does Greenfield Financial Group's prompt self-reporting, immediate remediation, and enhanced supervisory response following discovery of a registered representative's Form U4 disclosure failures satisfy the firm's supervisory obligations and support a finding of adequate compliance infrastructure?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

FINRA Rule 3110 requires broker-dealers to establish and maintain supervisory systems reasonably designed to achieve compliance, but does not impose strict liability for every representative's disclosure failure where the firm demonstrates adequate detection and remediation mechanisms. SEC ALJ decisions, including Horter, consistently recognize that prompt self-reporting, immediate corrective action, and engagement of independent compliance resources are significant mitigating factors that can defeat a finding of systemic supervisory failure. Here, Greenfield's annual review process functioned as designed, and the 48-hour turnaround for amended filings, combined with heightened supervision and third-party audit, demonstrates a functioning compliance infrastructure rather than willful disregard.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 1122 (Filing of Misleading Information); FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision); Exchange Act §15(b)(4)(E) — mitigating factors as recognized in SEC ALJ sanction determinations including Initial Decision Release No. 1414

KEY FACTOR
----------
Prompt self-disclosure to FINRA, immediate Form U4 amendments, and documented enhanced supervision and third-party audit constitute the hallmarks of a compliant supervisory response that ALJs treat as dispositive mitigation against systemic supervision charges.
