Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Harborview Private Equity Advisers, an SEC-registered investment adviser, manages three co-investment vehicles alongside its flagship $2.1 billion private equity fund. When a $45 million acquisition target fell through in due diligence, Harborview incurred $1.8 million in broken deal expenses including legal fees, accounting diligence costs, and travel. Harborview's LPA for the flagship fund and each co-investment vehicle contained specific, detailed expense allocation provisions requiring broken deal expenses to be allocated pro rata based on committed capital across all participating vehicles. Harborview's compliance team documented the allocation methodology in an internal memorandum, allocated $1.35 million to the flagship fund and $450,000 across the three co-investment vehicles in exact proportion to committed capital, and disclosed the allocation and methodology in the fund's quarterly investor letter. The firm's chief compliance officer reviewed the allocation against the SEC's 2015 enforcement guidance stemming from the KKR broken deal expense case.

QUESTION
--------
Did Harborview Private Equity Advisers comply with its fiduciary duty in allocating broken deal expenses across its funds and co-investment vehicles?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The SEC's 2015 KKR enforcement action, in which KKR paid nearly $30 million for misallocating more than $17 million in broken deal expenses to its flagship fund, established that private equity firms must maintain detailed expense allocation policies and disclose them transparently. Harborview followed industry best practices by maintaining explicit LPA provisions governing pro rata allocation based on committed capital, documenting the methodology internally, applying the formula consistently across all participating vehicles, and disclosing the allocation to investors. This approach directly addresses the SEC's concerns about conflicts of interest in expense shifting between funds and accounts.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §§ 206(1)-(2) – Fiduciary Duty; SEC Enforcement Action In re Kohlberg Kravis Roberts & Co. (June 29, 2015)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Harborview's detailed LPA allocation provisions, consistent pro rata application, internal documentation, and transparent investor disclosure directly addressed the SEC's concerns identified in the KKR broken deal expense enforcement action.
