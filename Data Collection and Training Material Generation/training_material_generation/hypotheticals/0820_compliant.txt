Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A mid-size RIA managing $310 million in retail AUM identified through its annual conflict review that two of its portfolio managers held personal positions in small-cap equity securities that were also held in 38 client accounts, creating a potential front-running conflict. The firm implemented a 30-day trading blackout for personal accounts around client trades, required pre-clearance of all personal securities transactions in covered securities, reported the conflict in its Form ADV Part 2A with specific identification of the affected security categories, verbally disclosed the policy to all affected clients during annual reviews, and maintained a compliance log reviewed quarterly by its CCO. GAO reports on SEC examination findings noted that firms maintaining written supervisory procedures with documented pre-clearance requirements and regular CCO review represented the compliance standard the SEC expected following Reg BI and adviser conduct rulemaking.

QUESTION
--------
Does the RIA's implementation of pre-clearance requirements, blackout periods, written ADV disclosure, and documented CCO oversight adequately mitigate the portfolio manager personal trading conflict under the investment adviser fiduciary standard?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The adviser fiduciary duty requires conflicts to be either eliminated or fully disclosed and mitigated through structural safeguards. GAO examination findings identified pre-clearance, blackout periods, and CCO oversight as the operational hallmarks of effective conflict management programs. Here, the firm's layered approach—pre-clearance, 30-day blackouts, ADV disclosure, oral client notification, and quarterly CCO review—addresses each vector through which the personal trading conflict could harm clients. The combination of prevention, disclosure, and independent oversight satisfies both the §204A code of ethics requirements and the broader fiduciary obligation articulated in the adviser conduct interpretation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §204A; SEC Rule 17j-1 (Code of Ethics Rule); SEC Interpretation Regarding Standard of Conduct for Investment Advisers (Release No. IA-5248) – Conflict of Interest Mitigation.

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's layered conflict mitigation program combining pre-clearance, blackout periods, written ADV disclosure, and documented CCO oversight meets the GAO-identified best-practice standard for managing personal trading conflicts under the investment adviser fiduciary duty.
