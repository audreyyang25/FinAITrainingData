ID: 866
Source Folder: 18_OCC_Bank_Trust_Guidance
Topic: trust_investment_management
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Granite Ridge Bank & Trust administers a $4.8 million testamentary trust for the benefit of Marcus Delacroix, age 34, a special needs beneficiary receiving Supplemental Security Income (SSI) and Medicaid, with a corporate co-trustee relationship established under the decedent's will. The bank's trust officer, seeking to boost yield amid low interest rates, invests $1.9 million (approximately 40% of trust assets) in a non-rated private placement note issued by a regional real estate developer, citing the instrument's 8.5% annual coupon. No independent credit analysis was performed, the investment policy statement on file contemplates conservative, diversified fixed-income holdings, and the trust officer failed to convene the required trust investment committee review prior to execution. The investment subsequently defaulted 14 months later, resulting in a $600,000 principal loss.

QUESTION
--------
Did the bank trust officer's decision to invest 40% of a special needs trust in a non-rated private placement note without investment committee review or independent credit analysis comply with OCC fiduciary investment standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a breach of the prudent investor duty and failure of required supervisory oversight under OCC fiduciary standards.

OCC asset management guidance requires that fiduciary investments be made consistent with the trust's governing document, the beneficiary's specific needs, and a documented prudent investor analysis considering risk, diversification, and liquidity. Investing 40% of a special needs trust—where preservation of capital is paramount to maintain the beneficiary's government benefit eligibility—in a non-rated speculative instrument directly contravenes the trust's conservative investment policy statement. The failure to conduct independent credit analysis and bypass the mandatory investment committee review also reflects a breakdown in the internal controls framework required under OCC Asset Management Operations and Controls guidance.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
OCC Comptroller's Handbook – Asset Management Operations and Controls (AM); OCC Comptroller's Handbook – Asset Management, Version 1.0 (June 2023); Uniform Prudent Investor Act; 12 CFR 9.11

KEY FACTOR
----------
The trust officer bypassed mandatory investment committee oversight and prudent investor analysis when concentrating 40% of a special needs trust in a speculative, non-rated private placement inconsistent with the trust's documented conservative mandate.
