Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer representative recommends that a recently retired 67-year-old client with $320,000 in liquid savings invest $250,000 (78% of liquid assets) into a non-traded REIT with a 10-year hold period and 12% upfront load. The client's stated investment objective is income with capital preservation, and she depends on her savings plus Social Security for all living expenses. The representative earns a $30,000 commission on the sale. The firm's product shelf includes investment-grade bond funds, dividend equity ETFs, and Treasury products, none of which are discussed or documented as alternatives.

QUESTION
--------
Is the recommendation of a highly illiquid, high-cost non-traded REIT to a retiree dependent on her savings for living expenses compliant with Reg BI and FINRA suitability standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates both the Reg BI Care Obligation and FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability).

Concentrating 78% of a retiree's liquid assets in a single illiquid product with a 10-year lock-up and 12% front-end load is inconsistent with the client's stated income and capital preservation objectives. The Reg BI Care Obligation requires the representative to consider the client's investment profile, including liquidity needs, when making a recommendation. FINRA Rule 2111's customer-specific obligation similarly prohibits recommendations that are unsuitable given the client's financial situation. Empirical research on investor harm consistently demonstrates that retirees suffer disproportionate losses from illiquid, high-fee products when liquidity events arise.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability – Customer-Specific Obligation)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The concentration of a retirement-dependent client's liquid assets in an illiquid, high-load product directly contradicts her stated income and preservation needs.
