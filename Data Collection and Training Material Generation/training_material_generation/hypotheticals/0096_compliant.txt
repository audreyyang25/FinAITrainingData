FACTS
-----
A broker recommends a diversified portfolio of index mutual funds (S&P 500 index, Total Bond Market index, International Developed Markets index with combined expense ratio of 0.12%) to a 35-year-old software engineer with $300,000 in a rollover IRA, 30-year time horizon to retirement, above-average risk tolerance, and stated goal of 'long-term wealth accumulation.' The broker documents a written investment profile and communicates that the portfolio will rebalance annually. No commissions are charged on the index funds.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy FINRA Suitability Rule 2111?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation satisfies FINRA Suitability Rule 2111.

The recommendation clearly satisfies all three suitability prongs. (1) Reasonable basis: diversified index fund portfolios are among the most well-documented, SEC-regulated investment strategies; their risk/return characteristics are published and transparent; index investing as a strategy has decades of peer-reviewed support. (2) Customer-specific: the recommendation is tailored precisely to this client's circumstances—a young professional with a 30-year horizon can tolerate equity market volatility; above-average risk tolerance supports a growth-oriented allocation; long-term wealth accumulation goal aligns perfectly with diversified equity/bond indexing. (3) Quantitative: expense ratios of 0.12% combined are exceptionally low (well below industry average of 0.50-0.75% for active management); annual rebalancing creates minimal turnover; no commissions or loads are charged. Documentation evidences customer profiling. This is a straightforward, compliant recommendation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a)(1) (reasonable basis), Rule 2111(a)(2) (customer-specific suitability), and quantitative suitability (low turnover, minimal costs)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Perfect alignment between client age/time horizon (35, 30 years), risk tolerance, investment objective, and low-cost diversified index strategy with minimal turnover.
