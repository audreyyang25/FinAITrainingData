Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Apex Capital Advisors serves as investment adviser to both Apex BDC, Inc., a publicly traded business development company, and Apex Private Partners LP, a private fund organized as a limited partnership. Apex Capital controls both vehicles. Without seeking any board approval, Apex BDC co-invests $12 million alongside Apex Private Partners LP in a mezzanine loan to a mid-market software company. The co-investment is arranged on identical terms. One of the limited partners of Apex Private Partners LP owns 7% of Apex BDC's outstanding voting securities, making that limited partner an affiliated person of the BDC and thus the Private Fund a second-tier affiliate through the common adviser relationship. Apex Capital's compliance team classifies this limited partner as a 'remote affiliate' of the BDC and does not present the co-investment transaction to the BDC's board of directors for approval.

QUESTION
--------
Did Apex Capital comply with the Investment Company Act of 1940's restrictions on BDC co-investment transactions with second-tier affiliates by classifying the limited partner as a remote affiliate and bypassing board approval?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the Investment Company Act of 1940's restrictions on transactions between BDCs and affiliated persons.

Under IM Guidance Update 2014-12, a second-tier affiliate's classification as a 'close affiliate' versus a 'remote affiliate' depends on various factors, and the determination cannot be made reflexively. A limited partner that holds 7% of the BDC's outstanding voting securities is an affiliated person of the BDC and likely qualifies as a close affiliate, meaning the co-investment transaction may be prohibited outright rather than merely subject to board approval. By failing to conduct a proper analysis and bypassing the required majority board approval process entirely, Apex Capital violated the 1940 Act's affiliate transaction restrictions designed to protect the BDC from undue influence and overreaching.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Company Act of 1940, Sections 57(a) and 57(f); IM Guidance Update No. 2014-12 (December 2014) – BDC Transactions with Second-Tier Affiliates

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser improperly classified a second-tier affiliate with significant BDC ownership as a 'remote affiliate,' bypassing both the required affiliate analysis and the mandatory board approval process for co-investment transactions.
