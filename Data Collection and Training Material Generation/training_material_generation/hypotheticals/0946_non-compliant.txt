Topic: discovery_abuse_document_withholding
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
During FINRA arbitration proceedings initiated by investor Robert Castellano against broker-dealer Apex Financial Group for churning and unauthorized trading, Apex's legal team received a discovery request for all internal supervisory review records, exception reports, and branch manager communications concerning Castellano's account over a three-year period. Apex produced only a curated subset of records and represented to the arbitration panel that no additional responsive documents existed. Internal emails later revealed through a whistleblower that Apex's compliance department had generated 47 exception reports flagging excessive trading in Castellano's account but had withheld them, directing staff to classify the documents as attorney-client privileged despite the reports being prepared in the ordinary course of supervisory business.

QUESTION
--------
Is a respondent broker-dealer's deliberate withholding of supervisory exception reports by misclassifying them as attorney-client privileged compliant with FINRA arbitration discovery obligations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure Rule 12513, discovery obligations, and constitutes discovery abuse that may warrant sanctions including adverse inference instructions or case dismissal.

FINRA arbitration rules impose an affirmative obligation on parties to produce all documents responsive to proper discovery requests, and routine supervisory exception reports generated in the ordinary course of compliance business do not qualify for attorney-client privilege merely because a party retroactively labels them as such. The misclassification of these documents constitutes a deliberate misrepresentation to the arbitration panel, violating Rule 12513's good faith discovery obligations and potentially Rule 12212 governing sanctions for misconduct. PIABA has specifically documented this pattern of discovery abuse in broker-dealer arbitration defense strategies, and arbitrators have authority under FINRA rules to impose case-dispositive sanctions for willful withholding of material documents.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure Rules 12505, 12513; FINRA Regulatory Notice 14-40; PIABA advocacy on discovery abuses in arbitration

KEY FACTOR
----------
Supervisory exception reports prepared in the ordinary course of compliance business are not attorney-client privileged, and deliberate misclassification to evade discovery constitutes sanctionable discovery abuse under FINRA arbitration procedure.
