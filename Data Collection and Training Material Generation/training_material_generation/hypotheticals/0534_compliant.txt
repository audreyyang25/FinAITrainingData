Source Folder: 03_FINRA_AWC
Topic: finra_awc_supervision
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Cornerstone Wealth Advisors, a FINRA member firm with 45 registered representatives, conducted a comprehensive review of its supervisory and compliance framework in early 2020 in anticipation of the June 30, 2020 Reg BI compliance date. The firm updated its written supervisory procedures to incorporate the four Reg BI component obligations (Care, Disclosure, Conflict of Interest, and Compliance). It developed and delivered Form CRS to all existing retail customers by the deadline and implemented a process to deliver it to prospective customers before or at the time of a recommendation. The firm established a conflict-of-interest inventory identifying compensation structures, revenue-sharing arrangements, and proprietary product incentives, and adopted mitigation measures including eliminating sales contests and reducing differential compensation on proprietary products. It conducted training for all associated persons and documented completion. During a subsequent FINRA examination, the examiner noted that one newly hired representative had inadvertently failed to deliver Form CRS to two retail customers during his first week, but the firm's supervisory review caught the error within five business days and the forms were promptly delivered with a follow-up notation in the compliance system.

QUESTION
--------
Did Cornerstone's Reg BI compliance framework and its handling of the isolated Form CRS delivery lapse satisfy its supervisory obligations?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

A firm's supervisory obligation under FINRA Rule 3110 and Reg BI's Compliance Obligation does not require perfection but rather a system reasonably designed to achieve compliance. Cornerstone established comprehensive written procedures, delivered Form CRS, built a conflict-of-interest inventory with mitigation measures, and trained all personnel. The isolated lapse by a new representative was detected and corrected promptly by the firm's existing supervisory controls, demonstrating the system was functioning as designed. This stands in contrast to cases like Alexander Investment Services where firms failed entirely to establish any Reg BI procedures.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Securities Exchange Act Rule 15l-1(a)(1) – Reg BI Compliance Obligation; FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's supervisory system was reasonably designed to achieve Reg BI compliance and promptly detected and corrected an isolated procedural lapse, demonstrating effective supervision rather than systemic failure.
