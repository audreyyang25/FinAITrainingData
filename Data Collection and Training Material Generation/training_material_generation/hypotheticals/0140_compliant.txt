FACTS
-----
A 62-year-old plan participant with $440,000 in a 401(k) at a small employer is considering a rollover. The 401(k) plan offers limited investment options (6 funds, mostly actively managed at 1.0%+ expense ratios) and does not permit in-service distributions. The participant wants to retire in 2 years and needs portfolio flexibility and lower costs. A fiduciary adviser (fee-only, no commissions) documents a written rollover analysis showing: (1) 401(k) all-in costs = 1.35%; (2) proposed IRA with low-cost custodian = 0.35% all-in (including custodial and blended fund expenses); (3) the participant's income tax situation suggests a direct rollover is appropriate; (4) the IRA would allow rebalancing and transition to a more conservative allocation aligned with the 2-year retirement timeline. The adviser discloses her 0.80% AUM fee (already reflected in the 0.35% all-in cost statement—clarified in writing), documents that the rollover is in the participant's best interest, and executes a written BIC agreement meeting all PTE 2020-02 requirements.

QUESTION
--------
Does this rollover recommendation satisfy DOL ERISA fiduciary duty and PTE 2020-02?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation is compliant with DOL ERISA fiduciary duty and PTE 2020-02.

The adviser satisfied all PTE 2020-02 requirements and ERISA fiduciary duties. First, she conducted a documented, reasoned best-interest determination showing material cost savings (1.35% vs. 0.35% all-in) and specific non-financial benefits (flexibility, conservative transition strategy aligned with 2-year retirement timeline). Second, she disclosed her material conflict of interest (0.80% AUM) clearly and in writing, and importantly, distinguished between the fee she earns and the total all-in cost to the client. Third, she executed a written BIC engagement. Fourth, she considered tax implications (direct rollover appropriateness) and plan-specific features (401(k) limitations on in-service distributions and rebalancing). The 3-to-1 cost advantage and documented non-financial benefits (portfolio flexibility, allocation transition) clearly support a finding that the rollover is in the participant's best interest under the prudence and loyalty standards.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
DOL ERISA §404(a)(1)(A)–(B); PTE 2020-02; 29 CFR §2550.408g-2; DOL Prohibited Transaction Advisory Opinion 2005-01A

KEY FACTOR
----------
Adviser documented material cost savings (1.35% to 0.35%), clearly disclosed her conflict of interest, provided a non-financial rationale tied to the client's 2-year retirement timeline, and executed a written BIC—satisfying all core PTE 2020-02 requirements.
