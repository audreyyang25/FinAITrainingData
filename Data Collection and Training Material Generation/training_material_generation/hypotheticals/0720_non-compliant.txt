ID: 720
Source Folder: 11_Case_Law
Topic: reg_bi_litigation
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Marcus Webb, a registered representative at Summit Securities, recommends that his client, 71-year-old retired schoolteacher Dorothy Hanson, invest $320,000 — representing approximately 85% of her liquid retirement savings — into a non-traded REIT with a 7-year lockup period, 12% upfront commission, and no secondary market liquidity. Mrs. Hanson's account documents indicate a conservative risk tolerance, a need for income to supplement her $1,400 monthly pension, and potential need for funds to cover assisted living costs within three to five years. Webb's rationale documented in his recommendation notes states only that 'client wants higher yield than CDs.' Summit Securities' compliance department approved the transaction through an automated system that flagged no alerts because the product was on the firm's approved list.

QUESTION
--------
Does Marcus Webb's recommendation of a non-traded REIT with a 7-year lockup to a 71-year-old retiree who may need liquidity within 3-5 years comply with Regulation Best Interest?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Regulation Best Interest's Care Obligation and the firm's Compliance Obligation.

Reg BI's Care Obligation requires a broker-dealer to exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill in making recommendations, considering the investment's risks, rewards, and costs in light of the specific customer's investment profile including age, liquidity needs, risk tolerance, and time horizon. Recommending an illiquid product with a 7-year lockup and 12% upfront load to a 71-year-old conservative investor who may need funds within 3-5 years for assisted living directly contradicts her documented investment profile. The firm's Compliance Obligation is also implicated because Summit's automated approval system failed to flag obvious red flags — concentration of 85% of liquid assets in a single illiquid product for an elderly, conservative client — indicating inadequate policies and procedures to achieve compliance with Reg BI.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iv) – Reg BI Compliance Obligation; Exchange Act § 15(l)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommending an illiquid, long-lockup product consuming 85% of a conservative elderly retiree's liquid savings, when her documented needs include near-term liquidity for potential assisted living costs, directly violates Reg BI's requirement to consider the customer's investment profile.
