Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Meridian Global Securities, Inc. is a registered broker-dealer whose wholly-owned subsidiary, Meridian Advisors LLC, serves as transfer agent for three micro-cap issuers whose shares trade on OTC Markets. Over a 26-month period, Meridian Global failed to file quarterly and annual reports on behalf of two of those issuers — Solartech Innovations Corp. and Pacific Basin Commodities Inc. — despite repeated SEC staff deficiency letters. Solartech's last Form 10-K was filed 31 months prior, and Pacific Basin's last Form 10-Q was filed 19 months prior. During this period, Meridian Global continued to make markets in both securities, earning approximately $2.1 million in spreads. The firm's compliance department was aware of the filing delinquencies but concluded that the reporting obligation belonged exclusively to the issuers, not the broker-dealer.

QUESTION
--------
Does Meridian Global Securities' continued market-making in the securities of delinquent issuers, while its affiliate served as transfer agent and the firm earned $2.1 million in trading spreads, give rise to liability under the Exchange Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Exchange Act Section 15(c) and reflects the type of issuer reporting delinquency that warrants registration revocation under Exchange Act Section 12(j), as illustrated in SEC ALJ proceedings against similarly situated issuers.

SEC Initial Decision Release No. 1361 establishes that persistent failure to file required periodic reports is sufficient grounds for revocation of a registered security under Exchange Act §12(j), reflecting the Commission's core investor-protection mandate. A broker-dealer that continues to make markets in chronically delinquent issuers — particularly where an affiliate performs transfer agent functions giving the firm constructive awareness of reporting failures — risks Exchange Act §15(c) liability for engaging in manipulative or deceptive practices. The firm's affiliate relationship with the transfer agent imputed knowledge of the delinquencies directly to the broker-dealer's compliance function. Earning $2.1 million in spreads while facilitating public trading in securities whose issuers have not filed required disclosures compounds the harm to retail investors who lack material information.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Exchange Act §§12(j), 15(c); SEC Initial Decision Release No. 1361 (Yanglin Soybean, Inc.) – registration revocation for failure to file periodic reports

KEY FACTOR
----------
The broker-dealer's affiliate relationship with the transfer agent imputed knowledge of the issuers' chronic filing delinquencies, making continued market-making in those securities a deceptive practice that cannot be insulated by attributing the reporting obligation solely to the issuers.
