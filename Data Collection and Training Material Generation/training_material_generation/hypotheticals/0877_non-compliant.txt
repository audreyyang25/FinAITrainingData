ID: 877
Source Folder: 18_OCC_Bank_Trust_Guidance
Topic: bank_fiduciary
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Cornerstone Federal Savings Association, operating a national fiduciary program consistent with OCC Interpretive Letter #995 (July 2004), accepted appointment as trustee for a $12 million special needs trust in a state where it maintains only a trust representative office. The trust was established for 34-year-old Marcus Hill, who receives SSI and Medicaid benefits. The bank's trust officer, based in another state, allocated $4.1 million to a variable annuity product issued by an affiliated insurance subsidiary, without obtaining a trust committee review or engaging independent counsel to evaluate the appropriateness of an annuity inside a special needs trust. The officer documented the allocation as 'aligned with long-term growth objectives' but made no reference to potential impacts on Marcus's government benefit eligibility, the surrender charges totaling 7% applicable in years one through five, or the bank's revenue-sharing arrangement with the affiliated insurer generating approximately $28,000 annually to the bank.

QUESTION
--------
Does Cornerstone FSA's allocation of special needs trust assets into an affiliated insurer's variable annuity product, without independent review of benefit eligibility implications or disclosure of the revenue-sharing arrangement, comply with its OCC fiduciary obligations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the duty of loyalty, the conflicts of interest disclosure obligation, and the duty of prudent administration specific to special needs trusts.

OCC Interpretive Letter #995 confirms that a bank operating a national fiduciary program must administer trust assets in accordance with the law of the trust situs and the specific terms of the trust instrument, which for a special needs trust includes preserving the beneficiary's eligibility for government benefits. The OCC Conflicts of Interest handbook identifies dealings with affiliated service providers as a high-risk conflict category requiring disclosure of all financial benefits flowing to related parties, including revenue-sharing arrangements — the $28,000 annual payment here was never disclosed to the trust protector or any oversight party. Placing a variable annuity with a 7% surrender charge inside a special needs trust without independent review of the benefit eligibility consequences and without documenting any analysis of alternatives constitutes a failure of prudent administration compounded by a material undisclosed conflict of interest.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
OCC Comptroller's Handbook – Conflicts of Interest (AM-CI), Version 1.0 (January 2015); OCC Interpretive Letter #995 (July 2004); 12 CFR 9.12; ERISA analogous fiduciary principles; applicable state special needs trust law

KEY FACTOR
----------
The bank's undisclosed $28,000 annual revenue-sharing arrangement with its affiliated insurer, combined with the absence of any independent review of the annuity's impact on the beneficiary's SSI and Medicaid eligibility, represents a paradigmatic self-dealing conflict requiring committee authorization and full disclosure under OCC fiduciary standards.
