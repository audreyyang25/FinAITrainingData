Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Marcus Webb, a dually registered broker-dealer representative and investment adviser representative at Summit Financial Group, recommends that his retail client, Helen Garza (age 71, retired teacher with a $340,000 IRA and moderate risk tolerance), allocate 65% of her IRA into a proprietary high-yield bond fund that carries a 1.75% expense ratio and a 2% front-end sales load. Summit Financial Group receives revenue-sharing payments from the fund sponsor. Marcus does not disclose the revenue-sharing arrangement or the existence of substantially similar lower-cost third-party bond funds. He documents only that the fund is 'suitable' for income-seeking retirees.

QUESTION
--------
Did Marcus Webb satisfy the Reg BI Care and Disclosure Obligations when recommending the proprietary high-yield bond fund to Helen Garza?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the Reg BI Care Obligation and Disclosure Obligation.

Under Regulation Best Interest, a broker-dealer must exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill to understand the costs and risks of a recommendation and evaluate reasonably available alternatives such that the recommendation is in the retail customer's best interest. The final rule (Release No. 34-86031) makes clear that the standard cannot be satisfied through disclosure alone and requires analysis of cost when reasonably available lower-cost alternatives exist. Marcus failed to consider lower-cost alternatives and also failed to disclose the material conflict of interest arising from Summit's revenue-sharing arrangement, violating both the Care and Disclosure Obligations.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Disclosure Obligation; Release No. 34-86031

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative recommended a high-cost proprietary fund without considering reasonably available lower-cost alternatives and without disclosing the revenue-sharing conflict of interest.
