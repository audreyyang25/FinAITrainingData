Source Folder: unknown
Topic: concentration
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 42-year-old client with $2.5 million in investable assets and moderate risk tolerance opens an advisory account. She explicitly tells her advisor: 'I want broad diversification across asset classes and sectors.' Her advisor builds a portfolio but includes a $750,000 (30%) allocation to a single private equity fund with a 10-year lockup, 2% annual management fee, and 20% carried interest. The advisor justifies this by saying: 'Private equity is part of a diversified portfolio.' The client does not receive detailed due diligence on the fund's historical performance, concentration of underlying investments, or liquidity constraints, and signs a brief one-page summary agreement.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the duty of care and client-specific suitability standard?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the applicable standard.

The advisor has violated the duty of care and suitability standard by: (1) recommending a 30% concentration in a single illiquid private equity fund despite the client's explicit request for 'broad diversification'; (2) failing to conduct adequate due diligence — no discussion of the fund's underlying concentration, performance, or risk profile; (3) misrepresenting diversification — a single 10-year lockup vehicle does not constitute diversification, even if the fund itself invests in multiple companies; (4) inadequate disclosure — a one-page summary is insufficient for a $750K, 10-year commitment; and (5) concentration compounded by illiquidity and fees — 30% in a single illiquid vehicle with 20% carry interest creates a suitability problem for a moderate investor who has not explicitly consented to alternative investment concentration. The advisor's statement that 'private equity is part of diversification' reflects a misunderstanding of concentration versus asset class allocation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act § 206 (fiduciary duty of care and loyalty); SEC Reg BI Rule 15l-1(b)(2) (reasonable basis includes understanding product features, risks, and liquidity); FINRA Rule 2111 (customer-specific suitability); SEC guidance on private equity due diligence.

KEY FACTOR
----------
30% concentration in a single illiquid private equity fund directly contradicts the client's explicit diversification request and lacks documented due diligence, making this a clear violation of the duty of care.
