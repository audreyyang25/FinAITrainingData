FACTS
-----
A 42-year-old retail customer with $150,000 in taxable savings, an aggressive risk tolerance (self-identified as 'willing to accept significant volatility'), and a 20+ year investment horizon was recommended a growth-focused ETF portfolio consisting of 85% U.S. large-cap equity ETFs (expense ratio 0.08%), 10% emerging markets ETF (0.15%), and 5% small-cap value ETF (0.12%). The broker prepared a written analysis showing the customer's time horizon and risk profile supported this allocation, compared it to three alternative allocations (conservative, moderate, and growth), and documented that the recommended portfolio had the lowest cost structure available for a growth strategy. The broker's compensation was identical (0.30% annual advisory fee) regardless of which allocation was selected.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the Reg BI care obligation?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation satisfies the Reg BI care obligation. The broker conducted a thorough suitability analysis, presented documented alternatives, minimized conflicts of interest, and selected the lowest-cost option consistent with the customer's objectives.

The broker satisfied all key elements of the Reg BI care obligation: (1) the recommended portfolio was suitable for a 42-year-old with aggressive risk tolerance and a 20+ year horizon; (2) the broker conducted a documented comparative analysis of alternative allocations, demonstrating consideration of the customer's specific circumstances; (3) the portfolio was constructed using the lowest available expense ratios (0.08%–0.15%) for each asset class, minimizing costs; (4) the broker's flat advisory fee structure eliminated compensation-driven conflicts (the broker earned the same fee regardless of which allocation was chosen); and (5) the broker documented the analysis in writing, creating an audit trail demonstrating the care obligation was satisfied. This approach mirrors FINRA AWC cases where firms that prepared written suitability analyses and presented documented alternatives were not charged with Reg BI violations, even when recommending more complex strategies.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Regulation Best Interest § 240.15l-1(a)(2)(i) (broker-dealer must act in retail customer's best interest, considering compensation, costs, rewards, risks, and alternatives); also consistent with FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability) and SEC Staff Examination Priorities (2021) stating that Reg BI requires documented analysis of alternatives and transparent discussion of conflicts.

KEY FACTOR
----------
The combination of documented comparative analysis, cost-minimization, elimination of compensation conflicts, and explicit alignment with the customer's stated risk tolerance and time horizon all reinforce that the broker prioritized the customer's best interest in accordance with Reg BI's care obligation.
