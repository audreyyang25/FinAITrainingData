Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Greenfield Wealth Advisors, a registered investment adviser managing $420 million in client assets, maintains revenue-sharing arrangements with three mutual fund families. These arrangements generate approximately $680,000 annually to the firm. Greenfield's Form ADV Part 2A states generally that 'the firm may receive compensation from third parties in connection with client investments,' but does not identify the specific fund families, the magnitude of the payments, or the fact that these payments create an incentive for advisors to recommend these funds over comparable lower-cost alternatives. In a compliance review, the SEC staff finds that 78% of client portfolios hold at least one fund from the revenue-sharing partners, and comparable non-revenue-sharing funds with lower expense ratios were available.

QUESTION
--------
Does Greenfield Wealth Advisors' generic disclosure of revenue-sharing arrangements satisfy its fiduciary duty of disclosure under the Investment Advisers Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. Greenfield's vague, boilerplate disclosure of revenue-sharing conflicts violates the Investment Advisers Act fiduciary duty of disclosure.

In Robare Group v. SEC, the D.C. Circuit evaluated whether an adviser's disclosure of revenue-sharing arrangements was sufficiently specific to satisfy fiduciary obligations. The court emphasized that advisers must provide disclosure adequate to allow clients to understand the nature, magnitude, and implications of conflicts. Greenfield's generic language—'may receive compensation from third parties'—fails to convey the specific fund families involved, the $680,000 annual magnitude, or the incentive structure, rendering the disclosure materially inadequate. The 78% concentration in revenue-sharing partner funds further evidences that the undisclosed conflict materially influenced recommendations.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 § 206(1)-(2); see Robare Group, Ltd. v. SEC, 922 F.3d 468 (D.C. Cir. 2019) (evaluating adequacy of conflict-of-interest disclosures regarding revenue-sharing arrangements)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Generic boilerplate language about potential third-party compensation is insufficient to satisfy an investment adviser's fiduciary disclosure obligation when specific, material revenue-sharing arrangements create identifiable conflicts of interest.
