Source Folder: 15_FINRA_Regulatory_Notices
Topic: complex_products
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Westfield Brokerage, a FINRA member firm, recruits a team of four high-producing advisors from a competing wirehouse by offering a combined recruitment package valued at $3.2 million, including upfront signing bonuses averaging $800,000 per advisor payable over three years. The recruited team manages approximately $480 million in client assets. Upon transfer, approximately $55 million of the transferred accounts hold proprietary structured products from the prior firm that must be liquidated and repositioned. The recruited advisors recommend replacing these positions with Westfield's proprietary Alt Funds, which carry higher expense ratios (averaging 1.85% vs. 1.20% for comparable third-party alternatives) and generate additional revenue-sharing income for Westfield. Neither the advisors nor Westfield disclose to affected clients the recruitment bonuses, the financial incentive to recommend proprietary products, or the availability of lower-cost third-party alternatives. The firm's compliance department does not flag the pattern of proprietary product recommendations following recruitment.

QUESTION
--------
Did Westfield Brokerage and its recruited advisors satisfy their disclosure and conflict-of-interest obligations in connection with the post-recruitment proprietary product recommendations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a failure to disclose material conflicts of interest and a violation of the Reg BI Conflict of Interest and Disclosure Obligations.

FINRA Regulatory Notice 15-19 specifically identified large recruitment bonuses (six-, seven-, and eight-figure signing packages) as creating serious conflicts of interest between recruited advisors and their transferred clients, particularly when proprietary products in existing accounts must be repositioned. Reg BI's Disclosure Obligation requires full and fair disclosure of all material facts relating to conflicts of interest, including compensation arrangements that incentivize particular recommendations. The Conflict of Interest Obligation further requires firms to establish policies to identify and mitigate conflicts arising from compensation structures. Westfield's failure to disclose the recruitment bonuses, the proprietary product incentives, or the existence of lower-cost alternatives violates both obligations.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) and (iii) – Reg BI Disclosure and Conflict of Interest Obligations; FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability); FINRA Regulatory Notice 15-19 (Recruitment Practices)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Multi-million-dollar recruitment bonuses created material conflicts that were never disclosed to clients whose accounts were systematically repositioned into higher-cost proprietary products benefiting the firm.
