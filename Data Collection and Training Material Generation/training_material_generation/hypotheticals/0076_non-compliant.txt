Topic: ia_duty_of_care
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 72-year-old widow with $2 million in liquid assets and $18,000 monthly Social Security stated her objective as 'generate $4,000/month income (beyond Social Security) while preserving principal for 25-year time horizon.' Her adviser recommended a portfolio of 100% investment-grade municipal bonds with an average duration of 8 years and annual yields of 3.5%. The adviser calculated that this would generate approximately $70,000 annually ($5,833/month), exceeding the stated income need. However, the adviser failed to investigate or document: (1) the client's other sources of income; (2) tax implications of the municipal bonds (whether the client was in a tax bracket where munis made sense); (3) inflation risk over a 25-year horizon; or (4) how the portfolio would be rebalanced or adjusted if interest rates rose or bond prices fell.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation comply with the duty of care?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the investment adviser's duty of care.

While the income calculation appears adequate on its surface ($5,833/month exceeds the stated $4,000 need), the adviser has failed to conduct a reasonable investigation required by Section 206(4). Specifically: (1) the adviser did not investigate whether 100% municipal bonds is appropriate without knowing the client's overall tax picture or tax bracket (municipal bonds are generally suitable only for high-tax-bracket investors); (2) the adviser failed to address inflation risk—at 3.5% yield and 2-3% expected inflation, real purchasing power erodes significantly over 25 years; (3) the adviser provided no analysis of duration risk (an 8-year duration means significant bond price risk if rates rise); and (4) there is no documented rebalancing plan or strategy to address interest rate changes. A reasonable adviser would have investigated these factors before recommending 100% exposure to one asset class. The recommendation lacks a reasonable basis because the investigation was materially incomplete.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206(4); SEC Rule 206(4)-1; SEC IA Examination Priorities regarding 'reasonable basis' for recommendations.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Failure to investigate tax implications of 100% municipal bond allocation, failure to address inflation risk over 25-year horizon, and failure to document duration risk or rebalancing strategy demonstrate inadequate reasonable investigation.
