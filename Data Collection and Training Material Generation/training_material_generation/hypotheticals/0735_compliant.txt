Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Wells Fargo Clearing Services, LLC filed a petition to confirm a FINRA arbitration award against former registered representative Eric Koter, who had been ordered to pay $127,000 in damages related to unauthorized trading in three customer accounts. Koter contested the confirmation in court, arguing the arbitration panel had exceeded its authority. However, an independent review of the arbitration record showed that the panel conducted a five-day evidentiary hearing, both parties had full opportunity to present witnesses and documentary evidence, and the panel's award was consistent with the evidence of unauthorized trades documented in trade confirmations, recorded phone calls, and customer testimony. Wells Fargo's compliance department had detected the unauthorized trading through its exception-reporting surveillance system within 45 days, immediately placed Koter on heightened supervision, and terminated him within 90 days after completing its internal investigation. Wells Fargo also voluntarily reimbursed the affected customers $127,000 before filing its arbitration claim against Koter for indemnification.

QUESTION
--------
Did Wells Fargo's supervisory response—detecting the unauthorized trading within 45 days, placing the representative on heightened supervision, terminating him within 90 days, and voluntarily reimbursing customers—satisfy its supervisory obligations?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

A firm cannot guarantee that no representative will ever engage in misconduct; the supervisory obligation under FINRA Rules 3110 and 3120 requires that firms maintain reasonably designed systems to detect and respond to violations. Wells Fargo's exception-reporting surveillance system detected the unauthorized trading within 45 days, the firm promptly placed Koter on heightened supervision, terminated him within 90 days after completing its investigation, and voluntarily made customers whole before pursuing indemnification. This sequence demonstrates a supervisory system that functioned as designed—detecting misconduct, escalating appropriately, and remediating customer harm. The firm's subsequent petition to confirm the arbitration award against the former representative (consistent with the Wells Fargo v. Koter proceeding) further demonstrates accountability measures.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision); FINRA Rule 3120 (Supervisory Control System); Wells Fargo Clearing Services v. Koter (FINRA-related civil proceeding, referencing Case GD-25-012719)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's surveillance system detected the unauthorized trading promptly, the firm took escalating disciplinary action culminating in termination, and voluntarily reimbursed all affected customers, demonstrating a reasonably designed and effectively implemented supervisory system.
