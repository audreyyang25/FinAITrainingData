Source Folder: unknown
Topic: concentration
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 58-year-old conservative investor with $1.2 million in a brokerage account and documented 'moderate-to-conservative' risk tolerance meets with her advisor. The advisor recommends investing $700,000 (58% of assets) in a single non-traded real estate investment trust (REIT) with a 7-year lockup, 2% annual management fee, 6% upfront sales charge, and no secondary market. The advisor emphasizes 'diversification within real estate' by purchasing three different funds from the same sponsor. The client's statement indicates she has not received a detailed prospectus before executing the purchase.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation comply with FINRA suitability rules and Reg BI?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the applicable standard.

This recommendation violates multiple dimensions of the suitability standard: (1) concentration risk — 58% in illiquid non-traded REITs is excessive for a conservative investor who may need liquidity; (2) lack of reasonable basis — 'diversification within real estate' does not cure concentration when all three funds are from the same sponsor and share common risks; (3) fee stacking — the combination of 7% total upfront costs (6% sales charge + 2% annual fee) impairs suitability for a conservative account; (4) illiquidity mismatch — a 7-year lockup is inconsistent with a moderate-to-conservative profile that typically requires flexibility; and (5) prospectus delivery failure suggests inadequate disclosure. FINRA has repeatedly disciplined advisors for excessive non-traded REIT concentrations in conservative accounts (see FINRA Disciplinary Actions involving non-traded REIT overconcentration).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability; must have reasonable basis, customer-specific basis); SEC Reg BI Rule 15l-1(b)(2) (reasonable basis includes identifying concentration risk); SEC guidance on non-traded REITs (concentration, liquidity, fee disclosure).

KEY FACTOR
----------
58% concentration in illiquid, fee-heavy non-traded REITs for a conservative investor with no documented liquidity needs justifying the lockup represents a paradigmatic suitability violation.
