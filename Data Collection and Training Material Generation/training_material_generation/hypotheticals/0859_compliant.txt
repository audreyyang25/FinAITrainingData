Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Sophia Reinholt, a registered representative at a full-service broker-dealer, recommended that her client, Daniel Okafor, a 45-year-old software engineer earning $175,000 annually with a $480,000 brokerage account and high risk tolerance, purchase shares of her firm's newly underwritten technology sector ETF (ticker: FTIX) at a $0.25 per-share commission, rather than a substantially similar competing ETF from a third-party issuer charging $0.18 per share. Reinholt documented in the client file a written best interest analysis noting that FTIX offered a marginally lower expense ratio (0.12% vs. 0.18%), superior intraday liquidity, and an equivalent five-year back-tested return profile compared to the competitor product, resulting in a projected net cost advantage of approximately $340 annually at Okafor's investment level of $85,000. The firm's Form CRS clearly disclosed that it earns underwriting fees from proprietary ETF recommendations and that this creates a conflict of interest; Reinholt also verbally summarized this conflict at the point of recommendation and documented the conversation. Congressional oversight hearings on Reg BI, including the March 2019 'Putting Investors First' hearing, acknowledged that Reg BI does not prohibit broker-dealers from recommending proprietary products so long as conflicts are disclosed and the recommendation is otherwise in the retail customer's best interest.

QUESTION
--------
Did Reinholt satisfy Reg BI's Care and Conflict of Interest Obligations when recommending the firm's proprietary ETF despite the underwriting conflict?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Reg BI does not categorically prohibit recommendations of proprietary or higher-commission products; it requires that the recommendation be in the retail customer's best interest after accounting for costs, risks, and the customer's investment profile — a standard expressly contemplated in the Dodd-Frank §913 legislative history and confirmed in Congressional oversight testimony. Reinholt conducted and documented a quantitative comparative analysis demonstrating that FTIX's lower expense ratio produced a net benefit to Okafor over the competing product, satisfying the Care Obligation's requirement to consider costs and reasonably available alternatives. The firm's Form CRS and Reinholt's verbal disclosure at the point of recommendation together satisfied the Conflict of Interest and Disclosure Obligations by clearly identifying the underwriting relationship and its financial implications. The documented client file showing the comparative rationale and conflict disclosure represents precisely the compliance infrastructure that Reg BI and its legislative history contemplated as sufficient to permissibly recommend a proprietary product.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iii) – Reg BI Disclosure Obligation; Dodd-Frank Act §913 legislative history

KEY FACTOR
----------
Reinholt's documented quantitative showing that the proprietary ETF produced a net cost advantage for the specific client, combined with clear written and oral conflict disclosure at the point of recommendation, demonstrates that a proprietary product recommendation can satisfy Reg BI even in the presence of an underwriting conflict.
