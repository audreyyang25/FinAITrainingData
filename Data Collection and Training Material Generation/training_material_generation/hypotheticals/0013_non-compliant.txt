Source Folder: unknown
Topic: reg_bi_care
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 68-year-old retired customer with $250,000 in liquid savings and stated conservative risk tolerance meets with a broker at a major firm. The broker recommends a portfolio consisting of 70% Class A mutual funds with 5.75% front-end load and 12b-1 fees of 0.85% annually, plus 30% in variable annuities with 7% surrender charges and 2.5% annual mortality and expense fees. The broker does not discuss lower-cost index funds, ETFs, or load-waived institutional share classes available to the customer.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the Reg BI care obligation to recommend products in the customer's best interest considering costs, risks, and alternatives?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the Reg BI care obligation.

The recommendation prioritizes high-commission products (front-end load funds, variable annuities with mortality charges totaling approximately 3.35% in annual costs) without documenting consideration of lower-cost alternatives such as no-load funds, ETFs (which typically charge 0.10-0.30% in expense ratios), or load-waived institutional share classes. The broker's failure to analyze alternative products that would deliver similar exposure with substantially lower costs, particularly material for a 68-year-old on fixed income, demonstrates breach of the care obligation. Reg BI explicitly requires the broker to consider costs relative to alternatives; the 3%+ annual cost difference represents material consideration that was never documented or discussed. This pattern mirrors FINRA disciplinary cases for unsuitable product selections based on compensation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Regulation Best Interest, Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i): broker-dealer must recommend securities in the customer's best interest, including consideration of costs, risks, rewards, and alternative investments available to the customer.

KEY FACTOR
----------
No documented analysis of materially lower-cost alternatives (index funds, ETFs, load-waived shares) for a conservative, fee-sensitive retiree, directly violating the costs/alternatives prong of Reg BI.
