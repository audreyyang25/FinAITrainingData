Source Folder: 11_Case_Law
Topic: reg_bi_litigation
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Marcus Webb, a registered representative at Pinnacle Securities, recommends that his client Dorothy Greer, a 72-year-old retired school teacher with $310,000 in savings and a stated goal of capital preservation and income generation, invest $185,000 (approximately 60% of her liquid assets) in a non-traded REIT with a 7-year lockup period and a 12% upfront commission. Webb's firm pays him a 7% commission on non-traded REIT sales versus 1% on investment-grade bond funds. Webb does not document any consideration of lower-cost, more liquid alternatives such as publicly traded REIT ETFs or bond funds that would meet Dorothy's income needs. Webb's transaction records show that non-traded REIT sales comprise 78% of his production credits for the year.

QUESTION
--------
Does the broker's recommendation of a concentrated, illiquid non-traded REIT position to an elderly client seeking capital preservation comply with applicable best interest and suitability standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Regulation Best Interest's Care Obligation and FINRA suitability requirements.

Reg BI's Care Obligation requires a broker to exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill in making a recommendation, including understanding the risks and costs of the product and having a reasonable basis to believe the recommendation is in the customer's best interest. Recommending that a 72-year-old retiree concentrate 60% of her liquid assets in an illiquid product with a 7-year lockup directly contradicts her stated capital preservation and income objectives. The failure to consider lower-cost, liquid alternatives and the pattern of disproportionate non-traded REIT sales strongly suggest the recommendation was driven by the broker's compensation incentive rather than the client's best interest.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; FINRA Rule 2111 – Suitability; see also SEC enforcement patterns regarding non-traded REIT concentration

KEY FACTOR
----------
Concentrating a retiree's assets in an illiquid, high-commission product without documenting consideration of suitable alternatives violates the Care Obligation's requirement to act in the customer's best interest.
