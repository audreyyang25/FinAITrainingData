Topic: reg_bi_care
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Jennifer Dao, a financial professional at a mid-sized broker-dealer, recommended that her client, Robert Engel (age 58, annual income $210,000, liquid net worth $1.4 million, moderate-to-aggressive risk tolerance, stated goal of growth before retirement in seven years), allocate $200,000 from a maturing CD into a diversified portfolio comprising a Class A mutual fund (equity growth fund with a 4.25% front-end load and a 0.72% expense ratio) and a no-load intermediate-term bond fund (0.45% expense ratio). Dao documented her consideration of three reasonably available alternatives: a lower-cost ETF portfolio, an advisory wrap account at a 1.25% annual fee, and a variable annuity with a 1.35% M&E charge and 7-year surrender period. In her written rationale, Dao explained that the Class A share breakpoint at $200,000 reduced the effective load, the client's preference for a commission-based transactional relationship and infrequent trading made the Class A share more cost-effective over the client's seven-year horizon than the wrap account, and that the variable annuity's surrender charges and higher ongoing costs were not justified given the client's timeline. Dao's firm had policies requiring documented cost comparisons for share-class recommendations and had conducted annual compliance reviews confirming enforcement of these policies.

QUESTION
--------
Did the financial professional satisfy the Reg BI Care Obligation when recommending the Class A mutual fund over lower-cost alternatives?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The Care Obligation requires that a financial professional exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill to understand the potential risks, rewards, and costs of a recommendation and have a reasonable basis to believe it is in the retail customer's best interest in light of the customer's investment profile. The SEC's January 2023 Risk Alert specifically flagged deficiencies where firms failed to consider reasonably available alternatives or to document cost comparisons, particularly for share-class recommendations. Here, Dao documented her evaluation of three alternatives with specific cost and suitability analysis, matched the recommendation to the client's profile (growth objective, seven-year horizon, preference for transactional relationship), and utilized available breakpoints to reduce costs. The firm's written supervisory procedures requiring cost comparisons and its annual compliance reviews further demonstrate satisfaction of the Compliance Obligation under Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iv).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Division of Examinations Risk Alert (Jan. 30, 2023) – Observations from Broker-Dealer Examinations Related to Regulation Best Interest

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative documented a thorough cost-benefit comparison of reasonably available alternatives, matched the recommendation to the client's specific investment profile and account-type preference, and the firm maintained and enforced written policies requiring such analysis.
