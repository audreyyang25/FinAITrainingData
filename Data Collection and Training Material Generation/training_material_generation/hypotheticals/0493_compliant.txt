Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Crestview BDC, Inc. is a business development company advised by Crestview Capital Management. Crestview Capital also manages Crestview Opportunity Fund LP, a private fund. The two vehicles share common control through Crestview Capital. Crestview BDC wishes to co-invest $8 million with Crestview Opportunity Fund LP in a senior secured loan to a healthcare services company. Crestview Capital's compliance team conducts a thorough analysis of all limited partners of Crestview Opportunity Fund LP and determines that none of them own any voting securities of the BDC or serve on the BDC's board, establishing that each limited partner is a 'remote affiliate' rather than a 'close affiliate.' Crestview Capital presents the co-investment transaction to the BDC's board of directors, and a required majority of independent directors approves the transaction after reviewing the terms, the allocation methodology, and the affiliate analysis.

QUESTION
--------
Did Crestview BDC comply with the Investment Company Act's restrictions on co-investment transactions with second-tier affiliates?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

IM Guidance Update 2014-12 clarifies that transactions between a BDC and remote affiliates are permitted subject to approval by a required majority of the BDC's directors. By confirming that none of the Private Fund's limited partners had ownership or board ties to the BDC—establishing them as remote rather than close affiliates—and by obtaining the required majority board approval after full review of the transaction terms and allocation, Crestview Capital followed the proper process outlined in the guidance to protect the BDC from undue influence and overreaching.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Company Act of 1940, Sections 57(a) and 57(f); IM Guidance Update No. 2014-12 (December 2014) – BDC Transactions with Second-Tier Affiliates

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser properly analyzed each limited partner's relationship to the BDC, correctly classified them as remote affiliates, and obtained required majority independent board approval before executing the co-investment.
