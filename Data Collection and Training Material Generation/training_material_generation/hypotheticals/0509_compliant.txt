Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Saxony River Wealth Advisors, LLC, a registered investment adviser with $620 million in AUM, utilizes a mix of mutual funds and ETFs across client portfolios. The firm receives 12b-1 fees from certain mutual fund families when client assets are invested in specific share classes. In its Form ADV Part 2A, the firm provides a detailed four-paragraph disclosure identifying each mutual fund family that pays 12b-1 fees, the range of fees received (15 to 25 basis points), and an explicit statement that these payments create a conflict of interest because the adviser has a financial incentive to recommend share classes that pay 12b-1 fees over lower-cost share classes that do not. The firm further discloses that clients may request investment in the lowest-cost share class available for any recommended fund. The firm's investment committee maintains written documentation showing that, for each fund selection, it evaluates total expense ratios, after-fee performance, and share-class availability, and it has transitioned 78% of eligible client assets to institutional share classes that do not pay 12b-1 fees. The remaining 22% are in accounts where the institutional share class is unavailable due to minimum investment thresholds.

QUESTION
--------
Is Saxony River Wealth Advisors in compliance with its fiduciary duties under the Advisers Act despite receiving 12b-1 fees from mutual fund families in which it invests client assets?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The Advisers Act does not categorically prohibit an adviser from receiving 12b-1 fees, but it requires full and fair disclosure of the conflict and conduct consistent with the adviser's duty of loyalty. Unlike the pattern seen in the Saxony Capital Management enforcement action—where disclosure of conflicts was inadequate—this firm provides specific, granular disclosure of which funds pay 12b-1 fees, the fee ranges, and the resulting conflict. The firm's affirmative efforts to transition the majority of assets to lower-cost institutional share classes, combined with documented justification for the remaining 22% where institutional shares are unavailable, demonstrate that the firm is managing the conflict in clients' interests rather than its own.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §206(2); SEC guidance on share-class selection and 12b-1 fee conflicts; cf. In re Saxony Capital Management, LLC (IA Release No. 5385, 2019)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm provided specific, quantified disclosure of 12b-1 fee conflicts and demonstrated through documented actions that it actively sought lower-cost share classes for clients wherever available.
