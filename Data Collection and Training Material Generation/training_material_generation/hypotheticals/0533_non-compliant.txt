Topic: finra_awc_suitability
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Robert Gaines, a registered representative at a national wirehouse, exercised discretion in 12 customer accounts between March 2023 and February 2024 without obtaining prior written authorization from the customers or written acceptance of the accounts as discretionary by his firm. Gaines placed trades in equity securities and options across these accounts based on his own judgment of market conditions. When questioned by his branch manager, Gaines stated that his customers had given him verbal permission to make trades without calling them first. The firm's compliance department confirmed that none of the 12 accounts had discretionary trading authorization on file, and the firm filed a Form U5 reporting that Gaines had been 'permitted to resign after a review of concerns involving unauthorized trading in client accounts.'

QUESTION
--------
Did Gaines' exercise of discretion without written authorization violate FINRA rules?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This is unauthorized exercise of discretion in violation of FINRA Rules 3260(b) and 2010.

FINRA Rule 3260(b) requires that no registered representative exercise discretionary power in a customer's account unless the customer has given prior written authorization and the member firm has accepted the account as discretionary in writing. Verbal authorization alone is insufficient. As demonstrated in the Goff AWC, exercising discretion across multiple accounts without the required written documentation constitutes a per-se violation of Rule 3260(b) and, by extension, Rule 2010's standards of commercial honor.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3260(b) – Discretionary Accounts; FINRA Rule 2010 (Standards of Commercial Honor and Principles of Trade)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Verbal permission from customers does not satisfy FINRA Rule 3260(b)'s requirement for prior written authorization before exercising discretionary trading authority.
