Source Folder: 20_SEC_ALJ_OALJ_Decisions
Topic: churning_elderly_variable_annuities
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Registered investment adviser Marcus Delray managed discretionary accounts for seven retirees aged 72-81, all with fixed-income objectives and moderate risk tolerance. Over 18 months, Delray executed 47 variable annuity exchanges across these accounts, generating $312,000 in commissions while clients incurred surrender charges averaging 8% per transaction. Delray disclosed none of the conflicts arising from his compensation arrangement with the annuity issuer. Client account values declined an average of 22% over the same period despite a flat broader market.

QUESTION
--------
Did Delray's pattern of undisclosed annuity exchanges in elderly discretionary accounts violate the Investment Advisers Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Investment Advisers Act §206(1) and §206(2) for fraudulent and negligent conduct in a fiduciary capacity.

Section 206 imposes a fiduciary duty on registered investment advisers, prohibiting both intentional fraud and negligent conduct that operates as a fraud on clients. Delray's failure to disclose his compensation conflicts and his pattern of unsuitable, high-cost exchanges for clients with fixed-income objectives satisfies both the scienter element of §206(1) and the negligence standard of §206(2). An ALJ would find that the volume, frequency, and client harm demonstrate conduct far exceeding any plausible investment rationale.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §206(1), §206(2); SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau, 375 U.S. 180 (1963)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Undisclosed compensation conflicts combined with unsuitable, high-cost exchanges for elderly conservative investors constitutes fraud under §206.
