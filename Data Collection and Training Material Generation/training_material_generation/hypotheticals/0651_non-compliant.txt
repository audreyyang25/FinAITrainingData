ID: 651
Source Folder: 08_State_Fiduciary_Rules
Topic: state_fiduciary_annuity
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A licensed insurance producer in New York recommends a 15-year surrender period indexed annuity from Pacific Life to Margaret Chen, a 78-year-old widow living on Social Security and a modest pension totaling $2,400/month. The annuity requires a $120,000 premium, which represents nearly all of Margaret's liquid savings. The producer documents Margaret's investment objective as 'growth' but does not record her liquidity needs, health status, or existing insurance coverage. The producer earns a 7% commission on the sale.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation comply with New York Regulation 187's best interest standard for annuity transactions?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates NY Reg 187's best interest and care obligations.

NY Reg 187 requires that a recommendation be in the best interest of the consumer, considering the consumer's age, liquidity needs, financial situation, and existing coverage. Recommending an illiquid 15-year surrender product to a 78-year-old client who would be tying up virtually all liquid assets fails the care obligation. Furthermore, the producer failed to document critical suitability factors including health status, liquidity needs, and existing insurance coverage, violating Reg 187's documentation requirements.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
NY Reg 187 – Best Interest Standard for Annuity Transactions (11 NYCRR 224)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommending a long-surrender annuity that locks up nearly all liquid assets of a 78-year-old client without documenting liquidity needs or health status violates Reg 187's best interest and documentation requirements.
