Source Folder: 16_Congressional_Materials
Topic: congressional_oversight
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Pinnacle Wealth Management, a dually registered investment adviser and broker-dealer, recommends that a 55-year-old engineer with $620,000 in investable assets and a moderate risk profile move $400,000 from a low-cost index fund portfolio (blended expense ratio 0.08%) into Pinnacle's proprietary wrap-fee program charging 1.95% annually, including 14 actively managed mutual funds with additional underlying expense ratios averaging 0.72%. The recommending advisor, Dana Forsythe, provides a written disclosure stating that Pinnacle 'may receive additional compensation from proprietary products' but does not quantify the firm's revenue difference between the two options, does not perform or document a cost-benefit analysis comparing the 1.95% wrap fee against the 0.08% index strategy, and does not explain how the active funds' historical underperformance is consistent with the client's moderate risk objective. Congressional hearing testimony before the Senate Banking Committee post-Reg BI adoption specifically cited the practice of dually registered firms using generic 'may receive' conflict disclosures as inadequate to satisfy the substantive best-interest standard.

QUESTION
--------
Did Dana Forsythe's recommendation and accompanying disclosure satisfy Reg BI's Care and Conflict of Interest Obligations for this dually registered firm?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Reg BI's Care Obligation and Conflict of Interest Obligation through a generic, non-quantified conflict disclosure and failure to document a cost-benefit analysis for a materially more expensive proprietary recommendation.

Congressional hearing testimony and the Reg BI adopting release both confirm that generic 'may receive' disclosures are insufficient for dually registered firms where the conflict is quantifiable and material to the recommendation. The Care Obligation requires a broker to consider reasonably available alternatives including cost, and a 187-basis-point annual cost differential on a $400,000 account ($7,480/year) against a backdrop of documented active-fund underperformance demands documented analysis. The SEC's adopting release specifically states that identifying a conflict without mitigating its influence on the recommendation does not satisfy the Conflict of Interest Obligation. Failure to document any cost comparison or explain the alignment of higher-cost active funds with a moderate risk profile independently violates the Care Obligation's documentation expectations.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iii) – Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation; SEC Reg BI Adopting Release (84 Fed. Reg. 33318) – Dual Registrant Guidance

KEY FACTOR
----------
The use of a generic, unquantified conflict disclosure by a dually registered firm recommending a proprietary product 187 basis points more expensive than the available alternative, without documented cost-benefit analysis, is the precise pattern flagged as inadequate in Congressional oversight hearings on Reg BI implementation.
