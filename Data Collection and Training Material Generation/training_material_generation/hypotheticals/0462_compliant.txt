Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
RIA Advisor Patel manages $8.4M for 64-year-old investment banker Thomas. Thomas holds a concentrated 28% position in his former employer's stock (worth $2.35M, acquired over 25 years with low basis of $340K), and 72% diversified portfolio. In 2023, Advisor Patel recommended Thomas maintain the concentrated position despite market guidance favoring diversification. Advisor Patel documented in writing: (1) Thomas's explicit instruction that the position remain for 'legacy and estate planning reasons'; (2) the embedded $1.91M long-term capital gain, generating estimated $483K tax liability at 25.3% combined rate if liquidated; (3) strategy to use gains harvesting in down-market years to gradually reduce basis and defer tax; (4) analysis showing the undiversified risk ($2.35M in single stock = 28% of $8.4M) was acceptable given Thomas's pension income ($4,200/month), age, and 15+ year horizon; (5) quarterly review documenting Thomas's continued acceptance of concentration risk. The stock underperformed market by 3.2% annually, but Thomas never requested sale.

QUESTION
--------
Did Advisor Patel comply with Reg BI care obligation and fiduciary duty by recommending retention of a concentrated position that underperformed the diversified portion?

ANSWER
------
Yes, fully compliant. Reg BI care obligation and IAA fiduciary duty permit advisers to accommodate client-directed concentrations when: (1) client provides explicit written instruction; (2) adviser documents the tax and economic consequences; (3) adviser demonstrates reasonable basis for the recommendation given the client's specific circumstances; and (4) adviser reviews the recommendation periodically. Advisor Patel satisfied all four criteria. The documented tax analysis ($483K liability avoidance) provides an economically rational basis for concentration despite underperformance. The SEC recognizes in IA Release 2774 that tax-sensitive investing may rationally justify deviating from diversification principles. Thomas's pension income ($4,200/month ≈ $50.4K annually) reduces his dependence on portfolio growth, supporting a concentrated-hold strategy. Underperformance alone does not breach duty when client has explicitly directed retention for tax reasons; the adviser's quarterly review demonstrates ongoing monitoring consistent with fiduciary care.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i); Investment Adviser Act §206(1) fiduciary duty; IA Release 2774 (tax-sensitive investing guidance)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented tax analysis ($483K deferral benefit), explicit written client instruction, and periodic review justify concentration retention despite underperformance.
