Source Folder: unknown
Topic: ia_duty_of_care
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An adviser manages a $600,000 account for a 62-year-old corporate executive with a stated objective of "growth with liquidity to fund a business acquisition within 3-5 years." The adviser recommends 65% diversified stock portfolio (including 10% concentrated position in the client's employer stock as requested), 20% short-term bond funds, 15% money market. The adviser documents the client's explicit request to maintain the employer stock concentration for strategic reasons, the 3-5 year acquisition timeline requiring liquidity, and the resulting need for a hybrid approach. The adviser establishes a separate policy to review the concentrated position annually and rebalance away from it if it exceeds 15% of total portfolio value or if the acquisition timeline changes. Over three years, the client's acquisition was delayed, and he did not ask to revise the objective; the portfolio allocation remained appropriate throughout.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation and ongoing conduct satisfy the duty of care?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this is compliant.

Although the 65/20/15 allocation with employer stock concentration is less conventional than a pure diversified approach, the adviser satisfied the duty of care by: (1) documenting the client's explicit business acquisition objective and liquidity need; (2) documenting the client's request to maintain employer stock for strategic reasons; (3) maintaining a reasonable overall allocation (65% diversified stocks plus 10% employer stock) with documented rationale; (4) establishing a written rebalancing policy to monitor concentration risk annually; and (5) tying the allocation directly to the client's stated, documented objectives. The adviser did not rubber-stamp an outdated recommendation—the policy to monitor and rebalance at 15% threshold shows ongoing duty of care. Periodic review without change is permissible when circumstances haven't materially altered the suitability analysis. The adviser's documented responsiveness to the client's specific goals (acquisition, employer stock retention) demonstrates reasonable basis and satisfies the duty of care.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206(2); duty of care permits recommendations responsive to client's stated objectives even if not conventional, provided adviser documents client's preferences, maintains reasonable basis, and conducts periodic review.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Explicit documentation of client's stated objectives (acquisition, employer stock for strategy), clear rationale for the hybrid allocation, and establishment of objective rebalancing thresholds that demonstrate ongoing monitoring of suitability.
