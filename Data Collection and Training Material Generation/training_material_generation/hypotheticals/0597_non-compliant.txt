Source Folder: 06_DOL_Fiduciary
Topic: pte_2020_02
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
David Kowalski, a 55-year-old manufacturing supervisor, has $210,000 in his employer's ERISA-governed 401(k) plan. After attending a workplace financial wellness seminar conducted by Pinnacle Financial Advisors, David meets one-on-one with Pinnacle advisor Sarah Lin, who recommends he take an in-service distribution and roll $150,000 into an IRA at Pinnacle. Lin's firm has adopted written policies and procedures acknowledging its fiduciary status under PTE 2020-02 and has a retrospective compliance review program. However, Lin places David's rollover assets into a non-traded REIT paying an 8% upfront commission and a Class C share mutual fund portfolio averaging 1.85% in annual expenses. Lin's internal compliance file contains a rollover analysis form, but the form is a pre-populated template with generic checkboxes; it does not reflect David's specific circumstances, his existing 401(k) fund lineup (which includes a stable value fund yielding 4.2% with no commission), or his moderate risk tolerance documented during the seminar intake.

QUESTION
--------
Does Pinnacle Financial Advisors' use of a generic, pre-populated rollover analysis template satisfy the documentation and care requirements of PTE 2020-02?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates PTE 2020-02's care obligation and its requirement for individualized rollover documentation.

PTE 2020-02 requires that the investment advice fiduciary exercise the care, skill, prudence, and diligence of a prudent person and must provide advice that is in the retirement investor's best interest at the time of the recommendation. The exemption specifically requires that rollover recommendations be supported by documentation reflecting consideration of the investor's particular circumstances, including the existing plan's fees, investment alternatives, and services. A generic, pre-populated template that fails to incorporate David's specific 401(k) fund lineup, his documented moderate risk tolerance, or any meaningful comparative fee analysis does not satisfy this individualized assessment requirement. The placement into a high-commission non-traded REIT and expensive C-share funds further suggests the recommendation was driven by compensation rather than the client's best interest.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
DOL PTE 2020-02, Section II(a) – Care Obligation; FAB 2021-02 Temporary Enforcement Policy on PTE 2020-02

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's rollover documentation was a generic pre-populated template that did not reflect the client's specific circumstances or a genuine comparison to the existing plan, rendering it insufficient under PTE 2020-02's individualized care and documentation requirements.
