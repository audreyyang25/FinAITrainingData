ID: 882
Source Folder: 19_CFTC_Guidance
Topic: commodity_conflicts
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A global asset manager, 'GlobalAlt Advisors,' operates both a registered investment company (RIC) and a separately managed commodity pool. On April 24, 2012, following the CFTC's February 8, 2012 rule amendments eliminating the exemption for RIC advisers investing in commodities, GlobalAlt became dually registered as both an SEC-registered investment adviser and a CFTC-registered CPO. GlobalAlt's RIC portfolio manager recommends that the fund increase its allocation to commodity-linked derivatives from 15% to 35% of net assets, generating substantially higher management fees under the CPO fee schedule than under the advisory fee schedule. The decision is approved internally without disclosure to the RIC's board of directors of the differential fee structure or the manager's personal financial incentive to favor the higher-fee CPO-regulated allocation strategy.

QUESTION
--------
Does GlobalAlt's undisclosed conflict of interest in recommending an increased commodity-linked derivatives allocation to its RIC, driven by differential CPO fee incentives, satisfy its fiduciary and conflict-disclosure obligations under the dual CFTC/SEC regulatory framework?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a material conflict-of-interest disclosure violation under CFTC Regulation 4.41, NFA Compliance Rule 2-29, and SEC fiduciary standards applicable to registered investment advisers.

Following the April 2012 CFTC rule amendments bringing RIC advisers into CPO registration requirements, dually registered entities are subject to both CFTC and SEC conduct standards simultaneously, as reinforced by the SEC-CFTC MOU on information sharing and coordinated oversight. A material conflict of interest arising from differential fee schedules that incentivize recommending higher-cost product structures must be disclosed to the RIC board and investors under both SEC fiduciary standards and CFTC anti-fraud provisions. The failure to disclose this structural incentive to the board constitutes a breach of duty under both regulatory regimes, with potential for coordinated enforcement action under the MOU framework.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
CFTC Regulation 4.41 – Advertising and Sales Material; NFA Compliance Rule 2-29 – Communications with the Public; SEC Investment Advisers Act Section 206 – Fiduciary Duty; CFTC/SEC MOU on Coordination in Areas of Common Regulatory Interest (June 28, 2018)

KEY FACTOR
----------
GlobalAlt concealed a direct financial incentive—higher CPO management fees—that drove its recommendation to increase the RIC's commodity-linked derivatives exposure, triggering concurrent CFTC and SEC conflict-of-interest violations.
