Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Greenfield Advisory Group, a SEC-registered investment adviser with $800 million under management, provides discretionary portfolio management to retail and institutional clients. In late 2022, following the SEC's publication of its Fiduciary Interpretation clarifying the investment adviser duty of care, Greenfield continues its practice of allocating all client accounts—regardless of client investment objectives, risk tolerance, or time horizon—to the same proprietary model portfolio consisting of 60% equities and 40% fixed income. The firm charges a uniform 1.25% advisory fee and does not conduct individualized investment profile reviews. A 34-year-old aggressive-growth client and a 78-year-old income-focused retiree with $190,000 in savings are both placed in the identical allocation. Greenfield's compliance manual has not been updated since 2018.

QUESTION
--------
Does Greenfield's uniform allocation of all clients to the same model portfolio regardless of individual investment profiles comply with the SEC's investment adviser duty of care under Section 206 of the Advisers Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates the investment adviser duty of care under Section 206 of the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, as articulated in the SEC's 2019 Fiduciary Interpretation.

The SEC's 2019 Fiduciary Interpretation articulated that the duty of care requires an investment adviser to provide advice that is in the best interest of the client, based on a reasonable understanding of the client's objectives. This includes a duty to provide personalized advice suited to each client's investment profile. Placing all clients in the same model portfolio without conducting individualized reviews of their risk tolerance, time horizon, and financial circumstances fails the duty of care's requirement of reasonable inquiry and personalized investment advice. The outdated compliance manual further evidences a systemic failure of the firm's compliance program.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206; SEC Fiduciary Interpretation (Release No. IA-5248, June 2019) – Duty of Care

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser failed to provide individualized investment advice based on each client's unique objectives, applying a one-size-fits-all allocation in violation of the duty of care.
