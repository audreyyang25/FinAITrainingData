Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered investment adviser (RIA) managing $95 million in retail client assets entered into a revenue-sharing agreement with a broker-dealer platform whereby the RIA received $0.02 per share in directed brokerage payments routed through a soft-dollar arrangement in exchange for recommending the platform's proprietary separately managed accounts (SMAs) to clients. The RIA disclosed the soft-dollar arrangement in its Form ADV Part 2A in a single paragraph buried on page 34 of a 40-page document using language stating 'the Adviser may receive research or brokerage benefits in connection with client transactions.' Congressional hearing testimony from a May 2024 House Financial Services Subcommittee session underscored that disclosure buried in lengthy documents without specific dollar quantification does not constitute meaningful transparency under fiduciary principles, particularly where retail clients lack the sophistication to parse boilerplate ADV language. Clients were never informed verbally of the arrangement or provided a separate written summary.

QUESTION
--------
Does the RIA's soft-dollar disclosure, limited to boilerplate ADV language without specific dollar quantification or separate written notice to retail clients, satisfy the investment adviser fiduciary duty of disclosure?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the investment adviser fiduciary duty of full and fair disclosure under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §206, as informed by congressional oversight findings on disclosure adequacy.

The investment adviser fiduciary duty requires disclosure that is 'full and fair'—not merely technically present but practically meaningful to the retail client. Congressional hearing testimony and SEC guidance both establish that disclosure buried in lengthy boilerplate documents without quantification of the economic benefit to the adviser fails this standard, because clients cannot make informed consent decisions without understanding the magnitude of the conflict. The soft-dollar arrangement created a direct incentive to recommend the platform's SMAs over potentially superior alternatives, and the §28(e) safe harbor does not protect arrangements where the primary benefit flows to the adviser rather than clients. The absence of verbal or separate written disclosure further undermines any claim of informed client consent.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §206(1) and §206(2); SEC Interpretation: Commission Interpretation Regarding Standard of Conduct for Investment Advisers (Release No. IA-5248); Exchange Act §28(e) soft-dollar safe harbor requirements.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Burying the soft-dollar conflict in undifferentiated ADV boilerplate without quantification or separate notice fails the 'full and fair' fiduciary disclosure standard that congressional oversight proceedings identified as the minimum threshold for meaningful investor protection.
