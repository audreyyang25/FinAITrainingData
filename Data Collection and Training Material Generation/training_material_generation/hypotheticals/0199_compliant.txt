FACTS
-----
A 62-year-old client with $180,000 in savings, limited investment knowledge, and conservative risk tolerance was recommended a variable annuity (VA) with a 2.5% annual insurance cost, 1.0% underlying fund expense ratio, and 7-year surrender charge. The advisor documented: (1) detailed discussion of VA features, costs, and surrender charge impact; (2) explanation that non-VA mutual fund alternatives cost 0.60% annually but lack the death benefit and income rider; (3) client's specific stated need for 'guaranteed income at age 70' and 'protection if I die before retirement'; (4) comparison showing the income rider cost (0.75% annually) was justified by the client's stated income guarantee objective; (5) completion of FINRA Form 2330 suitability documentation; and (6) client signature confirming understanding.

QUESTION
--------
Does this variable annuity recommendation satisfy FINRA Rule 2330 (variable annuity suitability) despite the high cost structure?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this is compliant.

FINRA Rule 2330 requires suitability analysis for VAs, acknowledging that VAs are appropriate for some investors despite higher costs than alternative investments. Compliance requires: (1) reasonable basis (advisor understands the product); (2) customer-specific suitability (product matches client's investment objective, risk tolerance, financial situation, and liquidity needs); and (3) documented disclosure under Form 2330. Here, all three are satisfied. The advisor documented the client's explicit objective for guaranteed lifetime income—a feature that distinguishes VAs from mutual funds and justifies the additional cost. The client is 62, approaching retirement, with limited investment knowledge and conservative risk tolerance—a profile where the death benefit and income rider appeal is rational, not exploitative. The advisor compared VA costs (3.5% blended) to non-VA alternatives and explained the 0.75% rider cost as the incremental fee for the income guarantee. The documented Form 2330 process and client signature satisfy the disclosure requirement. The 7-year surrender charge is material but disclosed; for a near-retiree not requiring liquidity, this is a reasonable tradeoff for guaranteed income. FINRA has consistently upheld VA recommendations for older, conservative clients with stated income guarantee needs, provided costs are disclosed and documented.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330 (variable annuity suitability), FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability), and SEC Regulation Best Interest

KEY FACTOR
----------
The advisor's explicit documentation of the client's stated income guarantee objective, detailed cost comparison to non-VA alternatives, and completion of FINRA Form 2330 suitability documentation transform the VA recommendation from a high-cost product to a suitability-compliant recommendation aligned with the client's specific retirement income needs.
