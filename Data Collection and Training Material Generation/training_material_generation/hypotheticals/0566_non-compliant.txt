ID: 566
Source Folder: 05_SEC_Exam_Findings
Topic: share_class_selection
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An SEC-registered investment adviser with $120 million in assets under management recommends Class C shares of the Putnam Growth Fund to 74-year-old retiree Margaret Collins, who invests $185,000 from her late husband's life insurance proceeds. The adviser's firm receives 12b-1 trailing commissions of 1.00% annually on Class C shares. A lower-cost Class A share with a front-end load of 4.25% was available, which would have resulted in significantly lower total costs over Margaret's 15-year investment horizon. The adviser's Form ADV Part 2A does not disclose the conflict of interest created by the 12b-1 fee revenue, and no comparative cost analysis was documented in the client file.

QUESTION
--------
Did the adviser comply with its fiduciary duty and SEC exam expectations regarding share class selection and conflict disclosure?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a fiduciary breach involving conflicted share class selection and failure to disclose material conflicts of interest.

The SEC Division of Examinations has consistently identified share class selection as a priority exam area, specifically targeting advisers who recommend higher-cost share classes that generate 12b-1 fee revenue for the adviser without adequate disclosure or cost analysis. The adviser failed to disclose the material conflict created by the 12b-1 revenue in its Form ADV Part 2A, violating the fiduciary duty of loyalty. Additionally, placing a 74-year-old retiree with a long-term horizon in Class C shares—which never convert to lower-cost shares and carry perpetual trailing fees—without documenting a comparative cost analysis violates the duty of care.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §§ 206(1) and 206(2) – Fiduciary Duty; SEC OCIE Share Class Selection Risk Alert (2018)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser selected the share class that maximized its own 12b-1 fee revenue without disclosing the conflict or documenting a cost comparison against lower-cost alternatives available to the client.
