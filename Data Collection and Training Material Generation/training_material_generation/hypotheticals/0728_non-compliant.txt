Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
The Robert and Vivian Delacroix Family Trust, a $2.8 million account managed at UBS Financial Services, was invested by financial advisor Nathan Pressley primarily in leveraged and inverse exchange-traded products, Puerto Rico municipal bond closed-end funds, and illiquid non-traded REITs over a three-year period from 2017 to 2020. The trust's investment policy statement specified a moderate risk tolerance and income-generation objective to fund the 78-year-old and 76-year-old grantors' living expenses of approximately $9,500 per month. At its peak, the portfolio held 38% in leveraged/inverse ETPs, 22% in Puerto Rico bond funds, and 15% in non-traded REITs, with only 25% in investment-grade fixed income. During the March 2020 market downturn, the portfolio lost $1.1 million. The trust filed a FINRA arbitration claim against UBS alleging breach of fiduciary duty, unsuitability, and common law fraud, seeking compensatory damages of $1.1 million plus attorneys' fees. UBS argued that the investments were disclosed and that the trust's co-trustee signed all account statements without objection.

QUESTION
--------
Does the concentration of a moderate-risk trust account in leveraged ETPs, speculative municipal bond funds, and illiquid non-traded REITs constitute a viable arbitration claim for unsuitability and breach of fiduciary duty despite signed account statements?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. The advisor's recommendations constituted violations of FINRA Rule 2111 (quantitative and customer-specific suitability) and common law breach of fiduciary duty owed to the trust.

FINRA has repeatedly warned that leveraged and inverse ETPs are unsuitable for buy-and-hold investors, particularly elderly clients with moderate risk tolerances and income needs. Concentrating 75% of a trust designed for income generation in leveraged ETPs, speculative municipal bond funds, and illiquid non-traded REITs is fundamentally inconsistent with the stated investment objectives. As reflected in trust-claimant arbitration cases like Weitzel Trust v. UBS (FINRA Case No. 16-03079), a customer's receipt of account statements does not constitute ratification of an unsuitable investment strategy, particularly where the advisor owes a fiduciary duty. The advisor's duty to recommend suitable investments is independent of the client's failure to object to account statements.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) (Suitability—Customer-Specific and Quantitative); Common Law Breach of Fiduciary Duty; FINRA Rule 2360 and Regulatory Notices regarding Leveraged/Inverse ETPs

KEY FACTOR
----------
A 75% concentration in leveraged ETPs, speculative bonds, and illiquid REITs is fundamentally unsuitable for a moderate-risk trust serving elderly grantors' income needs, regardless of signed account statements.
