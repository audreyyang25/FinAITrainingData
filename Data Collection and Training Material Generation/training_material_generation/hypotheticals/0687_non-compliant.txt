Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A dually-registered financial advisor at a mid-size broker-dealer recommends that Margaret Chen, a 62-year-old Gen X client with $410,000 in retirement savings, roll over her employer 401(k) into a proprietary variable annuity with a 7-year surrender schedule, 1.45% annual M&E fees, and a $15,000 surrender penalty. The advisor's firm pays a 5.25% upfront commission on the annuity versus 0.75% on a comparable low-cost IRA rollover alternative. The advisor documents that the annuity provides 'guaranteed income features suitable for retirement,' but does not document any comparison of costs, does not note that the client expressed concern about liquidity for potential medical expenses, and does not disclose that the firm's compensation grid provides a 15% production bonus for advisors exceeding $2 million in annual annuity sales. The advisor is $180,000 short of the bonus threshold at the time of the recommendation.

QUESTION
--------
Did the advisor satisfy Reg BI's Care and Conflict of Interest Obligations when recommending the proprietary variable annuity rollover?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation and Conflict of Interest Obligation.

Under Reg BI's Care Obligation, the advisor must exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill to understand the potential costs and risks of a recommendation, particularly for a rollover where the SEC has specifically identified heightened obligations. The failure to document any cost comparison between the proprietary annuity and a lower-cost IRA rollover, combined with ignoring the client's stated liquidity concerns in light of a 7-year surrender period, demonstrates a failure to evaluate reasonably available alternatives. Additionally, the undisclosed production bonus threshold creates a material financial incentive that the firm failed to mitigate or eliminate under the Conflict of Interest Obligation. Academic research on Reg BI effectiveness, consistent with the SEC's IA-4889 proposed interpretation, highlights that dual registrants face precisely these compensation-driven conflicts that the regulation was designed to address.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iii) – Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation

KEY FACTOR
----------
The advisor failed to perform or document any cost-benefit comparison of reasonably available alternatives for the rollover while simultaneously being driven by an undisclosed production bonus incentive.
