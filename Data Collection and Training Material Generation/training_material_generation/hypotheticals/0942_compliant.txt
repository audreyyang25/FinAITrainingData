Source Folder: 21_FINRA_OHO_Decisions
Topic: supervisory_system_written_procedures_compliance
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Compliance officer Rachel Nguyen at a mid-sized broker-dealer conducted quarterly exception report reviews of all accounts with turnover rates exceeding 4.0 or cost-to-equity ratios exceeding 15%, generating written findings and documenting supervisory follow-up actions. Upon identifying an account flagged in Q3, she contacted the responsible registered representative within five business days, reviewed the trade rationale memoranda, and escalated the matter to senior compliance when the representative's explanations were deemed insufficient. The firm maintained a Written Supervisory Procedures manual updated annually that specifically addressed leveraged product suitability reviews and concentrated position risks. All supervisory actions and findings were retained in the firm's compliance archive for a minimum of six years.

QUESTION
--------
Does the firm's supervisory system and Nguyen's execution of that system satisfy FINRA's supervision requirements?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Rule 3110 requires member firms to establish and maintain a supervisory system reasonably designed to achieve compliance with applicable securities laws and FINRA rules, including written supervisory procedures specifically tailored to the firm's business activities. The firm's use of quantitative exception reports, documented follow-up protocols, timely escalation procedures, and annually updated WSPs reflects the kind of reasonably designed system that FINRA OHO panels have found compliant. Retention of supervisory records for six years satisfies Rule 4511's books and records requirements.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3110(a) and (b); FINRA Rule 4511 (six-year retention requirement); FINRA Regulatory Notice 11-21 (supervision of registered persons)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The existence of specific, documented, and consistently executed exception-based supervisory reviews—coupled with proper recordkeeping—demonstrates a supervisory system reasonably designed to detect and address rule violations.
