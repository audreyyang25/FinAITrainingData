Topic: reg_bi_conflict
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Clearpath Wealth Partners, a dual-registered broker-dealer and investment adviser with 45 registered representatives, identifies through its annual conflicts inventory that representatives receive 25 basis points higher trailing compensation on Class A mutual fund shares from Fund Family Alpha compared to comparable funds from other families. Rather than eliminating the differential, Clearpath implements a multi-layered mitigation framework: (1) it prominently discloses the differential compensation arrangement in its Form CRS, Reg BI disclosure document, and at the point of recommendation; (2) it creates a product-neutral recommendation checklist requiring representatives to evaluate at least three comparable fund alternatives before recommending Fund Family Alpha products; (3) it establishes a supervisory review process where a principal reviews every Fund Family Alpha recommendation above $25,000 for documented cost and performance comparison; and (4) it conducts quarterly surveillance of Fund Family Alpha concentration across customer accounts. A 58-year-old client with $500,000 in retirement savings receives a recommendation for a Fund Family Alpha growth fund after documented comparison shows it has lower net expense ratios and superior 5- and 10-year risk-adjusted returns compared to two alternatives.

QUESTION
--------
Does Clearpath's recommendation of the higher-compensation Fund Family Alpha product violate the Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation given the differential compensation arrangement?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The Conflict of Interest Obligation under Reg BI does not require the elimination of all conflicts; rather, it requires the firm to identify and at a minimum disclose material conflicts, and to establish policies and procedures reasonably designed to identify and mitigate conflicts that create an incentive to place the firm's interest ahead of the customer's. Clearpath identified the differential compensation, disclosed it clearly to customers, implemented structural mitigation through a comparative analysis requirement and enhanced supervisory review, and conducted ongoing surveillance. The ultimate recommendation was supported by documented evidence that the specific fund was superior on cost and risk-adjusted performance, demonstrating the recommendation was made in the client's best interest notwithstanding the conflict.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iii) – Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Disclosure Obligation

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm identified, disclosed, and mitigated the differential compensation conflict through documented comparative analysis, enhanced supervisory review, and ongoing surveillance, and the specific recommendation was supported by objective cost and performance superiority.
