Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
RIA adviser Karen Chen recommended a $450,000 variable annuity (VA) with a 1.35% annual mortality & expense (M&E) charge plus 0.85% subaccount fees to her client Marcus, age 67, with $2.1M in investable assets. Marcus had just sold a commercial real estate property, realizing a $680,000 capital gain (long-term) taxable in the current year. His federal + state marginal tax rate was 42%. Karen documented that: (1) Marcus expressed concern about sequence-of-returns risk given his planned $95,000 annual retirement withdrawals starting age 70; (2) the VA's guaranteed minimum withdrawal benefit (GMWB) provided 5% annual withdrawals regardless of market performance, reducing longevity risk; (3) the tax deferral inside the VA would defer approximately $289,000 in taxes on $680,000 of gains over 10 years (assuming 4.25% annual growth), creating a net economic benefit exceeding the 2.2% annual cost differential vs. a low-cost mutual fund; (4) Marcus was a sophisticated investor with prior experience in real estate and stock options; (5) the VA allowed penalty-free withdrawals of up to 10% annually after year 3. Karen provided a detailed written analysis showing the tax-deferral benefit offset the higher costs, and Marcus signed a separate acknowledgment stating he understood the surrender charges (7% declining over 10 years) and chose the VA specifically for tax deferral and income certainty.

QUESTION
--------
Did Karen comply with Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI) duty of care and duty of loyalty in recommending this variable annuity?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Although the VA's 2.2% annual cost premium triggers heightened scrutiny under Reg BI §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i) (care obligation), Karen satisfied all prongs: (1) she conducted a reasonable investigation of Marcus's circumstances, identifying his specific tax situation, liquidity needs, and risk profile; (2) she made reasonable judgment that the recommendation was in his best interest by documenting the economic benefit of tax deferral ($289,000 over 10 years) which materially exceeded the 2.2% annual cost differential; (3) she disclosed material conflicts (VA commission structure) and obtained informed consent; (4) she considered lower-cost alternatives (mutual funds) and documented why the higher-cost product was superior in this specific context. The FINRA Rule 2330 (VA suitability) analysis also supports compliance: reasonable basis (tax-deferral benefit with GMWB), suitability (age, assets, risk profile, stated preference for income certainty), and disclosure of costs and surrender charges. This mirrors the compliance pattern in cases where advisers document specific economic rationales for recommending higher-cost products to suitable clients.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI) §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i) (care obligation—reasonable investigation, reasonable judgment, conflict disclosure); FINRA Rule 2330 (variable annuity suitability and disclosure)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser overcame the presumption against recommending high-cost products by documenting a specific, quantified economic benefit (tax deferral worth ~$289K) that exceeded the annual cost differential and aligned with client's stated objectives and circumstances.
