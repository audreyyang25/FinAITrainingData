Topic: pte_2020_02
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Robert Tanaka, age 67, contacts Atlas Wealth Management about rolling over $520,000 from his former employer's 401(k) into an IRA. Robert's Atlas advisor, Patricia Nguyen, is compensated through a combination of 12b-1 fees and upfront commissions on mutual fund sales—a compensation structure that creates an inherent conflict of interest. Atlas has acknowledged its fiduciary status under the Retirement Security Rule and relies on PTE 2020-02 for the receipt of conflicted compensation. Atlas has adopted comprehensive written policies requiring advisors to (1) document a comparative analysis of the existing plan versus the IRA, (2) consider at least three reasonably available alternatives including remaining in the plan, (3) disclose all material conflicts and compensation in advance, and (4) submit rollover recommendations for supervisory pre-approval. Patricia completes a detailed rollover comparison showing that Robert's 401(k) charges a $45/quarter administrative fee, offers only 8 investment options with expense ratios between 0.65% and 1.10%, and provides no advisory or financial planning services. Patricia recommends an IRA portfolio of A-share mutual funds with an average expense ratio of 0.72% at applicable breakpoints, generating a blended upfront commission of 3.25% and 0.25% annual 12b-1 fees. The written analysis documents that the IRA offers lower ongoing expenses, broader diversification, access to Patricia's ongoing retirement income planning, and that breakpoint pricing was applied. Patricia provides Robert with a written disclosure of all fees, her compensation, Atlas's conflicts of interest policy, and a copy of PTE 2020-02's required disclosures 48 hours before Robert signs the rollover paperwork. Atlas's compliance department pre-approves the recommendation and logs it in their retrospective review system.

QUESTION
--------
Can Atlas Wealth Management rely on PTE 2020-02 to receive conflicted commission-based compensation on this rollover despite the inherent conflict created by the advisor's compensation structure?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

PTE 2020-02 was specifically designed to permit investment advice fiduciaries to receive otherwise-prohibited conflicted compensation, provided they satisfy the exemption's conditions. Atlas and Patricia have met the key requirements: (1) the firm acknowledged fiduciary status, (2) Patricia rendered advice in Robert's best interest supported by a documented comparative analysis showing the IRA offered lower ongoing costs and broader services than the existing plan, (3) full written disclosure of compensation and conflicts was provided prior to the transaction, (4) the firm maintains and follows written policies and procedures governing rollover recommendations, and (5) the recommendation was subject to both pre-approval and retrospective compliance review. The fact that Patricia receives commission-based compensation does not itself disqualify the recommendation; rather, PTE 2020-02 provides the regulatory pathway for receiving such compensation when all conditions are satisfied.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
DOL PTE 2020-02, Sections II(a)-(d) – Impartial Conduct Standards, Disclosure, Policies and Procedures, Retrospective Review; Retirement Security Rule (April 23, 2024)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm satisfied all PTE 2020-02 conditions—including fiduciary acknowledgment, individualized best interest analysis, advance disclosure, written policies, supervisory pre-approval, and retrospective review—thereby permitting the receipt of otherwise-prohibited conflicted compensation.
