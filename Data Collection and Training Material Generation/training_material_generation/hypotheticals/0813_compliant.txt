Source Folder: 12_FINRA_Arbitration_Awards
Topic: arbitration_defenses
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Michael Hastings, a registered representative with 18 years of industry experience, was terminated by his broker-dealer, Kestra-affiliated firm Pinnacle Investment Services, LLC, following a single customer complaint alleging unauthorized trading in the customer's $285,000 IRA account. Pinnacle filed a Form U5 reporting the termination reason as 'permitted to resign' with a disclosure noting the customer complaint. Hastings filed a FINRA arbitration (similar to Case 18-01111) seeking expungement of the complaint from his CRD record, arguing the customer had provided standing verbal and written discretionary trading authorization for the account, which Pinnacle's compliance department had approved and documented. During the evidentiary hearing, the arbitration panel reviewed trade confirmations, the signed limited discretionary authorization form, contemporaneous email correspondence between Hastings and the customer discussing the trading strategy, and testimony from the firm's former compliance officer confirming the authorization was on file. The customer did not appear at the hearing and had previously withdrawn the complaint. The panel found the complaint was factually impossible and without merit, and recommended expungement under FINRA Rule 2080.

QUESTION
--------
Was the registered representative's conduct compliant and does the documented discretionary authorization and subsequent panel finding support expungement of the customer complaint from his CRD record?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Under FINRA Rule 3260, discretionary trading is permissible when the customer provides prior written authorization and the member firm accepts the account in writing. Hastings had a signed limited discretionary authorization on file that was approved by compliance, and contemporaneous communications confirmed the customer's awareness and approval of the trading activity. Under FINRA Rule 2080, a FINRA arbitration panel may recommend expungement when the claim is factually impossible, clearly erroneous, or false — criteria met here given the documented authorization, the customer's withdrawal of the complaint, and the customer's failure to appear. This mirrors the pattern in Case 18-01111, where an associated person sought expungement of an underlying customer complaint through FINRA arbitration after demonstrating the complaint lacked factual basis.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2080 (Obtaining an Order of Expungement from CRD); FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability); FINRA Rule 3260 (Discretionary Accounts)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The pivotal factor is the existence of a properly documented and compliance-approved written discretionary trading authorization, combined with corroborating contemporaneous communications, which negated the unauthorized trading allegation and supported expungement under FINRA Rule 2080.
