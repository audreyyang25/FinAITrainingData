ID: 865
Source Folder: 18_OCC_Bank_Trust_Guidance
Topic: bank_fiduciary
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
First Heartland National Bank serves as corporate trustee for a $2.1 million irrevocable family trust established by Eleanor Voss, an 82-year-old widow, whose three adult children are the income and remainder beneficiaries. The trust document grants the bank broad discretionary investment authority. Without disclosing the arrangement to the beneficiaries, the bank's trust officer directs 40% of the trust's assets into the bank's own proprietary money market funds, which carry a 0.75% management fee paid to the bank's affiliated investment subsidiary—nearly double the cost of comparable third-party funds. The bank's trust committee meeting minutes contain no analysis of whether this allocation serves the beneficiaries' interests, nor any conflict-of-interest disclosure or authorization.

QUESTION
--------
Does the bank's undisclosed allocation of trust assets into its own affiliated proprietary funds, without beneficiary authorization or documented best-interest analysis, satisfy OCC fiduciary standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a self-dealing conflict of interest in violation of OCC fiduciary duties.

The OCC Conflicts of Interest handbook specifically addresses self-dealing between related parties and fiduciary accounts, requiring that transactions benefiting affiliated entities be disclosed, authorized, and documented as consistent with the beneficiaries' interests. Here, the bank profited from an undisclosed arrangement by directing assets to its own subsidiary, generating excess fees without demonstrating the investment served the trust's purposes. The absence of any conflict-of-interest authorization, beneficiary notice, or trust committee analysis of comparative costs compounds the violation of the duty of loyalty identified in OCC fiduciary standards.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
OCC Comptroller's Handbook – Asset Management, Conflicts of Interest (AM-CI), Version 1.0 (January 2015); 12 CFR 9.12 (Self-Dealing Prohibition); Common Law Duty of Loyalty

KEY FACTOR
----------
The bank engaged in undisclosed self-dealing by investing trust assets in affiliated proprietary funds for its own financial benefit without beneficiary authorization or documented best-interest justification.
