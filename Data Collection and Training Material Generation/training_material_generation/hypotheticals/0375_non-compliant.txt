Topic: dol_rollover_analysis

Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Tanya, age 61, has $950,000 in a plan-sponsored 401(k) at Merrill Lynch offering institutional-class funds: Vanguard 500 Index (ER 0.05%), PIMCO Bond Fund (ER 0.32%), and Dodge & Cox Stock Fund (ER 0.52%). Total blended ER: ~0.25%. Advisor Kevin recommends rolling over to an IRA at his firm (AUM fee 0.90%) and investing in 'best-in-class' mutual funds he carefully selected: Vanguard 500 Index (ER 0.05%), Vanguard Bond ETF (ER 0.08%), and Dodge & Cox (ER 0.52%). Kevin documents: 'Rollover provides greater control over asset allocation, access to ETF wrapper, and firm-based reporting.' Kevin discloses his 0.90% fee. However, Kevin's fee (0.90%) plus the IRA-level costs (combined ~0.20% blended fund ER) total 1.10% ongoing cost vs. the plan's 0.25%, a 0.85 percentage-point annual increase ($8,075/year on $950k). Kevin's analysis does not quantify this fee differential. Kevin's 'control' and 'ETF access' benefits are immaterial because Tanya stated she is a passive investor uncomfortable making frequent changes.

QUESTION
--------
Does Kevin's rollover recommendation comply with PTE 2020-02 despite fee disclosure?

ANSWER
------
No. PTE 2020-02 requires compensation be 'reasonable' and advisers to 'not mislead' (§I(a)(1)). While Kevin disclosed his 0.90% fee in writing, his analysis is misleading by omission. The decisive facts: (1) Kevin's total cost is 1.10% vs. plan's 0.25% (0.85 pp increase = $8,075/year); (2) Kevin documented no quantified cost comparison; (3) Kevin's stated benefits (control, ETF access) are immaterial given Tanya's self-identified passive approach; (4) Kevin's analysis does not explain how Tanya benefits from paying 4.4× more annually; (5) the IRA investment options are not substantively superior—funds are identical except for wrapper. This violates PTE 2020-02's requirement to document that the recommendation serves the client's best interest. Bare fee disclosure without comparative analysis showing client benefit constitutes misleading conduct. Kevin should have either justified the cost differential (e.g., with quantified service value) or recommended the rollover only if costs were truly comparable.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
PTE 2020-02 §I(a)(1) (best interest, reasonable compensation, not mislead); Five-Part Test (documented comparative analysis)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommended rollover creating $8,075/year additional cost (0.85 pp) without documented analysis explaining how client benefits; fee disclosure alone does not cure misleading analysis.
