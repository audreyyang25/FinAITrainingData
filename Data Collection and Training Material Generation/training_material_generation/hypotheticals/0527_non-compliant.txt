Source Folder: 03_FINRA_AWC
Topic: finra_awc_suitability
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Zachary Brennan, a General Securities Representative registered with a mid-size broker-dealer, recommended that three senior customers—ages 72, 78, and 81—with balanced allocation investment profiles and moderate risk tolerances shift substantial portions of their portfolios into high-concentration positions in speculative technology equities and leveraged ETFs. Customer A (age 72, retired teacher, $420,000 portfolio, income need of $2,800/month) had 68% of her account placed into three speculative tech stocks. Customer B (age 78, retired, $310,000 portfolio, conservative-to-moderate risk tolerance) was placed into a 2x leveraged Nasdaq ETF representing 55% of his holdings. Customer C (age 81, widowed, $185,000 portfolio marked as 'capital preservation') had her account shifted from municipal bonds into small-cap growth equities. Brennan earned approximately $14,200 in commissions across the three accounts over a seven-month period. His firm terminated him after discovering the pattern, noting on his Form U5 that he was 'unable to provide sufficient documentary evidence to support his contention that he had authority for all trades in a client's account.'

QUESTION
--------
Did Brennan violate applicable suitability and best interest standards by recommending these concentrated, high-risk positions to senior customers with balanced or conservative investment profiles?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes violations of Reg BI Care Obligation (Exchange Act Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii)), FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability), and FINRA Rule 2010 (Standards of Commercial Honor).

The Reg BI Care Obligation requires that a recommendation be in the retail customer's best interest and that the associated person exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill, considering the customer's investment profile including age, risk tolerance, and financial needs. Recommending speculative, leveraged, or concentrated positions to elderly clients with balanced or conservative profiles and income or capital preservation needs is fundamentally inconsistent with their stated objectives. This pattern closely mirrors the Zachary Ellis Taylor AWC (No. 2022075083801), where FINRA found violations when a representative recommended unsuitable strategies to senior customers with balanced allocation profiles. The Form U5 disclosure regarding unauthorized trading suggests an additional layer of misconduct.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability – Customer-Specific Obligation); FINRA Rule 2010

KEY FACTOR
----------
The recommendations were fundamentally inconsistent with the senior customers' documented balanced/conservative investment profiles, income needs, and risk tolerances.
