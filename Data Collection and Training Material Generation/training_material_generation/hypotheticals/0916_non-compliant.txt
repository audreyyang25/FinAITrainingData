Source Folder: 20_SEC_ALJ_OALJ_Decisions
Topic: broker_unsuitable_concentration_recommendations
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Registered representative Trevor Hollis at Meridian Securities recommended that 14 retail clients, mostly schoolteachers with $150,000-$400,000 in investable assets and stated preservation-of-capital objectives, invest 60-80% of their portfolios in a single micro-cap energy company in which Hollis held undisclosed warrants. Hollis characterized the investment in client notes only as 'growth-oriented with moderate risk' and did not document any individualized suitability analysis. When the issuer filed for bankruptcy 11 months later, aggregate client losses totaled $2.1 million. Hollis's branch manager reviewed and signed off on the new account forms without inquiring into the concentration levels.

QUESTION
--------
Did Hollis's recommendation pattern and the branch manager's supervisory conduct violate Exchange Act provisions and FINRA suitability obligations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Exchange Act §10(b), Rule 10b-5, and constitutes a failure to supervise under Exchange Act §15(b)(4)(E).

Rule 10b-5 prohibits material omissions in connection with securities transactions; Hollis's failure to disclose his personal warrant interest was a material omission satisfying the scienter, materiality, and in-connection-with elements. The concentrated recommendations in a single micro-cap security were objectively inconsistent with documented preservation-of-capital objectives, establishing a suitability violation under FINRA Rule 2111's reasonable-basis and customer-specific components. The branch manager's perfunctory review without inquiry into concentration levels constitutes a failure to supervise reasonably, satisfying the §15(b)(4)(E) standard.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Securities Exchange Act of 1934 §10(b); Rule 10b-5; Exchange Act §15(b)(4)(E); FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Undisclosed personal financial interest in a recommended security, combined with demonstrably unsuitable concentration, establishes both fraud and suitability violations.
