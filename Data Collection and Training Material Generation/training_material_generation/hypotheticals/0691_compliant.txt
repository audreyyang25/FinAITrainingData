Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Rachel Hernandez, a dually-registered advisor affiliated with both an RIA and a broker-dealer, recommends that William Park, a 55-year-old Boomer executive with $2.1 million in investable assets and a high risk tolerance, allocate $200,000 to a private REIT offered through her broker-dealer. The private REIT charges a 6% upfront placement fee, has a 7-year lock-up, and targets 8-10% annual returns. Hernandez provides Park with a detailed written disclosure that explicitly states: (1) the exact placement fee and how it compares to publicly traded REIT alternatives; (2) the illiquidity risk and 7-year lock-up period; (3) that she will receive $12,000 in commission from the placement; (4) that comparable publicly traded REIT ETFs are available with expense ratios under 0.15% and full daily liquidity. Her written analysis documents that Park has no foreseeable liquidity needs for this portion of his portfolio, has significant other liquid assets ($1.9 million), is an accredited investor, has prior experience with alternative investments, and specifically requested exposure to private real estate after attending an investor education seminar. Park signs a detailed acknowledgment of all disclosed conflicts.

QUESTION
--------
Is the recommendation of a private REIT with a 6% placement fee and 7-year lock-up compliant with Reg BI given the advisor's commission and the availability of lower-cost alternatives?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Reg BI does not prohibit recommendations of higher-cost products; it requires that the recommendation be in the customer's best interest after full disclosure, reasonable diligence, and conflict mitigation. Hernandez satisfied the Disclosure Obligation by providing explicit, specific written disclosures of the commission, costs, and lower-cost alternatives. The Care Obligation is met through documented analysis showing the allocation is proportionate (less than 10% of total assets), the client has ample remaining liquidity, accredited investor status, and relevant experience. The Conflict of Interest Obligation is addressed through transparent commission disclosure and the client's informed written acknowledgment. Academic research on disclosure regulation, including the cross-country investor protection analysis, confirms that detailed, specific disclosure—as opposed to generic boilerplate—is the critical element in effective conflict management.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2) – Reg BI (all four component obligations: Disclosure, Care, Conflict of Interest, Compliance)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The advisor provided explicit, specific disclosure of her commission and lower-cost alternatives, documented the client's suitability and sophisticated investor profile, and obtained informed written acknowledgment of all conflicts.
