Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 50-year-old client with $800,000 in investment assets, age 50, earning $150,000 annually, stated his objective as 'long-term growth with moderate-to-high risk tolerance.' The adviser conducted a comprehensive financial plan that included: analysis of his $500,000 in employer stock options and restricted stock units; documentation of his 25-year investment horizon; analysis showing he would not need to liquidate investments for 10+ years; and a suitability analysis concluding that a 75% equity / 25% fixed-income allocation was appropriate. The adviser recommended individual dividend-paying stocks (25%), diversified U.S. equity index funds (30%), international equity funds (20%), and investment-grade bonds (25%). The adviser established a quarterly review schedule with documented performance monitoring against the client's stated objectives.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation comply with the duty of care?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation complies with the investment adviser's duty of care.

The adviser has satisfied all components of the duty of care: (1) conducted reasonable investigation including analysis of the client's human capital (employer equity compensation), income, time horizon, and stated risk tolerance; (2) developed a comprehensive financial plan showing the allocation is appropriate for a 50-year-old with 25-year horizon who will not need liquidations for 10+ years; (3) provided a 75% equity allocation that is reasonable for this time horizon and risk tolerance; (4) included diversification across U.S. large-cap (via index funds), individual dividend stocks, international equity, and fixed income; (5) documented a suitability analysis; and (6) established a periodic review schedule with documented monitoring. The recommendation has a reasonable basis grounded in the comprehensive financial plan and documented analysis of the client's specific circumstances.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206(4); SEC Rule 206(4)-1; SEC guidance on comprehensive financial planning and suitability.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Comprehensive financial plan addressing human capital, explicit 25-year time horizon, analysis showing 10+ years before liquidation need, and documented quarterly review schedule demonstrate full satisfaction of reasonable investigation and periodic review requirements.
