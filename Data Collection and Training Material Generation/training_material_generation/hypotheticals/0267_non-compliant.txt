Source Folder: unknown
Topic: ia_cherry_picking
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Platinum Wealth Advisors (RIA, $2.1B AUM) manages a growth-equity sleeve across 156 client accounts. On March 15, the firm executed a master order for 75,000 shares of BioGenX at $156.00/share as part of a sector rotation strategy. By 3:58 PM (2 minutes before close), the stock traded to $158.75. The chief investment officer waited until 4:02 PM (after-hours) to allocate shares: 12,000 shares ($1.905M) to three accounts for which the CIO earned highest advisory fees (0.95% vs. firm average 0.65%), and 63,000 shares to remaining accounts. The firm's ADV (written policy) specified 'proportional allocation based on model weightings determined at time of trade initiation.' No amendment or waiver was documented.

QUESTION
--------
Did Platinum Wealth's post-close allocation timing and fee-based selectivity violate the firm's own written allocation policy and IA fiduciary duty?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Platinum Wealth breached Investment Advisers Act Section 206(1) (duty of care) and Rule 17j-1 (requiring consistent application of written policies). The facts show: (1) ADV explicitly required allocation by 'time of trade initiation' (3:56 PM), not post-close; (2) actual allocation delayed until 4:02 PM to observe favorable 1.76% intra-day movement; (3) selectivity based on advisory fee rates contradicts stated model-weighting methodology; (4) no documented exception or waiver. SEC/FINRA enforcement pattern (similar to Wedbush 2019 AWC) treats deviation from documented procedures as breach, especially when timing allows adviser to observe performance before allocation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act Section 206(1) (duty of care and prudence); SEC Rule 17j-1(a) (requiring written policies and procedures for fair allocation)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Delay of allocation timing after performance observation and deviation from written policy without documented approval constitutes procedural breach enabling cherry-picking.
