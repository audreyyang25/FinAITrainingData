ID: 981
Source Folder: 23_International_Comparative
Topic: asic_best_interest_duty_conflicted_recommendation
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An Australian financial adviser employed by a vertically integrated wealth management group recommends that a 58-year-old client consolidate three industry superannuation funds into the group's proprietary MySuper product, which carries higher management fees than the client's existing funds. The adviser's statement of advice documents general retirement consolidation benefits but does not compare fund performance, insurance implications of consolidating, or the fee differential over the client's expected accumulation horizon. The adviser receives a volume-based incentive for in-house product placements, which is disclosed only in a lengthy general FSG rather than the specific SOA.

QUESTION
--------
Does the adviser's recommendation comply with the best interest duty and related obligations under the Corporations Act as interpreted through ASIC's regulatory guidance?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of ASIC best interest duty under Corporations Act §961B and the related safe harbour provisions.

Section 961B requires advisers to act in the client's best interests, which necessitates identifying and investigating all circumstances relevant to the advice, including a comparative analysis of fees, performance, and insurance implications when recommending fund consolidation. The failure to model the fee differential over the accumulation horizon and adequately disclose a volume-based conflict within the specific advice document means the adviser cannot rely on the safe harbour provisions under §961B(2). ASIC's REP 562 specifically identified failure to compare alternative products as a systemic deficiency in best interest duty compliance.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Corporations Act 2001 §961B; §961G (appropriate advice); ASIC RG 175; ASIC Report REP 562

KEY FACTOR
----------
Absence of comparative product analysis and inadequate conflict disclosure in the SOA for a recommendation driven by vertically integrated incentives.
