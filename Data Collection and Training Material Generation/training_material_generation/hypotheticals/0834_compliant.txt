Source Folder: 16_Congressional_Materials

Difficulty: medium
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An investment adviser registered with the SEC manages a $2.4 million discretionary portfolio for a 49-year-old technology executive with a high risk tolerance, a 20-year investment horizon, and documented experience trading individual equities including short positions. Following the client's written request for exposure to short-selling strategies, the adviser allocates 8% of the portfolio ($192,000) to a short-selling hedge fund that charges a 1.5% management fee and 15% performance fee. Prior to execution, the adviser provides the client a written disclosure document detailing the fund's fee structure, the adviser's lack of any revenue-sharing arrangement with the fund, the specific risks of short selling including unlimited loss potential, and an explanation of how the allocation fits within the client's overall diversified portfolio strategy. The adviser's written investment policy statement for this client explicitly contemplates alternative strategies up to 15% of portfolio value.

QUESTION
--------
Does the adviser's recommendation of a short-selling hedge fund allocation to this sophisticated client satisfy the fiduciary duty applicable to registered investment advisers?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Dodd-Frank §913's study and legislative history reaffirmed that registered investment advisers are subject to a fiduciary duty under the Advisers Act, requiring them to act in the client's best interest with full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts. Here, the adviser made no undisclosed profit from the recommendation, provided comprehensive written risk disclosure specifically addressing the mechanics and risks of short selling as identified in CRS background materials on the practice, and documented that the 8% allocation fell within the client's pre-established alternative investment band. The client's documented sophistication, long time horizon, and written request for this exposure further support the recommendation's suitability, and the absence of any revenue-sharing arrangement eliminates the primary conflict concern.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §206 – Fiduciary Duty; Dodd-Frank Act §913 (reaffirming fiduciary standard for investment advisers); SEC guidance on alternative investment disclosures

KEY FACTOR
----------
Full written disclosure of fees, risks, and the absence of any adviser revenue-sharing arrangement, combined with a pre-established investment policy statement authorizing the allocation, satisfies the fiduciary duty despite the complexity of the short-selling product.
