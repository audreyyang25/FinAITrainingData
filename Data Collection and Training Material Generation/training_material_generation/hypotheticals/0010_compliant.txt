FACTS
-----
A 58-year-old widow with $480,000 in liquid assets, low risk tolerance (marked 'preservation of capital, minimal volatility'), and a 7-year time horizon (ages 65-72 required minimum distributions) is recommended a variable annuity with a guaranteed income rider. The VA has: (1) 2.1% annual MER + 0.35% rider fee (2.45% total); (2) 5-year CDSC declining from 5% to 0%; (3) guaranteed floor of 90% of principal + 3% annual step-up. The advisor documents: (1) the client's specific need for income certainty during RMD years and her strong preference for 'no surprises'; (2) comparison to alternatives including: a bond ladder (3-year duration, 2.1% yield but exposed to interest-rate risk), a municipal bond fund (1.8% ER, 2.4% yield, exposed to credit risk), and a diversified equity/bond portfolio (0.4% ER but subject to sequence-of-returns risk in a low-rate environment); (3) analysis showing the rider's cost (2.45% vs. 0.4% for bonds) is justified by the client's explicit risk-aversion and need for certainty; and (4) explanation of the 5-year CDSC decline schedule and alternatives analysis showing the client's 7-year time horizon exceeds the surrender period.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the Reg BI care obligation?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this satisfies the care obligation under Reg BI §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i).

The advisor satisfied the care obligation through affirmative, documented analysis. While the VA's cost (2.45%) is high relative to a bond fund (1.8% ER), the advisor explicitly justified this cost differential by reference to the client's documented needs: income certainty, capital preservation, and low risk tolerance. The alternatives analysis was not conclusory—it addressed the specific trade-offs (bond ladder's interest-rate risk, equity portfolio's sequence-of-returns risk, bond fund's credit risk) and explained why the guaranteed floor and income rider addressed the client's stated preferences better than each alternative. The 5-year CDSC is disclosed and the client's 7-year time horizon is documented as exceeding the surrender period. The advisor also avoided a hidden pitfall: the income rider's 2.45% cost is high, but only for a client with low risk tolerance and explicit need for certainty; for a younger, aggressive client, this same recommendation would violate the care obligation. Here, costs are justified by the client's profile.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Regulation Best Interest §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i) — care obligation to consider the costs, risks, rewards, and alternatives in light of the retail customer's profile.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Detailed, client-specific alternatives analysis documenting that the product's higher cost (2.45% vs. 0.4-1.8%) was justified by the client's documented risk-aversion, need for income certainty, and time horizon.
