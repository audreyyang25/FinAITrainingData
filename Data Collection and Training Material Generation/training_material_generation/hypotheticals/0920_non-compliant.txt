ID: 920
Source Folder: 20_SEC_ALJ_OALJ_Decisions
Topic: undisclosed_conflict_advisory_fee
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Investment adviser Prism Wealth Management recommended that twelve retail clients invest in a private real estate fund managed by an affiliate of Prism's majority owner. Prism received a 1.5% solicitation fee from the fund for each client referral, a conflict that was disclosed in a footnote on page 34 of Prism's Form ADV Part 2A brochure but was never verbally discussed with clients at the point of recommendation. Three clients lacked sufficient liquidity for the illiquid ten-year fund and suffered significant losses when they required early redemption at steep discounts. Prism's written supervisory procedures did not require representatives to affirmatively discuss material conflicts at the time of recommendation.

QUESTION
--------
Did Prism Wealth Management satisfy its disclosure obligations under the Investment Advisers Act with respect to the affiliated fund referral fee?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Investment Advisers Act §206(2) for failure to make full and fair disclosure of a material conflict of interest.

Section 206(2) imposes a fiduciary duty on investment advisers requiring full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts so that clients can provide informed consent; burying a material conflict in boilerplate on page 34 of a lengthy brochure does not satisfy this standard. ALJs have consistently held that disclosure must be sufficiently specific and prominent that a reasonable client would appreciate the conflict's nature and magnitude at the time the recommendation is made. The failure of supervisory procedures to require point-of-sale conflict discussion further evidences a systemic violation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act §206(2); SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau, 375 U.S. 180 (1963); Form ADV disclosure obligations

KEY FACTOR
----------
Disclosure buried in an ADV brochure without affirmative point-of-recommendation discussion does not constitute full and fair disclosure under the fiduciary standard.
