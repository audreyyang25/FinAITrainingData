Topic: arbitration_liability
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Fortune Financial Services, a FINRA member firm headquartered in Monaca, Pennsylvania with approximately 280 registered representatives across 200 branch offices, failed to establish adequate written supervisory procedures for the sale of deferred variable annuities from July 2016 through July 2021. During this period, registered representative Mark Hollis recommended that 74-year-old retired schoolteacher Doris Engel exchange her existing deferred variable annuity—which had passed its surrender period—into a new deferred variable annuity with an 8-year surrender schedule and higher annual fees of 3.45% versus 2.10%, without documenting any material benefit to the client. The firm's supervisory system did not require principal review of variable annuity exchanges or comparison of costs, surrender periods, and benefits between the existing and proposed annuity. Doris filed a FINRA arbitration claim alleging unsuitable recommendations and failure to supervise, seeking $87,000 in damages representing excess costs and surrender charges.

QUESTION
--------
Did the firm's failure to maintain written supervisory procedures requiring principal review and cost-benefit comparison of variable annuity exchanges violate its supervisory obligations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. The firm violated FINRA Rules 3110 (Supervision) and 2111 (Suitability) by failing to establish and maintain a supervisory system reasonably designed to review variable annuity exchange recommendations.

FINRA Rule 2330 specifically requires that a registered principal review and approve each deferred variable annuity exchange transaction, considering whether the customer would incur a surrender charge, lose existing benefits, or face a new surrender period. FINRA Rule 3110 mandates that firms establish and maintain a system to supervise activities of each registered representative reasonably designed to achieve compliance. A five-year gap in supervisory procedures for annuity exchanges, as paralleled in the Fortune Financial AWC, constitutes a systemic supervision failure.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision); FINRA Rule 2330 (Members' Responsibilities Regarding Deferred Variable Annuities); FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm lacked any written supervisory procedures requiring principal review and documented cost-benefit analysis of variable annuity exchanges over a five-year period.
