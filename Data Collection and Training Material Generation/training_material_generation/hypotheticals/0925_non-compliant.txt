ID: 925
Source Folder: 21_FINRA_OHO_Decisions
Topic: excessive_trading_churning
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Broker Diana Flores managed a discretionary account for a 61-year-old semi-retired engineer with a moderate risk tolerance and a long-term growth objective. Over an 18-month period, Flores executed 312 trades in the account, generating an annualized turnover rate of 14.2 and a cost-to-equity ratio of 28%, meaning the account needed to earn 28% annually just to break even on commissions. The customer's net account value declined by $47,000 over the same period despite broadly favorable equity market conditions. Flores earned $63,000 in commissions from the account during this timeframe.

QUESTION
--------
Does the trading activity in this discretionary account constitute excessive trading in violation of FINRA Rules 2111 and 2010?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of FINRA Rules 2111 and 2010 (excessive trading/churning).

Quantitative suitability under Rule 2111 prohibits a registered representative from recommending a series of transactions that are excessive in light of the customer's investment profile. A cost-to-equity ratio of 28% and annualized turnover rate of 14.2 far exceed the thresholds courts and regulators typically associate with churning, indicating the trading was designed to generate commissions rather than serve the customer's long-term growth objective. The $63,000 commission disparity against a $47,000 customer loss further evidences that the broker's interests were placed ahead of the customer's.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Quantitative Suitability); FINRA Rule 2010 (Standards of Commercial Honor); FINRA Rule 2010 as applied to churning

KEY FACTOR
----------
A 28% cost-to-equity ratio and 14.2 annualized turnover rate demonstrate trading at a frequency inconsistent with the customer's investment profile and driven by commission generation.
