Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An SEC-registered investment adviser with $250 million in AUM provides advisory services to 45 clients over the age of 75, many of whom hold concentrated positions in dividend-paying equities and municipal bonds. In response to SEC EXAMS examination priorities emphasizing protections for senior investors, the firm implemented a Senior Client Protection Program in 2023 that includes: (1) designation of a trusted contact person for each senior client account, (2) enhanced suitability documentation for any portfolio changes involving more than 10% of account value, (3) cognitive assessment protocols where advisers document any observed signs of diminished capacity and escalate to the CCO, (4) a policy permitting temporary holds on disbursements when exploitation is suspected, with mandatory documentation and client notification within two business days, and (5) semi-annual portfolio reviews specifically evaluating liquidity needs, risk tolerance changes, and withdrawal patterns. In January 2025, the firm placed a 48-hour hold on a $95,000 wire transfer from the account of an 82-year-old client after the assigned adviser noted the request was inconsistent with the client's typical activity and the client appeared confused about the destination of the funds. The CCO contacted the designated trusted contact person—the client's adult daughter—who confirmed she was unaware of the transfer. The hold was documented, the client was notified in writing within one business day, and the matter was referred to Adult Protective Services. The wire was ultimately not processed after the client, upon further reflection with family present, confirmed she had been contacted by an unknown party requesting the funds.

QUESTION
--------
Did the adviser's senior investor protection protocols and temporary disbursement hold comply with regulatory expectations and fiduciary obligations, or did the hold constitute an improper restriction on the client's account access?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

SEC EXAMS has consistently identified protection of senior investors as a priority examination focus, and the framework established by FINRA Rule 2165 provides a regulatory blueprint for temporary holds when financial exploitation is reasonably suspected. The adviser's actions were proportionate—a 48-hour hold with prompt documentation, client notification within one business day, contact with the designated trusted contact, and referral to Adult Protective Services. The firm's broader Senior Client Protection Program demonstrates systematic compliance with examination priorities rather than ad hoc decision-making. The temporary restriction on account access was a reasonable exercise of fiduciary duty to protect a vulnerable client from suspected exploitation, not an improper limitation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act §§ 206(1) and 206(2); FINRA Rule 2165 (Financial Exploitation of Specified Adults); SEC EXAMS FY 2025/2026 Examination Priorities (Senior Investors); NASAA Model Act to Protect Vulnerable Adults

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser's temporary disbursement hold was supported by documented reasonable suspicion of financial exploitation, executed under a pre-established senior protection policy, and accompanied by prompt notification and appropriate escalation—satisfying both fiduciary obligations and regulatory expectations for senior investor protections.
