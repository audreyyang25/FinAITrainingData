Topic: compliance_program_design
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A mid-size broker-dealer with 180 registered representatives implements Regulation Best Interest compliance policies on June 30, 2020, the effective date. However, the firm's compliance program consists solely of distributing a written Reg BI policy manual to representatives via email, with no live training sessions, no assessments, and no mechanism to verify that representatives read or understood the materials. A 72-year-old retired teacher is subsequently recommended a high-commission variable annuity with a 7-year surrender period by a representative who, when questioned in a subsequent examination, cannot articulate the basic components of the Care Obligation or explain why the annuity was in the client's best interest. The SEC Division of Examinations identifies this deficiency during a routine exam aligned with its stated priorities of reviewing Reg BI compliance programs.

QUESTION
--------
Did the broker-dealer satisfy its supervisory and compliance obligations under Reg BI by distributing a written policy manual without conducting training or verifying representative understanding?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the firm's Reg BI Compliance Obligation and FINRA supervisory requirements.

The Reg BI Compliance Obligation requires broker-dealers to establish, maintain, and enforce written policies and procedures reasonably designed to achieve compliance with Reg BI. Merely distributing a manual without training, testing, or follow-up monitoring is not reasonably designed to achieve compliance. FINRA Rule 3110 similarly requires firms to supervise their associated persons' activities, which includes ensuring they are adequately trained on applicable regulatory obligations. The SEC's Division of Examinations has specifically identified Reg BI compliance program design and implementation as examination priorities.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iv) – Reg BI Compliance Obligation; FINRA Rule 3110 – Supervision

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm failed to implement any meaningful training or verification mechanism, rendering its Reg BI compliance program a paper exercise rather than a reasonably designed system.
