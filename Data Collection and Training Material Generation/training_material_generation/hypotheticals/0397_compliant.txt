FACTS
-----
A Massachusetts-registered broker-dealer (MA fiduciary rule, 2020) advises a Boston-based client, age 55, with $920,000 in investable assets and a 12-year investment horizon. The adviser recommends a diversified portfolio: 55% domestic equity index funds (0.04% expense ratio), 25% international equity ETFs (0.07% ER), 15% bond index funds (0.03% ER), 5% alternative real assets via low-cost fund (0.35% ER). Blended cost: 0.088%. The adviser discloses in writing (before engagement) that under MA law, broker-dealers owe a fiduciary duty equal to or exceeding federal Reg BI, documents the client's risk tolerance (moderate), and explains the recommendation's rationale and conflicts of interest. The firm receives no soft-dollar benefits on these funds and earns a flat advisory fee of 0.75% annually (all-in). Annual turnover is 8%. Client signs an updated Form CRS disclosing MA fiduciary status and annual cost illustration.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation comply with Massachusetts fiduciary duty for broker-dealers, which requires a standard at least as protective as federal Reg BI?

ANSWER
------
Yes. The adviser meets both Massachusetts fiduciary duty and federal Reg BI obligations. Massachusetts fiduciary rule (effective 2020) requires broker-dealers owed a duty to retail customers to act in the customer's best interest, disclose conflicts, and avoid self-dealing. Here, the adviser: (1) documents client goals and risk profile; (2) recommends low-cost, diversified index-based products; (3) discloses upfront (in Form CRS supplement) that MA law imposes fiduciary duty; (4) charges transparent all-in fee (0.75%) with no hidden conflicts; (5) avoids high-turnover or commission-generating strategies; (6) provides written conflict-of-interest disclosure; (7) maintains no undisclosed soft-dollar arrangements. The low blended cost (0.088%) and passive index structure demonstrate care and loyalty. This exceeds both MA state standard and federal Reg BI's best-interest requirement.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Massachusetts Fiduciary Rule (2020) — Broker-dealer fiduciary duty to retail customers; 17 CFR § 240.15l-1 (Reg BI) — Best Interest Obligation

KEY FACTOR
----------
Proactive written disclosure of MA fiduciary status, low-cost index strategy, no conflicts, and transparent fee structure satisfy the higher MA standard as well as federal Reg BI.
