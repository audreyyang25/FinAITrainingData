Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
James Whitfield, a broker-dealer agent registered in Massachusetts, recommends that Eleanor Graves, a 58-year-old executive with $890,000 in investable assets, a $175,000 annual salary, and a moderate-to-aggressive risk profile, invest $150,000 in a non-traded REIT with a 7-year liquidity horizon. James documents that Eleanor has expressed a desire for portfolio diversification beyond publicly traded equities and fixed income, that she has no anticipated liquidity needs for the committed capital within the next 10 years, and that she maintains $740,000 in liquid investments outside this position. He discloses the 6% upfront commission, the limited liquidity and valuation uncertainty of non-traded REITs, and provides Eleanor with a written comparison of two alternative non-traded REITs and a publicly traded REIT ETF, explaining why the selected product's income distribution history and sector focus align with her diversification goals. Eleanor signs a detailed acknowledgment of risks and compensation disclosures.

QUESTION
--------
Does this non-traded REIT recommendation by a Massachusetts-registered broker-dealer agent satisfy the state fiduciary standard under 950 CMR 12.207?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Massachusetts' fiduciary standard requires broker-dealer agents to act in the client's best interest and not place their own interests ahead of the client's. While non-traded REITs carry higher commissions and limited liquidity, the recommendation is not per se prohibited. Here, the agent documented the client's suitability profile showing substantial liquid assets, a long time horizon, and a specific diversification objective. The agent disclosed compensation, provided a comparative analysis of alternatives, and obtained a written risk acknowledgment. The allocation of approximately 17% to an illiquid product for a high-income client with substantial remaining liquidity is within reasonable bounds. The thorough documentation and transparent disclosure process satisfies both the care and loyalty components of the Massachusetts fiduciary duty.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
MA 950 CMR 12.207 – Fiduciary Conduct Standard for Broker-Dealers, Agents, Investment Advisers, and Investment Adviser Representatives

KEY FACTOR
----------
The agent's thorough documentation of suitability analysis, comparative alternatives review, full compensation disclosure, and written risk acknowledgment demonstrated compliance with the Massachusetts fiduciary standard despite the product's inherent illiquidity and higher commission.
