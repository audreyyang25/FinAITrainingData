Source Folder: 16_Congressional_Materials
Topic: legislative_history
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered broker-dealer representative at a regional firm recommended that a 72-year-old retired schoolteacher with a $180,000 IRA rollover and a conservative risk profile purchase a non-traded REIT paying a 7% commission. The representative disclosed only that the product carried 'some risk' and failed to explain the 10-year illiquidity period, the 15% total fees embedded in the offering, or that comparable publicly traded REITs were available with lower costs. The representative's branch manager reviewed the ticket but did not flag the recommendation for supervisory review despite the client's age and stated income need. Congress's Dodd-Frank §913 study specifically identified broker-dealer conflicts arising from commission-based compensation as a primary driver of investor harm requiring heightened disclosure obligations.

QUESTION
--------
Did the representative's failure to disclose material costs, liquidity restrictions, and conflicts of interest in recommending the non-traded REIT violate applicable standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Reg BI's Disclosure Obligation and the congressional investor protection principles codified through Dodd-Frank §913.

Dodd-Frank §913 directed the SEC to study and address the gap between broker-dealer and investment adviser standards, culminating in Reg BI, which requires full and fair disclosure of all material facts about the recommendation including costs, risks, and conflicts. The 15% embedded fee, 10-year illiquidity period, and 7% commission each constitute material facts the client needed to make an informed decision. The branch manager's failure to escalate the recommendation of an illiquid, high-commission product to a 72-year-old conservative investor also implicates the firm's Compliance Obligation under Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iv).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Disclosure Obligation; Dodd-Frank Act §913(g) (directing SEC to establish broker-dealer standard of conduct).

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative failed to disclose material costs, liquidity restrictions, and commission-driven conflicts as required by Reg BI's Disclosure Obligation, which was directly mandated by the Dodd-Frank §913 congressional directive.
