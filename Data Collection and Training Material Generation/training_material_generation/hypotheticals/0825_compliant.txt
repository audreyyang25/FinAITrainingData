Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer representative recommends that a 38-year-old public school teacher with a $95,000 taxable brokerage account and a stated moderate-growth objective purchase shares of a no-load S&P 500 index mutual fund with a 0.04% expense ratio, alongside a $12,000 allocation to a short-selling strategy ETF that uses regulatory short-selling mechanics consistent with SEC-regulated market practices. The representative provides a written Form CRS and a point-of-recommendation disclosure explaining that the short ETF is designed as a 5-10% portfolio hedge, carries risks of loss if markets rise, and that the firm receives no revenue sharing from either fund company. The representative documents in the client file that the short ETF allocation represents 12.6% of the portfolio, within the client's stated willingness to accept hedging strategies, and that the overall portfolio cost is 0.09% blended expense ratio.

QUESTION
--------
Does the broker-dealer representative's recommendation of a low-cost index fund paired with a regulated short-selling ETF as a portfolio hedge, with full written disclosure and no conflict of interest, satisfy Reg BI's Care and Disclosure Obligations?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Reg BI's Care Obligation is satisfied because the representative understood the characteristics and risks of both products, considered the client's investment profile including stated willingness to use hedging strategies, and selected the lowest-cost available instruments with a blended expense ratio of 0.09%. GAO findings on Reg BI implementation identified low-cost product selection and documented client profile matching as hallmarks of compliant Care Obligation analysis. The Disclosure Obligation is satisfied by the written Form CRS and point-of-recommendation disclosure explaining the hedge rationale, upside-market risks, and absence of revenue sharing conflicts. The 12.6% short ETF allocation is proportionate, documented, and consistent with the client's stated objectives, distinguishing this from the speculative or excessive-cost patterns flagged in GAO Reg BI compliance reviews.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) and (ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation and Disclosure Obligation; SEC short-selling regulatory framework (consistent with CRS Short Selling: Background and Policy Issues)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented suitability analysis, lowest-cost product selection, proportionate hedge allocation, and written conflict-free disclosure collectively demonstrate full Reg BI compliance despite the surface-level complexity of including a short-selling product.
