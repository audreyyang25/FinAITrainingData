Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 67-year-old male client with $485,000 in liquid assets, conservative risk tolerance, and a stated need for current income to supplement his $35,000 annual pension approached his FINRA-registered broker. The broker recommended a portfolio of six non-traded real estate investment trusts (REITs) totaling $425,000 (88% of assets), each with upfront fees of 8-10%, annual advisory fees of 1.5%, and redemption restrictions of 7+ years. The client had never invested in illiquid alternatives before. The broker documented only 'conservative investor' in the suitability memo without analyzing the client's actual liquidity needs, time horizon, or ability to access funds.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the FINRA Suitability Rule 2111 requirement that recommendations be suitable based on the customer's investment profile?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates FINRA Suitability Rule 2111 because the recommendation is not suitable given the customer's specific profile.

This recommendation fails the customer-specific prong of Rule 2111. The client is 67 years old, relies on the account for current income to supplement his pension, and has a stated conservative risk tolerance. Non-traded REITs with 7+ year redemption restrictions and 8-10% upfront fees are fundamentally unsuitable for a retiree with immediate income needs and no prior alternative investment experience. The 88% concentration in illiquid assets contradicts the customer's income objective and conservative profile. The broker's documentation—merely stating 'conservative investor'—shows a failure to conduct a specific, fact-based suitability analysis. FINRA has repeatedly sanctioned brokers for recommending illiquid alternatives to older clients with income needs (see AWC cases involving inappropriate REIT and interval fund recommendations). The high upfront fees (8-10%) further suggest the recommendation was driven by compensation rather than customer benefit.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) requires that a member have a 'reasonable basis to believe' a recommendation is suitable, considering the customer's age, other investments, financial situation and needs, investment experience, investment objectives, and other relevant circumstances. The three-prong test requires: (1) reasonable basis suitability; (2) customer-specific suitability; and (3) quantitative suitability (no excessive trading for commissions).

KEY FACTOR
----------
The customer's stated need for current income to supplement a fixed pension, combined with a 7+ year liquidity restriction, creates a fundamental mismatch between the recommendation and the customer's stated financial needs and objective.
