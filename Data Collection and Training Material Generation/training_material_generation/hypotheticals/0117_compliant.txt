FACTS
-----
A 58-year-old client with $450,000 invested in a non-qualified account seeks tax-deferral and long-term growth (15+ year time horizon). The broker recommends a variable annuity with a 6-year surrender charge, 1.2% M&E fee, and 0.60% subaccount fees (~1.8% total). The client has no liquidity needs before age 65 and is in a 32% federal/state tax bracket. The broker documents the client's tax situation, long time horizon, low probability of early withdrawal, and explains the surrender charge mechanics.

QUESTION
--------
Does this variable annuity recommendation satisfy FINRA Rule 2330 and Reg BI?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation is compliant.

The recommendation satisfies Rule 2330 because: (1) the client has a sufficiently long time horizon (15+ years) to allow surrender charges to be recovered through tax deferral benefits; (2) the client's stated tax bracket (32%) makes tax deferral economically meaningful in this non-qualified account context; (3) the client has explicitly stated no liquidity needs until age 65, reducing surrender charge risk; (4) the broker documented the suitability rationale, including the tax-deferral benefit analysis and surrender charge explanation; and (5) the fee structure (~1.8% total) is reasonable for a variable annuity with active subaccount management, particularly when tax benefits are factored in. The SEC's Reg BI guidance and FINRA case law (e.g., AWC against Raymond James, 2020) recognize that variable annuities can be suitable for long-term, tax-conscious, non-liquidity-constrained investors.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330(a)(1) (reasonable basis suitability) and Reg BI § 240.15l-1(d)(1) (care obligation regarding costs, risks, and benefits relative to client profile).

KEY FACTOR
----------
Long time horizon (15+ years), explicit absence of liquidity needs, stated tax-deferral motive, and thorough documentation of suitability reasoning all support compliance.
