Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An investment adviser registered with the SEC manages a $2.1 million discretionary account for a 45-year-old technology executive whose investment policy statement specifies a growth-oriented objective with 15-year time horizon, high risk tolerance, and specific authorization for up to 20% allocation in alternative investments. The adviser allocates $380,000 (approximately 18%) to a private placement in a venture-stage digital asset securities fund that qualifies as a security under SEC regulations governing capital markets, consistent with the client's signed alternative investment authorization. The adviser provides a detailed written disclosure documenting the illiquidity risk, lack of redemption rights for 7 years, speculative nature, and the adviser's $15,000 annual management fee on the overall account, which includes no additional compensation from the fund sponsor. The client, an accredited investor, acknowledges the disclosure in writing and the allocation remains within the IPS parameters.

QUESTION
--------
Does the investment adviser's allocation of 18% of a growth-oriented accredited investor's portfolio to a digital asset securities private placement, with full written disclosure and no third-party compensation, comply with the adviser's fiduciary duty?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

An investment adviser's fiduciary duty under the Advisers Act requires acting in the client's best interest and making full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts, both of which are satisfied here. The allocation is within the client's signed IPS parameters, appropriate for the 15-year time horizon and high risk tolerance of an accredited investor, and does not exceed the authorized alternative investment ceiling. The absence of third-party compensation eliminates the primary conflict risk, and the written acknowledgment by a sophisticated client of illiquidity and speculative risk satisfies the disclosure prong of fiduciary duty. Dodd-Frank §913's legislative history confirms Congress intended to preserve—not eliminate—the investment adviser fiduciary standard that permits suitable alternative allocations with proper disclosure.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §206 – Fiduciary Duty; SEC guidance on digital asset securities (consistent with CRS capital markets framework); Dodd-Frank Act §913 (affirming IA fiduciary standard)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Full written conflict disclosure, absence of third-party compensation, client-authorized IPS parameters, and accredited investor status collectively satisfy all elements of the investment adviser fiduciary standard.
