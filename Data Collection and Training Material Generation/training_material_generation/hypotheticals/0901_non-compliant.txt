Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Nicholas Sarto, a registered representative at a mid-size broker-dealer, was convicted in federal district court of wire fraud in connection with a scheme to divert $215,000 from elderly client accounts by forging withdrawal forms and directing proceeds to a personal bank account. Following his conviction, Sarto's broker-dealer did not file a Uniform Termination Notice and Sarto continued to appear on the firm's books as a registered representative for 90 days post-conviction. During this period, Sarto solicited two new clients, a 74-year-old retired nurse and a 68-year-old veteran, and collected $18,500 in new account fees. The firm's compliance department had received the court's sentencing document but failed to route it to the registration department.

QUESTION
--------
Does a broker-dealer's failure to terminate a registered representative's association following a federal fraud conviction, thereby allowing continued client solicitation, constitute a violation of Exchange Act Section 15(b)?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a statutory disqualification violation under Exchange Act §15(b)(4) and §15(b)(6), and constitutes a supervision failure exposing the firm to sanctions.

Exchange Act §15(b)(4)(B) mandates that a broker-dealer may not maintain association with a person subject to statutory disqualification, which includes criminal convictions for securities fraud or fraud involving financial transactions. As illustrated in Skaltsounis, the Commission will impose a permanent bar on individuals convicted of fraud, and broker-dealers that knowingly or negligently maintain such associations face independent sanctions. The firm's failure to route the sentencing document through compliance reflects a systemic supervisory breakdown that allowed a disqualified person to continue harming retail investors.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Securities Exchange Act of 1934 §15(b)(4)(B) (Statutory Disqualification); §15(b)(6); Investment Advisers Act §203(f) — as applied in In the Matter of Nicholas D. Skaltsounis, Initial Decision Release No. 729 (Dec. 31, 2014)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Continuing to employ a convicted fraudster and allowing client solicitation post-conviction constitutes statutory disqualification under Exchange Act §15(b)(4)(B), mirroring the bar imposed in Skaltsounis for association with a broker-dealer following a criminal conviction.
