ID: 971
Source Folder: 23_International_Comparative
Topic: asic_best_interest_duty_conflicted_remuneration
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An Australian financial adviser holds an Australian Financial Services Licence and recommends a client roll over their industry superannuation fund balance into a retail managed investment scheme administered by the adviser's dealer group, which pays the adviser a 1.2% ongoing trail commission. The adviser documents that the retail fund offers 'broader investment choice' but does not model or compare the client's projected retirement outcome under both options, nor does the advice document address the fee differential or the impact of losing the industry fund's group insurance cover. The client is 52 years old with a moderate risk profile and a retirement horizon of approximately thirteen years.

QUESTION
--------
Does the adviser's superannuation switching recommendation satisfy ASIC's best interest duty and related obligations under the Corporations Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of ASIC §961B best interest duty and §961G appropriate advice obligation under the Corporations Act 2001.

Section 961B requires advisers to act in the client's best interests, which demands a comparative analysis of the client's existing and proposed arrangements including fees, investment performance, and ancillary benefits such as insurance—none of which was adequately addressed in the statement of advice. The receipt of trail commission from the dealer group-affiliated product constitutes conflicted remuneration under §961J, and the failure to model comparative retirement outcomes means the adviser cannot demonstrate the advice was appropriate under §961G.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Corporations Act 2001 §961B (best interest duty); §961G (appropriate advice); §961J (conflicted remuneration); ASIC RG 175

KEY FACTOR
----------
Adviser failed to conduct a comparative retirement outcome analysis and received conflicted remuneration without demonstrating the recommendation served the client's best interests.
