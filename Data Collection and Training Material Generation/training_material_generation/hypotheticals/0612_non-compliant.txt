Source Folder: 04_RegBI_Compliance_Guidance
Topic: reg_bi_conflict
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Marcus Henley, a registered representative at a small broker-dealer with 30 registered representatives, recommended that seven retail customers invest a combined $385,000 in pre-IPO private placement interests in three early-stage technology companies between January 2021 and June 2021. Henley sourced the offerings and brought them to his firm for approval. In connection with each offering, Henley received a selling compensation of 15%, which exceeded the 10% maximum disclosed in the offering memoranda provided to customers. Henley did not disclose the additional 5% compensation to any of the seven customers, nor did he document any analysis of the risks, rewards, and costs of the private placements relative to other reasonably available alternatives. Several of the customers were retirees with moderate risk tolerances and annual incomes under $75,000.

QUESTION
--------
Did the registered representative comply with Regulation Best Interest's Disclosure and Care Obligations when recommending these private placement interests?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates the Reg BI Disclosure Obligation and Care Obligation.

Under the Reg BI Disclosure Obligation, the representative was required to provide full and fair disclosure of all material facts relating to the scope and terms of the relationship, including all compensation received in connection with the recommendation. Receiving compensation 50% above the disclosed maximum in the offering documents and failing to disclose this to customers is a material omission. Additionally, the Care Obligation required Henley to exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill to have a reasonable basis to believe each recommendation was in the customer's best interest, including understanding the risks, rewards, and costs. Recommending illiquid, high-risk pre-IPO private placements to retirees with moderate risk tolerances and modest incomes without documented analysis of these factors or consideration of alternatives fails the Care Obligation. This fact pattern closely mirrors the FINRA complaint against Gustave Schmidt (No. 2022074096805).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Disclosure Obligation

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative received undisclosed excess compensation exceeding the offering memorandum maximum by 50% and failed to perform a reasonable-basis analysis of the private placements' suitability for moderate-risk retirees.
