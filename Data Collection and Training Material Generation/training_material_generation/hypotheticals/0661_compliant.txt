Source Folder: 08_State_Fiduciary_Rules
Topic: state_fiduciary_bd
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer registered in Washington state conducts a retrospective review of all financial advisors who serviced brokerage customers that also hired the firm's investment advisory arm during 2022-2023. The firm's compliance department identifies 14 accounts where advisors recommended moving assets from brokerage accounts into advisory accounts, resulting in higher ongoing fees. For each of the 14 accounts, the firm's supervisory system had flagged the transitions at the time they occurred, the supervising principal had documented a review confirming the advisory fee structure was cost-justified by the client's trading frequency and need for ongoing portfolio management, and the clients had received written disclosures comparing brokerage versus advisory costs. The firm maintains all records of these reviews in its books and records consistent with NASAA model recordkeeping requirements.

QUESTION
--------
Does the broker-dealer's supervision of brokerage-to-advisory account transitions satisfy state regulatory expectations as reflected in the multistate Edward Jones investigation framework?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The multistate investigation into Edward Jones, coordinated through NASAA, focused on inadequate supervision of financial advisors who serviced brokerage customers that also hired the firm's investment adviser during a comparable review period. The key deficiency in that enforcement action was the failure to adequately supervise recommendations to move assets from lower-cost brokerage accounts to higher-cost advisory accounts. Here, the firm proactively flagged such transitions through its supervisory system, the supervising principal documented a cost-benefit analysis for each transition, clients received written cost comparison disclosures, and all records were maintained consistent with NASAA model recordkeeping rules.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Washington Securities Act RCW 21.20; NASAA Multistate Supervisory Standards (as referenced in Edward Jones Consent Order); NASAA Model Rule 203(a)-2 Recordkeeping Requirements

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's contemporaneous supervisory review, documented cost-justification analysis, and written client disclosures for each brokerage-to-advisory transition directly address the supervisory failures identified in the multistate Edward Jones investigation.
