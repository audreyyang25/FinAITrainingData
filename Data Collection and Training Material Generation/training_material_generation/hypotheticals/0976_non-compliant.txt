ID: 976
Source Folder: 23_International_Comparative
Topic: asic_best_interest_duty_conflicted_recommendation
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An Australian financial adviser recommends that a 58-year-old client consolidate three existing superannuation accounts into a single platform product managed by the adviser's licensee, which pays the licensee a significantly higher asset-based fee than the client's current funds. The adviser documents the recommendation by noting diversification reduction and fee transparency as client benefits but does not conduct a comparative analysis of the client's existing funds' performance, insurance cover, or total cost. The adviser's file note does not address why the recommended product is better suited to the client's retirement objectives than the existing arrangements.

QUESTION
--------
Does the adviser's conduct satisfy ASIC's best interest duty where the recommendation benefits the licensee financially and lacks comparative analysis of the client's existing products?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of ASIC Corporations Act §961B, which requires advisers to act in the best interests of the client and prioritise the client's interests over those of the adviser or licensee.

Section 961B requires an adviser to take reasonable steps to act in the client's best interests, which includes investigating and considering the client's existing financial products before recommending a switch. The absence of a comparative analysis of insurance cover, fund performance, and total cost means the adviser cannot demonstrate that the recommended strategy is in the client's best interests. The conflict priority rule under §961J further requires that where a conflict exists, the client's interests must be prioritised, which was not demonstrated here.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
ASIC Corporations Act 2001 §961B; §961J (Conflict Priority Rule); ASIC RG 175

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommendation to switch superannuation funds made without comparative product analysis in a conflicted remuneration context, violating §961B and §961J of the Corporations Act.
