ID: 966
Source Folder: 23_International_Comparative
Topic: asic_best_interest_duty_conflicted_recommendation
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An Australian financial adviser provides a Statement of Advice recommending that a 58-year-old client, with $420,000 in superannuation and ten years to retirement, roll over her entire existing industry super fund balance into a retail managed discretionary account managed by the adviser's licensee. The industry fund offered comparable investment options at 0.6% per annum in fees, while the recommended product charges 1.9% per annum. The adviser's SOA identifies the client's primary goal as maximising retirement savings but does not analyse the fee differential, does not consider whether the existing fund could meet the client's objectives, and does not document any basis for preferring the retail product. The adviser receives a volume-based trail commission from the licensee.

QUESTION
--------
Does the adviser's recommendation to switch superannuation funds, without analysing the existing product's adequacy or the material fee differential, satisfy ASIC's best interest duty?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of ASIC Corporations Act §961B (best interest duty) and §961G (appropriate advice obligation), as the adviser failed to prioritise the client's interests and did not demonstrate that the recommended product was more likely to achieve the client's objectives than the existing fund.

Section 961B requires an adviser to act in the best interests of the client by, among other steps, identifying the client's relevant circumstances, conducting a reasonable investigation into financial products that could meet those objectives, and basing the advice on that investigation. The failure to analyse the existing fund's capability to meet retirement objectives or to account for the 1.3% annual fee disadvantage means the adviser cannot satisfy the safe harbour steps under §961B(2). Furthermore, the trail commission creates a conflict of interest that must be prioritised in the client's favour under §961J, which the adviser plainly failed to do.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
ASIC Corporations Act 2001 §961B; §961G; §961J (conflict priority rule); ASIC RG 175

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommending a materially more expensive product without investigating the existing product's adequacy, while receiving conflicted remuneration, breaches the best interest duty and conflict priority rule.
