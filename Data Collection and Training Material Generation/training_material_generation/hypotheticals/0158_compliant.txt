FACTS
-----
A 42-year-old employed client with $280,000 in a taxable brokerage account and a stated risk tolerance of 'aggressive growth' works with a fee-only RIA who manages the portfolio on a discretionary basis. Over two years, the advisor executes 18 trades (annualized turnover ratio of 0.6x), rebalancing quarterly between a 70/30 equity-bond allocation and occasionally harvesting tax losses. The advisor charges 0.75% AUM fee. The client receives quarterly reports documenting the rebalancing rationale and tax-loss harvesting benefit (approximately $2,100 annually).

QUESTION
--------
Does this trading activity comply with suitability and anti-churning obligations?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant with FINRA Rule 2111(a) and FINRA Rule 2010.

The advisor's trading pattern—0.6x annualized turnover on a discretionary aggressive-growth account—falls within acceptable parameters for actively managed portfolios. Quarterly rebalancing is a recognized portfolio management technique documented in the investment policy statement and disclosed to the client. The advisor's fee-only compensation model (0.75% AUM) eliminates commission bias and conflict-of-interest, which is a key factor in anti-churning analysis. Tax-loss harvesting generates documented client benefit independent of transaction volume. The suitability is clear: a 42-year-old with aggressive risk tolerance appropriately receives tactical rebalancing rather than buy-and-hold passive investing. The advisor's written quarterly reports demonstrate deliberate portfolio management rather than commission-driven trading. This matches FINRA guidance that reasonable trading frequency coupled with fee-only compensation and documented rationale satisfies Rule 2111(a).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) (suitability); FINRA Rule 2010; FINRA Regulatory Notice 02-36 (guidance on determining reasonableness of trading frequency and compensation structure).

KEY FACTOR
----------
The combination of a 0.6x annualized turnover ratio (within industry norms), fee-only compensation structure (eliminating commission bias), and documented quarterly rebalancing/tax-loss harvesting rationale establishes compliance with anti-churning obligations.
