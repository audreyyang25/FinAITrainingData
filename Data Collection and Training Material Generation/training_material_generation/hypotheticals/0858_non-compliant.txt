Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Marcus T. Hendricks, a registered representative at a mid-sized broker-dealer, recommended that his 68-year-old client, Ruth Vasquez, roll over her $215,000 IRA into a suite of high-commission variable annuity products offered exclusively through his firm's proprietary product shelf. During the March 2019 Congressional hearing 'Putting Investors First? Examining the SEC's Best Interest Rule,' lawmakers and witnesses highlighted that a central concern motivating Reg BI's passage was broker-dealers steering retail customers toward higher-cost proprietary products due to conflicts of interest without meaningful disclosure. Hendricks disclosed the commission structure only in a dense, 40-page prospectus provided at closing and never orally summarized the conflict or offered a comparison to lower-cost non-proprietary alternatives that would have produced equivalent risk-adjusted returns. Vasquez, a retired school librarian with moderate risk tolerance and a fixed pension income of $1,800 per month, was not informed that Hendricks would earn a 7% upfront commission ($15,050) versus a 1% trail on the comparable non-proprietary option. No record of a best interest analysis comparing alternatives was retained in the client file.

QUESTION
--------
Did Hendricks satisfy Reg BI's Care and Conflict of Interest Obligations when recommending the proprietary variable annuity to Vasquez?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the Reg BI Care Obligation and Conflict of Interest Obligation.

Dodd-Frank §913 directed the SEC to study and address gaps in standards of care for retail customers, expressly including the problem of broker-dealers recommending higher-cost products due to undisclosed conflicts of interest — the precise legislative concern aired at the March 2019 House hearing. Reg BI's Care Obligation requires a broker-dealer to have a reasonable basis to believe a recommendation is in the retail customer's best interest, including evaluating reasonably available alternatives, which Hendricks failed to document. The Conflict of Interest Obligation separately requires broker-dealers to establish, maintain, and enforce written policies and procedures to identify and, at a minimum, disclose or eliminate material conflicts — here, the 7% proprietary commission conflict was buried in a prospectus rather than meaningfully disclosed. The absence of a documented comparative analysis and the steering toward a materially more expensive product without adequate oral disclosure renders both obligations violated.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Conflict of Interest Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; Dodd-Frank Act §913 (legislative mandate to address standards-of-care gaps for retail customers)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Hendricks failed to document any comparative analysis of reasonably available alternatives and buried a material $15,050 commission conflict in a prospectus rather than providing clear, meaningful disclosure, violating both the Care and Conflict of Interest Obligations under Reg BI.
