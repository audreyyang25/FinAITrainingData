Source Folder: unknown
Topic: ia_duty_of_care
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 55-year-old client with $500,000 in an IRA stated her objective as 'steady income for 10 years until retirement, then principal preservation.' Her adviser initially recommended a 50/50 portfolio of dividend-paying stocks and investment-grade bonds. For the past 5 years, the adviser has not rebalanced the portfolio despite the equity portion growing to 72% due to stock appreciation. The client has received no communication about the drift, and the allocation is now inconsistent with her stated 10-year income objective.

QUESTION
--------
Does the adviser's failure to rebalance comply with the duty of care?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the investment adviser's duty of care.

The adviser has breached the duty of care by failing to periodically review and rebalance the portfolio in accordance with the client's stated objectives. After 5 years of equity appreciation, the portfolio has drifted from 50/50 to 72/28, materially increasing risk exposure beyond what is consistent with a client who seeks 'steady income' with a specific 10-year horizon. The SEC requires advisers to conduct periodic reviews and rebalance when allocations drift materially from the stated objective. The adviser's failure to communicate with the client about this drift, and failure to restore the allocation to one suitable for income generation, demonstrates breach of the duty of care. This is particularly serious because the client is now overexposed to equity risk at age 55 with only 10 years until the stated goal of principal preservation begins.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206(4); SEC Rule 206(4)-1; SEC IA Examination Priorities (duty to periodically review and rebalance); FINRA case 2015-046144 (similar rebalancing failure).

KEY FACTOR
----------
Five-year failure to rebalance despite material portfolio drift (50/50 to 72/28 equity) and explicit lack of communication demonstrates failure to conduct periodic review required under Section 206(4).
