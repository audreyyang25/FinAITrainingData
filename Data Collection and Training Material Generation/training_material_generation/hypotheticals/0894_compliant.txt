Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A global asset manager structured as a holding company across real estate, infrastructure, and private equity sectors seeks an interpretive determination—similar to CFTC Letter No. 17-68—that certain of its holding company subsidiaries do not fall within the definition of a 'commodity pool' under Section 1a(10) of the Commodity Exchange Act and CFTC Regulation 4.10(d)(1), because their commodity derivatives usage is solely incidental to operating their underlying businesses (e.g., fuel hedges for logistics subsidiaries). The asset manager's registered investment adviser affiliate separately manages a registered fund that, following the February 8, 2012 CFTC rule amendments, registered as a CPO because it invests in commodity-linked derivatives as a principal investment strategy. The adviser maintains completely separate compliance programs for the registered fund's CPO obligations and the holding company's operational hedging activities, and it does not commingle investor assets with the operating subsidiaries' hedge positions. The CPO's offering documents clearly delineate the registered fund's strategy and risks from the holding company's operational activities.

QUESTION
--------
Is the registered investment adviser in compliance with its CPO obligations with respect to the registered fund where it maintains separate compliance infrastructure and does not commingle the fund's commodity derivatives positions with the holding companies' operational hedges?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

CFTC Letter No. 17-68 confirms that holding company structures using commodity derivatives solely incidental to operating underlying businesses—rather than as a principal investment activity—do not fall within the CEA Section 1a(10) commodity pool definition, meaning no CPO registration is required for those entities. The registered fund, by contrast, correctly registered as a CPO following the February 8, 2012 rule amendments because its commodity-linked derivatives exposure constitutes a principal investment strategy rather than operational hedging. By maintaining completely separate compliance programs and avoiding commingling of investor assets with operating subsidiary positions, the adviser avoids regulatory arbitrage and correctly applies the commodity pool definition to only the appropriate entity. This bifurcated structure, supported by clear offering document disclosure, reflects sophisticated and compliant navigation of the dual CFTC/SEC regulatory framework.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
CFTC Regulation 4.10(d)(1) – Definition of Commodity Pool; Section 1a(10) CEA – Commodity Pool Definition; CFTC Letter No. 17-68 – Interpretive Guidance on Commodity Pool Definition

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser correctly distinguished between registered-fund commodity derivatives requiring CPO registration and holding company operational hedges exempt from the commodity pool definition under Letter No. 17-68, maintaining structural separation that validates both treatments.
