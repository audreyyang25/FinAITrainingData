Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Investment adviser David Park recommended that his client Jennifer, age 45 with $8.2M in assets, maintain a 28% concentrated position in Acme Corp. stock (cost basis $1.2M, current value $2.3M, unrealized gain $1.1M). Jennifer had inherited the shares from her father's estate and expressed concern about 'locking in taxes' on the gain. David documented in his Investment Policy Statement that: (1) Jennifer's time horizon was 20+ years until retirement; (2) she was highly risk-tolerant and had expressed explicit preference to avoid recognizing the gain; (3) Acme represented a diversified, large-cap technology company with strong fundamentals (dividend yield 1.8%, PE ratio 18x, no single-product dependency); (4) she had stated 'I don't want to pay the 20% capital gains tax on $1.1M'; (5) David reviewed the position quarterly and recommended gradual tax-loss harvesting in adjacent tech holdings to offset future gains; (6) David obtained Jennifer's written consent to hold the concentrated position, explicitly acknowledging it as an exception to his standard diversification recommendation and the associated risks (single-stock volatility, concentration risk, market correlation to tech sector). The IPS included a documented plan to systematically reduce the position via 'step-down sales' beginning at age 55 (10 years out) to allow for tax-gain averaging.

QUESTION
--------
Did David comply with his fiduciary duty of care and duty of loyalty under the Investment Adviser Act in maintaining this concentrated position?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Although concentrated single-stock positions typically raise fiduciary concerns, David satisfied the IAA's duty of care (17 CFR §206(4)-1) and duty of loyalty by: (1) conducting a detailed analysis showing Acme's business quality and valuation were sound relative to its sector; (2) documenting Jennifer's explicit instruction and tax-motivated rationale, which is a permissible consideration when a client knowingly accepts concentration risk; (3) obtaining informed, written consent acknowledging the concentration as an exception to standard diversification; (4) implementing a documented systematic plan to reduce concentration over time (step-down sales starting at age 55); (5) conducting quarterly reviews and maintaining the position only because it aligned with Jennifer's stated preferences and risk tolerance. The decisive factor is that Jennifer, a sophisticated investor with substantial assets, made an informed choice to prioritize tax efficiency over diversification—a preference David documented and honored rather than overrode. This complies with the IAA standard because the adviser exercised care in investigating the circumstances, obtained informed consent, and did not profit from the arrangement at the client's expense.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act §206(4), Fiduciary Duty of Care and Loyalty (duty to act in client's best interest, investigatory and monitoring obligations); SEC IA Release No. 2628 (fiduciary standard for IAA advisers)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser satisfied fiduciary duties by documenting the client's explicit, tax-motivated preference for concentration, obtaining written informed consent, and implementing a systematic de-concentration plan—converting a discretionary portfolio decision into a client-directed exception that was regularly monitored.
