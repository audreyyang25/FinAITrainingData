Topic: predispute_arbitration_clause_waiver_of_class_action
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A regional broker-dealer, Westgate Securities LLC, updated its customer account agreement to include a predispute arbitration clause that explicitly waives the customer's right to participate in any class action lawsuit or class arbitration proceeding. A retail investor sustained losses in a structured product sold by Westgate and sought to join a class action filed by similarly situated investors. Westgate moved to compel individual arbitration pursuant to the agreement's class action waiver provision.

QUESTION
--------
Does a predispute arbitration clause that waives a retail investor's right to participate in class action proceedings violate Securities Exchange Act Section 29(a)?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a potential violation of Securities Exchange Act Section 29(a) and conflicts with investor protection principles recognized by PIABA.

Section 29(a) of the Securities Exchange Act voids any provision that waives compliance with the Act, and PIABA has consistently advocated that mandatory arbitration clauses stripping investors of class action rights may constitute an impermissible waiver of substantive rights afforded under the securities laws. FINRA Rule 2268 also limits the scope of permissible predispute arbitration language, requiring that customers not be discouraged from pursuing FINRA arbitration while prohibiting clauses that limit the customer's legal rights in a manner inconsistent with FINRA rules.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Securities Exchange Act Section 29(a); FINRA Rule 2268; investor protection principles

KEY FACTOR
----------
The class action waiver in the predispute arbitration clause may constitute an impermissible waiver of substantive investor rights under Section 29(a).
