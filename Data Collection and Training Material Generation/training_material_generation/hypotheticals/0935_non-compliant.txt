ID: 935
Source Folder: 21_FINRA_OHO_Decisions
Topic: excessive_trading_churning
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Registered representative Marcus Teel managed a discretionary account for a 58-year-old client with moderate risk tolerance and a long-term growth objective. Over an 18-month period, Teel executed 214 trades in the account, generating an annualized cost-to-equity ratio of 42% and a turnover rate of 11.3. The client's account lost $61,000 net of commissions while Teel earned approximately $47,000 in commissions from the account during the same period. Teel's supervisory principal reviewed monthly commission reports but never flagged the account for elevated trading activity despite the firm's written supervisory procedures requiring review of accounts with turnover rates exceeding 6.

QUESTION
--------
Did Teel's trading activity in this discretionary account constitute excessive trading in violation of FINRA rules?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of FINRA Rule 2111 (quantitative suitability/excessive trading) and FINRA Rule 2010.

Rule 2111's quantitative suitability obligation prohibits a representative with de facto or actual control over an account from engaging in excessive trading in light of the customer's investment profile. A turnover rate of 11.3 and a cost-to-equity ratio of 42% are far in excess of thresholds that FINRA OHO panels have consistently found indicative of churning, and the disparity between client losses and representative commissions supports an inference of self-dealing. The supervisory principal's failure to act on the firm's own WSP trigger further compounds the regulatory exposure.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) (Quantitative Suitability); FINRA Rule 2010 (Standards of Commercial Honor)

KEY FACTOR
----------
A turnover rate more than double the firm's own internal review threshold, combined with a 42% cost-to-equity ratio, establishes excessive trading under the quantitative suitability prong of Rule 2111.
