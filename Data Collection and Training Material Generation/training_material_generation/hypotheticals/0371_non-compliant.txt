Topic: dol_rollover_analysis
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Sarah, age 58, has $425,000 in a Vanguard 401(k) invested in low-cost institutional index funds (0.03% ER). Her employer plan allows in-service distributions. Advisor Marcus recommends rolling over the entire balance to a self-directed IRA at his firm, where Marcus charges 1.25% AUM fee. Marcus documents in his CRS that the rollover will provide 'greater investment flexibility and access to alternative investments.' In fact, the 401(k) offered substantially identical index funds with the same 0.03% expense ratio, and Sarah had expressed no interest in alternatives. The only material change is the fee increase from $127.50/year to $5,312.50/year. Marcus provides no written analysis comparing the plans' investment options or fee structures.

QUESTION
--------
Did Marcus comply with PTE 2020-02 best interest standards when recommending the rollover?

ANSWER
------
No. Under PTE 2020-02, a fiduciary must act in the client's 'best interest' and ensure compensation is 'reasonable.' Marcus failed both prongs. The decisive facts: (1) Sarah's 401(k) offered substantially identical, lower-cost investments; (2) the rollover's sole material effect is increasing Marcus's compensation by $5,185/year (4,000% increase); (3) Marcus documented no substantive analysis of plan features, investment options, or fee comparison; (4) Sarah expressed no need for the purported benefit (alternatives). This mirrors DOL enforcement patterns where advisers recommend rollovers primarily to increase AUM fees. PTE 2020-02 §I(a)(1) requires documented analysis showing the rollover serves the client's interest, not the adviser's financial interest. Marcus's generic flexibility rationale, without particularized analysis, fails this standard.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
PTE 2020-02, Five-Part Test §I(a)(1) (best interest determination); ERISA §408(b)(14) (reasonable compensation)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Rollover recommended solely to increase adviser's AUM fee from $127.50 to $5,312.50/year when 401(k) offered substantially identical, lower-cost alternatives; no documented comparative analysis.
