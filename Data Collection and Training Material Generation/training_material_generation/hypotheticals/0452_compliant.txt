FACTS
-----
Broker recommended Class B mutual fund shares (5.5% contingent deferred sales charge) to 72-year-old client with $180,000 to invest for 4-year time horizon. Broker documented that: (1) client explicitly stated no intention to sell or transfer before 4 years; (2) Class A shares would charge 5% front-end load ($9,000 upfront) + 0.45% annual 12b-1 fee; (3) Class B shares charge zero upfront but 1.1% annual 12b-1 and 5% CDSC declining by 1% annually; (4) breakeven point was 6.2 years; (5) at 4-year holding period, Class B total cost was 7.2%, Class A was 9.8%. Written suitability memo filed; client signed acknowledgment of CDSC risk.

QUESTION
--------
Was the broker's Class B recommendation compliant with FINRA Suitability Rule 2111 despite front-loaded 12b-1 and CDSC?

ANSWER
------
Yes. FINRA Rule 2111 requires suitability; it does not prohibit higher-cost share classes if they are suitable given holding period and client profile. Broker documented: (1) four-year time horizon matching breakeven analysis; (2) quantitative comparison showing Class B lower total cost; (3) client's explicit statement of hold period. This mirrors FINRA guidance (Regulatory Notice 18-10) permitting higher-fee share classes when documented analysis and client profile support them. Cost alone is not disqualifying; demonstrable advantage over alternatives is decisive.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability); Cost Comparison Analysis

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented breakeven analysis showing Class B lower total cost at client's stated 4-year holding period satisfies suitability despite higher ongoing fees.
