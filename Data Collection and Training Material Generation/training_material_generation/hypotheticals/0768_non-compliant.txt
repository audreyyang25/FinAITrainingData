Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Crescent Digital Advisors, LLC, a Michigan state-registered investment adviser, marketed a proprietary 'Digital Asset Growth Fund' to retail clients, raising $3.2 million from 41 investors, most of whom were retirees and schoolteachers with moderate risk tolerances. The fund invested primarily in unregistered digital asset tokens that Crescent's principal described as 'investment contracts with guaranteed blockchain yields of 18% annually.' Crescent did not register the pooled investment vehicle's interests as securities with the State of Michigan, did not provide clients with audited financial statements, and commingled fund assets with the firm's operating account at a commercial bank rather than maintaining them with a qualified custodian. When Michigan's Corporations, Securities, and Commercial Licensing Bureau conducted a coordinated examination under NASAA's 2025 initiative, examiners discovered the fund had lost 60% of its value, and Crescent had been paying earlier investors with new investor capital.

QUESTION
--------
Did Crescent Digital Advisors violate state securities laws by offering unregistered securities, failing custody requirements, and operating a fraudulent scheme?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes multiple violations including offer and sale of unregistered securities, custody rule violations under NASAA Model Rule 102(e)(1)-1, and fraud under state anti-fraud provisions.

Crescent violated state law on multiple fronts. First, the interests in the Digital Asset Growth Fund constituted investment contracts under the Howey test—investors pooled money with an expectation of profits derived from Crescent's efforts—and were never registered with Michigan. Second, commingling fund assets with the firm's operating account violated the NASAA Model Custody Rule requiring a qualified custodian to hold client funds in segregated accounts. Third, paying existing investors with new investor money constitutes a Ponzi-like fraudulent scheme under state anti-fraud statutes. NASAA's coordinated examination initiative, which examined 1,086 advisers in 2025, is precisely designed to uncover these overlapping violations, especially among advisers being examined for the first time.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Michigan Uniform Securities Act (2002) §§ 301, 501, 502 (registration of securities and anti-fraud); NASAA Model Rule 102(e)(1)-1(a)(2) (qualified custodian requirement); NASAA Coordinated Examination Program standards

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm sold unregistered investment contract interests, commingled pooled fund assets with its own operating account, and used new investor capital to pay existing investors, constituting a multi-layered fraud uncovered during a state coordinated examination.
