Source Folder: 07_Practitioner_Commentary
Topic: training_supervision
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A mid-size broker-dealer with 120 registered representatives implemented a Reg BI compliance program before the June 30, 2020 deadline that included written policies addressing the Care, Disclosure, and Conflict of Interest obligations. However, the firm's training consisted solely of distributing a two-page summary memo to representatives via email, with no requirement to acknowledge receipt, no testing of comprehension, and no follow-up training. The firm's supervisory procedures did not require supervisors to review whether representatives were actually applying the best interest standard when making recommendations; instead, supervisors continued using pre-Reg BI suitability review checklists that did not address cost comparison, reasonably available alternatives, or whether the representative was placing the customer's interest ahead of their own. Over the next 18 months, multiple representatives recommended high-commission variable annuities to retirees without documenting consideration of lower-cost alternatives, and supervisory reviews flagged none of these recommendations.

QUESTION
--------
Did the firm's Reg BI compliance program satisfy the Compliance Obligation despite having written policies, given the deficiencies in training and supervisory implementation?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the Reg BI Compliance Obligation due to inadequate training and failure to enforce policies through reasonably designed supervisory procedures.

The Reg BI Compliance Obligation requires firms not merely to establish written policies but to 'maintain and enforce' them through reasonably designed procedures. The FINRA Reg BI Checklist specifically asks whether firms have 'procedures and training in place to assess recommendations using a best interest standard,' implying that both must be substantive and effective. Using outdated suitability checklists that omit cost-comparison and alternatives-analysis requirements means the supervisory system was not reasonably designed to enforce the Care Obligation. The passive email distribution of a summary memo, without acknowledgment, testing, or periodic refresher training, does not constitute adequate training, particularly where the deficiency resulted in systematic failures to document consideration of reasonably available alternatives for retail customers.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iv) – Reg BI Compliance Obligation; FINRA Rule 3110 – Supervision

KEY FACTOR
----------
Supervisory procedures used outdated suitability checklists that did not incorporate Reg BI's Care Obligation requirements, rendering the compliance program ineffective despite the existence of written policies.
