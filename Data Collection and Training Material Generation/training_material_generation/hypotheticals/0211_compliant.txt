Topic: reg_bi_care_expanded

Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
RIA Advisor Patel recommended that a 58-year-old client with $420,000 in a Fidelity 401(k) (4.2M beneficiary balance, 0.80% weighted average ER) and $180,000 in liquid savings remain in the 401(k) rather than execute an IRA rollover to a low-cost brokerage (0.15% blended ER). The advisor documented: (1) the 401(k) offered employer match continuation (4% match through age 65) valued at $16,800 annually; (2) the Fidelity plan's proprietary fund lineup included several low-cost options (Fidelity Zero Small Cap 0.05%, Fidelity Index 500 0.015%); (3) the client had access to Fidelity's fiduciary-grade advisor tools; (4) IRA rollover would forfeit ongoing match; (5) written recommendation explained the match value preservation. The client stayed in the 401(k).

QUESTION
--------
Did Advisor Patel comply with Reg BI in recommending retention of the 401(k) despite lower costs available in an IRA rollover?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Reg BI's care obligation (§240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)–(iii)) requires analysis of the customer's best interest considering the entire financial situation, not merely product costs. Here, the advisor: (1) documented a quantified financial benefit (employer match preservation: $16,800 annually); (2) identified low-cost fund alternatives within the plan; (3) analyzed the trade-off between plan fees and match; (4) made a recommendation prioritizing total financial benefit over cost minimization. Under DOL ERISA guidance and SEC enforcement patterns, a rollover recommendation is compliant only if the advisor addresses the match loss; conversely, a stay recommendation is compliant if documented quantitatively. This advisor satisfied both Reg BI and DOL fiduciary standards (PTE 2020-02) by preserving material employer benefits.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation (17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)–(iii)) & DOL ERISA Fiduciary Duty (PTE 2020-02)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented quantification of employer match value (exceeding IRA cost savings) justifies 401(k) retention recommendation under best-interest standard.
