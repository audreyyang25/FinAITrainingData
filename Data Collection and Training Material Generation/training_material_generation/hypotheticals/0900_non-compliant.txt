Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Drew Horton, the sole principal of Horton Investment Management LLC, a registered investment adviser managing approximately $180 million in client assets, directed his supervised representatives to recommend proprietary wrap-fee programs to clients without disclosing that the firm received additional compensation from the program sponsors. Between 2018 and 2021, at least 47 retail clients, including several retirees living on fixed incomes, were placed into these wrap programs at annual fees of 2.25%, generating approximately $890,000 in undisclosed compensation to the firm. Horton received written internal complaints from two representatives questioning the disclosures but took no remedial action. The firm's Form ADV Part 2A failed to disclose the material conflict of interest arising from these revenue-sharing arrangements.

QUESTION
--------
Does Horton Investment Management's failure to disclose revenue-sharing compensation from wrap-fee program sponsors in its Form ADV, combined with Horton's failure to act on internal supervisory red flags, constitute a violation of the Investment Advisers Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Investment Advisers Act §206(2) and §207, and constitutes a supervisory failure under §203(e) and §203(f).

Section 206(2) prohibits an investment adviser from engaging in any transaction, practice, or course of business that operates as a fraud or deceit upon a client, including material omissions of conflicts of interest. Failure to disclose revenue-sharing arrangements in Form ADV Part 2A constitutes a materially false filing under §207. The ALJ in Horter imposed civil penalties and a supervisory collateral bar precisely because the principal ignored internal warning signs, demonstrating willful disregard of supervisory obligations under §203(e) and §203(f).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act §206(2); §207 (Material Misstatements); §203(e)/(f) (Supervision) — as applied in Horter Investment Management, LLC, Initial Decision Release No. 1414 (Mar. 20, 2023)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's undisclosed revenue-sharing conflict of interest, combined with the principal's deliberate failure to act on internal supervisory red flags, mirrors the precise conduct sanctioned with third-tier civil penalties in Horter Investment Management, ALJ Initial Decision No. 1414.
