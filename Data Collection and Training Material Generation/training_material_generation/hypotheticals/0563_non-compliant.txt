ID: 563
Source Folder: 04_RegBI_Compliance_Guidance
Topic: reg_bi_care
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Edward Garrison, a registered representative associated with a mid-sized FINRA member firm, recommended that two elderly retired clients — Margaret, age 72, with $340,000 in retirement savings, and Howard, age 68, with $280,000 in retirement savings — each take out home equity lines of credit (HELOCs) against their primary residences to generate approximately $150,000 and $120,000, respectively, in investable cash. Garrison then recommended the clients invest the HELOC proceeds into a portfolio of speculative small-cap equities and high-yield bond funds with annual expense ratios exceeding 1.8%. Neither client had prior experience with leveraged investing, both had conservative risk profiles documented in their account opening paperwork, and both relied on their retirement savings and Social Security for living expenses. Garrison earned approximately $8,100 in upfront commissions across both accounts.

QUESTION
--------
Did the representative violate Reg BI's Care Obligation by recommending these clients leverage their homes to invest in speculative securities?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. The representative violated the Reg BI Care Obligation by failing to act in the customers' best interest.

The Care Obligation requires that a broker-dealer exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill to understand the potential risks, rewards, and costs of a recommendation, and have a reasonable basis to believe the recommendation is in the retail customer's best interest based on the customer's investment profile, including age, financial situation, risk tolerance, and liquidity needs. Recommending that conservative, elderly, retired clients with limited assets take on mortgage-based leverage to invest in speculative, high-cost securities is fundamentally inconsistent with their documented profiles. The recommendation placed the clients' primary residences at risk to generate commission income for the representative. This pattern mirrors the Beslagic AWC (No. 2023079509301), where FINRA found a representative violated Reg BI by recommending clients access home equity line proceeds to invest in securities.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative recommended conservative, retired clients leverage their homes to invest in speculative securities wholly inconsistent with their risk profiles and financial needs.
