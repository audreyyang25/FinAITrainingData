Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Greenfield Capital Advisors serves as the investment adviser to both a publicly traded BDC, Greenfield Growth BDC Inc., and a private limited partnership fund, Greenfield Opportunities LP. Greenfield controls the BDC and serves as the general partner of the Private Fund. A limited partner of Greenfield Opportunities LP, Meridian Holdings LLC, which owns a 7% limited partnership interest, wishes to co-invest alongside the BDC in a $15 million mezzanine loan to a portfolio company. Meridian Holdings is a second-tier affiliate of the BDC by virtue of its limited partnership interest in Greenfield Opportunities LP. The BDC's investment committee approves the co-investment without presenting the matter to the board of directors, treating Meridian as entirely outside the affiliate transaction restrictions of the 1940 Act because its interest is 'only' a limited partnership stake.

QUESTION
--------
Did Greenfield Growth BDC Inc. comply with the Investment Company Act of 1940's affiliate transaction provisions when it allowed the co-investment with Meridian Holdings without board approval?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the co-investment affiliate transaction restrictions under the Investment Company Act of 1940.

Under IM Guidance Update 2014-12, a second-tier affiliate of a BDC — such as a limited partner of a partnership under common control with the BDC — may qualify as either a 'close affiliate' or a 'remote affiliate' depending on various factors. Even if Meridian qualifies as a remote affiliate, transactions between a remote affiliate and a BDC are permitted only subject to approval by a required majority of the BDC's directors as specified in the 1940 Act. The BDC's investment committee bypassed the board entirely, failing to obtain the requisite board approval and violating the 1940 Act's protections against undue influence and overreaching.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Company Act of 1940, Sections 57(a) and 57(f); IM Guidance Update No. 2014-12 – BDC Transactions with Certain Second-Tier Affiliates

KEY FACTOR
----------
The BDC failed to obtain required majority board approval for a co-investment transaction with a second-tier affiliate, improperly treating the affiliate as outside the scope of the 1940 Act's affiliate transaction restrictions.
