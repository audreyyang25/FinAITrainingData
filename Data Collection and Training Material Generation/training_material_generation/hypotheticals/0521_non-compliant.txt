Source Folder: 02_SEC_Enforcement_Actions
Topic: sec_enforcement_ia
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Greenfield Capital Management, LLC, a registered investment adviser managing three private equity funds with combined AUM of $1.8 billion, routinely directed fund portfolio companies to engage an affiliated consulting firm, Greenfield Advisory Services, for operational improvement projects. The consulting firm charged portfolio companies fees totaling $12.4 million over a four-year period. Greenfield Capital's Form ADV Part 2A and the fund limited partnership agreements made no mention of the affiliated consulting firm, the fee arrangement, or the conflict of interest arising from the adviser's ability to direct portfolio company spending to a related entity. Fund investors, including several public pension funds and university endowments, were never informed of these arrangements through any supplemental disclosure or advisory committee review.

QUESTION
--------
Did Greenfield Capital Management violate its fiduciary obligations under the Investment Advisers Act by failing to disclose the affiliated consulting arrangement and associated fees to fund investors?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes a violation of Sections 206(2) and 206(4) of the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 for failure to disclose material conflicts of interest.

Under Section 206(2) of the Advisers Act, an investment adviser owes a fiduciary duty to its clients, which includes a duty to disclose all material conflicts of interest. The arrangement to direct portfolio companies to an affiliated consulting firm created a direct financial conflict because Greenfield benefited economically at the expense of fund portfolio companies, thereby reducing fund returns. This pattern mirrors the SEC's findings in In the Matter of First Reserve Management, L.P. (IA-4529), where the Commission found that a private equity adviser's failure to adequately disclose financial conflicts involving affiliated entities to fund clients and investors constituted a breach of fiduciary duty. The omission from Form ADV Part 2A and fund governing documents rendered the nondisclosure a material omission.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Sections 206(2) and 206(4); SEC Rule 206(4)-8 (prohibiting fraud on investors in pooled investment vehicles)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser's complete failure to disclose, in any format, the existence of an affiliated entity receiving fees from fund portfolio companies at the adviser's direction constituted a material omission of a conflict of interest.
