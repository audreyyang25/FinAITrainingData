Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Meridian Wealth Partners is a dual-registered broker-dealer and investment adviser. One of its institutional clients is the Harrington Family Office, which manages $340 million in assets for a single ultra-high-net-worth family. The family office employs a CFA-credentialed chief investment officer, has its own compliance infrastructure, and meets the definition of a 'family office' under Rule 202(a)(11)(G)-1 of the Advisers Act. When Meridian's registered representatives recommend a portfolio of structured notes and alternative credit funds to the Harrington Family Office, the firm does not apply its standard Regulation Best Interest documentation and disclosure procedures — including Form CRS delivery — that it uses for retail customers. Meridian relies on the SIFMA no-action letter dated December 23, 2020, treating the Harrington Family Office as an 'Institutional Family Office' rather than a 'retail customer' for Reg BI purposes, after confirming the family office meets all conditions specified in the no-action letter, including that it has at least $50 million in assets under management and is staffed by individuals with sufficient financial sophistication to evaluate the recommendations independently.

QUESTION
--------
Is Meridian Wealth Partners compliant in not applying Regulation Best Interest retail customer obligations when making recommendations to the Harrington Family Office?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The December 23, 2020 SIFMA no-action letter provides assurance that Staff would not recommend enforcement action against broker-dealers that do not treat qualifying 'Institutional Family Offices' as 'retail customers' for purposes of Reg BI and Form CRS. The Harrington Family Office meets the conditions described in the letter: it qualifies as a family office under Advisers Act Rule 202(a)(11)(G)-1, manages substantial assets, and employs financially sophisticated personnel. Because Meridian verified all qualifying conditions before relying on the no-action position, its decision not to apply Reg BI retail customer procedures is consistent with the Staff's stated position.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Staff No-Action Letter re SIFMA (Dec. 23, 2020) – Institutional Family Offices and Reg BI (SEC Rule 15l-1); Exchange Act Rule 15l-1(b)(1) definition of 'retail customer'

KEY FACTOR
----------
The family office satisfied every condition specified in the SIFMA no-action letter, including the family office rule definition, asset threshold, and financial sophistication requirements, making reliance on the no-action position appropriate.
