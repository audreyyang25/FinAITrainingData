Topic: damages_methodology_consequential_damages_calculation
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
In a FINRA arbitration involving a retired teacher who suffered losses of $280,000 from unsuitable leveraged ETF investments, the claimant's expert witness presented a damages model using the out-of-pocket method calculating the difference between the purchase price and the sale or current value of the securities. The expert also presented an alternative benefit-of-the-bargain model showing what the investor would have earned had her funds been placed in a suitable conservative bond portfolio over the same period, disclosing both methodologies to the panel with a full explanation of assumptions. The respondent's expert challenged the alternative model but did not object to the out-of-pocket calculation.

QUESTION
--------
Does presenting dual damages methodologies with full disclosure of assumptions and an evidentiary basis for each calculation comply with expert witness and damages standards applicable in FINRA arbitration?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

FINRA arbitration panels have broad discretion to award damages and are not bound by a single damages methodology, and presenting both out-of-pocket and benefit-of-the-bargain calculations with transparent assumptions and a supporting evidentiary record is consistent with PIABA's damages advocacy that investors be made whole for the full economic harm caused by broker misconduct. The expert's disclosure of both models and their underlying assumptions satisfies the transparency requirements that arbitration panels use to evaluate competing damages theories under Rule 12604, which governs the presentation of evidence at hearings.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure Rule 12604; Securities Exchange Act Section 28(a); common law compensatory damages principles

KEY FACTOR
----------
Full disclosure of assumptions underlying both damages methodologies, with supporting evidence, provides the arbitration panel with a compliant and complete basis for a damages award.
