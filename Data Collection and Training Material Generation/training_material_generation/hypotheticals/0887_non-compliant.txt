Source Folder: 19_CFTC_Guidance
Topic: commodity_conflicts
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Meridian Asset Management is a registered investment adviser to a large registered investment company (mutual fund) with $1.2 billion in assets under management. Following the CFTC's February 8, 2012 rule amendments requiring CPO registration by investment advisers to registered investment companies investing in commodity-linked derivatives, Meridian's commodity exposure—primarily through S&P GSCI-linked total return swaps and exchange-traded crude oil futures representing 8.3% of the fund's NAV—triggers the CPO registration requirement. Meridian's general counsel determines that the fund qualifies for a de minimis exemption under CFTC Regulation 4.13(a)(3) and does not register as a CPO. However, Meridian simultaneously earns undisclosed revenue-sharing payments from the swap counterparty on the commodity-linked swaps, a fact omitted from both the fund's prospectus and its Form ADV Part 2. The fund's board is not informed of the revenue-sharing arrangement.

QUESTION
--------
Does Meridian's failure to disclose the revenue-sharing arrangement with its commodity swap counterparty, combined with its CPO registration analysis, comply with applicable CFTC and SEC requirements?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of CFTC conflict of interest disclosure obligations and SEC fiduciary disclosure requirements under the Investment Advisers Act.

While the CFTC's 2012 rule revisions created CPO registration obligations for advisers to registered investment companies investing in commodity-linked derivatives, an adviser claiming an exemption under Rule 4.13(a)(3) must still satisfy conditions including honest dealing free of material conflicts. The undisclosed revenue-sharing payment from the swap counterparty constitutes a material conflict of interest that must be disclosed to the fund's board and investors under both CFTC anti-fraud provisions under Regulation 4.41 and the SEC's fiduciary duty framework. The SEC-CFTC MOU on information sharing means dual regulatory exposure is heightened in cases involving registered investment companies with commodity derivatives. Failure to disclose to the board alone constitutes a breach of fiduciary duty independent of the CPO analysis.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
CFTC Regulations 4.13(a)(3) and 4.41; SEC Investment Advisers Act Section 206; SEC-CFTC MOU on Coordination in Areas of Common Regulatory Interest (June 28, 2018)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Undisclosed revenue-sharing payments from the commodity swap counterparty to the investment adviser constitute a material, undisclosed conflict of interest triggering violations under both CFTC and SEC frameworks.
