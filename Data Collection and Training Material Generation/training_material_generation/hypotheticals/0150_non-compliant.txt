Source Folder: unknown
Topic: churning_excessive
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker at a major wirehouse recommended a variable annuity with a 7% surrender charge and 2.5% annual expense ratio to a 71-year-old client with $380,000 in liquid retirement savings and conservative risk tolerance (stated as 60% bonds, 30% stocks, 10% cash). Three months after the purchase, market conditions (mildly negative, –3%), the client called requesting a 'less risky' allocation. The broker recommended switching to a different variable annuity with a higher expense ratio (2.8%) and 6% surrender charge, generating a new commission of $9,500 for the broker. This was the third variable annuity purchased by the client in 4.5 years, each after a short holding period (8 months, 11 months, and 3 months). Total expenses and surrender charges paid across the three annuities exceeded $22,000.

QUESTION
--------
Does this conduct violate FINRA Rule 2111 or Rule 2330?

ANSWER
------
No, this does not satisfy the legal standard. The conduct violates FINRA Rule 2111(a) (suitability) and Rule 2330 (variable annuity suitability).

This conduct violates Rule 2111 and Rule 2330 because: (1) A third variable annuity replacement in 4.5 years is excessive and demonstrates unsuitable churning within a single product class; Rule 2330 explicitly requires brokers to consider whether replacement is justified by the customer's needs; (2) The 71-year-old conservative client should not own variable annuities (complex, illiquid instruments with surrender charges) — less complex fixed-income alternatives (bond funds, CDs, fixed annuities) were more suitable; (3) The switch after only 3 months for a modest market decline (–3%) shows the first recommendation was unsuitable; a conservative elderly investor should not have been positioned in an aggressive variable annuity in the first place; (4) The broker's commission incentive ($9,500) for the third annuity sale creates a conflict and appears to motivate the unsuitable recommendation; (5) Total costs ($22,000) eroded returns significantly. FINRA has sanctioned advisors for variable annuity churning at lower frequencies; see FINRA Case #2015046639 (2017) (sanction for two variable annuity replacements in 3.5 years in a conservative account).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) (suitability — must be based on reasonable basis and suitable for the customer); FINRA Rule 2330(a)–(b) (variable annuity suitability rule — broker must obtain and review customer profile; annuity must be suitable given customer's age, other investments, needs; prior annuity must not be replaced without reasonable basis); FINRA Regulatory Notice 07-66 (variable annuity churning guidance).

KEY FACTOR
----------
Three variable annuity replacements in 4.5 years combined with short holding periods, high surrender charges, and suitability concerns for a 71-year-old conservative investor constitute churning and unsuitable recommendations under Rule 2330.
