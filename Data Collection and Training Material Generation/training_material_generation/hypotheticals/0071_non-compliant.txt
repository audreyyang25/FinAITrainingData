Topic: ia_duty_of_care
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 68-year-old retired client with $800,000 in a taxable brokerage account stated her objective was 'income and capital preservation.' Her adviser recommended a portfolio of 80% small-cap growth stocks and 20% emerging market bonds, with an annual turnover rate of 95%. The client's prior three statements showed similar high-turnover trading patterns with no documented rationale for the trades.

QUESTION
--------
Does this portfolio recommendation and trading pattern comply with the investment adviser's duty of care?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the investment adviser's duty of care.

The adviser has breached the duty of care by recommending a portfolio allocation that is fundamentally inconsistent with the client's stated objective of 'income and capital preservation.' A 68-year-old retiree seeking capital preservation would not reasonably pursue 80% small-cap exposure. Moreover, the 95% annual turnover and lack of documented rationale for individual trades suggests churning—trading for the purpose of generating commissions rather than for sound investment reasons. Under Section 206 of the Advisers Act, an adviser must act in the client's best interest and provide advice on a basis that reasonably could be expected to be in the client's interest. The recommendation fails this standard because it prioritizes growth over the client's stated preservation objective.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206; SEC Rule 206(4)-1 (fiduciary duty); FINRA AWC cases involving churning and unsuitability.

KEY FACTOR
----------
High portfolio turnover (95% annually) combined with portfolio allocation (80% small-cap growth) that directly contradicts the client's stated objective of 'capital preservation' demonstrates lack of reasonable basis.
