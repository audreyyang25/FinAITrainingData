ID: 528
Source Folder: 03_FINRA_AWC
Topic: finra_awc_churning
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Robert Danforth, a General Securities Representative associated with a national wirehouse, exercised discretion in 12 customer accounts between January 2023 and November 2023 without obtaining prior written authorization from the customers or having the accounts accepted as discretionary by his firm. Danforth would routinely rebalance client portfolios—selling equity positions and purchasing fixed-income securities—based on his own market outlook without contacting clients beforehand. Several clients later stated they were 'generally fine' with the trades, but none had signed discretionary account agreements. The firm's compliance system flagged the activity when it detected a pattern of same-day order entry across multiple accounts without corresponding client contact notes. Danforth's firm permitted him to resign and filed a Form U5 reporting concerns involving 'unauthorized trading in client accounts.' Total commissions generated across the 12 accounts during this period were approximately $38,500.

QUESTION
--------
Did Danforth violate FINRA rules by exercising discretion in client accounts without written authorization?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes violations of FINRA Rules 3260(b) and 2010.

FINRA Rule 3260(b) explicitly prohibits a registered representative from exercising discretionary power in a customer's account unless the customer has given prior written authorization and the account has been accepted by the member firm as a discretionary account. The fact that some clients retroactively expressed comfort with the trades does not cure the violation, as written authorization must precede the discretionary activity. This fact pattern directly mirrors the Robert Wylie Goff AWC (No. 2024082784301), where Goff was suspended for one month and fined $5,000 for exercising discretion without written authorization in 12 customer accounts at Wells Fargo.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3260(b) – Discretionary Accounts; FINRA Rule 2010 – Standards of Commercial Honor and Principles of Fair Trade

KEY FACTOR
----------
Danforth exercised trading discretion in 12 customer accounts without obtaining prior written authorization from the customers or having the accounts accepted as discretionary by the firm.
