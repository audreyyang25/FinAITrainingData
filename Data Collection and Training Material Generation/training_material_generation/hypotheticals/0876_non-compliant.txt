ID: 876
Source Folder: 18_OCC_Bank_Trust_Guidance
Topic: trust_investment_management
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
First Meridian Bank acts as corporate trustee for a $5.7 million testamentary trust created under the will of deceased business owner Robert Vasquez, with his surviving spouse Elena (age 71) as income beneficiary and two minor grandchildren as remainder beneficiaries. The trust department's investment committee, without conducting a formal account review or updating the investment policy statement, allocated 68% of trust assets into a single sector — technology growth equities — based on the prior year's strong performance. Elena Vasquez relies on the trust for approximately $8,500 per month in living expenses. When technology markets declined 34% over the following 18 months, the trust's income-generating capacity dropped below Elena's monthly need, and the bank's records showed no contemporaneous analysis of the allocation's impact on the current income beneficiary versus the remainder interest.

QUESTION
--------
Does First Meridian Bank's concentrated sector allocation without documented balancing of income beneficiary and remainder beneficiary interests satisfy the prudent investor standard applicable to bank trustees?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the prudent investor standard and the duty to balance beneficiary interests.

The OCC Asset Management handbook requires bank trustees to manage trust assets in accordance with the prudent investor standard, which demands diversification to minimize risk and explicit consideration of the differing interests of income and remainder beneficiaries. A 68% concentration in a single volatile sector, absent documented analysis, is inconsistent with prudent portfolio management for a trust supporting a 71-year-old income beneficiary with fixed monthly living expenses. The failure to update the investment policy statement or document the trade-off analysis between current income needs and long-term growth further evidences a breakdown in the bank's fiduciary process obligations under OCC operations and controls guidance.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
OCC Comptroller's Handbook – Asset Management, Version 1.0 (June 2023); Uniform Prudent Investor Act; 12 CFR 9.6 – Investment of Fiduciary Funds

KEY FACTOR
----------
The bank failed to document any analysis balancing the current income beneficiary's demonstrated monthly cash flow needs against the concentrated growth equity allocation, violating both the prudent investor standard and the duty of impartiality between beneficiaries.
