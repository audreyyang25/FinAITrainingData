Source Folder: unknown
Topic: ia_fiduciary_duty
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An RIA managing $2.3M for a 58-year-old conservative investor recommends a concentrated position of 40% in a micro-cap biotech stock. The RIA's spouse owns 15% of the biotech company and receives quarterly distributions. The RIA did not disclose this conflict to the client or document why a 40% micro-cap concentration aligned with the client's stated low-risk tolerance and goal of steady retirement income in 7 years.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the RIA's fiduciary duty of loyalty and care under the Investment Adviser Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the fiduciary duty of loyalty and care.

The RIA has a material undisclosed conflict of interest (spouse's ownership stake in the recommended security) that directly creates incentive to favor the biotech investment. The 40% concentration in a micro-cap position is inconsistent with a 58-year-old conservative investor's stated risk tolerance and 7-year retirement timeline, violating the duty of care. Under Capital Gains and SEC guidance, RIAs must affirmatively disclose all material conflicts and must base recommendations on client's stated objectives, not adviser financial benefit. The RIA failed both prongs: no disclosure and unsuitable recommendation for the specific client profile.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act § 206; SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau, 375 U.S. 180 (1963); SEC Rule 206(4)-1 (fiduciary duty includes disclosure of conflicts and duty to put client interest ahead of adviser's own)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Undisclosed material conflict of interest combined with recommendation that contradicts documented client risk tolerance and investment objectives.
