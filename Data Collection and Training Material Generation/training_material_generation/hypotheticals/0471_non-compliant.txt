Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Financial adviser Marcus T. managed a $450,000 brokerage account for Jennifer, age 58, with moderate risk tolerance. Over 18 months, Marcus executed 47 trades generating $18,900 in commissions (4.2% annualized). The account turnover ratio was 3.2x, well above the client's stated buy-and-hold strategy. Additionally, Marcus earned $2,400 in undisclosed revenue sharing from the mutual fund complex used in 12 of the 47 trades. The adviser never disclosed this conflict in writing or obtained explicit client consent. Marcus documented the trades as "rebalancing" in CRM notes despite no changes to the target allocation percentages. The client complained after discovering commission statements and the revenue-sharing arrangements in a regulatory filing.

QUESTION
--------
Did Marcus violate both FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability) and SEC Regulation Best Interest's conflict-of-interest disclosure requirement?

ANSWER
------
Yes, Marcus violated both standards. Under FINRA 2111, the 3.2x turnover ratio and 4.2% annual commission burden constitute excessive trading (churning) unsuitable for a buy-and-hold investor. The documented rebalancing justification contradicts the lack of allocation changes—a red flag pattern similar to FINRA enforcement in 2021-2022. Under Reg BI 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii), advisers must disclose all material conflicts, including revenue sharing, in writing before effecting transactions. Marcus's failure to disclose the $2,400 annual revenue sharing violates the Care Obligation. The two violations compound: undisclosed conflicts create incentive to churn, and churning itself breaches suitability. No client consent cures either violation retroactively.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability); Reg BI Care Obligation 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(ii) (Conflict Disclosure)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Undisclosed revenue sharing combined with excessive trading activity demonstrates both conflict concealment and suitability breach, creating dual liability.
