Source Folder: 16_Congressional_Materials
Topic: legislative_history
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered representative at a mid-sized broker-dealer recommends that a 71-year-old retired schoolteacher with $180,000 in savings and a fixed pension income invest $120,000 (67% of her liquid assets) in a leveraged inverse ETF tied to short-selling strategies on small-cap equities. The representative tells the client the product is 'just like a regular stock fund' without disclosing that leveraged inverse ETFs reset daily and are designed for sophisticated short-term traders, not long-term retirement investors. The representative earns a $3,600 commission on the transaction. The broker-dealer's own compliance manual, updated after the SEC's Reg BI implementation in 2020, explicitly flags leveraged and inverse ETFs as requiring heightened suitability review for customers over age 65.

QUESTION
--------
Does the representative's recommendation of the leveraged inverse ETF to this retired client satisfy the Care Obligation under Regulation Best Interest?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the Reg BI Care Obligation.

Dodd-Frank §913 directed the SEC to study and potentially establish a uniform fiduciary standard for broker-dealers, culminating in Reg BI's Care Obligation, which requires a broker to act in the retail customer's best interest and not place the firm's financial interest ahead of the customer's. Allocating 67% of a retiree's liquid assets into a complex, daily-resetting leveraged inverse product is facially inconsistent with the client's long-term, capital-preservation profile. The representative's misleading characterization of the product as 'just like a regular stock fund' compounds the violation by undermining the client's ability to make an informed decision, directly contravening the SEC's investor-protection mandate articulated in the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and reinforced by Dodd-Frank.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation; Dodd-Frank Act §913 (mandating SEC study and rulemaking on broker-dealer standards of conduct)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative recommended a complex, high-risk leveraged inverse ETF to a risk-averse retiree using the majority of her liquid assets, without adequate disclosure, in violation of the Reg BI Care Obligation.
