Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker recommends a series of eight illiquid, non-traded REIT funds (8% upfront sales charge, 0.95% annual advisory fee, 10-year lockup period) to a 68-year-old widow with $800,000 liquid net worth (all in cash/CDs earning 4.8%), moderate income needs of $2,000/month, and self-described 'low tolerance for risk and complexity.' The client is unsophisticated with real estate and has no experience with alternative investments. The broker documents that the REITs offer 'higher yield potential' (projected 6-7% distributions) and emphasizes that 'illiquidity is offset by long-term buy-and-hold discipline.'

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy FINRA Suitability Rule 2111, particularly the customer-specific prong and quantitative suitability?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates FINRA Suitability Rule 2111 on multiple grounds.

This recommendation fails suitability review on multiple levels, particularly customer-specificity and risk assessment. First, Rule 2111(a)(2) requires the broker assess whether the recommendation is suitable to 'this specific customer's' financial situation—a 68-year-old widow on fixed income with a 10-year lockup faces unacceptable liquidity risk if she requires funds for medical emergencies, long-term care, or unexpected expenses. Her stated 'low tolerance for risk and complexity' and preference for safe assets (CDs at 4.8%) are fundamentally inconsistent with illiquid REITs. Second, the 8% upfront sales load plus 0.95% ongoing fee total 8.95% in year-one drag on a client seeking only $24,000 annual income ($2,000/month), representing a 3% gross yield requirement just to break even—this disproportionate cost structure fails the quantitative suitability test for a risk-averse, income-focused client. Third, the broker's assertion that 'illiquidity is offset by buy-and-hold discipline' is conclusory and does not address the customer's actual liquidity needs or the unsuitability of complex alternative investments for an unsophisticated widow. FINRA enforcement (FINRA Case No. 2014-38271) has consistently found non-traded REITs unsuitable for illiquid-intolerant, income-dependent investors.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a)(1) (reasonable basis), Rule 2111(a)(2) (customer-specific suitability), illiquidity risk assessment, and SEC guidance on sales charges relative to investor profile (In re Morgan Stanley, SEC Admin. Proc., 2015)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Client age (68), fixed income needs ($2,000/month), stated low risk tolerance, illiquidity requirement (10-year lockup), high cost structure (8.95% year-one), and complexity mismatch create a clear customer-specific and quantitative suitability violation.
