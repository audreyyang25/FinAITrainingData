Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An investment adviser recommends that a 58-year-old client with $450,000 in retirement savings and moderate risk tolerance transfer her existing $200,000 IRA from a competitor to the adviser's platform. The adviser receives a $3,000 referral fee for the transfer but does not disclose this compensation to the client. The recommendation is otherwise suitable for the client's profile.

QUESTION
--------
Does this conduct violate the Investment Adviser Act fiduciary duty of loyalty?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this violates the fiduciary duty of loyalty under the Investment Adviser Act.

An investment adviser owes an absolute duty of loyalty and must disclose all material conflicts of interest, including compensation tied to specific client transactions. Here, the adviser's receipt of a $3,000 referral fee for the transfer creates a direct financial incentive to recommend the transfer that may conflict with the client's best interests (remaining assets at the competitor may have lower fees or better performance). The failure to disclose this compensation violates the core fiduciary obligation. Capital Gains establishes that advisers cannot put their interests ahead of clients' interests without explicit, informed consent. The non-disclosure prevents the client from evaluating whether the adviser's recommendation is truly in her best interest or motivated by the referral fee.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act §206(2) and SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau, 375 U.S. 180 (1963) (advisers owe undivided loyalty and must disclose all material conflicts of interest); SEC Rule 206(4)-1 (custody rule requires clear written disclosure of compensation)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Undisclosed compensation directly tied to the recommendation creates a material conflict of interest that breaches the duty of loyalty under the Investment Adviser Act.
