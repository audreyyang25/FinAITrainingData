ID: 649
Source Folder: 08_State_Fiduciary_Rules
Topic: state_vs_federal_standards
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An investment adviser representative dually registered in Nevada and operating under a federal covered adviser recommends that Priya Sharma, a 45-year-old physician with $2.1 million in investable assets, execute a series of complex options strategies in her advisory account, including selling uncovered puts on volatile biotech stocks. The representative does not obtain written acknowledgment from the client regarding the risks specific to uncovered options, relying instead on a general risk disclosure signed at account opening two years prior. The representative has been engaging in frequent trading in the account, generating $47,000 in transaction-based fees over 12 months while the account has declined 18% in value. The representative also fails to disclose that he receives a per-trade ticket charge that incentivizes higher trading frequency. Under NASAA Model Rule 102(a)(4)-1, recommending transactions without reasonable grounds that the recommendation is suitable is deemed an unethical business practice.

QUESTION
--------
Does this conduct violate state-level fiduciary obligations under the NASAA Model Rule as adopted by Nevada?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of NASAA Model Rule 102(a)(4)-1 regarding unethical business practices of investment advisers, including recommending unsuitable transactions and failing to disclose material conflicts.

NASAA Model Rule 102(a)(4)-1 establishes that an investment adviser is a fiduciary with a duty to act primarily for the benefit of clients. The rule specifically identifies as unethical the practice of recommending transactions to a client without reasonable grounds that the recommendation is suitable based on the client's financial situation, needs, and objectives. The excessive trading generating $47,000 in undisclosed per-trade fees while the account lost 18% demonstrates a pattern where the adviser's interest was placed ahead of the client's. Additionally, the failure to disclose the per-trade compensation conflict and reliance on a stale general risk disclosure for complex uncovered options strategies violates the fiduciary duty of loyalty and care. The rule applies to federal covered advisers to the extent the conduct is fraudulent or deceptive.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
NASAA Model Rule 102(a)(4)-1 – Unethical Business Practices of Investment Advisers, Investment Adviser Representatives, and Federal Covered Advisers

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser engaged in excessive, unsuitable trading driven by undisclosed per-trade compensation while failing to obtain current risk acknowledgments for complex options strategies, violating fiduciary duties under NASAA Model Rule 102(a)(4)-1.
