Source Folder: 21_FINRA_OHO_Decisions
Topic: new_account_suitability_documentation
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Registered representative Priya Nair onboarded a 45-year-old software executive as a new customer seeking aggressive growth with a 20-year investment horizon and a stated liquid net worth of $1.4 million. Nair collected and documented the customer's full investment profile, including prior investment experience with equity and options markets, tax considerations, and a written acknowledgment from the customer that he understood and accepted the risks of small-cap growth equity recommendations. Before recommending a concentrated position in three early-stage technology companies totaling 22% of the portfolio, Nair conducted a written needs analysis, cross-referenced the recommendation against the customer's documented profile, and obtained principal approval per the firm's written supervisory procedures. The firm retained all documentation for the required period.

QUESTION
--------
Did Priya Nair's recommendation and documentation process satisfy FINRA Rule 2111 suitability requirements for the new customer?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Rule 2111 requires that before making a recommendation, a registered representative have a reasonable basis to believe the recommendation is suitable based on the customer's investment profile, including financial situation, risk tolerance, investment objectives, and experience. Nair satisfied the reasonable-basis and customer-specific suitability prongs by thoroughly documenting the customer's profile, conducting and retaining a written needs analysis, and obtaining principal pre-approval consistent with the firm's supervisory procedures. The aggressive growth objective, long investment horizon, substantial liquid net worth, and prior market experience collectively support the suitability of early-stage technology equity exposure at the recommended concentration level.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability); FINRA Rule 4511 (Books and Records); FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Comprehensive pre-recommendation documentation of the customer's investment profile, combined with a written needs analysis and principal approval, demonstrated a reasonable basis for the recommendation consistent with the customer's aggressive growth objective and financial capacity.
