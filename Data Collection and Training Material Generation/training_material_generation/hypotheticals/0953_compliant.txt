Topic: compliant_damages_methodology_in_arbitration_award
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
In a FINRA arbitration involving alleged unsuitable concentration of a customer's retirement assets in speculative energy sector stocks, the claimant's damages expert, Dr. Rachel Okafor, applied the 'out-of-pocket' loss methodology, calculating the difference between the price paid for each security and its value at the time of the arbitration hearing, adjusted for any dividends received. Dr. Okafor also presented an alternative 'benefit-of-the-bargain' calculation using a suitable benchmark portfolio of diversified index funds to show what the customer's account would have been worth absent the alleged misconduct, clearly labeling each methodology and its underlying assumptions for the panel. The arbitration panel explicitly addressed both methodologies in its written award explanation, adopted the out-of-pocket measure as the primary damages figure, and articulated why the benchmark comparison supported but did not independently control the award.

QUESTION
--------
Did the claimant's presentation of damages methodology and the arbitration panel's adoption of it comply with sound damages principles recognized in FINRA arbitration practice?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The out-of-pocket loss methodology is a well-recognized and judicially accepted damages measure in securities arbitration, designed to restore investors to the position they would have occupied absent the wrongdoing, and its application here with full disclosure of assumptions satisfies the transparency requirements that arbitration panels and appellate courts expect. Presenting an alternative benchmark methodology as a corroborative measure rather than a standalone claim reflects methodological rigor consistent with expert testimony standards and does not constitute improper double recovery. PIABA's advocacy on damages methodology supports precisely this kind of clear, well-documented expert analysis that enables arbitration panels to render informed awards fully explained in the record.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure Rule 12904; SEC v. Rauscher Pierce Refsnes, Inc.; PIABA damages methodology guidance; Restatement (Third) of Restitution §51

KEY FACTOR
----------
The transparent dual-methodology presentation with clearly articulated assumptions and the panel's reasoned adoption of the primary measure fully satisfies FINRA arbitration damages standards.
