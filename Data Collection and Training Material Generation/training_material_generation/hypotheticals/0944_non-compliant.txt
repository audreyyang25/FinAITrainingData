Source Folder: 22_PIABA_Materials
Topic: expungement_standard_factually_impossible
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Broker James Whitfield settled a customer complaint for $45,000 after the customer alleged unsuitable variable annuity recommendations. No finding of factual impossibility or clear error was made by the arbitration panel. Whitfield subsequently filed a separate FINRA arbitration solely to seek expungement of the settled complaint from the Central Registration Depository. The arbitrator granted expungement based solely on Whitfield's testimony that he believed the settlement was made for business convenience.

QUESTION
--------
Does granting expungement of a settled customer complaint based solely on the broker's assertion of business convenience satisfy the required FINRA expungement standard?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of FINRA Rule 2080 and the expungement standards requiring a finding that the claim is factually impossible, clearly erroneous, or that the broker was not involved.

FINRA Rule 2080 requires arbitrators to affirmatively find one of three specific grounds before recommending expungement: the claim is factually impossible or clearly erroneous, the broker was not involved in the alleged conduct, or the claim is false. A business convenience settlement does not satisfy any of these grounds, and the arbitrator's reliance on the broker's self-serving testimony without an independent factual basis fails to meet the elevated evidentiary threshold required.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2080; FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure Rule 12805; PIABA expungement reform advocacy standards

KEY FACTOR
----------
Expungement requires an affirmative finding of factual impossibility, clear error, or non-involvement, not a broker's subjective characterization of why a settlement occurred.
