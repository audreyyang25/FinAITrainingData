Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 78-year-old retiree with $450,000 in liquid savings and a stated risk tolerance of 'conservative' is recommended to purchase a structured note linked to the S&P 500 with a 7-year maturity, 2% annual fee, and principal-at-risk feature. The advisor documents the client as 'sophisticated investor' without discussing the illiquidity, leverage, or complexity of the product. The client has no prior experience with derivatives or structured products.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy FINRA Rule 2010 (General Conduct Standards) and SEC guidance on senior investor recommendations?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates applicable standards for senior investor protection.

This recommendation fails the suitability standard under FINRA Rule 2111 because the product is demonstrably unsuitable for a self-described conservative 78-year-old retiree with no experience in complex instruments. The structured note's principal-at-risk feature and 7-year lock-up directly conflict with the client's stated conservative risk tolerance and the liquidity needs typical of a retiree. The SEC's 2013 guidance emphasizes that seniors should not be sold complex, illiquid products without clear suitability documentation and discussion of risks. The advisor's labeling of the client as 'sophisticated' without evidence of actual knowledge or experience violates Rule 2010's requirement for fair dealing and constitutes a red flag under FINRA's heightened scrutiny framework for senior investors (see FINRA Notice 13-25 on senior investor protections).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2010 (fair dealing and best execution); SEC Office of Investor Education and Advocacy guidance on senior investor protection (2013 Risk Alert); FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability Rule)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The absence of documented suitability analysis linking a complex, illiquid, principal-at-risk product to the client's stated conservative risk tolerance, combined with the client's age (78) and lack of prior structured product experience.
