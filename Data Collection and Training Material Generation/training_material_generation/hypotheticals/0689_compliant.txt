FACTS
-----
Sandra Willoughby, an investment adviser representative at an SEC-registered RIA, advises James Thornton, a 68-year-old recent retiree with $1.2 million in investable assets, a $4,200/month pension, Social Security income of $2,800/month, and moderate risk tolerance. Thornton expresses anxiety about outliving his savings given his family history of longevity. Willoughby recommends allocating $300,000 (25% of assets) to a fee-only, low-cost fixed indexed annuity with a 5-year surrender period, no commission (the RIA charges its standard 0.85% AUM fee on remaining assets and receives no annuity compensation), and a guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit rider costing 0.95% annually. She documents a comprehensive retirement income analysis comparing the annuity allocation against a bond ladder, systematic withdrawal strategy, and TIPS portfolio. Her written analysis notes the annuity's illiquidity risk, higher internal costs relative to direct bond holdings, and explains that the remaining $900,000 in liquid assets provides approximately 15 years of supplemental income at current spending levels, offering ample liquidity. The client signs a detailed acknowledgment of the cost comparison and illiquidity trade-offs.

QUESTION
--------
Does the allocation of 25% of a retiree's assets to a fixed indexed annuity comply with the Advisers Act fiduciary duty of care and loyalty given the product's surrender period and costs?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Under the Advisers Act fiduciary standard as interpreted in SEC Release IA-4889, an investment adviser must provide advice in the best interest of the client, considering the client's full financial picture. Willoughby's recommendation demonstrates thorough due diligence: she documented a comprehensive comparison of alternatives, addressed the client's specific longevity concern, ensured the illiquid allocation was proportionate (25%) relative to ample remaining liquid assets, and received no additional compensation from the annuity product. The adviser's duty of loyalty is satisfied because the fee-only structure eliminates compensation conflicts. Academic literature on retirement planning, including studies on Gen X and Boomer retirement confidence gaps, supports that addressing longevity risk with partial annuitization is a recognized, evidence-based planning strategy when properly documented.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Sections 206(1) and 206(2) – Fiduciary Duty of Care and Loyalty; SEC Release IA-4889 Proposed Interpretation

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser documented a thorough alternatives analysis, maintained a conflict-free compensation structure, and ensured the illiquid allocation was proportionate to the client's overall liquidity needs.
