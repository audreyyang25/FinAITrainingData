Source Folder: 07_Practitioner_Commentary
Topic: industry_best_practices
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A private equity adviser manages three funds: Fund I ($800 million flagship fund with external investors), Fund II ($200 million co-investment vehicle), and a proprietary balance-sheet account. When a potential $50 million acquisition target fell through, the adviser allocated the entire $2.3 million in broken deal expenses—including due diligence costs, legal fees, and travel—exclusively to Fund I, even though all three vehicles had participated in the due diligence process and stood to benefit from the potential acquisition. The limited partnership agreement for Fund I stated that broken deal expenses would be allocated 'in accordance with the adviser's expense allocation policy,' but the adviser had no written expense allocation policy. The adviser disclosed in its Form ADV Part 2A only that 'certain expenses may be allocated among funds,' without specifying the methodology or potential conflicts inherent in allocating expenses disproportionately to the external fund.

QUESTION
--------
Did the private equity adviser satisfy its fiduciary duty by allocating the entire $2.3 million in broken deal expenses to its flagship external fund without a written allocation policy and with only generic disclosure?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a breach of the investment adviser's fiduciary duty involving misallocation of broken deal expenses and inadequate disclosure of conflicts of interest.

As illustrated by the SEC's enforcement action against KKR for misallocating more than $17 million in broken deal expenses to its flagship funds, the SEC views disproportionate allocation of expenses to external funds—particularly when the adviser or co-investment vehicles also participated—as a breach of fiduciary duty. The Willkie memorandum emphasizes that private equity firms must 'maintain detailed expense allocation policies among the funds and accounts they manage' and enhance disclosures around conflicts of interest. The absence of any written allocation policy and the use of vague, generic disclosure language in Form ADV is insufficient to meet the adviser's obligation of full and fair disclosure regarding material conflicts.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Sections 206(1) and 206(2) – Fiduciary Duty and Anti-Fraud Provisions

KEY FACTOR
----------
Allocating 100% of broken deal expenses to the external fund when multiple vehicles participated, without a written allocation policy or specific conflict disclosure.
