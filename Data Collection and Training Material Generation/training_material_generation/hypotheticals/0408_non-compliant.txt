Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A New York-licensed insurance agent sold a 7-year surrender-charge annuity (6% annual surrender charge, declining 1% per year) to Margaret Chen, age 62, residing in Brooklyn. Margaret had $280,000 in liquid savings and stated her goal was to access funds within 3–5 years for potential long-term care costs. The agent recommended the annuity to provide 'guaranteed income and principal protection,' earning 3.2% annually. The agent disclosed the surrender charges in the prospectus but did not discuss the conflict between the 3–5 year liquidity need and the 7-year surrender period. Margaret would face $16,800 in surrender charges if she withdrew funds in year 3. The recommendation was documented as suitable under FINRA standards but was not revised after learning of the specific liquidity timeline.

QUESTION
--------
Did the agent comply with NY Regulation 187's fiduciary duty standard when recommending the surrender-charge annuity to a client with stated short-term liquidity needs?

ANSWER
------
No. NY Reg 187 §227.2(a) requires recommendations in the 'best interest' of the customer, including consideration of suitability with particular attention to the customer's financial situation, needs, and objectives. The disclosure of surrender charges in the prospectus alone does not satisfy this duty. The agent had actual knowledge that Margaret needed access within 3–5 years yet recommended a product with a 7-year surrender period imposing $16,800 in penalties. The 'best interest' standard under NY 187 is materially higher than FINRA suitability—it requires affirmative analysis of alignment between product structure and stated goals. The agent's failure to address the liquidity mismatch or to propose alternative products (e.g., a shorter-duration annuity or liquid fixed-income portfolio) violates the fiduciary obligation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
NY Regulation 187 §227.2(a) — 'Best Interest' Duty for Annuity Recommendations

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommending a 7-year surrender-charge annuity to a client with documented 3–5 year liquidity needs violates the state 'best interest' fiduciary standard even if compliant with federal FINRA suitability rules.
