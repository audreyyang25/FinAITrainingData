Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An SEC-registered investment adviser manages $42 million across 85 client accounts. The adviser's principal officer also serves as a director of a small-cap REIT. The adviser allocates 12-18% of client portfolios to shares of the REIT, including for clients with moderate risk tolerances and 3-5 year time horizons. The adviser's Form ADV Part 2A discloses that 'personnel may have outside business activities,' but does not specifically identify the principal's directorship at the REIT, the compensation received (approximately $75,000 annually in director fees plus equity grants), or the inherent conflict of interest in recommending the REIT to advisory clients. When a client approaching retirement questions the concentration, the adviser states the REIT is 'a great diversifier' without disclosing the personal financial interest.

QUESTION
--------
Does the adviser's generic Form ADV disclosure of outside business activities satisfy the fiduciary duty of loyalty and full disclosure under the Investment Advisers Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates the Investment Advisers Act fiduciary duty of loyalty and the duty of full and fair disclosure of material conflicts.

As articulated in SEC Release IA-4889, the fiduciary duty under the Advisers Act requires full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts of interest, sufficiently detailed so that a client can provide informed consent. A generic disclosure that 'personnel may have outside business activities' is insufficient when the principal has a direct directorship and receives material compensation from a specific security being recommended to clients. The SEC's proposed interpretation emphasizes that disclosure must be specific enough to allow the client to understand and evaluate the conflict. The 12-18% portfolio concentration in a single illiquid REIT linked to the adviser's personal financial interest, without specific written disclosure, constitutes a breach of the duty of loyalty.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Sections 206(1) and 206(2); SEC Release No. IA-4889 (Proposed Interpretation Regarding Standard of Conduct for Investment Advisers)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The pivotal factor is the adviser's failure to make specific, individualized disclosure of the directorship and associated compensation—rendering the generic Form ADV language materially inadequate for informed client consent.
