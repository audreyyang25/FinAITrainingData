ID: 180
Source Folder: unknown
Topic: concentration
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 71-year-old widow with $800,000 in a joint brokerage/IRA account structure requires regular distributions of $4,000/month for living expenses and has moderate-conservative risk tolerance. Her advisor recommends concentrating 50% ($400,000) in a single dividend-paying stock (mature utility company) and 20% ($160,000) in a single high-yield bond fund. The advisor states: 'This gives you income you need, with lower volatility than growth stocks.' No analysis is conducted on the concentration risk of a two-holding portfolio or the reinvestment risk if either holding faces credit deterioration.

QUESTION
--------
Does this income-focused concentration strategy satisfy FINRA suitability and fiduciary duty under the Investment Adviser Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the applicable standard.

The advisor has failed the duty of care by: (1) concentrating 70% of a modest $800,000 portfolio in just two holdings without analyzing what happens if either deteriorates; (2) assuming income-generation justifies concentration without considering the client's liquidity and safety needs; (3) failing to address that the widow, age 71, needs stability and diversification to ensure her $4,000/month distribution can be maintained over a 15-25+ year horizon; (4) not analyzing concentration risk of an income portfolio — if the utility faces dividend cut or the high-yield fund faces credit crisis, both income streams fail simultaneously. A 71-year-old moderate-conservative investor with fixed income needs requires diversification to reduce single-issuer and single-sector risk, which this concentration approach fails to provide.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability; must consider financial situation, needs, and objectives); Investment Adviser Act § 206(2) (fiduciary duty of care includes analyzing concentration risk); SEC Reg BI Rule 15l-1(b)(2)(ii) (reasonable basis requires understanding concentration and liquidity risk).

KEY FACTOR
----------
70% concentration in two holdings for an income-dependent retiree without any documented analysis of concentration risk, sector risk, or what happens if either holding deteriorates violates the duty of care.
