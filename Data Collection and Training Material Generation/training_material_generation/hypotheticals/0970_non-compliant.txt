ID: 970
Source Folder: 23_International_Comparative
Topic: fca_consumer_duty_fair_value_assessment
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A UK retail bank distributes a structured investment product manufactured by a third party to mass-market retail customers. The bank charges a 3.5% distribution fee layered on top of the manufacturer's 2% annual management charge, resulting in a total cost that materially erodes projected returns for the target market. The bank conducts no independent fair value assessment and relies solely on the manufacturer's product approval documentation, which was prepared eighteen months ago and has not been updated to reflect current market conditions. Customers are not informed of the cumulative fee impact on net returns.

QUESTION
--------
Does the bank's distribution approach comply with FCA Consumer Duty obligations regarding fair value and product governance?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of FCA Consumer Duty (PRIN 12) and PROD 4 product governance rules.

Consumer Duty requires distributors to independently assess whether products deliver fair value to the target market, including the cumulative impact of their own charges alongside manufacturer costs, and to ensure customers receive the information needed to make informed decisions. Relying on stale manufacturer documentation without conducting a current fair value assessment and failing to disclose the compound fee impact to customers breaches both the Price and Value outcome and the Consumer Understanding outcome under PRIN 12.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FCA Consumer Duty PRIN 12.3 (Price and Value outcome); FCA PROD 4.2; PS22/9

KEY FACTOR
----------
Distributor's failure to conduct an independent, current fair value assessment and non-disclosure of cumulative cost impact to retail customers.
