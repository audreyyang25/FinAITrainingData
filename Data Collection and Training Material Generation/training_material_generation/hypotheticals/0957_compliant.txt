Topic: expungement_three_arbitrator_panel_requirement
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Broker Sandra Kim filed a straight-in expungement arbitration after a customer complaint was settled for $12,000 arising from allegations of misrepresentation in the sale of a mutual fund. The expungement hearing was conducted before a three-arbitrator panel as required by FINRA's 2022 expungement rule amendments. The panel reviewed the settlement agreement, the original customer complaint file, and testimony from the claimant's former client, ultimately finding that the weight of the evidence demonstrated the customer's claim was clearly erroneous because the broker had provided adequate disclosure documents and the customer had acknowledged receipt.

QUESTION
--------
Does the expungement proceeding comply with FINRA's amended expungement rules when a three-arbitrator panel makes a clearly erroneous finding supported by the evidentiary record?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

FINRA's 2022 expungement rule amendments, effective October 2023, require that straight-in expungement requests be heard by a three-arbitrator panel composed of arbitrators from a specialized roster, directly addressing PIABA's longstanding advocacy concern that single-arbitrator panels were too permissive in granting expungement. The panel properly conducted an evidentiary hearing, reviewed the underlying complaint record, and made a specific finding of clearly erroneous claim supported by the evidence, satisfying the mandatory requirements of FINRA Rule 2080 and Rule 12805.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2080; FINRA Rules 12805 and 13805 as amended effective October 2023; FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure

KEY FACTOR
----------
A three-arbitrator panel made a specific, evidence-based finding of one of the three required statutory grounds under FINRA Rule 2080, satisfying amended expungement procedural requirements.
