Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Jonathan Mercer is a financial advisor at Summit Advisory Group who, under the DOL's 2024 Retirement Security Rule, qualifies as an investment advice fiduciary when providing recommendations to IRA holders. Jonathan advises Maria Santos, age 67, a recently widowed retiree who inherited her husband's $780,000 traditional IRA. Maria has limited investment knowledge, needs to begin required minimum distributions, and has expressed a conservative risk tolerance. Jonathan recommends Maria invest 65% of the IRA in a portfolio of non-traded REITs and business development companies (BDCs) with 7-year lock-up periods, limited liquidity provisions, and front-end loads totaling 7%. Jonathan discloses the commission structure and provides a written acknowledgment of his fiduciary status, but his documented rationale states only that 'the client desires income-producing investments.' Summit Advisory Group has written policies and procedures for PTE 2020-02 compliance but its retrospective compliance review is conducted only annually and has not yet reviewed any of Jonathan's 2024 recommendations.

QUESTION
--------
Does Jonathan's recommendation of illiquid alternative investments to the widowed IRA holder satisfy PTE 2020-02's best interest standard despite his disclosure of compensation?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates PTE 2020-02's care obligation and the Retirement Security Rule's best interest standard.

While Jonathan provided written disclosures and acknowledged his fiduciary status, PTE 2020-02's best interest standard requires more than disclosure—it demands that the advice itself reflect the care, skill, prudence, and diligence of a knowledgeable professional. Recommending that a 67-year-old widowed retiree with conservative risk tolerance and imminent RMD obligations place 65% of her inherited IRA in illiquid, long-lock-up alternative products with steep front-end loads fails the prudence standard on its face. The documented rationale is woefully inadequate, and the liquidity mismatch between Maria's distribution needs and the 7-year lock-ups demonstrates the recommendation was not in her best interest. Disclosure of conflicts does not cure a fundamentally imprudent recommendation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
DOL PTE 2020-02, Section II(a) – Care Obligation; 2024 Retirement Security Rule – Investment Advice Fiduciary Definition and Best Interest Standard

KEY FACTOR
----------
Disclosure of compensation does not satisfy PTE 2020-02 when the underlying recommendation is fundamentally imprudent given the client's age, liquidity needs, RMD obligations, and conservative risk tolerance.
