Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 74-year-old retired teacher with $350,000 in a rollover IRA, a fixed pension of $2,800/month, and stated goal of 'capital preservation and supplemental income' is a client of a registered representative at a wirehousse firm. The advisor recommends concentration in a single junk-bond closed-end fund (CEF) yielding 9.5% (leveraged 2:1, expense ratio 1.8%), representing 45% of the IRA, funded by liquidating diversified mutual funds. The advisor documents: 'Client aggressive, suitable for CEF.' The client expresses hesitation ('seems risky'), but the advisor assures her the yield is 'guaranteed' and states 'CEFs always distribute.' The advisor does not discuss leverage, distribution risk, market risk, or tax treatment (though in a non-qualified account, the 9.5% distribution would create substantial ordinary income). The advisor records the sale as 'at customer's direction' to avoid suitability documentation.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability), Rule 2010 (General Conduct Standards), and DOL ERISA fiduciary duty for retirement accounts?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates multiple applicable standards.

This recommendation violates suitability in multiple respects. First, the concentration in a single leveraged CEF (45% of the IRA) is unsuitable for a 74-year-old with a stated goal of 'capital preservation,' as leverage amplifies downside risk contrary to the client's express objective. Second, the advisor misrepresented the yield as 'guaranteed' when CEF distributions are discretionary and may be paid from capital, violating Rule 2010's prohibition on deceptive conduct. Third, the advisor failed to discuss leverage risk, distribution risk, or the fact that leverage creates tax-inefficiency inside a tax-deferred account, demonstrating failure to conduct the Rule 2111 suitability analysis required before recommending a complex product to a senior. Fourth, the advisor's notation 'at customer's direction' appears pretextual, as the client's hesitation ('seems risky') was documented, yet the advisor proceeded without addressing the concern. Finally, as a rollover IRA, this account is subject to DOL ERISA fiduciary duties, which require a prudent diversification analysis—the concentration in a single leveraged CEF fails the prudence test under 29 U.S.C. § 1104(a)(1)(B). The client's age (74), modest asset base, and stated capital preservation goal all support a finding of unsuitability for a 2:1 leveraged junk bond fund.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability Rule); FINRA Rule 2010 (fair dealing); DOL ERISA Section 404(a) fiduciary duty (prudent person rule, diversification); SEC senior investor guidance; Investment Adviser Act Section 206 (if applicable based on firm status)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The combination of concentration in a single leveraged product, misrepresentation of yield as guaranteed, absence of documented suitability analysis despite a complex product, documented client hesitation that was not addressed, and conflict with the client's stated capital preservation goal—all in a retirement account subject to heightened fiduciary scrutiny.
