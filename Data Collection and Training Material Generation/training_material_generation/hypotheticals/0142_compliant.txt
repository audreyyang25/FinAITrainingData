FACTS
-----
A 48-year-old executive with $950,000 in a 401(k) and a high income ($280,000/year) meets with an independent RIA that is a fiduciary under ERISA when providing advice on the 401(k). The executive plans to leave the firm in 18 months and retire at 55. The 401(k) offers a limited menu of 15 low-cost institutional funds (average ER: 0.12%) and does not permit in-service distributions or Roth conversions. The RIA documents in writing that a rollover to a self-directed IRA would allow: (1) access to specific ESG-focused funds and direct real estate investments aligned with the executive's stated values, (2) more flexible withdrawal and conversion strategies for early retirement planning, and (3) negotiated fee-only advisory services at 0.60% AUM (lower than the bundled 1.5% institutional advisory fee the executive was considering). The RIA conducts a written needs analysis, compares the 401(k) plan and IRA fee structures, and explicitly documents that the executive's circumstances—pending departure, early retirement goals, and specific investment preferences—make the rollover in the executive's best interest despite the advisory fee component.

QUESTION
--------
Does this rollover recommendation comply with DOL ERISA fiduciary duty and PTE 2020-02 requirements?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation satisfies DOL ERISA fiduciary duty and the conditions of PTE 2020-02.

The RIA's rollover recommendation satisfies all four core requirements of PTE 2020-02. First, it demonstrates reasonable inquiry: the RIA conducted a detailed written needs analysis specific to the executive's age, income level, pending job transition, early retirement timeline (55), and stated investment values (ESG, real estate exposure). Second, the fee analysis is thorough and transparent: the RIA documented the 401(k)'s 0.12% average ER, the alternative bundled fee of 1.5%, and the proposed rollover fee of 0.60%, showing the rollover fee is competitive and justified by the expanded investment access and specific planning benefit (in-service conversions, withdrawal flexibility). Third, the recommendation is not tainted by compensation incentive: the 0.60% AUM fee on a $950,000 account ($5,700/year) is modest and disclosed; the RIA earned the same fee whether the assets remained in the 401(k) plan or rolled to an IRA (the 401(k) did not offer RIA advisory services, so the RIA had no competing compensation). Fourth, the documented business justification is sound: the executive's specific circumstances (upcoming departure, stated investment objectives, early retirement planning needs) make the rollover rationally supportable under 29 CFR 2550.404a-1(b)(4)(viii), which permits rollover advice when the fiduciary acts in the participant's best interest and the recommendation is not based on the fiduciary's compensation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
DOL ERISA Section 406(b) (fiduciary duty); DOL Prohibited Transaction Exemption 2020-02 (February 27, 2021); 29 CFR 2550.404a-1(b)(4) (impartial conduct; duty to act solely in participant's interest).

KEY FACTOR
----------
The RIA documented a comprehensive written analysis tied to the participant's specific facts (pending departure, early retirement, investment preferences), compared all material fees, and charged reasonable compensation, establishing that the rollover was based on the participant's demonstrable best interest rather than fiduciary compensation incentive.
