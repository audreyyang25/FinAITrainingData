Source Folder: 03_FINRA_AWC
Topic: finra_awc_supervision
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Pinnacle Wealth Advisors, a FINRA member firm with 45 registered representatives, implemented a comprehensive Reg BI compliance program effective June 15, 2020, two weeks before the compliance deadline. The program includes updated written supervisory procedures addressing all four Reg BI component obligations, a conflicts-of-interest inventory covering proprietary products and revenue-sharing arrangements, a Form CRS delivered to all existing retail customers, and mandatory annual training for all associated persons. The firm's supervisory system includes automated surveillance alerts for concentrated positions, excessive trading, and suitability mismatches based on customer investment profiles. In a recent internal audit, the firm discovered that one representative had recommended a complex structured product to a 67-year-old retiree. The firm's supervisory review flagged the recommendation, the designated principal reviewed it within 48 hours, determined the product was unsuitable given the client's conservative profile, and the trade was reversed before settlement with no loss to the client. The firm documented the review, counseled the representative, and placed a heightened supervision letter in his file.

QUESTION
--------
Is Pinnacle Wealth Advisors in compliance with its Reg BI and FINRA supervisory obligations given that one representative made a potentially unsuitable recommendation that was caught and reversed by the firm's supervisory system?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Reg BI's Compliance Obligation and FINRA Rule 3110 require firms to establish, maintain, and enforce written supervisory procedures reasonably designed to achieve compliance—not to guarantee that no individual violation will ever occur. The firm's system functioned as designed: it detected the potentially unsuitable recommendation, a principal promptly reviewed and reversed it, and the firm took corrective action with the representative. This is precisely the type of robust supervisory framework whose absence led to sanctions in the Alexander Investment Services AWC. A firm is not in violation simply because an associated person makes an error, provided the supervisory system is reasonably designed and effectively catches and remediates issues.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(iv) – Reg BI Compliance Obligation; FINRA Rule 3110 – Supervision

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's reasonably designed supervisory system detected, reversed, and documented the unsuitable recommendation before customer harm occurred, demonstrating effective compliance with Reg BI and FINRA Rule 3110.
