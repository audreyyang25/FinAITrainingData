Topic: ia_cherry_picking

Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Quantified Advisors, a $520M RIA, managed a performance-fee portfolio (Portfolio X) under an explicit written agreement dated May 2022. The agreement stated: 'Client Portfolio X will participate in a performance-based fee structure in which Adviser receives 1% base fee plus 15% of net outperformance above the Russell 2000 Index. To accommodate incentive alignment, Adviser may allocate block trades using a performance-allocation methodology: (i) trades generated by Adviser's proprietary research flagged as "high-conviction" may receive proportional allocation to Portfolio X, pro-rata with other client portfolios of similar strategy, subject to (ii) explicit quarterly reporting to the client, and (iii) the allocation shall not exceed the client's pro-rata AUM share by more than 5% in any single quarter.' In Q3 2023, Quantified executed 12 block trades labeled "high-conviction." Portfolio X (representing 8% of total AUM) received 8.3% of the allocations from these trades, a 0.3% overweight within the 5% tolerance band. Three such trades outperformed market by more than 200 basis points. All allocations were documented, and a detailed Q3 allocation report was delivered to Portfolio X's client on October 15, 2023, before year-end statements. The client had received the original performance-fee agreement and allocation methodology disclosure at account inception.

QUESTION
--------
Does this performance-allocation methodology comply with Section 206 and Rule 17j-1, given the explicit client agreement and disclosure?

ANSWER
------
Yes. This complies with Section 206(1) and Rule 17j-1. The adviser met all compliance elements: (1) a written allocation policy existed and was explicitly incorporated into the client's advisory agreement before any allocation occurred; (2) the policy was disclosed to the client in advance; (3) the allocation methodology was clearly defined and included a guardrail (5% tolerance) limiting proprietary preference; (4) the adviser applied the policy consistently (allocations stayed within the 5% band); (5) the adviser reported allocations transparently and promptly to the client with sufficient detail to assess compliance; and (6) there was a legitimate business rationale (performance-fee alignment). Rule 17j-1 and Section 206(1) do not prohibit adviser self-dealing; they require advance written disclosure, consistent application, and fair dealing. The fact that some high-conviction trades outperformed does not invalidate the allocation—the policy permits it, the client agreed, and the allocation was documented and reported. This mirrors SEC no-action letters permitting performance-based allocations under explicit client agreements.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act Section 206(1) (fiduciary duty of loyalty, permitting agreed self-dealing with disclosure); SEC Rule 17j-1(a), (b)(1), (c)(1) (written policy, consistent application, advance disclosure, and reporting)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Explicit advance client agreement to performance-allocation methodology, consistent application within disclosed guardrails, and transparent quarterly reporting satisfy Section 206(1) and Rule 17j-1 requirements.
