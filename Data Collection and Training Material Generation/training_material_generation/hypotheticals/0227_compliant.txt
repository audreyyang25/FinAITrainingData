FACTS
-----
Advisor Thompson at a fee-only RIA recommended that her 68-year-old client, a retired manufacturing executive with $3.2M in investable assets, roll over his current 401(k) balance ($580,000) from his former employer Acme Corp into a self-directed IRA. The 401(k) held a stable value fund (3.5% guaranteed return, 0.15% annual fee) and a proprietary company stock fund (30% of 401(k), dividend yield 2.1%, ER 0.08%, highly concentrated). Thompson documented: (1) detailed rollover vs. stay analysis showing IRA would provide better investment options (access to low-cost ETFs vs. limited 401(k) menu); (2) tax impact analysis (no immediate tax consequence, as same institution custodying both); (3) withdrawal rule analysis (client was age 68, past 59½, so no early withdrawal penalty if needed); (4) fee comparison ($1,740 annual 401(k) cost vs. $1,270 estimated IRA management cost under Thompson's 0.35% AUM model on consolidated assets); (5) note that company stock represented undue concentration and would be better managed in IRA; and (6) alternative recommendation: remain in 401(k) for access to low-interest 401(k) loan provisions (client had no stated liquidity need for loans). Thompson had no rollover-referral revenue incentive (flat-fee advisor model).

QUESTION
--------
Did Advisor Thompson comply with the Reg BI Care Obligation by recommending a 401(k) rollover to an IRA?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Thompson complied with Reg BI's Care Obligation for rollover recommendations (17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)). The standard requires advisors to: (1) conduct reasonable diligence regarding both staying and rolling over; (2) document analysis of fees, investment options, and withdrawal rules; (3) disclose any conflicts (Thompson had none—flat-fee model); and (4) recommend based on the client's best interest. Thompson's documentation exceeded these requirements: quantified fee savings ($470 annually), analyzed concentration risk (company stock), explained tax-neutral treatment, confirmed no early withdrawal penalties, and explicitly noted the 401(k) loan alternative (though not applicable here). The rollover recommendation was supported by superior investment options, lower aggregate fees, and de-concentration benefits. The client's age, financial sophistication, and lack of stated loan needs made the rollover appropriate. Thompson's analysis exemplifies compliant Reg BI rollover advice.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation (Rollover Recommendations), 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)(A)-(C); SEC Rollover Guidance IA-3461

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented comprehensive analysis of rollover vs. stay, quantified fee savings, addressed concentration risk, and disclosed absence of advisor conflict supports Reg BI-compliant rollover recommendation.
