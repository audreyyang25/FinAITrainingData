Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
David Patel, an investment adviser representative registered in Massachusetts, manages a $450,000 portfolio for a retired schoolteacher, Sandra Wilkins, age 67. Without disclosing that he receives 12b-1 trail fees, David recommends moving Sandra's portfolio from low-cost Vanguard index funds into a series of Class C mutual funds with 1.00% annual 12b-1 fees and higher expense ratios. David's written investment policy statement for Sandra states her objectives are capital preservation and income. David does not present any lower-cost alternatives or explain why the higher-cost funds would better serve Sandra's stated objectives.

QUESTION
--------
Does David's conduct comply with the Massachusetts fiduciary standard under 950 CMR 12.207?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates the Massachusetts fiduciary duty standard under 950 CMR 12.207.

Under Massachusetts' fiduciary conduct standard (950 CMR 12.207), investment adviser representatives owe a fiduciary duty to act in the client's best interest, which includes a duty of loyalty requiring full disclosure of material conflicts such as 12b-1 fee compensation. Moving a capital-preservation client from low-cost index funds to higher-cost Class C shares without disclosing the trail fee conflict or demonstrating how the recommendation serves the client's stated objectives violates both the duty of care and the duty of loyalty. The failure to present lower-cost alternatives further evidences a recommendation driven by the adviser's compensation interest rather than the client's best interest.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
MA 950 CMR 12.207 – Fiduciary Duty of Investment Advisers and Their Agents

KEY FACTOR
----------
Undisclosed 12b-1 fee conflicts combined with a move from low-cost to high-cost funds inconsistent with the client's capital preservation objective violates the Massachusetts fiduciary duty of loyalty and care.
