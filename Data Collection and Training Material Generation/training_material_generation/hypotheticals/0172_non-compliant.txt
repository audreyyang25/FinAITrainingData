Source Folder: unknown
Topic: undisclosed_conflict
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An RIA with $500M AUM manages a $2.8M portfolio for a 58-year-old client seeking growth with moderate risk tolerance. The RIA recommends a $1.2M allocation to a proprietary mutual fund complex (managed by the RIA's parent company) that charges a 0.95% management fee. The RIA receives an undisclosed 12 basis point revenue-sharing payment directly from the fund sponsor, in addition to the client's regular advisory fees. The RIA's written disclosure documents are silent on this arrangement.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation and compensation structure satisfy the RIA's fiduciary duty of loyalty and Reg BI Conflict Obligation?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the RIA's fiduciary duty of loyalty and Reg BI's Conflict Obligation.

The RIA receives two separate forms of compensation from the same transaction without the client's knowledge: advisory fees plus revenue-sharing. Under the Investment Adviser Act § 206(2), an adviser must 'disclose to such clients in writing' any conflicts of interest that could impair the adviser's ability to provide impartial advice. The SEC has consistently held that compensation arrangements creating incentives to favor certain products are material conflicts requiring written disclosure (IA Release No. 2759). Here, the 12 bp revenue-sharing creates a financial incentive to overweight the proprietary fund relative to unaffiliated alternatives. Because this arrangement is completely undisclosed in the advisory contract or brochure, the RIA has violated its core fiduciary duty of loyalty. The fact that the recommendation may be suitable does not cure the disclosure violation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act § 206(1) & (2) (fiduciary duty to act in client's best interest and disclose material conflicts); SEC Reg BI § 211(a)(1)(A) (broker-dealers must disclose material conflicts of interest); SEC IA Guidance on Conflicts of Interest (IA Release No. 2759, 2014) establishing that undisclosed compensation arrangements are material conflicts requiring affirmative disclosure.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Undisclosed revenue-sharing compensation creates an undisclosed material conflict of interest that directly violates Investment Adviser Act § 206(2) duty to disclose conflicts in writing.
