Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
James, age 81, widow with $310,000 in savings, no pension, relies on Social Security ($1,950/month). James takes out a reverse mortgage on his home (appraised value $520,000), receiving a $180,000 lump sum. James does not consult Advisor Garcia beforehand. James then meets Garcia and says, 'I have $180,000 to invest and want growth.' Garcia, sensing an opportunity, recommends: $120,000 into a 3x leveraged inverse ETF (seeks to profit from stock market declines; -$10 for each 1% S&P gain); $60,000 into a small-cap momentum mutual fund (2.2% ER, high turnover 156%, annualized risk 18%). Garcia documents: 'Client seeks growth and can tolerate volatility.' Garcia does not inquire whether James understands the reverse mortgage implications (he will owe repayment + interest if he sells the home or his estate will repay from sale proceeds). James mentions he lives alone and wants to leave 'something' to his grandson. Within 8 months, the S&P 500 rallies 11%; James's $180,000 drops to $158,000 (leveraged inverse losses + fund volatility). James, distressed, calls Garcia asking to stop the decline.

QUESTION
--------
Did Advisor Garcia comply with Reg BI Care Obligation and FINRA Suitability Rule 2111 in recommending leveraged inverse ETF and high-risk small-cap fund to James?

ANSWER
------
No, substantial violations on multiple fronts. (1) Reg BI Care Obligation (§240.15l-1(a)(2)(i)) requires reasonable inquiry into 'material facts concerning the customer's investment profile.' Garcia failed to inquire: Does James understand the reverse mortgage mechanics (repayment obligations, non-recourse nature, interest costs)? What is James's true time horizon—he is 81 with no pension. Does he really seek 'growth' or income stability? Rule 2111 suitability similarly requires understanding of objectives and needs. (2) Product mismatch: A 3x leveraged inverse ETF is a complex tactical short instrument unsuitable for an 81-year-old with finite assets, no income buffer, and no demonstrated derivatives sophistication. Garcia has no documentation that James understands he profits only if equities decline—opposite to his stated 'growth' goal. (3) High-turnover (156%), concentrated-risk small-cap momentum fund at 2.2% ER is unsuitable for retiree. (4) Sequence-of-returns risk: James has no job income; losses early in his drawdown phase are particularly harmful. (5) Estate planning conflict: James mentioned leaving assets to grandson; leveraged bets may erode legacy. SEC and FINRA enforcement (similar to FINRA AWC 2020-007) shows leveraged/inverse products sold to seniors as presumptively unsuitable absent clear documented understanding and expressed hedging need. Garcia's failure to inquire into reverse mortgage context and James's actual financial situation constitutes negligence under both Reg BI and Rule 2111.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i); FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability); SEC Senior Investor Report 2018, pp. 25–27 (leveraged products for seniors)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Advisor recommended complex leveraged inverse ETF and high-turnover small-cap fund to 81-year-old with reverse mortgage and no income buffer, absent reasonable inquiry into understanding or need.
