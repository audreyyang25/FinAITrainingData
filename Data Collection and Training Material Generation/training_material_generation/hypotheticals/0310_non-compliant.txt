Topic: ia_fee_arrangements
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Heritage Capital Partners, a $2.1 billion RIA, offers a performance fee arrangement to qualified clients (as defined under Rule 205-3(d)(1)—net worth exceeding $2.1 million individually or $5.25 million jointly). The adviser's Form ADV Part 2A discloses that performance fees are charged only to qualified investors at rates of 15% of net gains (with a high-water mark and lookback period of 36 months). However, in April 2023, Heritage Capital enters into a side letter with its largest client, Zenith PE Fund ($156 million AUM managed separately), reducing Zenith's performance fee to 10% and waiving the 36-month lookback (effective immediately for a single year). This side letter is signed by the adviser's founder and is not disclosed to other qualified clients or reflected in Zenith's Form ADV Part 2A. Zenith qualifies as a qualified client. Nine other qualified clients, each with $18M–$45M in Heritage Capital accounts, pay the full 15% rate with the standard 36-month lookback and high-water mark.

QUESTION
--------
Did Heritage Capital comply with Rule 205-3 and its fiduciary disclosure obligations by offering Zenith a materially more favorable performance fee arrangement via side letter without disclosing the differential terms to other qualified clients?

ANSWER
------
No. Although Rule 205-3(d)(1) permits performance fees charged to qualified clients, Rule 205-3(c) requires all performance fees to comply with the written agreement standard and Form ADV Part 2A disclosure requirements. Rule 205-3(d)(2) does not create an exception for side letters offering material fee reductions to subset of clients. Heritage Capital's failure to disclose the Zenith side letter to other qualified clients violates the fiduciary duty of loyalty—the adviser usurped a business opportunity (favorable fee terms) for one client without equal treatment disclosure. SEC enforcement (e.g., SEC v. Advisers matter patterns) treats non-uniform fee arrangements as undisclosed conflicts of interest. The 5% fee reduction and waiver of lookback creates a material conflict that should have been disclosed in Form ADV and individual investment advisory contracts, or offered uniformly. Zenith's status as a qualified client does not exempt the adviser from disclosing the differential to comparably situated clients.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Rule 205-3(c), (d)(1)–(2); 17 CFR §275.205-3; Investment Adviser Act §206(1) (Duty of Loyalty)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Non-disclosure of materially more favorable side letter performance fee terms to one qualified client while charging other qualified clients standard rates breaches the fiduciary duty of loyalty and Rule 205-3 written disclosure requirements.
