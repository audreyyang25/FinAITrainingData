Source Folder: 03_FINRA_AWC
Topic: finra_awc_suitability
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Robert Gennaro, a General Securities Representative associated with Wells Fargo Clearing Services, exercised discretionary trading authority in 12 customer accounts from January 2023 to April 2024 without obtaining prior written authorization from the customers or having the accounts accepted as discretionary by the firm. Gennaro made between 3 and 15 unsolicited trades per account, rotating among equity and fixed-income positions, generating modest profits in 9 of the 12 accounts. Several customers verbally told Gennaro to 'use his best judgment' during phone calls, but no written discretionary authorizations were ever executed. When the firm's compliance department flagged the pattern, Gennaro stated that the customers had given him oral permission and that the trades were profitable, so no harm was done.

QUESTION
--------
Did Gennaro's exercise of discretion based on oral customer authorization, without written authorization or firm acceptance, comply with FINRA rules even though most accounts were profitable?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. Gennaro violated FINRA Rule 3260(b) (Discretionary Accounts) and FINRA Rule 2010 by exercising discretion without written customer authorization and firm acceptance.

FINRA Rule 3260(b) explicitly requires that no member or registered representative shall exercise discretionary power in a customer's account unless the customer has given prior written authorization and the account has been accepted by a designated principal of the member firm. Verbal authorization, regardless of how clearly given, does not satisfy this written authorization requirement. Profitability of the trades is irrelevant to the violation—the rule is prophylactic in nature. This fact pattern directly mirrors the Robert Wylie Goff AWC, where identical conduct in 12 accounts at Wells Fargo resulted in a one-month suspension and $5,000 fine.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3260(b) – Discretionary Accounts; FINRA Rule 2010 – Standards of Commercial Honor and Principles of Just and Equitable Trade

KEY FACTOR
----------
Oral authorization to trade at discretion does not satisfy FINRA Rule 3260(b)'s explicit requirement for prior written customer authorization and firm acceptance of the discretionary account.
