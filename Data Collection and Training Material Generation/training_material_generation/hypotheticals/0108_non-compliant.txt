Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 58-year-old widow with $320,000 in savings, minimal investment experience, and self-described 'need for capital preservation' worked with a broker for three years. The account generated $47,000 in commissions over that period through frequent trading (average holding period 3.2 months) despite a stated goal of long-term buy-and-hold investing. The holdings rotated through speculative microcap stocks (average cost basis $0.82/share), leveraged inverse ETFs, and oil exploration penny stocks. The account declined from $320,000 to $198,000 (38% loss) over the three-year period. When the widow questioned the strategy, the broker's suitability review (conducted annually) simply noted 'customer indicated comfort with growth' and 'active trading' without any analysis of whether the rapid turnover was consistent with her stated capital preservation objective or whether the individual securities were suitable for a conservative, inexperienced investor.

QUESTION
--------
Does this conduct violate FINRA Suitability Rule 2111?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct violates FINRA Suitability Rule 2111 on multiple grounds.

This conduct violates Rule 2111 on at least three grounds. First, it fails the quantitative suitability prong: $47,000 in commissions over three years with an average 3.2-month holding period constitutes churning in violation of Rule 2020, particularly given the 38% account decline. The rapid turnover generated commissions for the broker while harming the customer. Second, it violates the customer-specific suitability prong: the widow's stated objective was 'capital preservation,' yet the broker recommended speculative microcaps, penny stocks, and leveraged inverse ETFs—high-risk, complex instruments fundamentally unsuitable for a conservative, inexperienced investor approaching retirement. Third, the 'suitability review' memorializing only 'comfort with growth' is pretextual documentation that does not reflect actual analysis of whether the specific securities or trading strategy suited her profile. FINRA has sanctioned brokers in nearly identical scenarios (see cases involving churned accounts with losses and speculative products sold to conservative clients), emphasizing that a broker's subjective characterization of customer risk tolerance cannot override objective facts (the stated 'capital preservation' goal and the customer's age, experience, and account decline).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) requires a reasonable basis for suitability. FINRA Rule 2111(b) requires suitability recommendations to be made only when the member has 'reasonable basis to believe' the recommendation is suitable to the customer based on their investment profile. FINRA Rule 2020 prohibits 'excessive' trading ('churning') which violates the duty of best execution and fair dealing. FINRA Rule 2030(b) requires that all recommendations be made 'in the best interests' of the customer.

KEY FACTOR
----------
The combination of stated 'capital preservation' objective with active trading generating high commissions (resulting in 38% account losses), coupled with recommendations of speculative microcaps and penny stocks unsuitable for an inexperienced, conservative investor, constitutes both a quantitative (churning) and customer-specific suitability violation.
