ID: 745
Source Folder: 12_FINRA_Arbitration_Awards
Topic: arbitration_defenses
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
TD Ameritrade registered representative Lisa Cho managed a self-directed brokerage account for 52-year-old software engineer Kevin Tan, who had $750,000 in liquid assets and described himself as having 'aggressive' risk tolerance with 20 years of active trading experience. Tan directed Cho to purchase $85,000 in a concentrated position of a single biotech stock, representing approximately 11% of his portfolio. Cho documented Tan's directive in a contemporaneous email, flagged the concentration risk in writing, and noted that Tan acknowledged the risk and wished to proceed. The stock subsequently lost 60% of its value, and Tan filed a FINRA arbitration claim alleging unsuitability and failure to supervise.

QUESTION
--------
Is the representative liable for an unsuitable recommendation based on the concentrated biotech stock position?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

When a sophisticated, self-directed customer with aggressive risk tolerance and extensive trading experience initiates a specific investment directive, and the representative documents the unsolicited nature of the trade, provides a written concentration risk disclosure, and obtains the customer's written acknowledgment, the representative has met the suitability and supervisory obligations. An 11% position in a single stock within a $750,000 portfolio of an aggressive investor does not constitute an inherently unsuitable concentration. The contemporaneous documentation serves as a strong affirmative defense in arbitration, as panels regularly consider the customer's sophistication, directive nature of the trade, and written risk acknowledgment.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability — Customer-Specific Obligation); FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative documented the client-directed nature of the trade, provided written concentration risk warnings, and obtained the sophisticated client's written acknowledgment, establishing a strong defense against suitability claims.
