Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Registered Representative James T. (age 52, employed at Midtown Securities for 8 years) recommended to Client A (age 67, $385,000 liquid portfolio) a switch from the Vanguard Growth A-share mutual fund (VEXAX, front-end load 5.75%, current holding period 6 years, NAV $24.18) to the identical fund held at the firm under a different share class arrangement (A-shares again, new front-end load 5.75%, cost $22,111). The switch occurred in January 2023. Client held the original fund specifically to avoid future loads. No documented recommendation letter or suitability statement was created. James earned $1,271 in gross commission on the switch (5.75% × $22,111). The firm's compliance department discovered the transaction in a Q2 audit but took no action.

QUESTION
--------
Did James T.'s recommendation to switch from A-shares to A-shares of the same fund comply with FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability) and FINRA Rule 2010 (Fair Dealing)?

ANSWER
------
No. The switch violates FINRA Rule 2111's best-execution suitability obligation and Rule 2010's prohibition on fraudulent, deceptive conduct. James recommended a switch that generated a new 5.75% load ($1,271 commission to him) on identical securities the client already owned. The 6-year holding period demonstrated the client's intent to avoid multiple loads. No legitimate business purpose (tax loss harvesting, strategic reallocation, or performance improvement) justified the trade. The absence of a recommendation statement compounds the violation. FINRA enforcement precedent (similar to 2019 patterns) treats A-to-A same-fund switches as presumptively unsuitable unless documented with compelling rationale. The firm's supervisory failure (Rule 3110) to flag and prevent this represents compounding liability.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) (Suitability), FINRA Rule 2010 (Standards of Commercial Honor and Fair Dealing), FINRA Rule 3110(b)(1) (Supervisory Procedures)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommending an A-to-A identical mutual fund switch that generated a new front-end load for the representative's benefit with no documented business rationale is presumptively unsuitable and violates duty of fair dealing.
