Source Folder: 18_OCC_Bank_Trust_Guidance
Topic: trust_investment_management
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Atlas National Bank's trust department serves as sole trustee for a $9.8 million charitable remainder unitrust (CRUT) with a 5.2% annual payout rate, where a 58-year-old income beneficiary receives distributions and a university endowment receives the remainder. The board of directors has approved a written policy permitting the trust department to allocate up to 20% of eligible fiduciary accounts to alternative investments, including private credit funds, where the investment policy statement expressly authorizes such allocations. Pursuant to this policy, the trust officer allocates $1.4 million (approximately 14.3%) to a private credit fund managed by an unaffiliated third-party manager, documents the liquidity analysis confirming the trust's unitrust payout can be funded from the remaining liquid assets for at least 36 months, and presents the full allocation rationale — including fee structure, lock-up period, and expected yield of 8.5% net — to the trust investment committee, which approves and minutes the decision. No affiliated party receives any compensation from the fund manager.

QUESTION
--------
Does Atlas National Bank's allocation of 14.3% of a charitable remainder unitrust to an unaffiliated private credit fund, pursuant to board-approved policy and documented committee review, comply with OCC fiduciary investment standards despite the illiquid nature of the asset?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

The OCC Asset Management handbook and operations and controls guidance recognize that alternative and illiquid investments are permissible in fiduciary accounts when the bank has established board-approved policies authorizing such allocations, the investment policy statement for the specific account permits the asset class, and the trust committee documents a thorough analysis of liquidity risk relative to the trust's distribution obligations. Atlas's 36-month liquidity stress analysis directly addressed the key risk for a CRUT with mandatory annual payouts, the 14.3% allocation was within the board-approved 20% ceiling, and the absence of any affiliated party compensation eliminates conflict-of-interest concerns. The documented committee deliberation and approval reflect the sound governance process OCC examiners assess under management and operations standards, and the use of an unaffiliated manager further insulates the decision from self-dealing scrutiny.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
OCC Comptroller's Handbook – Asset Management, Version 1.0 (June 2023); OCC Asset Management Operations and Controls (AM-2); Uniform Prudent Investor Act – Delegation and Diversification Provisions

KEY FACTOR
----------
The combination of a board-approved alternative investment policy, account-specific IPS authorization, documented 36-month liquidity analysis sufficient to cover CRUT distribution obligations, and complete absence of affiliated party compensation rendered an otherwise scrutiny-prone illiquid allocation fully compliant with OCC fiduciary investment management standards.
