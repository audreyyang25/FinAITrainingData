Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Midwestern University sponsors a defined contribution retirement plan with $1.8 billion in assets and 12,000 participants. The plan's investment committee conducts a comprehensive review of all plan investment options every three years, supplemented by quarterly performance monitoring. During the 2023 triennial review, the committee evaluates the plan's existing suite of 22 mutual funds and discovers that three funds have institutional share classes available at lower expense ratios — ranging from 8 to 14 basis points lower — than the retail share classes currently offered. The committee documents its analysis, negotiates with the recordkeeper to transition to institutional shares, and completes the share class conversion within 90 days. A participant subsequently files suit alleging the committee breached its fiduciary duty by originally selecting and retaining the higher-cost retail share classes for approximately two years before converting, citing Tibble v. Edison International.

QUESTION
--------
Did the university's investment committee satisfy its ongoing duty to monitor plan investments under ERISA despite initially offering higher-cost retail share classes?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

In Tibble v. Edison International, the Supreme Court held that ERISA fiduciaries have a continuing duty to monitor trust investments and remove imprudent ones within a reasonable time. However, this duty requires a prudent process, not a guarantee of the lowest-cost option at every moment. Here, the committee maintained a documented, systematic review process with quarterly monitoring and triennial comprehensive reviews. Upon discovering the availability of lower-cost institutional share classes, the committee acted promptly — completing the transition within 90 days. The two-year period during which retail shares were offered is defensible because the committee's monitoring process was reasonable and the fiduciaries acted within a reasonable time after discovering the issue.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
ERISA § 404(a)(1)(B) – Duty of Prudence; Tibble v. Edison International, 575 U.S. 523 (2015)

KEY FACTOR
----------
A documented, systematic investment monitoring process with prompt corrective action upon discovering lower-cost share class alternatives satisfies the continuing duty to monitor under Tibble v. Edison.
