Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Jennifer Ross, a dual-registered representative and investment adviser representative operating in Nevada, recommends that her client, Thomas Grant (age 55, $1.2M in investable assets, moderate risk tolerance), allocate $200,000 into a variable annuity with a guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit rider. Thomas has already maximized his 401(k) contributions and has adequate liquidity in a separate brokerage account with $600,000. Jennifer provides Thomas with a detailed written comparison of three variable annuity products, documents why the selected product's rider best fits his retirement income gap analysis, discloses her $8,500 commission, and notes on file that the recommendation was evaluated under both Nevada's fiduciary standard (NRS 628A) and SEC Reg BI. She also documents that lower-cost alternatives were considered but that the guaranteed income feature addressed Thomas's specific concern about longevity risk.

QUESTION
--------
Does Jennifer's recommendation comply with Nevada's enhanced fiduciary standard and applicable state-level best interest requirements?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Nevada's fiduciary statute (NRS 628A) requires financial advisors to act in the client's best interest. Jennifer's conduct satisfies this standard because she performed a documented needs analysis identifying a specific retirement income gap, considered and documented lower-cost alternatives, disclosed her compensation conflict, and provided a written comparison of competing products. The variable annuity is suitable given Thomas's age, adequate remaining liquidity, and the specific longevity risk concern that the guaranteed withdrawal rider addresses. Her dual compliance documentation under both state and federal standards reflects best practices.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Nevada NRS 628A – Fiduciary Duty for Financial Advisors; NY Reg 187 Best Interest Principles (used as comparative benchmark)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Thorough documentation of needs analysis, product comparison, disclosure of commission, and consideration of lower-cost alternatives satisfies Nevada's fiduciary standard despite the higher-commission product recommendation.
