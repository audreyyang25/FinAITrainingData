ID: 804
Source Folder: 15_FINRA_Regulatory_Notices
Topic: complex_products
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Apex Broker-Dealer launches a new initiative to market alternative mutual funds across its 45 branch offices. The firm creates a one-page product summary for three Alt Fund families—covering managed futures, long-short equity, and market neutral strategies—and distributes it to all 380 registered representatives via email. The summary describes the funds as 'providing portfolio diversification and downside protection' but does not mention the use of leverage, derivatives, short selling, or counterparty risk. No formal training sessions are conducted; instead, a recorded 12-minute webinar is made available but not mandatory. The firm's supervisory review for Alt Fund trades consists of a principal reviewing whether the customer's account is approved for mutual fund purchases generally. Over 18 months, representatives recommend $47 million in Alt Fund investments across 1,200 customer accounts, including 290 accounts belonging to investors over age 65 with conservative or moderate risk profiles. Several customers later complain that they did not understand the products could lose value through derivatives exposure even when the underlying market was stable.

QUESTION
--------
Did Apex Broker-Dealer satisfy its sales practice, communication, and supervisory obligations for the sale of alternative mutual funds?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates FINRA sales practice and supervisory obligations for alternative mutual funds.

Regulatory Notice 22-11 identifies precisely these deficiencies—firms that fail to provide adequate representative training, that use marketing materials omitting material risk factors like leverage and derivatives exposure, and that apply generic mutual fund supervisory frameworks to complex Alt Funds. The one-page summary describing Alt Funds as providing 'downside protection' without disclosing derivatives and leverage risks violates FINRA Rule 2210's fair and balanced communication requirements. The absence of mandatory training and the use of a generic mutual fund approval process for complex products with distinct risk profiles constitutes a failure to maintain a reasonably designed supervisory system under Rule 3110. The concentration of sales to 290 conservative senior accounts without heightened suitability review compounds the violation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rules 2210 (Communications with the Public), 2111 (Suitability), 3110 (Supervision); FINRA Regulatory Notice 22-11 (Alternative Mutual Funds)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm used misleading marketing materials omitting material risks, imposed no mandatory product training, and applied a generic mutual fund supervisory framework to complex alternative mutual funds—matching the exact deficiency patterns described in FINRA Regulatory Notice 22-11 enforcement actions.
