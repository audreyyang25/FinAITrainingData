Source Folder: unknown
Topic: esg_sustainable_investing
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
RIA advisor recommends Vanguard ESG U.S. Stock ETF (EUSA, 0.08% expense ratio) to 58-year-old client with $850,000 in taxable account and moderate risk tolerance. Client's stated investment objective is 'balanced growth with capital preservation.' Advisor does not disclose that ESG screening historically underweights energy, financials, and defense sectors, which have outperformed the broad market by 3.2% annualized over the prior 10 years. Client portfolio is rebalanced quarterly, generating 18% annual turnover. Advisor documents in writing only: 'Client approved ESG investing.' No discussion occurs of performance implications, sector concentration risk, or alternative diversified non-ESG funds with comparable fees. Six months later, client's ESG fund underperforms Russell 1000 by 2.8% while incurring 0.12% rebalancing costs annually.

QUESTION
--------
Did the advisor comply with the Reg BI care obligation and Investment Adviser Act fiduciary duty of care when recommending EUSA without disclosing material performance drag and sector concentration?

ANSWER
------
No. Under Reg BI 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i) (care obligation), the advisor must understand the product, its costs, and material risks, and must recommend based on the client's profile. The IA 1940 §206(1) fiduciary duty requires advisors to disclose all material facts that would affect the client's investment decision. The 3.2% historical underperformance of ESG vs. broad market during the relevant period is a material fact given the client's objective of 'balanced growth'—not ESG preference. The advisor's generic 'Client approved ESG' documentation is insufficient; approval requires informed consent to known trade-offs. The quarterly rebalancing at 18% turnover without disclosure of incremental costs compounds the breach. The advisor failed the care obligation prong by not analyzing whether ESG sector concentration was suitable for a moderate-risk, capital-preservation mandate.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i); IA Act §206(1) fiduciary duty of care; SEC Marketing Rule IA-5653 (substantiation of ESG claims)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Material non-disclosure of historical ESG underperformance vs. broad market without client's express preference for ESG values integration.
