ID: 576
Source Folder: 05_SEC_Exam_Findings
Topic: share_class_selection
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A dually-registered investment adviser and broker-dealer with approximately $120 million in assets under management systematically recommended Class A mutual fund shares with 5.75% front-end loads to new retail clients when lower-cost Class I institutional shares were available on the firm's platform for accounts over $25,000. The firm's compliance policies did not include any procedures for comparing share class costs, and the firm received 12b-1 trail commissions from the Class A shares. Over a two-year period, 340 retail clients collectively paid approximately $1.8 million in unnecessary front-end sales charges. The firm's Form ADV Part 2A disclosed that it 'may receive compensation from mutual fund companies' but did not specifically disclose the conflict created by share class selection or that lower-cost share classes were available.

QUESTION
--------
Did the investment adviser comply with its fiduciary duty under the Investment Advisers Act and SEC exam expectations regarding share class selection?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a fiduciary breach through conflicted share class selection and inadequate disclosure of material conflicts of interest.

The SEC Division of Examinations has repeatedly identified share class selection as a priority area, noting that advisers breach their fiduciary duty when they recommend higher-cost share classes that generate additional compensation without adequate disclosure. The firm failed to adopt compliance procedures to evaluate available share classes and provided only generic disclosure about potential compensation from fund companies, which is insufficient to inform clients of the specific conflict. An adviser registered with the SEC (managing over $100 million in AUM) has a heightened obligation to act in clients' best interests and make full and fair disclosure of all material conflicts.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §§ 206(1) and 206(2); SEC OCIE Risk Alert on Mutual Fund Share Class Selection (April 2018); SEC Division of Examinations Exam Priorities (FY 2025-2026)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm systematically selected higher-cost share classes that generated additional compensation for the firm when lower-cost alternatives were available on its own platform, without adequate disclosure or a reasonable basis to believe the recommendation was in clients' best interest.
