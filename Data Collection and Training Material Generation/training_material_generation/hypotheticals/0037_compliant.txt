Topic: reg_bi_disclosure

Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer firm recommends a 42-year-old client with $750,000 in investable assets a discretionary managed account with a 0.75% annual advisory fee. Prior to the recommendation, the firm provides a Form CRS that discloses (1) the firm's standard services including portfolio management, (2) the exact fee structure (0.75% annually, tiered to 0.60% on assets above $1 million), (3) that the firm receives compensation from fund sponsors whose funds may be recommended to the client, and (4) the firm's practice of occasionally recommending proprietary mutual funds where the advisor earns higher distribution fees. The firm also provides a detailed written investment policy statement and quarterly account statements showing fees deducted.

QUESTION
--------
Does this disclosure satisfy Reg BI's transparency requirements regarding fees and conflicts?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this is compliant.

The broker-dealer has satisfied Reg BI's disclosure obligations by providing a Form CRS that clearly states the compensation structure (specific percentages), discloses material conflicts of interest (fund sponsor payments and higher distribution fees on proprietary products), and uses plain language accessible to a retail customer. The firm goes beyond the minimum by providing an investment policy statement and regular account statements showing actual fees deducted. The disclosure of tiered pricing is material because it affects the client's incentive to consolidate assets. The early and pre-recommendation disclosure allows the client to understand the economic relationship before committing to the recommendation. This approach aligns with the SEC's guidance that Reg BI requires affirmative, concise disclosure of material facts.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Regulation Best Interest (17 CFR 240.15l-1), Form CRS, Item 2 (relationship summary with clear fee and conflict disclosures), and SEC Reg BI Frequently Asked Questions (2020).

KEY FACTOR
----------
Affirmative, pre-recommendation disclosure of specific fee percentages, tiered pricing, material conflicts (fund sponsor payments and proprietary product incentives), and ongoing account statement documentation of actual fees deducted.
