FACTS
-----
A 35-year-old software developer with $750,000 in investable assets, annual income of $220,000, and expressed aggressive risk tolerance approached a broker with the stated goal of building wealth over 25+ years until retirement. The developer had previous experience investing in individual stocks and understood volatility. The broker recommended a portfolio consisting of 60% diversified U.S. equity mutual funds, 25% international equity ETFs, and 15% emerging-market index funds, with a total expense ratio of 0.35%. The broker documented the customer's age, income, time horizon, investment experience, and aggressive risk tolerance in the suitability analysis. The customer was advised that the recommendation was designed for long-term growth and would experience volatility. No trading occurred for at least 12 months after the initial recommendation.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy FINRA Suitability Rule 2111?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation complies with FINRA Suitability Rule 2111.

This recommendation satisfies all three prongs of Rule 2111. First, the broker has a reasonable basis: a 25+ year equity-focused portfolio (100% equities across diversified asset classes) with low expense ratios (0.35%) is a well-established strategy suitable for long-term wealth accumulation. Second, the recommendation is customer-specific: the broker documented the customer's age (35), income ($220,000), time horizon (25+ years), investment experience (prior individual stock investing), and risk tolerance (aggressive). All these factors align with an equity-forward allocation. The customer's youth, high income, and stated wealth-building objective support this recommendation. Third, there is no quantitative suitability issue: the recommendation was made once with no rapid trading, consistent with a stated long-term objective. The broker also appropriately set expectations about volatility, demonstrating transparency.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a)(1)-(3) requires: (1) reasonable basis to believe the recommendation is suitable; (2) the recommendation is suitable to the specific customer's investment profile; and (3) quantitative suitability (no excessive trading).

KEY FACTOR
----------
The alignment between the customer's long-term time horizon (25+ years), aggressive risk tolerance, prior investment experience, and an all-equity diversified portfolio with low fees, combined with the absence of excessive trading, satisfies all three prongs of Rule 2111.
