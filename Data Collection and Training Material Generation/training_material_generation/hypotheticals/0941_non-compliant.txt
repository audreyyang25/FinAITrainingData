ID: 941
Source Folder: 21_FINRA_OHO_Decisions
Topic: undisclosed_outside_business_activity
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Registered representative Thomas Hale operated a private real estate syndication business through an LLC he formed three years prior, through which he solicited six of his brokerage clients to invest a combined $1.1 million in private placement interests. Hale disclosed the LLC to his firm on his initial Form U4 as a passive investment holding company, but never updated the disclosure when the entity began actively soliciting investors. His firm's written supervisory procedures required prompt written notification and prior written approval for any outside business activity involving securities-related transactions. The syndication subsequently failed, and investors recovered less than 15 cents on the dollar.

QUESTION
--------
Did Hale's operation and non-disclosure of his evolving outside business activity comply with applicable FINRA conduct standards?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of FINRA Rules 3270 (outside business activities) and 2010 (standards of commercial honor).

Rule 3270 requires registered persons to provide prompt written notice to their member firm before engaging in any outside business activity, and the character of Hale's entity materially changed from passive holding to active securities solicitation, triggering a new and independent disclosure obligation. The failure to update his Form U4 to reflect this material change also constitutes a recordkeeping violation under Rule 4511 and an independent violation of Rule 2010, as maintaining an inaccurate Form U4 is inconsistent with just and equitable principles of trade. The solicitation of existing brokerage clients further implicates selling away concerns under FINRA Rule 3280.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3270; FINRA Rule 2010; FINRA Rule 4511 (books and records for Form U4 accuracy)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The critical failure was Hale's non-disclosure of the entity's transformation from passive to active securities solicitation, which independently triggered Rule 3270's prior written notice and approval requirements.
