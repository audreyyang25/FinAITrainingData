Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered representative at a broker-dealer recommends that Martha Nguyen, a 72-year-old retired schoolteacher with $180,000 in retirement savings and a conservative risk profile, invest $140,000 in a non-traded municipal revenue bond fund with a 10-year lock-up and a 5.5% upfront commission. The representative does not document any consideration of reasonably available alternatives, such as shorter-duration investment-grade municipal bond funds or Treasury securities. The representative's Form CRS was delivered at account opening two years ago and has not been updated or re-delivered. The recommendation is made solely at the point of sale with no written rationale in the file.

QUESTION
--------
Did the registered representative satisfy the Care Obligation under Regulation Best Interest when recommending the illiquid municipal bond fund to this retail customer?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the Care Obligation under Regulation Best Interest.

Regulation BI's Care Obligation requires a broker-dealer to exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill in making a recommendation, including understanding the reasonably available alternatives and having a reasonable basis to believe the recommendation is in the retail customer's best interest. Concentrating 78% of a conservative, elderly retiree's assets in an illiquid, long-lock-up product with a high upfront commission—without documenting any analysis of alternatives—fails the Care Obligation. The MSRB's parallel alignment efforts under Rule G-19 further underscore that municipal securities recommendations must meet best interest standards.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Regulation Best Interest Care Obligation

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative failed to consider or document reasonably available alternatives and recommended an illiquid, high-commission product unsuitable for the client's age, liquidity needs, and risk tolerance.
