ID: 786
Source Folder: 15_FINRA_Regulatory_Notices
Topic: complex_products
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A mid-sized broker-dealer's registered representatives recommended alternative mutual funds (liquid alts) to approximately 340 retail customers over an 18-month period. The firm had no written supervisory procedures specific to Alt Funds, and the existing general mutual fund procedures did not address the unique risk characteristics of these products, such as the use of leverage, derivatives, and short-selling strategies. Representatives were not required to complete any product-specific training before recommending Alt Funds. One representative placed 35% of a 72-year-old retiree's $420,000 IRA into a single managed futures Alt Fund with a 2.15% expense ratio and significant volatility, without documenting why this concentration was suitable for the client's moderate risk tolerance and income-oriented objectives.

QUESTION
--------
Did the firm meet its supervisory obligations under FINRA rules when it permitted representatives to recommend alternative mutual funds without product-specific supervisory procedures, training, or documented suitability analyses?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct constitutes a supervisory violation and a suitability violation.

FINRA Regulatory Notice 22-11 specifically reminds firms that they must establish and maintain a reasonably designed supervisory system for recommendations of alternative mutual funds, noting that FINRA has taken enforcement action against firms failing to do so. The firm's lack of Alt Fund-specific WSPs, absence of required product training, and failure to document a reasonable basis for the concentrated recommendation in a senior investor's retirement account all constitute the exact deficiencies identified in RN 22-11 examinations and enforcement actions.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision) and FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability); FINRA Regulatory Notice 22-11 (Alt Funds Sales Practice Obligations)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm had no supervisory procedures specifically designed for alternative mutual funds despite actively recommending them, which is the precise deficiency FINRA identified in RN 22-11 enforcement actions.
