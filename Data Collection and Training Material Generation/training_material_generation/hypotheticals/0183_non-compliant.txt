ID: 183
Source Folder: unknown
Topic: concentration
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A FINRA-registered broker-dealer advisor recommends that a 58-year-old client with $350,000 in liquid net worth and moderate risk tolerance allocate $280,000 (80%) of her IRA to a single large-cap technology stock (Apple, trading volume >50M shares/day). The client has no prior experience with individual stock picking and expressed desire for 'diversified growth.' The advisor documented no suitability analysis and stated verbally: 'Tech stocks always outperform over time.'

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy FINRA Suitability Rule 2111 and SEC Regulation Best Interest?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates both standards.

The 80% concentration in a single security fails the diversification requirement implicit in both FINRA 2111 and Reg BI, particularly for a customer of moderate risk tolerance nearing retirement (age 58) who explicitly stated desire for diversified growth. The absence of any documented suitability analysis violates FINRA Rule 2111(a)(1)(A), which requires suitability analysis before recommending purchases. The advisor's blanket statement that 'tech stocks always outperform' is unsupported opinion that ignores sector volatility and client-specific risk tolerance. Under Reg BI's obligations, the firm failed to provide a recommended strategy that served the customer's interest, considering alternatives and compensation conflicts. This concentration pattern appears consistently in FINRA disciplinary cases (e.g., FINRA AWC cases involving single-stock concentration without documented justification).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability rule) and SEC Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI), 17 CFR 240.15l-1. Both require that recommendations be suitable for the customer's investment profile (age, risk tolerance, financial situation, needs) and that the firm act in the customer's best interest.

KEY FACTOR
----------
80% concentration in a single stock without documented suitability analysis, combined with client's explicit diversification preference and moderate risk tolerance, creates clear breach of both FINRA 2111 and Reg BI.
