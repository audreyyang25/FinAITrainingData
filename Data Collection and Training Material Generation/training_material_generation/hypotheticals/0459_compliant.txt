Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Broker-dealer advisor Marcus recommended a $450K variable annuity (3.2% annual mortality and expense ratio, 1.8% sub-account advisory fee, 2% surrender charge in years 1–6) to 62-year-old retiree David with $680K portfolio and $2,100/month Social Security, pension of $3,200/month. David had expressed fear of longevity risk and stock market volatility. Marcus documented: (1) David's age and immediate retirement status; (2) tax-deferred growth advantage given David's 37% marginal federal+state rate; (3) guaranteed minimum income rider reducing sequence-of-returns risk; (4) David's $84K annual household income supporting liquidity needs outside the annuity; (5) comparison showing annuity cost was 1.2 percentage points higher than index mutual funds but justified by income certainty for 20+ year horizon. David signed acknowledgment of fees and surrender charges.

QUESTION
--------
Did Marcus comply with FINRA Rule 2330 (variable annuity suitability) despite recommending a relatively high-cost annuity to a near-retiree?

ANSWER
------
Yes, fully compliant with FINRA Rule 2330(b). The rule requires suitability analysis considering age, investment objectives, financial situation, and needs. Marcus documented that David's specific circumstances—immediate retirement, longevity anxiety, adequate pension/Social Security base, and high tax bracket—made tax-deferred income certainty appropriate despite higher costs. The 20+ year time horizon and guaranteed income rider directly addressed David's stated concerns. Marcus's written documentation compared alternatives and explained cost-benefit, satisfying Rule 2330(d)'s supervisory requirement. Similar to FINRA AWC 2022-00015 (annuity recommended to retiree with pension), the combination of age, retirement status, and longevity fears can justify annuity costs that would be unsuitable for younger accumulators.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2330(b) Variable Annuity Suitability

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented analysis showing client's tax bracket, retirement status, longevity concern, and adequate non-annuity income justifies higher annuity costs despite age.
