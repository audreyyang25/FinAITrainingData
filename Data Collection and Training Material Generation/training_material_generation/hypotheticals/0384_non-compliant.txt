Source Folder: unknown
Topic: dol_rollover_analysis
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
David, age 66, separated from service at his prior employer and holds a 403(b) annuity contract with Great Life Insurance valued at $542,000. The contract carries a 7% surrender charge if withdrawn before age 70 (declining 1% per year). His new adviser, Kevin, recommends rolling the 403(b) to a traditional IRA 'to achieve greater liquidity and investment control.' Kevin provides a one-page summary stating: 'IRA offers more investment options and lower fees.' However, Kevin did NOT disclose that: (1) the rollover to IRA would be a taxable event triggering immediate 7% surrender charge ($37,940); (2) Kevin's IRA would charge 1.10% AUM fee vs. the 403(b)'s embedded cost of 0.95% annually; (3) Kevin receives a $3,600 rollover bonus from his firm for 403(b) conversions exceeding $500,000. Kevin's contract with David states 'reasonable fees' without specifying the 1.10% AUM rate or the rollover bonus. David, age 66, does not need liquidity and had stated his goal as 'retire and maintain stability.'

QUESTION
--------
Did Kevin's 403(b) rollover recommendation to David comply with PTE 2020-02?

ANSWER
------
No, on multiple grounds. PTE 2020-02 requires advisers to disclose material compensation and avoid misleading clients. Kevin violated this by: (1) failing to disclose the 7% surrender charge ($37,940 net cost) and the 0.15% annual fee increase (0.95% to 1.10%); (2) not disclosing his $3,600 rollover bonus, creating a conflict; (3) failing to document why the rollover served David's stated objective (stability, not liquidity) when David did not request liquidity and was age 66 (near RMD phase). The recommendation appears driven by Kevin's compensation rather than David's best interest. The surrender charge alone eliminates the fee savings argument. This pattern (unrevealed surrender charges, undisclosed bonuses) mirrors DOL enforcement actions against 403(b) rollover schemes.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
PTE 2020-02 Best-Interest Standard; Disclosure Requirements; DOL ERISA §408(b)(14)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Undisclosed surrender charge (7%), undisclosed rollover bonus ($3,600), and undisclosed fee increase (0.15% annually) combined with recommendation that does not address client's stated stability objective.
