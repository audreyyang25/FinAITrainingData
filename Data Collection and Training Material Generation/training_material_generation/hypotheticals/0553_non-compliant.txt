Topic: reg_bi_care
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Edward Shelton, a former General Securities Representative registered through Laidlaw & Associates from 2013 to 2022, recommended that three elderly retail customers—ages 72, 78, and 81—liquidate diversified portfolios of blue-chip equities and investment-grade bonds to concentrate their assets into two illiquid, high-commission private placement offerings in real estate debt funds. The customers had moderate risk tolerances, income-focused investment objectives, and annual incomes between $45,000 and $62,000. The private placements had 7-year lockup periods, carried total sales charges of 10%, and paid Shelton's firm a 7% selling concession. Shelton did not document any consideration of reasonably available alternatives such as publicly traded REITs or diversified bond funds, and the customers' account records did not reflect any basis for why the illiquid, high-cost products were in their best interest given their ages and liquidity needs. Shelton was permitted to resign after the firm discovered the pattern.

QUESTION
--------
Did Shelton satisfy the Care Obligation under Reg BI when recommending illiquid, high-commission private placements to elderly, income-focused retail customers without documenting consideration of reasonably available alternatives?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates the Reg BI Care Obligation.

The Care Obligation requires that a broker-dealer exercise reasonable diligence, care, and skill to understand the potential risks, rewards, and costs of a recommendation, consider reasonably available alternatives, and ensure the recommendation is in the retail customer's best interest without placing the financial interest of the broker-dealer ahead of the customer. Recommending illiquid private placements with 7-year lockups and 10% total sales charges to elderly customers in their 70s and 80s with moderate risk tolerance and income-focused objectives—without any documented consideration of lower-cost, liquid alternatives—fails each prong of the Care Obligation. The pattern echoes the Edward Scott Short AWC (No. 2020065683302), involving a long-tenured representative who was permitted to resign following unsuitable recommendations to retail customers.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative failed to consider or document any reasonably available alternatives and recommended illiquid, high-cost products fundamentally mismatched with the elderly customers' liquidity needs, risk tolerance, and investment objectives.
