Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 68-year-old retired widow, Mariko Hayashi, with $620,000 in investable assets and a conservative risk tolerance, filed a FINRA arbitration claim against her broker, Craig Millburn, and his member firm, alleging unsuitable recommendations, breach of fiduciary duty, and common law fraud. Millburn concentrated 78% of Hayashi's portfolio in leveraged inverse ETFs and speculative biotech equities, generating an annualized turnover rate of 11.2 and a cost-to-equity ratio of 34%. Hayashi, a Japanese-American with limited English proficiency, testified she told Millburn she needed income to cover $3,200 in monthly living expenses and wanted to preserve capital. Millburn's firm had received two prior customer complaints about his trading activity in elderly clients' accounts, both of which were settled confidentially for $45,000 and $62,000, but the firm took no enhanced supervisory action. Hayashi's account declined from $620,000 to $187,000 over 22 months.

QUESTION
--------
Is the broker and firm likely to face arbitration liability for churning and failure to supervise based on these facts?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant — it constitutes churning in violation of FINRA Rule 2111 and SEC Rule 10b-5, and the firm failed to supervise under FINRA Rule 3110.

An annualized turnover rate of 11.2 and a cost-to-equity ratio of 34% far exceed the thresholds (turnover of 6 and cost-to-equity of 20%) that FINRA arbitration panels and courts have consistently found presumptively indicative of churning. Concentrating a conservative, income-dependent retiree's portfolio in leveraged inverse ETFs and speculative equities is quantitatively and qualitatively unsuitable under Rule 2111's customer-specific obligation. The firm's failure to implement heightened supervision after two prior settled complaints about the same broker's trading in elderly accounts demonstrates a supervisory system failure under Rule 3110. The pattern here reflects the types of customer-vs-member claims seen in FINRA arbitration awards where panels assess both compensatory damages and, in egregious cases involving elderly or vulnerable investors, potential punitive damages.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability), FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision), SEC Rule 10b-5 (Fraud), and common law breach of fiduciary duty

KEY FACTOR
----------
The combination of an 11.2 turnover rate, 34% cost-to-equity ratio, and concentration in products wholly inconsistent with a conservative retiree's stated objectives creates overwhelming quantitative and qualitative evidence of churning.
