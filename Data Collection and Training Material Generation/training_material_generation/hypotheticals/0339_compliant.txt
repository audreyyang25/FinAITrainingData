FACTS
-----
Registered Representative Laura M. (age 41, at GentrySec for 6 years) recommended to Client D (age 58, $680,000 portfolio, aggressive risk tolerance, 15+ year horizon, high income, $245,000 annual W-2 salary) a switch from a Vanguard Balanced Index Fund A-share position (held 4 years, cost basis $156,000, current value $189,000, front-end load paid in year 2020 = 4.5%) to the Dimensional Research Small-Cap Equity Fund A-share (new front-end load 5.5%, cost of switch = $10,395). Laura's suitability memo documented: (1) Client's expressed goal to increase equity exposure to capture higher long-term growth in retirement decade; (2) 4-year holding period demonstrated comfort with load structure; (3) quantified the $1,400 unrealized gain as non-triggering for tax consequences in non-retirement account; (4) explained the Small-Cap fund's 1.2% annual expense ratio vs. Balanced Fund's 0.18%, noting this trade-off for equity risk premium; (5) documented the client's verbal approval and risk tolerance confirmation. The switch occurred in June 2023. The firm's compliance department reviewed the file within 30 days and found no deficiency.

QUESTION
--------
Did Laura M.'s recommendation to switch from A-share Balanced Fund to A-share Small-Cap Fund comply with FINRA Rule 2111 and Rule 2010?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Laura's recommendation complies with FINRA Rule 2111 and Rule 2010. The switch is suitable because: (1) it aligns with the client's documented goal to increase equity exposure in a 15+ year retirement window; (2) the 4-year holding period of the prior A-share fund demonstrates the client's acceptance of front-end loads and long-term investment horizon; (3) the cost differential (5.5% new load) is material but justified by the strategic shift in asset allocation, not driven by advisor compensation; (4) Laura explicitly quantified the M&E trade-off (higher expense ratio 1.2% vs. 0.18%) and explained the economic rationale; (5) the suitability memo is thorough and contemporaneous; (6) no tax consequences (non-retirement account, minimal gain). FINRA enforcement distinguishes between unsuitable frequent trading for compensation and strategic reallocation with documented client rationale. This scenario mirrors FINRA approval patterns where a switch with loads is deemed suitable when: (a) the client initiates or clearly endorses the strategy, (b) the new investment materially differs in objective/risk profile, and (c) documentation is complete.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) (Suitability Obligation), FINRA Rule 2010 (Standards of Commercial Honor), FINRA Rule 3110(b)(1) (Supervisory Procedures)

KEY FACTOR
----------
A mutual fund switch with front-end loads is compliant when it reflects a documented strategic shift in asset allocation aligned to client goals, the client has demonstrated comfort with load structures, and the advisor provides transparent cost-benefit analysis.
