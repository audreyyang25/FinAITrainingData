Source Folder: unknown
Topic: ia_cherry_picking
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Registered investment adviser Sterling Capital Management executed a block purchase of 5,000 shares of Acme Corp at $48/share on March 15, 2023 for a $240,000 position. The firm allocated the shares as follows: 2,500 shares to its own proprietary trading account (cost basis $48), 1,200 shares to client Portfolio A (cost basis $48), and 1,300 shares to client Portfolio B (cost basis $49.50). By end of day, Acme rose to $51. The adviser's account realized an $15,000 gain; Portfolio A's allocation cost the client an effective price of $51.20 per share. The firm had no written allocation policy, and the allocation decision was made informally by the portfolio manager. The adviser had never disclosed this practice to any client.

QUESTION
--------
Did Sterling Capital comply with Investment Advisers Act Section 206(2) and SEC Rule 17j-1(a) regarding fair allocation and disclosure of cross-trades and principal transactions?

ANSWER
------
No. Sterling Capital violated Section 206(2) and Rule 17j-1(a). The block trade executed at a single price ($48) should have been allocated consistently across all accounts pro rata or via a pre-established, written policy. Instead, the adviser allocated the lowest-cost shares to its own account and filled client accounts at worse effective prices. Rule 17j-1(a) requires advisers engaged in principal transactions or cross-trades to have a written policy and disclose it to clients before any such transaction. The absence of any written policy, combined with self-preferential allocation post-execution without client consent, is a classic cherry-picking violation. The fact that the allocation was informal and not disclosed compounds the breach.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act Section 206(2) (prohibits fraudulent, deceptive conduct); SEC Rule 17j-1(a) (written policy for principal/cross-transactions); Investment Advisers Act Section 206(1) (fiduciary duty of loyalty)

KEY FACTOR
----------
No written allocation policy and post-hoc favoring of adviser's own account with best-execution shares violates Section 206 and Rule 17j-1.
