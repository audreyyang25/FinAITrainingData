ID: 632
Source Folder: 07_Practitioner_Commentary
Topic: training_supervision
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A broker-dealer firm files its Form CRS with the SEC and begins delivering it to new retail customers in July 2020. However, the Form CRS omits any mention of the firm's reportable disciplinary history, including a 2018 FINRA AWC in which the firm paid a $150,000 fine for supervisory failures related to variable annuity switches. The form also fails to include the required link to the SEC's investor education page at investor.gov/crs. A 72-year-old retired retail customer opens an account and receives this deficient Form CRS before investing $340,000 in a proprietary mutual fund recommended by the firm's associated person. The firm's compliance department had delegated Form CRS drafting to a junior marketing associate with no compliance training.

QUESTION
--------
Did the firm satisfy its Form CRS obligations by delivering a relationship summary that omitted its disciplinary history and the investor.gov/crs link?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Form CRS content requirements and reflects inadequate supervisory procedures over Form CRS preparation.

Form CRS requires registered firms to disclose 'whether the firm and its financial professionals currently have reportable legal or disciplinary history' and must include 'a link to the SEC's investor education page at investor.gov/crs.' The firm's omission of both required elements renders the Form CRS materially deficient, depriving the retail investor of critical information needed to evaluate the firm. Additionally, delegating Form CRS preparation to an untrained junior marketing associate without compliance review reflects a failure of the firm's supervisory system to ensure regulatory filings meet mandatory content requirements.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Form CRS (17 CFR 249.2) – General Requirements; FINRA Rule 3110 – Supervision

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm's Form CRS omitted two mandatory disclosure elements — reportable disciplinary history and the investor.gov/crs link — due to the absence of compliance oversight over the document's preparation.
