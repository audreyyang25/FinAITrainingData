Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Martin Greystone is the sole portfolio manager at Greystone Wealth Management, a registered investment adviser. Greystone personally acquires a 12% equity stake in a publicly traded company, NovaTech Inc. (market cap $180 million), making him a beneficial owner of more than 10% of NovaTech's outstanding shares. Over the following four months, Greystone sells his entire NovaTech position at a substantial profit of $2.3 million. During this same period, Greystone allocates NovaTech shares to seven client accounts without disclosing his personal ownership stake or his active trading in the same security. None of the clients are informed that their adviser is simultaneously unwinding a large personal position in the same stock.

QUESTION
--------
Did Greystone violate his fiduciary obligations and securities law by trading NovaTech shares in client accounts while simultaneously liquidating his own 10%-plus position without disclosure?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of the Investment Advisers Act fiduciary duty and Section 16(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934.

As a 10% beneficial owner under Section 16(b) of the Exchange Act, Greystone's purchase and sale of NovaTech shares within a six-month window subjects him to short-swing profit disgorgement requirements, as the Second Circuit analyzed in Packer v. Raging Capital Management. Additionally, under Advisers Act §§ 206(1) and 206(2), Greystone has a fiduciary duty to disclose material conflicts of interest to clients, including his personal ownership of and trading in the same securities he allocates to client accounts. The failure to disclose his 12% stake and concurrent liquidation constitutes a material omission and a breach of the duty of loyalty.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act §§ 206(1), 206(2); Securities Exchange Act § 16(b); See Packer v. Raging Capital Management, LLC, No. 23-367-cv (2d Cir. 2024)

KEY FACTOR
----------
An adviser who is a 10% beneficial owner of a security must both comply with Section 16(b) short-swing profit rules and disclose the material conflict when trading the same security in client accounts.
