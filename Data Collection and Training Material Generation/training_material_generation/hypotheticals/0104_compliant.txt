Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 52-year-old executive with $2 million in assets, $300,000 annual income, and a stated 'high risk tolerance' requested concentrated exposure to emerging markets and small-cap growth. The broker documented that the client held significant real estate assets and stable pension income, was an experienced investor with prior experience in volatile strategies, and had a 15-year time horizon. The broker recommended a 30% allocation to emerging markets (diversified across 3 country and 2 sector-focused ETFs), 20% to small-cap growth funds, and 50% to core positions. The recommendation included written analysis of volatility (historical annual standard deviation 18-25%), sequence-of-returns risk, and hedging alternatives the client declined. The broker reviewed concentration risk annually.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy FINRA Rule 2111 suitability?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this is compliant.

The broker's recommendation satisfies Rule 2111 despite the elevated risk exposure. The reasonable-basis prong is met: the broker documented why emerging markets and small-cap growth are appropriate diversification tools for a high-risk-tolerance investor, understood the historical volatility (18-25% annual), and considered the client's ability to weather drawdowns. The customer-specific prong is clearly satisfied: the 52-year-old client has high risk tolerance, $2 million in assets providing cushion, $300,000 stable income, real estate diversification, pension income, a 15-year time horizon, and prior experience with volatile strategies. The 30% + 20% = 50% allocation to growth/emerging markets is quantitatively reasonable for this risk profile and represents appropriate diversification (not concentration in a single security). The broker's written documentation of volatility ranges, sequence-of-returns analysis, and discussion of hedging alternatives (which the client declined) demonstrates thorough customer-specific suitability analysis. Annual concentration review shows ongoing compliance monitoring.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a)(1) requires reasonable basis considering the nature and risk of the recommended security, and (a)(2) requires customer-specific consideration of age, financial situation, needs, and investment experience.

KEY FACTOR
----------
An experienced high-risk-tolerance investor with 15-year time horizon, diversified assets, stable income, and prior growth-stock experience can sustain a 50% allocation to emerging markets and small-cap growth; detailed documentation of volatility analysis and alternatives considered demonstrates Rule 2111 compliance.
