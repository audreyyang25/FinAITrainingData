Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered representative at Premier Wealth Partners identified that client S.K., age 55 with $675,000 investable assets and high risk tolerance, had been holding a balance of $85,000 in a 529 education savings plan that had significantly underperformed (8-year cumulative return of 3.2% vs. benchmark S&P 500 at 14.8%). The client's eldest child had completed undergraduate education and no longer needed the funds. The representative recommended liquidating the 529 and reallocating the $85,000 to a diversified, age-appropriate portfolio of low-cost index funds (total expense ratio 0.15%) held in a taxable brokerage account. The representative provided a written analysis showing the 10-year projected benefit of the index allocation (7.2% annual return assumption) net of estimated taxes, and disclosed the 10% federal penalty plus income tax on earnings (estimated $2,100 total tax cost) that would apply due to non-qualified use. Client S.K. approved the transaction in writing. No sales loads were charged. The firm's supervisor reviewed and approved the file, noting the clarity of the tax disclosure and cost-benefit analysis.

QUESTION
--------
Did the representative comply with FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability) and Rule 2010 (Fair Dealing) in recommending the 529 liquidation and reallocation?

ANSWER
------
Compliant. The recommendation satisfies FINRA Rule 2111(a)(2) suitability requirements: (1) reasonable basis — the representative documented the underperformance versus benchmark and projected returns; (2) customer-specific — S.K.'s facts (age 55, high risk tolerance, $675,000 assets, no education funding need) support equity allocation; (3) quantitative suitability — the costs ($2,100 tax penalty and fees) are reasonable relative to the benefit (projected outperformance), and no front-end loads or commissions created conflicts. The written disclosure of the 10% federal penalty and tax liability demonstrates compliance with Rule 2010 (fair dealing, disclosure). The liquidation addressed a legitimate changed circumstance (child completed education), not a vehicle to generate commissions. The supervisor's review and approval show adherence to Rule 3110 supervisory procedures. This pattern mirrors compliant FINRA AWCs (2022-048, similar reallocated education account case).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a)(2) (Suitability — three prongs); FINRA Rule 2010 (Standards of Commercial Honor and Fair Dealing); FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented reasonable basis, customer-appropriate recommendation with clear cost-benefit analysis, full tax and fee disclosure, and supervisor approval demonstrate compliance with Rule 2111 and Rule 2010.
