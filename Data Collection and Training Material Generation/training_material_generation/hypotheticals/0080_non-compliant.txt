Topic: ia_duty_of_care
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 72-year-old widow with $800,000 in assets and stated need for $30,000 annual income was initially placed in a balanced portfolio (50/50 stocks/bonds) four years ago. The client's circumstances have not materially changed. The adviser continues to recommend the same 50/50 allocation at each review without reassessing whether this allocation continues to generate the required $30,000 income or remains appropriate given the client's increasing age and unchanged income need. Bond allocations have declined in yield; the portfolio now generates only $18,000 in annual income, requiring additional portfolio liquidation. The adviser made no recommendation to rebalance or adjust.

QUESTION
--------
Does the adviser's conduct satisfy the duty of care?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the duty of care.

Although the initial 50/50 recommendation may have been suitable, the adviser's failure to reassess and adjust in light of changed market conditions (declining bond yields reducing income generation) and the client's ongoing, documented income need constitutes a breach of the duty of care. The adviser's passive approach—making no recommendation despite the portfolio failing to meet the client's stated objective of $30,000 annual income—violates the requirement to review recommendations periodically and adjust when circumstances warrant. The client is now forced to liquidate principal to cover shortfalls, which increases sequence-of-returns risk and is unsuitable for a 72-year-old. The duty of care requires active review of whether recommendations continue to achieve stated objectives, not static recommendations regardless of material changes.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206(2); duty of care includes obligation to periodically review recommendations and adjust when client circumstances or market conditions make original recommendations no longer suitable; see SEC enforcement precedent (In re Merrill Lynch Investment Managers).

KEY FACTOR
----------
Failure to reassess suitability or recommend adjustment when the original portfolio allocation no longer achieves the client's stated income objective of $30,000 annually, despite material deterioration in bond yields.
