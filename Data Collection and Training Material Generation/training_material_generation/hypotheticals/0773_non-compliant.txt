Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Digital Asset Advisors, Inc., a state-registered investment adviser in Michigan, launches a pooled investment vehicle called the 'DeFi Growth Fund' that invests client assets in various digital asset protocols marketed as generating 18-22% annual yields. The firm raises $4.7 million from 52 retail investors, many of whom are retirees. The offering documents describe the tokens as 'utility tokens' that are not securities, despite the fact that investors are pooling money with an expectation of profit derived solely from the efforts of the fund manager. The firm does not register the offering under state securities laws, does not file Form D with the state, and relies on the assertion that the underlying tokens are not investment contracts. During a coordinated NASAA state examination—the first time the firm has been examined by the state—examiners discover the unregistered offering and that the fund's actual returns have been approximately 3%, while marketing materials continue to promise 18-22%.

QUESTION
--------
Does the firm's conduct comply with state securities laws regarding the registration and marketing of the pooled investment vehicle?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct constitutes unregistered securities fraud, including offering unregistered securities in violation of state registration requirements and making materially misleading statements about expected returns.

Under the Howey test, which NASAA has urged Congress and courts to protect as the controlling investment contract analysis, the pooled fund constitutes a security because investors pool money with an expectation of profit derived from the managerial efforts of the adviser. NASAA's 2025 letter to Congress specifically warns against rulemaking that would narrow investment contract law and undermine state enforcement against exactly this type of scheme. The firm's failure to register the offering under state law, combined with materially misleading marketing materials overstating returns by 500-700%, constitutes securities fraud actionable by state regulators. The coordinated NASAA examination program, which conducted over 1,086 examinations in 2025 including 365 first-time adviser exams, is precisely designed to detect these violations.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
State Securities Registration Requirements (Uniform Securities Act §§ 301, 501); NASAA Position on Investment Contract Analysis; NASAA Support Anti-Fraud Enforcement (SAFE) Act Principles

KEY FACTOR
----------
Offering an unregistered pooled investment vehicle to retail investors while misrepresenting it as not involving securities and materially overstating investment returns.
