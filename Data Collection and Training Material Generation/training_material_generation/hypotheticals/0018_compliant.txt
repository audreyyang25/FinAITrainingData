FACTS
-----
A 62-year-old customer with $2 million in assets, sophisticated investment background (former finance professional), stated aggressive risk tolerance, and 30-year time horizon (planning to age 92) meets with an independent fee-only advisor. The customer requests exposure to emerging markets and wants to understand active versus passive approaches. The advisor recommends a $300,000 position (15% of portfolio) in an actively managed emerging markets mutual fund with 1.25% expense ratio and 3-year track record of 4.2% outperformance after fees versus the iShares MSCI EM ETF (0.70% expense ratio). The advisor provides detailed written analysis showing: (1) historical data on emerging markets active manager persistence; (2) the specific fund's philosophy (value-focused, concentrated in 40 holdings); (3) cost-benefit analysis showing that 4.2% annual outperformance justifies the 0.55% cost differential; (4) comparison to three alternative emerging markets options (broad EM ETF, diversified EM fund, individual EM stocks); (5) documentation that the customer acknowledged understanding the risks of a concentrated, actively managed position; and (6) confirmation that the advisor's compensation is unaffected by the choice between active and passive EM exposure.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the Reg BI care obligation despite the higher cost of active management?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation is compliant with the Reg BI care obligation.

This recommendation satisfies Reg BI's care obligation despite recommending a higher-cost product because the advisor conducted a thorough, documented cost-benefit analysis supported by specific facts. The advisor did not simply recommend active management based on risk tolerance; instead, the advisor: (1) documented explicit performance data (4.2% outperformance) that, if reliable, justifies the 0.55% cost difference for this sophisticated customer; (2) provided comparative analysis of multiple EM alternatives, demonstrating consideration of cheaper options; (3) matched the recommended fund's investment strategy (value, concentrated) to the customer's expressed interest in a specific approach; (4) confirmed that the sophisticated customer understood the risks and costs; and (5) eliminated compensation bias (advisor paid same fee regardless of active/passive choice). Reg BI does not require the cheapest product; it requires that costs be considered in light of risks, rewards, and alternatives. Here, the advisor's documented outperformance analysis and transparency about costs affirmatively demonstrate that consideration occurred. For a sophisticated customer with 30-year horizon and high risk tolerance, a higher-cost fund with documented outperformance can be in the customer's best interest.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Regulation Best Interest, Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i): broker-dealer or investment adviser must recommend securities in the customer's best interest, considering costs, risks, rewards, and alternative investments; Reg BI does not prohibit higher-cost products if they are in the customer's best interest given facts and circumstances.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Documented cost-benefit analysis with specific performance data (4.2% outperformance) supporting higher-cost recommendation, combined with comparative analysis of alternatives and elimination of compensation conflicts, affirmatively demonstrates care in considering costs relative to rewards.
