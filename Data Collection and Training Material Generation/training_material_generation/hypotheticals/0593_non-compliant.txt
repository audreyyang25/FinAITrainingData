Source Folder: 06_DOL_Fiduciary
Topic: erisa_plan_fiduciary
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Sandra Liu serves as the plan fiduciary for a $12 million ERISA 401(k) plan covering 180 employees of a mid-sized manufacturing company. The plan's existing recordkeeper charges 25 basis points and offers a menu of 18 institutional-class funds. Liu's brother-in-law, Thomas Reeves, is a regional sales director at Apex Retirement Services. Without conducting an RFP or benchmarking analysis against other providers, Liu moves the plan to Apex, which charges 55 basis points for recordkeeping and offers a more limited menu of 12 funds, several of which are retail share classes with higher expense ratios. Liu documents that Apex offers 'superior participant education webinars' but does not evaluate whether the increased cost — approximately $36,000 more per year to participants — is justified by the claimed service enhancement. Liu does not disclose her familial relationship with Reeves to the plan's investment committee.

QUESTION
--------
Does the plan fiduciary's selection of the new recordkeeper satisfy ERISA's fiduciary duties of prudence and loyalty?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of ERISA §404(a)(1)(A) and (B) — the duties of loyalty and prudence — and likely constitutes a prohibited transaction under ERISA §406(b).

ERISA §404(a)(1)(B) requires fiduciaries to act with the care, skill, prudence, and diligence that a prudent person would use, which includes conducting a thorough process — such as an RFP or benchmarking study — before selecting a service provider. Liu's failure to conduct any competitive analysis and her selection of a more expensive provider with fewer investment options does not reflect a prudent process. Additionally, ERISA §404(a)(1)(A) requires fiduciaries to act solely in the interest of participants, and ERISA §406(b) prohibits self-dealing. The undisclosed familial relationship with the service provider's sales director creates a clear conflict of interest that was never disclosed to the investment committee, undermining the loyalty obligation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
ERISA §404(a)(1)(A)–(B) (Duties of Loyalty and Prudence); ERISA §406(b) (Prohibited Transactions – Self-Dealing); IRC §4975

KEY FACTOR
----------
The fiduciary selected a costlier recordkeeper without a competitive process and failed to disclose a familial relationship with the provider, violating both the prudence and loyalty duties under ERISA §404(a).
