Source Folder: 08_State_Fiduciary_Rules
Topic: state_vs_federal_standards
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A dual-registered financial professional (both a broker-dealer representative and an investment adviser representative) operates in Massachusetts, which imposes a fiduciary duty on broker-dealers under 950 CMR 12.207. The representative recommends that Rosa Delgado, a 55-year-old municipal employee with $180,000 in savings, roll her 403(b) plan into a brokerage IRA and invest the proceeds into a proprietary high-yield bond fund that carries a 1.85% expense ratio and a 4.5% front-end load. A substantially similar third-party bond fund is available with a 0.65% expense ratio and no load. The representative's firm receives revenue-sharing payments from the proprietary fund. The representative discloses the conflict on a Form CRS but does not mitigate it, and recommends the proprietary fund citing its 'strong internal research team' without documenting why it is superior to the lower-cost alternative.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the Massachusetts fiduciary standard applicable to broker-dealers under 950 CMR 12.207?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates the Massachusetts fiduciary standard for broker-dealers under 950 CMR 12.207.

Massachusetts 950 CMR 12.207 imposes a fiduciary duty on broker-dealers and their agents, requiring that recommendations be in the client's best interest and that material conflicts of interest be mitigated, not merely disclosed. Recommending a high-cost proprietary fund when a substantially similar, significantly cheaper alternative exists—while the firm receives revenue-sharing payments—represents an unmitigated conflict of interest. Mere disclosure on Form CRS is insufficient under the Massachusetts standard, which exceeds the federal Reg BI disclosure-only approach and requires actual mitigation or elimination of the conflict.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Massachusetts 950 CMR 12.207 – Fiduciary Duty of Broker-Dealers and Agents

KEY FACTOR
----------
The representative failed to mitigate a material conflict of interest arising from revenue sharing on a proprietary fund recommendation, which Massachusetts's fiduciary standard—unlike federal Reg BI—requires beyond mere disclosure.
