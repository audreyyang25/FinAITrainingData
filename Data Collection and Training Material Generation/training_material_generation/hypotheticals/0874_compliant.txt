Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Midwest Federal Savings Association, operating under OCC trust authority extended to federal savings associations as of January 6, 2012, serves as directed trustee for a $14.2 million irrevocable family trust in which the trust protector — an independent attorney appointed by the grantor — holds exclusive investment direction authority under the trust agreement. The trust protector directs the bank to liquidate $2.1 million in diversified equity holdings and reinvest the proceeds in a concentrated position in a single publicly traded energy company. The bank's trust investment officer believes the concentration is imprudent and documents her concern in a formal written objection filed in the trust's administrative record. After confirming that the trust protector holds valid, unrestricted investment direction authority under the governing instrument and applicable state law, and that the energy company is not a prohibited investment under ERISA or any applicable regulation, the bank executes the directed transaction while preserving its written objection in the trust file.

QUESTION
--------
Is the bank's execution of the trust protector's investment direction — over the trust officer's documented prudence objection — compliant with OCC fiduciary standards for directed trustees?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

OCC fiduciary guidance recognizes that the trustee's obligations must be read in light of the governing trust instrument, and where a trust protector or co-fiduciary holds exclusive investment direction authority under a valid governing document and applicable state law, the directed trustee's duty to follow those directions — rather than to exercise independent investment discretion — is itself the fiduciary obligation. The OTS Trust Handbook Section 110 places compliance with the provisions of the governing instrument at the apex of a trustee's duties, and the bank correctly subordinated its investment judgment to the trust protector's authority. The trust officer's act of filing a written objection in the administrative record demonstrates appropriate risk documentation and protects the bank from liability for following a lawful direction it believed imprudent. Because the directed investment involved no ERISA prohibited transaction and no regulatory prohibition, the bank's conduct — including both the written objection and the ultimate execution — reflects sound directed-trustee practice.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
OCC Comptroller's Handbook – Asset Management (2023), Fiduciary Services; OTS Trust Handbook Section 110 – Fiduciary Duties (provisions of the governing instrument); Common Law Directed Trustee Doctrine; Applicable State Trust Code (Uniform Trust Code § 808)

KEY FACTOR
----------
A directed trustee that executes a lawful investment direction from a trust protector with exclusive authority under the governing instrument, while preserving a documented prudence objection, fulfills rather than breaches its OCC fiduciary duties.
