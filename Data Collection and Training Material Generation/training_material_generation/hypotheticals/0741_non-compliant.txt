ID: 741
Source Folder: 12_FINRA_Arbitration_Awards
Topic: arbitration_liability
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Fortune Financial Services registered representative Mark Hallen recommended that his 74-year-old retired client, Dorothy Schultz, exchange her existing deferred variable annuity for a new deferred variable annuity with a 7-year surrender period. The new annuity had higher internal expenses (2.35% vs. 1.80%) and the client incurred a $14,200 surrender charge on the existing annuity. Hallen earned a 5.5% commission on the new product. The firm's supervisory system for reviewing variable annuity exchanges consisted solely of a single principal reviewing a one-page switch form with no documentation of the comparative cost analysis, and this system had been in place without updates from July 2016 through July 2021.

QUESTION
--------
Did the firm's supervisory system and the representative's recommendation of the variable annuity exchange comply with FINRA rules?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct involves both a suitability violation and a supervisory failure.

Under FINRA Rule 2330, a registered representative recommending a variable annuity exchange must have a reasonable basis to believe the exchange is suitable, considering the surrender charges incurred, the new surrender period, and comparative costs. Here, the client incurred substantial surrender charges and moved into a higher-cost product with no documented benefit. Additionally, as seen in the Fortune Financial Services AWC, the firm's supervisory system lacked adequate written supervisory procedures for reviewing variable annuity exchanges over a multi-year period, violating FINRA Rules 3110 and 3120.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability); FINRA Rule 2330 (Variable Annuity Exchanges); FINRA Rules 3110 and 3120 (Supervisory Systems)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The variable annuity exchange imposed higher costs and a new surrender period on an elderly retired client with no documented comparative benefit, compounded by the firm's inadequate supervisory system for reviewing such exchanges.
