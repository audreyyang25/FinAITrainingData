Source Folder: 12_FINRA_Arbitration_Awards
Topic: arbitration_liability
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
The Robert and Nancy Weitzman Family Trust, with $1.8 million in assets managed at UBS Financial Services, was an elderly couple's primary retirement vehicle. Their financial advisor at UBS recommended a concentrated position of 42% in a single structured product tied to emerging market debt, despite the trust's investment policy statement specifying no more than 15% in any single asset class and a moderate-conservative risk tolerance. When emerging market currencies declined sharply, the trust lost approximately $310,000 in 11 months. The advisor had received three prior compliance warnings about concentration risk in client accounts but was never formally disciplined or placed on heightened supervision. The trust filed a FINRA customer arbitration claim against UBS alleging unsuitability, breach of fiduciary duty, and failure to supervise.

QUESTION
--------
Did the advisor's recommendation to concentrate 42% of a moderate-conservative trust account in a single structured product violate suitability requirements?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. The recommendation violated both customer-specific suitability and the trust's own investment policy statement, and the firm failed to supervise a representative with a documented history of concentration risk issues.

Under FINRA Rule 2111(a), a recommendation must be suitable in light of the customer's investment profile, including risk tolerance and investment objectives. A 42% concentration in a single emerging-market structured product directly contradicts the trust's documented moderate-conservative risk tolerance and its 15% single-asset-class limit. This pattern is consistent with the type of claims seen in trust-based arbitrations such as The Robert A. Weitzel Trust v. UBS Financial Services (FINRA No. 16-03079). Additionally, the firm's failure to escalate supervision after three prior compliance warnings regarding the same advisor's concentration practices constitutes a Rule 3110 supervisory failure.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111(a) (Suitability – Customer-Specific Obligation); FINRA Rule 3110(a) (Supervision)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The advisor placed 42% of a moderate-conservative trust in a single emerging-market structured product in direct violation of the trust's written 15% concentration limit, while the firm ignored three prior compliance warnings about the same advisor's concentration practices.
