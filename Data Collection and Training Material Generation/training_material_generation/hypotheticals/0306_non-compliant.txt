Topic: ia_fee_arrangements

Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Sterling Advisory Partners managed $58 million in AUM across 140 retail and institutional clients. The adviser's standard AUM fee was 0.90% per its published Form ADV Part 2A. However, Sterling negotiated side letters with five of its largest institutional clients (each with >$4 million AUM), offering them a 0.55% AUM fee—a 39% reduction. These side letters were not disclosed in the Form ADV Part 2A or in any general brochure supplement provided to other clients. A 42-year-old retail client, Jennifer M., with $620,000 in assets, paid the full 0.90% fee ($5,580/year). When Jennifer discovered the side letter arrangement during a compliance audit (after filing a complaint with the SEC), Sterling had charged her an estimated $38,060 in excess fees over 5+ years compared to the rate offered to large institutional clients. Sterling's adviser argued that 'side letter discounts are standard practice in the industry and do not require disclosure because they are negotiated individually.'

QUESTION
--------
Did Sterling Advisory Partners violate the Advisers Act by failing to disclose side letter fee arrangements?

ANSWER
------
Yes. Section 206(2) of the Investment Adviser Act and Item 5 of Form ADV Part 2A require disclosure of all material conflicts of interest, including differential fee arrangements. The SEC has enforced this principle consistently (e.g., SEC v. Steady Growth Fund). If some clients pay materially different fees than others, and the differences are based on AUM or other factors not disclosed in the primary brochure, the adviser must either (1) disclose the variation in the brochure with a clear statement that fees are negotiable, or (2) apply the lower rate uniformly. Sterling's failure to disclose that favored clients paid 39% less constitutes a material conflict and omission under §206(2). The argument that 'side letters are standard' does not excuse the disclosure obligation—materiality does not depend on industry practice. Sterling must provide retroactive disclosures and restitution.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act §206(2); Form ADV Part 2A Item 5 (Fee Disclosure); SEC Exam Priorities (2024)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Adviser offered materially different fee rates to similarly-situated clients via undisclosed side letters, violating disclosure obligations under §206(2).
