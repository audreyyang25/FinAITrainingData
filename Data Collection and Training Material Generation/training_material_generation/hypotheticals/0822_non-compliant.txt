ID: 822
Source Folder: 16_Congressional_Materials
Topic: gao_findings
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A dually registered financial advisor (registered as both a broker-dealer representative and an investment adviser) manages a $450,000 brokerage account for a 58-year-old engineer approaching retirement. Over 14 months, the advisor executes 47 transactions in individual equity securities, generating $18,700 in commissions, while the account's annualized turnover ratio reaches 6.2 and the cost-to-equity ratio reaches 19.8%. GAO reports examining Reg BI implementation flagged dual registrant conduct as a high-risk area, noting that switching between BD and IA hats without clear disclosure obscures which standard of conduct applies at any given moment. The advisor's Form CRS discloses dual registration in general terms but does not explain to the client, at the time of specific recommendations, whether the advisor is acting as a broker or an adviser, or which standard governs each transaction.

QUESTION
--------
Does the dual registrant's trading pattern and failure to clarify the applicable standard of conduct at the time of each recommendation comply with Reg BI and Form CRS obligations?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Reg BI's Care Obligation and Form CRS relationship summary disclosure requirements.

A cost-to-equity ratio of 19.8% and annualized turnover of 6.2 are hallmark indicators of excessive trading (churning) that fails the Care Obligation's requirement that each recommendation reflect the customer's best interest considering costs. GAO findings on Reg BI implementation specifically highlighted that dual registrants frequently failed to disclose which hat they were wearing at the point of recommendation, creating investor confusion about the applicable standard of conduct. Form CRS requires advisors to explain the nature of the relationship, but boilerplate general disclosure does not satisfy the obligation to clarify the governing standard at the moment of individual recommendations. The cumulative costs imposed on a pre-retirement investor with a moderate risk profile are objectively inconsistent with the client's best interest.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Care Obligation; SEC Rule 17a-14 – Form CRS; Dodd-Frank Act §913(g) (authority to establish standards for personalized investment advice)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Excessive trading costs combined with failure to identify the applicable standard of conduct at the point of each recommendation render the dual registrant's conduct non-compliant under both Reg BI and Form CRS.
