Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A registered representative recommends a $95,000 investment in an interval fund (Gladstone Capital, closed-end fund with redemption gates) to a 59-year-old client with $540,000 in assets and a stated 10-year time horizon to retirement. The rep's memo documents: 'Client interviewed regarding liquidity needs over next 10 years. Client confirmed: no major home/health expenses anticipated; pension income begins at 65; current emergency fund ($45,000) covers 18 months expenses. Interval fund recommended because: (1) 8.2% distribution yield addresses client's stated income goal, significantly above comparable alternatives (bond ETFs 3.1%, dividend stock funds 2.4%); (2) 10-year horizon permits client to weather redemption gates (typically allow 5% quarterly redemptions); (3) advisor compared to alternatives and quantified yield differential: 5.1% higher annual income = $4,845/year; (4) client acknowledges redemption constraints in signed acknowledgment. Fund expense ratio is 1.45%; advisory fee $0.' The rep receives no additional commission for the interval fund recommendation.

QUESTION
--------
Did the representative comply with Reg BI's care obligation when recommending an illiquid interval fund?

ANSWER
------
Compliant. The representative established a clear, documented reasonable basis for the recommendation: (1) specific client need (income yield of 8.2%) tied to a measurable alternative (bond ETFs at 3.1%, stock dividend funds at 2.4%); (2) verification that the client's liquidity profile (10-year horizon, $45k emergency fund, pension income at 65) supports the illiquidity constraints of the interval fund (redemption gates allowing 5% quarterly); (3) explicit comparison of costs and yield benefits quantified in dollars ($4,845/year advantage); (4) absence of advisor compensation conflicts (no extra commission). The client's signed acknowledgment of redemption constraints further demonstrates informed consent. While interval funds are complex and illiquid, Reg BI does not prohibit recommending them; rather, advisors must document that the recommendation is in the client's interest given the client's specific circumstances. This case demonstrates care by analyzing the critical tension (illiquidity vs. yield) and resolving it with client-specific facts, not generic marketing.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Care Obligation, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i); reasonable basis standard permits illiquid products if client profile and documented analysis support it

KEY FACTOR
----------
Representative documented specific liquidity analysis, quantified yield advantage, and verified client's 10-year horizon and emergency reserves adequately support interval fund illiquidity constraints.
