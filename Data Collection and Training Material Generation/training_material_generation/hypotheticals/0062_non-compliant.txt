Topic: ia_fiduciary_duty
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An RIA advises a 72-year-old widow with $890K in liquid savings and a $180K annual pension. Her stated goal is capital preservation and monthly income to supplement her pension. The RIA recommends she allocate 60% ($534K) to structured notes with 8-year maturities, 3.5% coupons, and embedded leverage on emerging market currency baskets. The RIA earns a 2.25% placement fee from the issuing bank on structured note sales. The RIA provides only a brief one-page summary of risks ('complex instrument, potential for loss') and does not explain the currency risk, leverage mechanics, or liquidity constraints. The client is unsophisticated in structured products and has limited investment experience.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the RIA's fiduciary duty of care?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the fiduciary duty of care and loyalty.

The recommendation fails multiple elements of the duty of care. First, it is unsuitable for the client: an unsophisticated 72-year-old seeking capital preservation and income should not hold 60% in 8-year leveraged structured notes that carry significant market, currency, and liquidity risks. Second, the adviser failed in its disclosure and explanation obligation—a complex product like this requires detailed, written risk disclosure, not a one-page summary, especially for an unsophisticated client. Third, the RIA's 2.25% placement fee creates a material conflict of interest (duty of loyalty violation) that makes the recommendation suspect; the high compensation suggests the adviser prioritized the fee over the client's profile. The 8-year maturity is also misaligned with a 72-year-old's potential liquidity needs and life expectancy concerns.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act § 206 (duty of care); SEC Rule 206(4)-1; SEC IA Exam Priorities (suitability of complex products for retail clients); Capital Gains (adviser must act in client's best interest, not adviser's)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Recommendation of a complex, illiquid product with embedded leverage to an elderly, unsophisticated client seeking income and capital preservation, combined with material adviser compensation conflict and inadequate risk disclosure.
