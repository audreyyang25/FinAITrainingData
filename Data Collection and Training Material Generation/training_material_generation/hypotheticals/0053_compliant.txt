Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An adviser manages a $2.8M portfolio for a 62-year-old client with moderate-to-aggressive risk tolerance. The adviser's investment strategy includes 15% allocation to a proprietary mutual fund (Adviser Fund A, expense ratio 0.95%) that slightly underperforms a comparable third-party index fund (expense ratio 0.25%). The adviser discloses in the advisory agreement that she manages the proprietary fund and earns advisory fees from it, and provides the client with a written side-by-side comparison of fees and performance. The client signs a written acknowledgment that he understands and accepts the conflict. The recommendation is otherwise appropriate for his risk profile and needs.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation comply with the fiduciary duty of loyalty and duty of care?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct satisfies the fiduciary duty of loyalty and duty of care under the Investment Adviser Act.

The adviser satisfies the fiduciary duty of loyalty by: (1) making full, material disclosure of the conflict in writing (ownership of and compensation from the proprietary fund); (2) providing specific performance and fee comparisons so the client can make an informed decision; (3) obtaining written informed consent acknowledging the conflict. Under Capital Gains, an adviser cannot avoid conflicts, but must disclose them completely and allow the client to consent freely. The underperformance and higher fee are disclosed, not hidden. The 15% allocation to the proprietary fund is not unreasonable given it aligns with the client's risk tolerance, and the majority of the portfolio (85%) can include other vehicles. The adviser's duty of care is also satisfied because the recommendation is appropriate for the client's age, risk tolerance, and financial objectives. Disclosure plus informed consent cures the potential conflict.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act §206(2); SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau, 375 U.S. 180 (1963) (conflicts may be permissible if disclosed and client consents); Reg. BI §11b-1(b)(1) (best interest standard requires consideration of conflicts of interest)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Full written disclosure of the conflict combined with clear performance/fee comparison and documented client consent permits the adviser to recommend the proprietary fund despite the competing interest.
