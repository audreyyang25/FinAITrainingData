FACTS
-----
A 45-year-old employee with $180,000 in a 401(k) and high risk tolerance seeks to invest an additional $25,000 in equities. A broker-dealer advisor recommends a portfolio of three low-cost index funds (total ER 0.08%) consisting of 60% U.S. equity, 25% international equity, and 15% emerging markets. The advisor documents: (1) the client's risk tolerance and 20-year time horizon; (2) a comparison to two alternative allocations (40/40/20 and 70/20/10); (3) fee analysis showing the selected portfolio's 0.08% ER is in the lowest quartile; and (4) the absence of conflicts of interest (no proprietary products recommended).

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the Reg BI care obligation?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this satisfies the care obligation under Reg BI §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i).

The advisor satisfied each element of the care obligation. The recommendation is aligned with the client's 20-year time horizon and high risk tolerance (aggressive equity allocation is suitable). Costs are exceptionally low (0.08% ER) and the advisor documented a comparative analysis of alternative allocations, demonstrating thoughtful consideration of risk/return trade-offs. The absence of proprietary or high-commission products removes conflicts. Documentation shows the advisor evaluated rewards (equity upside potential over 20 years) and risks (volatility, sequence-of-returns risk). This fact pattern reflects best practices: transparent documentation, cost-conscious product selection, alternatives analysis, and absence of conflicts.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Regulation Best Interest §240.15l-1(a)(2)(i) — care obligation to consider the costs, risks, rewards, and alternatives in light of the retail customer's profile.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Comprehensive documentation of alternatives analysis, explicit cost comparison, alignment with client profile, and absence of conflicted incentives all demonstrate genuine care.
