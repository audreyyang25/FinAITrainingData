Topic: predispute_arbitration_clause_waiver_of_class_action
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Meridian Wealth Advisors requires all retail brokerage customers to sign a predispute arbitration agreement that includes a clause explicitly waiving the customer's right to participate in any class action lawsuit or class arbitration for any securities-related dispute, including claims under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. The firm's compliance officer reviewed the clause and approved it as standard industry practice. A customer who suffered losses in a Ponzi scheme structured product later sought to join a class action but was barred by the clause.

QUESTION
--------
Is the predispute arbitration clause's class action waiver enforceable with respect to claims brought under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of Securities Exchange Act Section 29(a) and established investor protection principles.

Section 29(a) of the Securities Exchange Act voids any condition, stipulation, or provision that binds a person to waive compliance with any provision of the Act. Courts and PIABA have argued that class action waivers embedded in predispute arbitration clauses may effectively strip investors of substantive rights under the Exchange Act when individual claims are too small to litigate separately. Additionally, FINRA Rule 12204 expressly prohibits class action claims from being arbitrated under the FINRA Code, but does not permit firms to contractually bar investors from pursuing class actions in court.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Securities Exchange Act of 1934 §29(a); Shearson/American Express Inc. v. McMahon, 482 U.S. 220 (1987); FINRA Rule 12204

KEY FACTOR
----------
The clause impermissibly waives the investor's right to pursue class action litigation in court for Exchange Act claims, violating Section 29(a).
