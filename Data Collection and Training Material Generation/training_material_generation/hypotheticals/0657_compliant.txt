Topic: pte_2020_02
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
James Whitfield, a registered representative at a dually registered broker-dealer/RIA, recommends that Robert Chen, a recently retired 67-year-old engineer with a $520,000 traditional IRA, reallocate from a money market fund into a diversified portfolio of proprietary mutual funds managed by the firm's affiliated asset manager. The proprietary funds carry expense ratios ranging from 0.55% to 0.72%, which are lower than comparable third-party actively managed funds but higher than passive index alternatives. James earns a modest 25-basis-point trail commission on the proprietary funds. Before making the recommendation, James documents a detailed analysis showing that Robert's money market position yields effectively 0% real return after inflation, that Robert needs growth to sustain a 30-year retirement, and that the proprietary funds have consistently ranked in the top quartile of their Morningstar categories over 10 years. The firm maintains written policies and procedures under PTE 2020-02, disclosed all material conflicts including the affiliation and trail compensation in writing to Robert before the transaction, conducted a retrospective compliance review of James's rollover and reallocation recommendations in the prior quarter, and James acknowledged the firm's fiduciary status in the written disclosure.

QUESTION
--------
Does the advisor's recommendation of proprietary mutual funds for the IRA, which generate trail compensation for the advisor, comply with PTE 2020-02 despite the inherent conflict of interest?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

PTE 2020-02 does not prohibit conflicted compensation or recommendations of proprietary products; rather, it requires that such recommendations satisfy the Impartial Conduct Standards, meaning the advice must be in the retirement investor's best interest, compensation must be reasonable, and the fiduciary must not make materially misleading statements. Here, the advisor documented a thorough analysis demonstrating the suitability and best-interest basis for the recommendation (addressing Robert's need for real returns over a long retirement horizon), the proprietary funds were competitively priced relative to comparable active alternatives and had strong track records, and the firm made full written disclosure of the affiliated relationship and compensation. The firm's written policies, retrospective review program, and acknowledgment of fiduciary status satisfy PTE 2020-02's additional procedural conditions, thereby preserving the exemption despite the surface appearance of conflict.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
DOL PTE 2020-02 – Impartial Conduct Standards (Best Interest, Reasonable Compensation, No Misleading Statements); Sections II(a)-(d) including Policies and Procedures and Retrospective Review Requirements

KEY FACTOR
----------
The advisor's thorough documentation of the best-interest rationale, full written disclosure of the proprietary fund affiliation and compensation, and the firm's retrospective review program satisfied all PTE 2020-02 conditions despite the conflicted compensation arrangement.
