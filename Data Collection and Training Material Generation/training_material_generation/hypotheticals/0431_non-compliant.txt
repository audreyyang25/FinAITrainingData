Topic: ai_robo_adviser
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
RoboWealth Inc. operates a fully automated robo-adviser platform serving 12,000 retail clients. The platform's algorithm was designed to allocate all client portfolios into a single basket of 5 proprietary ETFs managed by RoboWealth's affiliate, RoboWealth Asset Management. The ETFs carry expense ratios of 0.89%, 0.92%, 0.87%, 0.91%, and 0.88% respectively. The algorithm does not evaluate alternative low-cost index ETFs (e.g., Vanguard, iShares with ER of 0.03%-0.15%). No disclosure of the affiliate relationship appears in the account opening documents or ongoing reporting. A client, Sarah Chen, age 34, invested $150,000. Over 18 months, she paid approximately $2,145 in excess fees attributable solely to the ER differential. Sarah had no opportunity to opt out or review the recommendation before execution.

QUESTION
--------
Did RoboWealth's robo-adviser algorithm comply with Reg BI's conflict-of-interest disclosure requirement and the duty of loyalty under IA Act Section 206?

ANSWER
------
No. Under Reg BI 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(1), an adviser must disclose all material conflicts of interest in a manner that enables clients to understand the nature and severity of conflicts. Routing all clients into affiliated products with ERs 6-30x higher than available alternatives constitutes a material conflict (quantifiable economic incentive to recommend affiliate products). The undisclosed affiliate relationship violates both Reg BI disclosure and IA Act §206(1) duty of loyalty. SEC IM Guidance 2017-02 emphasizes that robo-advisers must have adequate oversight mechanisms to prevent algorithmic bias toward proprietary products. The absence of any alternative-product analysis or client opt-out mechanism constitutes a breach.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Reg BI Conflict Disclosure, 17 CFR §240.15l-1(a)(1); IA Act §206(1) Duty of Loyalty; SEC IM Guidance 2017-02 (Feb. 2017)

KEY FACTOR
----------
Undisclosed algorithmic steering into affiliated high-fee products without alternative analysis or client choice triggers both Reg BI and fiduciary duty violations.
