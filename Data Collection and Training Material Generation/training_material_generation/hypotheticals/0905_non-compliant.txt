Source Folder: 20_SEC_ALJ_OALJ_Decisions
Topic: alj_ia_violations
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Horton Capital Management, LLC, a registered investment adviser, had a written supervisory policy requiring all client account reviews to be documented monthly and all unusual trading activity to be escalated to the Chief Compliance Officer within 48 hours. The firm's principal, Dale Horton, was aware that a supervised associate, Marcus Webb, was recommending a proprietary variable annuity product to elderly, low-income clients with fixed Social Security income averaging $1,800 per month and liquid assets under $50,000. Over 14 months, Webb placed 23 clients into 7-year surrender-period annuities with a 7% back-end load without conducting written suitability analyses. Horton reviewed monthly account summaries but took no action despite repeated red flags, including surrender charges appearing in client statements. The firm earned $187,000 in undisclosed compensation from the annuity issuer during this period.

QUESTION
--------
Did Horton Capital Management, LLC and its principal Dale Horton satisfy their supervisory obligations under the Investment Advisers Act?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a failure of supervisory obligation and undisclosed conflict of interest under the Investment Advisers Act.

As illustrated in SEC Initial Decision Release No. 1414 concerning Horter Investment Management, a principal who is aware of red flags indicating unsuitable recommendations by supervised personnel but fails to act violates supervisory obligations under the Advisers Act. The receipt of $187,000 in undisclosed compensation from the annuity issuer constitutes a material conflict of interest that must be disclosed to clients under §206(2) of the Advisers Act. The ALJ in Horter imposed third-tier civil penalties of $250,000 on the firm and $125,000 on the principal for substantially similar supervisory failures. Elderly clients with limited fixed income placed in long-surrender-period products without written suitability analysis further compounds the violation.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Advisers Act of 1940 §§203(e), 203(f); SEC Initial Decision Release No. 1414 (Horter Investment Management)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The principal's deliberate inaction in the face of documented red flags, combined with receipt of undisclosed compensation from the annuity issuer, constitutes both a supervisory failure and a fraudulent omission under the Advisers Act.
