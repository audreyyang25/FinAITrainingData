Source Folder: unknown
Topic: concentration
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 35-year-old high-net-worth entrepreneur with $5 million in investable assets and aggressive risk tolerance meets with her advisor. She owns a private business worth $8 million and has substantial real estate. She requests that her advisor build a concentrated portfolio strategy allocating 30% ($1.5M) to emerging market growth equities and 25% ($1.25M) to technology sector ETFs. The advisor documents her risk tolerance, investment timeline (20+ years), and explains the concentration and sector risk. The client executes with full understanding and a signed investment policy statement reflecting this strategy.

QUESTION
--------
Does this concentrated allocation recommendation comply with suitability and fiduciary standards?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this is compliant.

The advisor has satisfied the suitability standard by: (1) assessing the client's profile — aggressive risk tolerance, 20+ year horizon, and diversified net worth (business and real estate reduce overall portfolio concentration risk); (2) documenting the client-specific basis for the concentrated strategy through an investment policy statement; (3) explaining the concentration and sector risks; and (4) obtaining informed consent. The 30% + 25% = 55% concentration in growth/emerging/tech is defensible for a 35-year-old aggressive investor with substantial outside assets and long time horizon. The advisor's documentation and communication satisfy the duty of care under both FINRA Rule 2111 and SEC Reg BI.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 (suitability; customer-specific basis includes financial situation, investment objectives, and profile); SEC Reg BI Rule 15l-1(a)(1) (care obligation satisfied by understanding client circumstances).

KEY FACTOR
----------
Client sophistication, time horizon, diversified net worth outside the advisory account, documented risk tolerance profile, and signed IPS transform high concentration into compliant advisory practice.
