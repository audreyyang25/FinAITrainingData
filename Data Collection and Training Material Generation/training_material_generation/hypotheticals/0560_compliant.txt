Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Emerson-Pacific Securities, LLC, a dual-registered broker-dealer and investment adviser, delivers its Form CRS to a prospective client, Margaret Chen, age 66, a recently retired engineer with $1.2 million in rollover IRA assets. The Form CRS clearly describes: (1) the material differences between brokerage and advisory services offered by the firm; (2) the firm's fee structures for both relationships, including that brokerage accounts pay per-transaction commissions while advisory accounts pay a 1.10% annual wrap fee; (3) that the firm's representatives have a financial incentive to recommend the advisory relationship because it generates ongoing revenue; (4) that the firm was the subject of a 2022 state regulatory action resulting in a fine for late filing of certain disclosure documents, with a hyperlink to the firm's BrokerCheck and IAPD pages; and (5) the required conversation starters. Margaret, after reviewing the Form CRS and discussing both options with her representative, elects the advisory wrap account. The representative documents that the wrap fee is cost-effective given Margaret's anticipated rebalancing frequency of quarterly trades across a diversified portfolio and her preference for ongoing financial planning.

QUESTION
--------
Did Emerson-Pacific satisfy its Form CRS delivery and content requirements, including disclosure of disciplinary history and conflicts, even though the client ultimately chose the relationship that generates more ongoing revenue for the firm?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this conduct is compliant.

Form CRS requires a dual registrant to describe the differences between brokerage and advisory services, fee structures, conflicts of interest including financial incentives favoring one relationship type, and the firm's disciplinary history, along with conversation starters to help investors evaluate the relationship. Emerson-Pacific's Form CRS addressed each of these elements, including honest disclosure of its own regulatory history and the financial incentive to recommend the advisory relationship. The client's informed decision to elect the advisory account, combined with the representative's documented cost-effectiveness analysis supporting the wrap fee given anticipated trading frequency and ongoing planning needs, demonstrates compliance with both the Disclosure and Care Obligations. The fact that the chosen relationship generates more ongoing revenue for the firm does not, by itself, create a violation when the conflict is disclosed and the recommendation is supported by reasonable analysis.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Form CRS (Rule 17a-14); SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i) – Reg BI Disclosure Obligation; SEC Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(ii) – Reg BI Care Obligation

KEY FACTOR
----------
The firm delivered a complete Form CRS with all required content—including its own disciplinary history and the financial incentive to recommend the advisory relationship—and the representative documented a reasonable basis for the client's chosen relationship type.
