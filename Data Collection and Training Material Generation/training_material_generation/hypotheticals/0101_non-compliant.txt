Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 72-year-old widow received a $400,000 insurance proceeds check and opened a brokerage account. Her account profile noted 'moderate risk tolerance' and 'income needed within 5 years.' Over 18 months, the broker executed 34 trades in her account (turnover ratio 2.1x), including frequent switches between similarly-positioned large-cap value funds and multiple purchases of speculative penny stocks. Average holding period was 3.2 months. The broker earned $8,400 in commissions (2.1% of assets annually). No documentation exists showing analysis of the client's investment objectives or rationale for the trading frequency.

QUESTION
--------
Does this conduct satisfy FINRA Rule 2111 suitability?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the suitability standard.

This conduct violates the quantitative (churning) component of Rule 2111. A 72-year-old widow who stated she needed income within 5 years has no investment objective compatible with 34 trades generating 2.1x annual turnover. The frequent switching between similar large-cap funds and speculative penny-stock purchases are not coordinated with her moderate risk tolerance or income needs. The 2.1% annual commission rate (compared to a typical 0.3-0.5% for passive management) suggests the primary beneficiary of the trading is the broker, not the client. The complete absence of written rationale for the trading strategy demonstrates lack of reasonable basis. FINRA cases consistently find suitability violations when portfolio turnover ratios exceed 1.5x annually without documented justification tied to the customer's objectives (In re Shearson, 2007).

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2111 requires reasonable basis suitability and customer-specific suitability. The quantitative component prohibits recommending a level of trading inconsistent with the customer's financial situation and needs (churning rule, In re Shearson Lehman Brothers Inc., 2007 FINRA Disciplinary Decision).

KEY FACTOR
----------
A 2.1x turnover ratio generating $8,400 in annual commissions for a 72-year-old income-focused investor, without documented investment rationale, evidences excessive trading unsuitable for the customer's age and objectives.
