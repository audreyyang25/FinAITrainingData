Verdict: COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 72-year-old widow with $850,000 in a non-qualified account, moderate-to-conservative risk tolerance, and a 10-year investment horizon was advised to retain a concentrated 22% position in a single large-cap dividend stock (her deceased husband's original purchase, worth $187,000). The advisor documented in writing that: (1) the client explicitly stated she wanted to keep the stock for 'sentimental reasons and to avoid triggering capital gains'; (2) the advisor explained the concentration risk in detail; (3) the advisor modeled portfolio impact if the position were diversified; (4) the client signed a statement confirming she understood the risks and preferred to retain it; (5) the advisor conducted annual reviews reaffirming this decision; and (6) the account otherwise was well-diversified across 28 holdings.

QUESTION
--------
Does retention of this concentrated position satisfy the advisor's fiduciary duty under Reg BI and the Investment Adviser Act despite the concentration risk?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this is compliant.

Under Reg BI and the fiduciary standard, advisors are not prohibited from accommodating client preferences that create concentration, provided: (1) the advisor provides clear, documented disclosure of the risks; (2) the advisor obtains informed client consent after the client has had opportunity to understand the tradeoffs; (3) the advisor does not have an undisclosed conflict of interest driving the recommendation; and (4) the advisor periodically reviews the strategy. Here, all four elements are satisfied. The advisor documented the client's explicit preference, explained concentration risk, modeled alternatives, obtained signed acknowledgment, and annually reconfirmed. The 22% concentration in a high-quality dividend stock in an otherwise diversified 30-holding portfolio for a 72-year-old is not inherently unsuitable; the tax-deferral benefit and client control are legitimate factors. The advisor's duty of care is satisfied by the documented process; the duty of loyalty is satisfied because no conflict of interest exists (the advisor gains no additional compensation from holding the stock). This represents compliant edge-case handling of client preference within fiduciary bounds.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Regulation Best Interest (Rule 15l-1), Investment Adviser Act Section 206 fiduciary duty (duty of loyalty and care), and FINRA Rule 2111 (Suitability)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The advisor's comprehensive written documentation of the client's stated preference, the risks of concentration, alternative strategies, and annual reconfirmation of the decision transforms what appears to be a risky concentration into a documented client-directed, advisor-monitored arrangement consistent with fiduciary duty.
