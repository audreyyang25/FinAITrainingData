Topic: discovery_abuse_document_withholding
Difficulty: hard
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
During a FINRA arbitration proceeding, the respondent broker-dealer Crestline Securities produced a document index in response to the claimant's discovery requests but systematically withheld internal supervisory review emails and branch manager communications that directly addressed the registered representative's trading pattern in the claimant's account. Crestline's counsel characterized the withheld documents as protected by attorney-client privilege without providing a privilege log or any in camera submission to the arbitration panel. The panel chairperson requested a privilege log twice, and Crestline failed to comply within the ordered deadline.

QUESTION
--------
Did Crestline Securities comply with its discovery obligations under the FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure when it withheld documents and failed to produce a privilege log despite panel orders?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is a violation of FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure Rules 12507 and 12511, constituting discovery abuse.

FINRA Rule 12507 requires parties to produce documents listed in the FINRA Discovery Guide and respond to additional document requests in good faith, while Rule 12511 grants panels authority to impose sanctions for failure to comply with discovery orders. Claiming attorney-client privilege without producing a privilege log and failing to comply with panel-ordered deadlines constitutes an abuse of the discovery process that prejudices the investor claimant's ability to establish supervisory failures. PIABA has documented this pattern of discovery abuse by broker-dealers as a systemic problem that distorts arbitration outcomes against retail investors.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Code of Arbitration Procedure Rules 12507, 12511, 12212; FINRA Discovery Guide

KEY FACTOR
----------
Respondent withheld material documents under a blanket privilege claim without a privilege log and defied panel discovery orders, constituting sanctionable discovery abuse.
