Source Folder: 03_FINRA_AWC
Topic: finra_awc_suitability
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
Robert Ainsley, a General Securities Representative registered with a regional broker-dealer, manages the accounts of 12 customers. From June 2023 to May 2024, Ainsley begins exercising discretion in the accounts without obtaining prior written authorization from any customer or prior written acceptance of the accounts as discretionary by his firm. Ainsley selects securities, determines quantities, and decides timing of trades on his own, sometimes informing customers after the fact. Although most of the trades are profitable, at least four customers did not authorize specific transactions. The firm's compliance manual explicitly requires written authorization for discretionary trading, and the firm had not accepted any of these accounts as discretionary. Ainsley's supervisor does not review trade blotters for patterns of discretionary activity.

QUESTION
--------
Did Ainsley violate FINRA rules by exercising discretion in customer accounts without obtaining written authorization from the customers or written acceptance from the firm?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct is not compliant. This constitutes unauthorized discretionary trading in violation of FINRA Rules 3260(b) and 2010.

FINRA Rule 3260(b) requires that no member firm or registered representative exercise any discretionary power in a customer's account unless the customer has given prior written authorization and the account has been accepted in writing by the firm. As demonstrated in the Robert Wylie Goff AWC (No. 2024082784301), FINRA consistently sanctions representatives who trade without written discretionary authorization, regardless of whether the trades are profitable. The fact that some customers may have given verbal after-the-fact approval does not satisfy the rule's written authorization requirement. The supervisor's failure to monitor for discretionary patterns is an additional supervisory concern.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 3260(b) – Discretionary Accounts; FINRA Rule 2010 – Standards of Commercial Honor and Principles of Trade

KEY FACTOR
----------
Exercising trading discretion without prior written customer authorization and written firm acceptance is a per se violation of FINRA Rule 3260(b), regardless of trade profitability.
