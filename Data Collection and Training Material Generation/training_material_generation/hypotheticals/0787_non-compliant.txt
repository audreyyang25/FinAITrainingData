Source Folder: 15_FINRA_Regulatory_Notices
Topic: senior_investor_protection
Difficulty: easy
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
An 81-year-old widow, Margaret, has a $650,000 brokerage account and has been diagnosed with early-stage Alzheimer's disease. Her financial advisor, aware of the diagnosis, continues to execute frequent trades in her account—47 trades over six months—generating $38,000 in commissions. Margaret's son contacts the firm's branch manager to express concern that his mother cannot understand the transactions being made. The branch manager acknowledges the call but takes no further action, does not place a hold on the account, does not escalate the matter, and does not contact a trusted contact person. The advisor continues trading the account for another three months.

QUESTION
--------
Did the branch manager and the firm comply with FINRA rules regarding the protection of senior investors when they failed to act on the reported concerns about the elderly client with diminished capacity?

ANSWER
------
No, this conduct violates FINRA's senior investor protection rules and supervisory obligations.

FINRA Rule 2165 permits firms to place temporary holds on disbursements when there is a reasonable belief of financial exploitation of a senior investor. The branch manager received direct notice of potential exploitation of an elderly client with diminished capacity and failed to escalate or act. Additionally, the firm's failure to utilize trusted contact person provisions under Rule 4512 and the continued unsupervised excessive trading in the account of a vulnerable senior investor reflects the exact patterns AARP and FINRA have highlighted as critical failures in senior investor protection.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
FINRA Rule 2165 (Financial Exploitation of Specified Adults); FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision); FINRA Rule 4512 (Trusted Contact Person)

KEY FACTOR
----------
The branch manager received explicit notice of potential financial exploitation of an elderly client with diminished capacity and took no protective action whatsoever, failing to utilize the tools FINRA rules provide for senior investor protection.
