Topic: ia_duty_of_care
Difficulty: medium
Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A 68-year-old client with a $450,000 IRA portfolio stated in writing that her primary objective was 'capital preservation and income' due to her imminent retirement in 18 months. The adviser recommended a concentrated position in three micro-cap growth stocks (representing 72% of the portfolio) with high volatility and no dividend yield, explicitly noting in internal emails that the client 'wants safe income but these picks will appreciate.' The adviser rebalanced the portfolio quarterly but never updated the asset allocation model to reflect the client's stated objective, despite three market downturns triggering 28% portfolio declines.

QUESTION
--------
Does the adviser's recommendation and ongoing management satisfy the Investment Adviser Act duty of care?

ANSWER
------
No, this violates the Investment Adviser Act duty of care.

The adviser's duty of care requires recommendations to be suitable and consistent with the client's stated objectives and risk profile—here explicitly documented as capital preservation and income. The micro-cap growth stock concentration directly contradicts this objective and exposes the 68-year-old to volatility incompatible with imminent retirement needs. The adviser's internal emails reveal subjective disregard for the stated objective ('wants safe income but these picks will appreciate'), indicating the recommendation was not made on a reasonable investigative basis or with adequate consideration of the client's circumstances. Although the adviser rebalanced quarterly, mechanical rebalancing without updating the underlying investment model to match the client's actual needs does not constitute adequate duty of care. Under Capital Gains Research Bureau, an adviser cannot recommend investments that conflict with the client's disclosed objectives, and failure to conduct a reasonable investigation into suitability is breach of fiduciary duty.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act of 1940, §206(1) and §206(2) (fiduciary duty of care); SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau, 375 U.S. 180 (1963) (adviser must make suitable recommendations consistent with client objectives and on reasonable investigative basis); Regulation S-V Part 1a (adviser supervisory obligation to ensure recommendations align with client profiles).

KEY FACTOR
----------
The adviser documented knowledge that the client's primary objective was capital preservation and income, yet recommended concentrated, high-volatility micro-cap growth stocks—a direct and material misalignment that the adviser's own emails prove was intentional.
