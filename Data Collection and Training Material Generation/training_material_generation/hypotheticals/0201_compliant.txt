FACTS
-----
A 51-year-old client with $520,000 in investable assets ($180,000 in a traditional IRA, $340,000 in a taxable account), moderate risk tolerance, and a 15-year horizon was advised to remain 35% in a mid-cap value fund (18% of total portfolio) and 17% in an emerging markets fund (9% of total). The advisor documented: (1) the client's stated preference for 'value and international exposure based on 30-year market-long view'; (2) analysis showing the combined 27% allocation aligned with the client's risk tolerance and time horizon; (3) annual reviews showing the allocation drifted within 2 percentage points and was rebalanced when drift exceeded 3%; (4) cost analysis showing the value fund (0.65% ER) and EM fund (0.72% ER) were in the 25th percentile of expense ratios for their categories; (5) tax-loss harvesting implementation in the taxable account when positions declined; and (6) no compensation or incentive differential between recommending this allocation versus alternatives.

QUESTION
--------
Does this allocation recommendation satisfy Reg BI and the Investment Adviser Act fiduciary standard despite the elevated concentration in non-core holdings?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this is compliant.

An allocation that concentrates 27% of assets in mid-cap value and emerging markets—both more volatile than broad index funds—is not inherently problematic under Reg BI or the Investment Adviser Act if supported by documented process and client circumstances. Here, compliance is established through: (1) documented client objective ('30-year value/international view') that rationally supports the allocation; (2) analytical justification (27% combined allocation is reasonable for a 51-year-old with 15-year horizon and moderate risk tolerance); (3) low-cost implementation (0.65%–0.72% ERs are competitive); (4) active rebalancing discipline (monitoring drift, rebalancing at 3% threshold); (5) tax-loss harvesting in taxable account (shows consideration of tax efficiency); and (6) absence of conflicts of interest (no different compensation for this versus alternative allocations). The advisor's duty of care is satisfied by the documented analysis and ongoing review. The duty of loyalty is satisfied because the allocation reflects client preference and there are no undisclosed conflicts. This is a realistic edge case: the allocation has elevated risk relative to a broad index approach, but is compliant because the advisor has documented the client preference, analytical basis, and ongoing stewardship. This contrasts with cases where advisors over-concentrate in high-fee products without justification.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Investment Adviser Act Section 206 (fiduciary duty of loyalty and care), Regulation Best Interest (Rule 15l-1), and SEC guidance on reasonableness of allocations

KEY FACTOR
----------
The advisor's documented analysis of client objectives, cost competitiveness (25th percentile ERs), disciplined rebalancing, and absence of compensation conflicts transform an allocation that concentrates 27% in value/EM holdings into a defensible, fiduciary-compliant recommendation.
