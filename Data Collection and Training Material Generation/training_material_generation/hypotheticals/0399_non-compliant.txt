Verdict: NON-COMPLIANT

FACTS
-----
A Nevada-registered financial planner (subject to Nevada fiduciary rule, NRS 645A.240) advises a 48-year-old Las Vegas resident with $380,000 in combined retirement and non-retirement assets. The planner recommends a variable annuity (VA) with a 6% surrender charge, 1.25% subaccount mortality and expense ratio, 0.85% annual advisor fee (in addition to VA costs), and a 6-year contingent deferred sales charge schedule. Commission to the planner: $18,000 (4.7% of invested amount). The client's stated objective is 'long-term growth for retirement in 12 years' with moderate risk tolerance. The planner does not disclose that Nevada law imposes a fiduciary duty, does not compare the VA to alternative products (low-cost index IRAs, ETFs), and provides no written analysis of why the VA's embedded costs (total: ~2.1% annually) serve the client's interest better than alternatives. The planner's engagement letter does not reference NRS 645A.240.

QUESTION
--------
Does the Nevada financial planner's variable annuity recommendation comply with Nevada's fiduciary rule (NRS 645A.240)?

ANSWER
------
No. Nevada's fiduciary rule (NRS 645A.240) imposes a broad fiduciary duty on financial planners to act in the client's best interest, place the client's interest first, and avoid conflicts unless fully disclosed and consented to in writing. The planner violates this duty by: (1) Recommending a high-cost VA (2.1% total annual cost) without comparing to lower-cost alternatives like IRA/index strategy (~0.10% total cost); (2) Failing to disclose the $18,000 commission (4.7%) or explain how it serves the client's interest; (3) Not disclosing Nevada fiduciary status in the engagement letter; (4) Recommending a product with liquidity constraints (6-year CDSC) when client needs are 12-year horizon (liquidity not the issue, but the excess cost is); (5) Providing no written conflict analysis or best-interest determination. The VA's 2.1% blended cost significantly underperforms a simple index-IRA strategy. Nevada's fiduciary rule requires affirmative disclosure and an evidence-based best-interest determination before recommending high-cost products.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
Nevada Financial Planner Fiduciary Rule (NRS 645A.240) — Duty to Place Client Interest First, Avoid Undisclosed Conflicts

KEY FACTOR
----------
Failure to disclose fiduciary status, $18,000 commission, and lack of written best-interest analysis against lower-cost alternatives violate Nevada's fiduciary duty for financial planners.
