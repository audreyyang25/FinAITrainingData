FACTS
-----
A 38-year-old customer with $150,000 annual income, $75,000 in savings, moderate risk tolerance, and a 25-year time horizon to retirement meets with a FINRA-registered financial advisor. The advisor recommends a diversified portfolio of 70% total stock market index ETF (expense ratio 0.03%), 25% total bond market index ETF (expense ratio 0.04%), and 5% international equity ETF (expense ratio 0.08%). The advisor documents that the 0.05% blended expense ratio is appropriate for the customer's long time horizon and moderate risk profile, explains that actively managed funds in this category charge 0.50-1.50% annually, and confirms that the recommended allocation matches the customer's stated objectives and ability to absorb market risk. The advisor charges a flat $500 annual advisory fee regardless of the amount invested or products selected.

QUESTION
--------
Does this recommendation satisfy the Reg BI care obligation?

ANSWER
------
Yes, this recommendation is compliant with the Reg BI care obligation.

This recommendation clearly satisfies the Reg BI care obligation through cost-conscious analysis. The advisor recommended index-based investments with a blended expense ratio of 0.05%, which is material (approximately 0.50-1.45% less than active management alternatives for similar asset classes). The advisor documented comparative analysis showing awareness of active management costs, selected low-cost alternatives appropriate for the customer's 25-year time horizon (where cost compounding has significant impact), and recommended a diversified allocation (70/25/5) that aligns with both moderate risk tolerance and the customer's ability to sustain this allocation through market cycles. The flat-fee compensation structure eliminates incentive to recommend higher-cost products. The recommendation and documentation affirmatively demonstrate that the advisor considered costs and alternatives in recommending products in the customer's best interest.

APPLICABLE STANDARD
-------------------
SEC Regulation Best Interest, Rule 15l-1(a)(2)(i): broker-dealer must recommend securities in the customer's best interest, considering costs, risks, rewards, and alternative investments.

KEY FACTOR
----------
Low-cost index-based allocation with documented comparison to active management costs, combined with flat-fee compensation structure that removes commission-based incentive to recommend higher-cost products.
